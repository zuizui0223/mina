# PR189 proportional-baseline correction — Beaufort Island

**Date:** 2026-10-06  
**Status:** interpretive correction made before Ross Island breeding-pair magnitudes are opened.

## Error being corrected

The first PR189 synthesis treated the fact that 97.0% of Beaufort's 2004–2010 net gain occurred in the established main colony as evidence for "intensification before expansion."

That is not a valid comparison because the main colony already held 99.05% of breeders in 2004. Under exactly proportional growth, almost all absolute gain would necessarily occur there.

## Observed counts

| unit | 2004 | 2010 | absolute change | proportional change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| established main colony | 47,725 | 63,760 | +16,035 | +33.60% |
| new subcolony | 460 | 957 | +497 | +108.04% |
| total | 48,185 | 64,717 | +16,532 | +34.31% |

## Correct proportional baseline

Starting share of new subcolony:

    460 / 48,185 = 0.0095465

If the 16,532-pair island gain were allocated in exact proportion to 2004 composition, expected gain of the new subcolony would be:

    16,532 * 0.0095465 = 157.82 pairs

Observed gain was:

    497 pairs

Therefore:

    observed / proportional-expected gain = 497 / 157.82 = 3.149

The new subcolony received about **3.15 times** the gain expected under proportional growth.

Equivalent endpoint comparison:

    expected 2010 new-subcolony count under fixed 2004 composition = 617.82
    observed 2010 count = 957
    residual = +339.18 pairs

The main colony has the balancing residual of -339.18 pairs.

## Composition and effective-number change

New-subcolony share:

- 2004: 0.9547%
- 2010: 1.4787%

Two-unit effective number:

- 2004: E = 1.019275
- 2010: E = 1.030012

Endpoint abundance–space slope:

    delta log(E) / delta log(N) = +0.03552

All three composition-based diagnostics point in the same direction: Beaufort became slightly **less concentrated** within the island while total abundance increased.

## Correct biological interpretation

The valid conclusion is not:

> established-colony intensification preceded expansion.

It is:

> the new within-island breeding unit expanded disproportionately relative to starting composition, while published individual resightings indicate that export from Beaufort to Ross Island declined after local nesting habitat increased.

Thus the interesting pattern is a change in the **spatial scale of redistribution**:

    more within-island spreading
    together with
    less between-island export

## Claim boundary

This one case does not establish that island boundaries are universally mechanistic. It establishes that Beaufort provides a natural experiment consistent with local breeding capacity modifying inter-island movement, while its internal census simultaneously shows disproportionate within-island expansion.

This correction is the basis for the V2 Ross Island and island-capacity hypotheses.
