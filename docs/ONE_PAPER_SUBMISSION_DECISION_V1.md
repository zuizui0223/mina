## The paper being submitted

The active paper is the revised cross-scale concentration manuscript.

Its empirical core is:

1. Palmer discovery of non-proportional concentration during decline.
2. Prospective Signy Adélie replication.
3. Prospective Signy chinstrap cross-species replication.
4. Frozen MAPPPD regional extension, including the three increasing networks as a central interpretive boundary.

## Central question

> **Is breeding-space concentration simply the spatial consequence of population decline, or can breeding-space organization change partly independently of population trend?**

## Central empirical answer

- Within Palmer and Signy breeding systems, concentration during decline is strongly replicated beyond proportional thinning and count error.
- At the regional MAPPPD scale, all four declining networks show the same abundance-linked concentration direction, but all three increasing networks also finish with lower effective breeding-site number.
- Therefore the regional result does not support a simple decline-only explanation.

## Theory framing

The manuscript will introduce two broad competing interpretations.

### Reversible basin / negative-density habitat use

High-quality breeding components are retained first during decline, while numerical recovery should progressively repopulate secondary components.

Qualitative prediction:

\[
N\downarrow \Rightarrow E\downarrow,
\qquad
N\uparrow \Rightarrow E\uparrow.
\]

### Socially structured habitat selection

Low-density positive social effects, conspecific attraction, public information or density-dependent settlement costs can make already occupied components attractive and generate concentration even when intrinsic habitat differences are weak.

Qualitative prediction:

\[
E\downarrow
\]

can persist during either sign of \(N\) change until crowding or saturation reverses the benefit of aggregation.

The current paper **does not identify this mechanism**. It treats socially structured habitat selection as a hypothesis generated by the regional boundary result.

## Why not title the paper as an Allee/IFD mechanism paper

The internal mechanism evidence is insufficient.

- Palmer count-space colony-size/reproductive allocation is positive in pooled analyses but strongly island-dependent and not robust to every deletion/overdispersion sensitivity.
- The repository's formal decision is that Palmer-wide positive density dependence and a general Allee-like reproductive mechanism are unsupported.
- Palmer performance-linked redistribution has a supported lag-2 association, but its prospectively frozen Signy primary replication is unsupported (beta=0.0213, p=0.279).
- Aggregate counts cannot establish individual settlement, prospecting, public-information use or movement.

Therefore the manuscript may say:

> **consistent with socially structured habitat selection**

but not:

> **social habitat selection causes the concentration pattern**.
