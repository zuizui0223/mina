# SMP spatial-recovery hysteresis novelty boundary v1

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** prospective positioning before SMP outcome opening.

## What is already established

### Recolonization is not the same as first colonization

Bled, Royle & Cam (2011, *Ecology*, doi:10.1890/10-0392.1) modeled Black-legged Kittiwake nest-site occupancy over 20 years and explicitly separated:

- persistence;
- first colonization;
- recolonization after desertion.

They also showed that these processes respond to local density, site reproductive success and neighboring reproductive success.

Therefore the present project must **not** claim novelty for distinguishing recolonization from initial colonization or for proposing that social information can affect site occupancy.

### Hysteresis in island systems is already a theoretical concept

Burger et al. (2019, *Frontiers of Biogeography*, doi:10.21425/F5FBG44383) developed a constraint-based dynamic island-biogeography model in which colonization and extinction can be controlled by different constraints, so similar current environmental conditions can produce different states depending on history.

Therefore the present project must **not** claim to discover the concept of island-biogeographic hysteresis.

### Allee-driven recolonization barriers are already predicted

Schippers et al. (2011, *Ecological Modelling*, doi:10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2011.05.022) used a spatially explicit seabird metapopulation model to show that Allee effects can lengthen recolonization times and reduce effective recolonization distance.

Therefore the project must not claim novelty for predicting that coloniality or Allee effects can slow recolonization.

### Reversible buffer effects across growth, decline and recovery are already empirically tested

Bennett et al. (2022, *Journal of Animal Ecology*, doi:10.1111/1365-2656.13674) tested site-dependent regulation in common guillemots across population increase, decline and recovery, linking site occupancy to site quality and population size.

Therefore the project must not claim novelty for simply comparing occupancy across positive and negative population phases.

## Candidate empirical novelty

The candidate contribution is narrower:

> **For the same repeatedly monitored breeding SiteID, directly pair the surrounding population state at abandonment with the state at later recolonization, then test prospectively across multiple seabird species whether recovery crosses a systematically higher threshold than collapse after controlling structured temporal alignment with the same observed MasterSite abundance trajectories.**

This is a direct empirical test of whether the recovery path retraces the collapse path at the same places.

The primary paired quantity is:

\[
H=A_{\mathrm{recolonize}}-A_{\mathrm{abandon}}.
\]

This directly measures whether the spatial pathway of population recovery retraces the spatial pathway of population loss.

## Why same-site pairing matters

Cross-site colonization/extinction comparisons can confound history with persistent differences among places.

The same-SiteID design removes stable site identity from the contrast:

\[
\text{same place} + \text{different occupancy history}.
\]

It does not remove time-varying habitat change, disturbance, predators, management or measurement change; those remain mechanism boundaries.

## Why the trajectory-drift null matters

A positive \(H\) can arise mechanically if recolonization happens later during a long-term regional recovery.

Therefore confirmatory support additionally requires the observed species-balanced \(H\) to exceed a frozen null that:

- keeps the same SiteID;
- keeps the same species × MasterSite;
- keeps the observed leave-one-SiteID-out abundance trajectory;
- keeps the exact elapsed time between abandonment and recolonization;
- moves the paired transition window to other pre-frozen eligible positions.

The result is therefore not simply a comparison of early versus late years.

## What the paper may claim if supported

Allowed:

> **Spatial recovery required a higher surrounding population state than spatial loss at the same breeding sites.**

> **Colonial population recovery did not retrace the spatial pathway of collapse.**

> **Extinction and recolonization thresholds were empirically asymmetric across multiple seabird species.**

Mechanistic interpretation:

> This pattern is consistent with biological memory such as site fidelity, social attraction or other positive feedback, but the observational test does not uniquely identify those mechanisms.

## What the paper may not claim

Do not claim:

- first demonstration that seabird recolonization differs from first colonization;
- discovery of conspecific attraction;
- discovery of Allee effects;
- discovery of island-biogeographic hysteresis;
- proof that social information causes the threshold asymmetry;
- removal of all habitat-quality confounding.

## Link to the penguin discovery

The Antarctic penguin analysis generates the hypothesis because:

- decline repeatedly concentrates breeding effort beyond proportional thinning;
- regional effective breeding-site number can remain low while abundance increases.

The SMP test is independent and stronger because it directly asks whether the **same places** are recovered at the same population state at which they were lost.

The two datasets are not pooled for inference.
