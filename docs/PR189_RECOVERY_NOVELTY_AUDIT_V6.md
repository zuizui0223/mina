# PR189 novelty audit v6 — after prospective Emperor test

**Status:** superseded by `PR189_RECOVERY_NOVELTY_AUDIT_V7.md` after calibrating the Ross effect size and distinguishing sign-unlocked dynamics from statistical independence.

## Established background

Do not claim novelty for:

- aggregate versus local recovery mismatch in metapopulations;
- abundance versus composition recovery mismatch in communities;
- spatially heterogeneous disturbance;
- patch-specific productivity/carrying capacity;
- Allee effects or hysteresis;
- Hill/Simpson effective-number metrics.

These are established concepts.

## Empirical structure now available

### Ross — direct natural disturbance/rebound

Aggregate shock loss restored by 2002:

    96.97%.

But local restoration fractions span:

    10.8% to 109.8%.

All fixed components rebound from the trough, yet the pre-shock local-abundance vector is not restored.

This is the focal recovery result.

### Bird Island Gentoo — prospective positive-net-change allocation test

Frozen endpoints:

    N +34.2%
    E +15.7%.

The full temporal audit contains all four delta-N/delta-E sign combinations.

Bird is not a preidentified disturbance-recovery phase; its role is prospective sign-decoupling evidence.

### Global Emperor — prospective decline-allocation test

Frozen 50-colony point-estimate endpoints:

    N -12.4%
    E -12.8%.

Local signs:

    30 down
    20 up.

The predeclared eight-region sensitivity contains all four N/E sign combinations.

This supplies a third species, global spatial scale and sea-ice breeding system.

## What is stronger after Emperor

The general result no longer depends on comparing one Ross recovery case with one Bird long interval.

The same non-fixed relationship appears:

- through time within Bird Island;
- among regions within Emperor penguins;
- across species/systems;
- across positive and negative aggregate change.

Therefore the robust empirical proposition is:

> **the direction of aggregate breeding-abundance change does not uniquely specify the direction of spatial reallocation among breeding nodes.**

## What remains narrower and potentially distinctive

The broad concept is not new.

The paper's useful empirical niche is:

> **within-species abundance allocation among persistent colonial breeding nodes.**

Ross adds a particularly sharp reference-state comparison:
- a documented disturbance;
- near-complete aggregate rebound;
- continued occupancy of the monitored nodes;
- every node rebounds from the trough;
- yet local deficits and overshoot leave a different abundance vector.

That differs from hidden-collapse scenarios driven mainly by patch loss/nonrecovery.

Bird and Emperor then show that the sign of spatial reallocation is conditional rather than tied to aggregate direction.

## Mathematical contribution boundary

The decomposition:

    d log N/dt = r_bar
    d log p_i/dt = r_i - r_bar

is algebra, not a novel theorem.

Its role is explanatory:
- common mode -> total abundance;
- contrast modes -> spatial composition.

The empirical contribution is showing these modes behave differently in audited colonial-breeder data.

## Uncertainty boundary for Emperor

The Emperor primary endpoint uses posterior medians of colony abundance indices.

The original global trend is uncertain, and the public colony-level output does not expose joint posterior draws needed to propagate E while retaining cross-colony covariance.

Therefore say:

> prospective point-estimate external support

not:

> statistically confirmed global decline-concentration effect.

## Strongest novelty statement

> **A colonial population can nearly regain the breeding abundance lost to a disturbance without restoring where that abundance occurs; across independently audited breeding networks, aggregate increase or decline has no fixed relationship with concentration versus equalization among breeding nodes.**

## Conservation relevance

Aggregate targets answer:

    how many breeders?

Spatial allocation answers:

    where are they concentrated?

Those questions can diverge even without patch extinction.

The practical implication is not to replace aggregate monitoring with E, but to avoid treating aggregate recovery as sufficient evidence that the breeding network has returned to its prior state.
