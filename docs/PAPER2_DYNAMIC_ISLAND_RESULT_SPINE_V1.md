# Paper 2 result spine v1 — opportunity does not guarantee use

**Date:** 2026-10-04  
**Status:** frozen H1 real outcome closed; frozen H2 marine route closed at measurement support.  
**Paper 1 remains unchanged.**

## Core island-ecology question

> **When terrestrial breeding opportunity changes through time, do Antarctic penguin populations redistribute breeding use toward the sites that gain that opportunity?**

The Antarctic system makes this question unusually clean because the physical breeding landscape itself changes on ecological timescales. Summer-exposed terrestrial opportunity can increase or decrease while penguin breeding sites remain embedded in a separately changing marine matrix.

The key distinction is:

\[
\text{physical opportunity} \neq \text{realized population use}.
\]

## 1. The breeding landscape is physically dynamic

The outcome-blind remote-sensing pipeline measured persistent summer-exposed terrestrial opportunity inside fixed site-centered support before the new demographic association was opened.

Across the frozen measurement roster:

- **77 physical sites**;
- **57 positive**, **16 negative**, and **4 zero** primary exposure changes;
- median primary change \(\Delta A_{\mathrm{available}}=+0.03046\);
- six represented APBP regions.

Regional pattern:

- Central-west Antarctic Peninsula: 24 positive, 5 negative, 4 zero; median +0.0302;
- South Shetland Islands: **16/16 positive**; median +0.2750;
- Victoria Land: 13 positive, 11 negative; median +0.00462;
- Southwest Antarctic Peninsula: 2/2 positive;
- Elephant Island: 1/1 positive;
- Northeast Antarctic Peninsula: 1/1 positive.

For the outcome-blind Adélie subset:

- 37 sites;
- 25 positive and 12 negative primary changes;
- p50/p67 sign agreement at 28/37 sites;
- p50/p67 correlation \(r=0.832\);
- robust same-direction classification: 22 increase, 6 decrease, 9 threshold-sensitive.

The Beaufort Island positive control recovered increased summer exposure under both frozen thresholds.

Thus terrestrial opportunity is neither static nor nearly invariant. There is enough longitudinal signal for a genuine change-to-change ecological test.

**Boundary:** the metric is persistent summer exposure inside fixed current Antarctic Ecosystem Inventory support. It is not literal total newly deglaciated nestable rock.

## 2. Frozen H1: do penguins track that changing opportunity?

The frozen H1 analysis used:

- **85 site × species units**;
- **1,293 site-season observations**;
- six supported species × region networks.

The model removed:

- site mean differences;
- contemporaneous species-specific region × season state.

The focal coefficient therefore asks whether sites with greater terrestrial-opportunity gain gained breeding use **relative to contemporaneous conspecific sites in the same regional network**.

Inference used 9,999 within-species × region exposure permutations and a frozen Holm correction across the three species.

### Adélie penguin

Primary p50 exposure:

\[
\beta_A = +0.0713
\]

- one-sided permutation \(p=0.0624\);
- Holm-adjusted \(p=0.1872\);
- precision-weighted \(\beta_A=+0.0776\).

Frozen p67 sensitivity:

\[
\beta_A = +0.0350
\]

- permutation \(p=0.1683\);
- Holm-adjusted \(p=0.5049\);
- precision-weighted \(\beta_A=+0.0378\).

**Interpretation:** Adélie shows a bounded positive directional tendency, but it does not satisfy the prespecified confirmatory criterion and weakens under the frozen measurement sensitivity.

Do not call this a positive confirmatory result.

### Chinstrap penguin

Primary:

\[
\beta_A=-0.0258
\]

- permutation \(p=0.5627\);
- Holm \(p=0.869\);
- precision-weighted \(\beta_A=-0.0208\).

The p67 sensitivity changes sign to \(+0.0584\), but remains unsupported.

**Interpretation:** no stable opportunity-tracking direction is supported.

### Gentoo penguin

Primary:

\[
\beta_A=+0.00853
\]

- permutation \(p=0.4345\);
- Holm \(p=0.869\);
- precision-weighted \(\beta_A=+0.0140\).

The p67 sensitivity is effectively zero and slightly negative.

**Interpretation:** no opportunity-tracking signal is supported.

### Cross-species H1 conclusion

Two of three primary coefficients are positive, but the median coefficient is only \(+0.00853\), not all species have the same sign, and **no species passes the frozen Holm criterion**.

Therefore:

> **Across the frozen Antarctic Pygoscelis design, sites gaining more summer-exposed terrestrial opportunity did not show a confirmed general tendency to gain relative breeding use.**

## 3. Biological interpretation

This is not a static area null.

The physical opportunity surface itself changed strongly and heterogeneously through time. The biological result is that **population use did not consistently track that changing physical opportunity**.

A concise formulation is:

> **Antarctic breeding landscapes can become more physically available without producing a corresponding, general redistribution of penguin breeding use.**

This is a dynamic **capacity–use decoupling** result.

It must not be inflated to the stronger claim that physical opportunity and literal occupied breeding footprint moved in opposite directions, because a longitudinal \(A_{\mathrm{occupied}}\) footprint measure did not pass independent measurement recovery.

## 4. Why this matters for island ecology

A simple island-capacity intuition is:

\[
A \uparrow \Rightarrow \text{population capacity/use} \uparrow.
\]

The Antarctic result requires a more explicit decomposition:

\[
A_{\mathrm{available},t}
\rightarrow
A_{\mathrm{used},t}
\rightarrow
N_t
\]

with the first arrow not guaranteed.

Physical space can be exposed or created, yet that opportunity is not automatically translated into population redistribution.

This matters because island area is often treated as a quasi-static proxy for ecological capacity. In Antarctic breeding systems, opportunity is itself dynamic, and its ecological value depends on processes not encoded by terrestrial space alone.

## 5. Relation to Paper 1

### Paper 1

> Declining Antarctic Pygoscelis populations can concentrate breeding effort into fewer effective components beyond proportional thinning.

This establishes **how decline occupies space**.

### Paper 2

> Temporal expansion of summer-exposed terrestrial opportunity does not consistently attract or retain relative breeding use across the broader penguin system.

This shows that **changing terrestrial capacity alone does not explain that spatial reorganization**.

Together:

> **Population-space contraction is real, but it is not reducible to a simple loss—or gain—of terrestrial opportunity.**

This is the core bridge from penguin breeding ecology to island ecology.

## 6. Frozen H2 marine route: unresolved, not negative

The single prespecified marine metric was breeding-season distance to open water (<15% sea-ice concentration).

The actual measurement gate returned:

- 77 candidate terrestrial sites;
- 58 passing;
- **75.3% support**;
- frozen continuation threshold = **80%**.

Coverage failure was concentrated especially in Victoria Land.

Therefore the predeclared H2 continuation gate failed **before any land × marine interaction outcome was computed**.

Consequences:

- no land × marine interaction claim is allowed;
- the 80% gate is not relaxed;
- no alternative sea-ice threshold, month set, search radius, or ocean covariate is opened as a same-paper rescue;
- marine mechanism remains unresolved, not rejected.

The correct Discussion statement is:

> **The failure of terrestrial opportunity to explain redistribution leaves the marine matrix and other behavioral or demographic constraints as plausible mechanisms, but the frozen marine-access route did not meet its measurement-support requirement and cannot adjudicate among them.**

## 7. Direct occupied-footprint route

The strongest possible spatial formulation would compare:

\[
\Delta A_{\mathrm{available}}
\quad\text{with}\quad
\Delta A_{\mathrm{occupied}}.
\]

That route remains methodologically unresolved.

What succeeded:

- published Adélie guano references overlap 22/44 long-term sites at the frozen 5 km radius;
- the published Landsat-7 ETM+ classifier matrix and rule were numerically recovered;
- 45/47 published source scenes were directly crosswalked to Collection-2;
- follow-up diagnosis shows one major unmatched scene is an archival crosswalk issue rather than a missing historical scene.

What closed:

- same-sensor ETM+ longitudinal route: 0/44 sites have the frozen 2016–2021 late ETM+ support;
- direct application of ETM+ classifier coefficients to OLI remains unauthorized without an independently validated spectral bridge.

Thus the current Paper 2 response must be called **relative breeding use**, not literal occupied breeding footprint.

## 8. What not to claim

Do not write:

- “new ice-free habitat is unused” as a literal occupancy statement;
- “marine limitation explains the mismatch”;
- “Adélie tracks habitat expansion”;
- “area does not matter for penguins”;
- “physical habitat and occupied footprint move in opposite directions”;
- “the Antarctic island-area relationship is reversed.”

Use:

- “summer-exposed terrestrial opportunity”;
- “relative breeding use”;
- “not sufficient to explain redistribution”;
- “bounded positive Adélie tendency”;
- “marine mechanism unresolved.”

## One-sentence conclusion

> **Across Antarctic Pygoscelis breeding networks, summer-exposed terrestrial opportunity changed substantially through time, but sites gaining more opportunity did not consistently gain relative breeding use, showing that dynamic physical capacity alone is insufficient to explain the spatial reorganization of changing penguin populations.**
