# Ecosphere manuscript spine v7 — spatial architecture of Adélie decline

## One-sentence contribution

A strongly regional Adélie decline is accompanied by progressive within-island concentration of breeders among a smaller effective set of breeding components, beyond what proportional thinning and severe independent count error predict; colony organization also carries a separate weak association with subsequent demographic performance.

## Ecological story

1. **Regional direction.** Five neighboring islands undergo a highly coherent multi-decadal Adélie decline.
2. **Different island fate.** Local endpoints differ, including extinction, low-abundance persistence and species replacement in the broader local network.
3. **Internal reorganization.** In every stable-roster island, effective colony number declines through time: about 19% on Cormorant, 51% on Humble and 83% on Litchfield before extinction.
4. **Not proportional thinning.** A fixed-composition null retaining the exact island-total trajectory and adding Poisson, CV10% or CV20% independent count error cannot reproduce the three observed concentration trends; under CV20%, island p values are 0.038, 0.000010 and 0.000010, and joint p=0.000010.
5. **Demographic relevance without prediction.** Higher N_eff remains positively associated with next-year growth after current abundance, island, time and two prior growth intervals are controlled, but the held-out predictive gain is tiny and null-compatible.
6. **Independent spatial convergence.** Torgersen physically contracts from 23 historic active subcolonies to five active footprints, with habitat-structured extinction. This supports the phenomenon of within-island contraction but not identifier-level correspondence.
7. **Buffering stays secondary.** The Wang–Loreau beta hierarchy is observed but compatible with an uncalibrated CV20% error-only null, so it is not used to claim biological spatial insurance.

## Main ecological hypothesis

**Proportional thinning versus structural concentration.** If decline simply scales a stable breeding distribution downward, fixed colony-code shares combined with the observed island-total trajectory and count error should reproduce N_eff trends. More-negative observed slopes indicate directional reorganization among breeding components.

## Biological interpretation

The Palmer decline has a spatial-demographic architecture. Population loss is accompanied by concentration of remaining breeders, suggesting that the internal breeding structure of an island erodes or reorganizes as decline proceeds. The census alone cannot identify which physical nesting patches are lost or why.

## Not claimed

- colony codes are physical GIS subcolonies;
- concentration causes decline;
- snow, topography or skua predation is the identified mechanism;
- N_eff is an early warning signal or supported predictor;
- beta variability proves spatial insurance;
- CV20% estimates actual Palmer counting error.

## Highest-value next evidence

1. colony-code ↔ GIS-polygon crosswalk;
2. replicate-observer error calibration;
3. direct habitat attributes for the retained/lost breeding components;
4. independent replication in another monitored Adélie archipelago.

## Terminal rule

No alternate concentration index, intermediate CV, tuned time window, roster reconciliation or transformed rescue endpoint may be opened in the core paper. New mechanistic claims require external spatial, observer-error or independent-population evidence.
