# Ecosphere cover-letter draft — v0.7

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Hierarchical demography of Antarctic penguin breeding islands: divergent fate and measurement-sensitive buffering,”** for publication as an Article in the Animal Ecology subject track of *Ecosphere*.

Using a complete 1991–2017 census of five neighboring Adélie penguin breeding islands near Palmer Station, the manuscript asks a scale-explicit question: do the spatial units that retain different demographic fates also define the level at which temporal variability is reduced? The regional decline is exceptionally coherent, yet island states and endpoints remain distinct. Among the three islands with stable colony-code rosters, the observed Wang–Loreau beta transition is larger from colony-code components to islands than from islands to the aggregate.

A central feature of the manuscript is that we do not equate this observed hierarchy with biological buffering without auditing measurement error. Under a fully synchronous latent-subcolony null, the observed raw hierarchy is far larger than expected from independent Poisson error or the inherited Gamma–Poisson 10% multiplicative-CV sensitivity, but it is compatible with an intentionally severe, uncalibrated 20% sensitivity. Historical Palmer protocols used repeated independent counts with tighter agreement targets, yet replicate-level errors are not available in the public analysis table. We therefore present the result as an observed scale hierarchy with an explicit identifiability boundary, not as proof of spatial insurance. Detrended and annual-growth analyses are retained only as sensitivities because they do not escape the error-only null.

The manuscript also reports a separate within-island state signal. Effective colony number retains a small positive association with next-year population growth after current abundance, island identity, time, and the preceding two growth intervals are controlled, and the coefficient remains unusual under serial-structure-preserving and frozen count-error nulls. Its held-out predictive increment is weak and is not presented as prediction, causality, or early warning. Independent Torgersen mapping provides phenomenon-level evidence of habitat-structured subcolony attrition, while the unresolved census-code-to-GIS crosswalk prevents identifier-level validation.

We believe this study fits *Ecosphere* because it combines long-term island demography with a transparent hierarchy of claims: robust regional and island-state structure, an observed but measurement-sensitive lower-level variability pattern, and a bounded colony-organization association. More generally, it shows why hierarchical stability analyses require an observation-error audit before lower-level asynchrony is interpreted biologically.

The source data are publicly archived, and the analysis code, frozen contracts, and result receipts are accessible for peer review through the project repository. A permanent version-of-record archive will be provided if the manuscript is accepted.
