# Integrated v0.4 submission positioning

**Manuscript:** *Dynamic colony history reveals limits of static island architecture for predicting Antarctic penguin demography*

This memo concerns journal fit and framing only. It does not change the scientific evidence.

## Current manuscript identity

The paper is not primarily a public-information paper and not primarily a habitat-model paper. Its general contribution is a cross-scale information problem:

> local demographic history can be measurable in a dynamically updated colony state without becoming a confirmed, scale-invariant rule in static landscape descriptors.

The evidence is deliberately asymmetric rather than uniformly positive:

- Palmer local history: supported under the bounded lag-2 fixed-specification validation;
- independent monitored-nest productivity: does not replicate the frozen primary association;
- Antarctic-wide static area × heterogeneity rule: directionally concordant but non-confirmatory;
- radius dependence: sensitivity, not established biological scale dependence;
- individual movement mechanism: prospective, not current evidence.

This mixture is a strength for a journal interested in scale, generalization and falsification, but a weakness for a venue expecting one decisive positive mechanism.

## Journal fit

### 1. Global Ecology and Biogeography — strongest fit if the paper is sold as a generalization/transferability study

Current scope emphasizes broad spatial, temporal or taxonomic patterns, macroecological/comparative methods and conceptual conclusions relevant beyond a local area or specific taxon.

**Why it fits**
- Part II is Antarctic-wide, three-species and explicitly macroecological.
- The conceptual question is whether local demographic information transfers into static broad-scale landscape descriptors.
- The manuscript now makes a general distinction among local explanatory structure, dynamic historical information and transferable macroecological structure.
- Negative/non-confirmatory evidence is scientifically meaningful because the paper asks a transferability question rather than searching for a positive landscape predictor.

**Editorial risk**
- The paper-level Antarctic-wide test is non-confirmatory.
- The strongest positive result is Palmer-specific and Adélie-specific.
- GEB explicitly expects global relevance and commonly desk-rejects work that reads as a local/taxon case study.

**Framing required**
- Abstract must lead with the cross-scale transfer problem, not Palmer natural history.
- Figure 1–4 sequence must make the broad-scale inferential structure obvious.
- Cover letter should say explicitly that the contribution is a test of **when ecological information fails to transfer across scale**, not a local penguin mechanism paper.
- Avoid presenting the macro null as an unfortunate failed validation; make it the intended falsification boundary.

### 2. Ecography — very strong fit if the paper is framed around scale, spatial support and non-equivalent information across space/time

Current journal identity is “a journal of space and time in ecology,” with emphasis on macroecology, biogeography, spatial/temporal patterns, theory testing and interpretation of ecological phenomena.

**Why it fits**
- The manuscript explicitly spans within-island spatial redistribution, temporal history and Antarctic-wide spatial support.
- Radius sensitivity is central to the interpretation rather than a side diagnostic.
- Ecography is comfortable with a specific model system when it addresses a general spatiotemporal issue.
- The “information valid at one hierarchical level need not transfer to another” principle maps naturally onto the journal’s space/time emphasis.

**Editorial risk**
- Part II is not a classic species-distribution or biodiversity-pattern paper.
- The local Palmer section is long and could dominate the macro/spatial message.

**Framing required**
- Emphasize hierarchy of spatial grains and the loss of information during aggregation.
- Keep “island architecture” as a spatial representation problem rather than a classic island-biogeography claim.
- The v0.4 title already suits this framing well.

### 3. Journal of Animal Ecology — strong alternative if the paper is sold as a demographic-state and scale paper rather than macroecology

Current scope emphasizes animal ecology that develops, tests and advances broad ecological principles across population, individual and behavioural ecology.

**Why it fits**
- The data are deeply demographic.
- The Palmer result is about population reorganization and short-lived state information.
- The REPRO failure is biologically informative and prevents mechanism inflation.
- The future individual-level carrier question is naturally animal-ecological.

**Editorial risk**
- The Antarctic-wide static architecture section may appear over-engineered relative to the animal-demography core.
- Without Ross individual results, the behavioural mechanism remains unresolved.

**Framing required**
- Lead with history-dependent demographic redistribution.
- Treat Part II as a generalization test showing why local state cannot be replaced by static landscape covariates.
- Reduce explicit island-biogeography rhetoric if submitting here.

### 4. Ecology Letters — aspirational only in the current evidence state

Current scope prioritizes concise, highly novel general ecological findings and explicitly discourages work that merely confirms or extends prior results.

**Why it could fit conceptually**
- The distinction between local information and transferable information is general.
- The study has unusually disciplined falsification and independent non-replication.

**Why it is currently risky**
- The Antarctic-wide focal test is non-confirmatory.
- The local positive association extends a literature in which one-year performance-linked growth and public-information hypotheses already exist.
- The individual mechanism is not identified.
- The manuscript is structurally larger and more qualified than a typical decisive Letter.

**Trigger for reconsideration**
- A strong, genuinely outcome-blind Ross individual settlement result that directly connects prior colony state to first-breeding choice could raise the mechanistic novelty enough to reconsider Ecology Letters. That future result must remain independent of the current manuscript until the frozen Ross access gate is opened.

## Recommended submission strategy

**Current evidence state:** target **GEB or Ecography**, with journal-specific framing rather than changing the analyses.

- Choose **GEB** if the cover letter and Abstract can make the transferable macroecological principle the dominant contribution.
- Choose **Ecography** if the strongest conceptual emphasis is the spatial/temporal hierarchy of information and sensitivity to spatial support.
- Choose **JAE** if editors/reviewers are likely to value the Palmer demographic result more than the non-confirmatory macroecological test.
- Do not lead with public information for any of these journals.

## Cover-letter core

> This study asks a general macroecological question that is often assumed rather than tested: when local demographic organization is history-dependent, can that information be compressed into static landscape descriptors that transfer across populations and species? Using Antarctic penguins across nested scales, we find that a short-lived local colony state carries demographic information within Palmer, but that the state is not interchangeable with independent nest productivity and static island descriptors do not yield a confirmed scale-invariant Antarctic-wide rule. The contribution is therefore not a new public-information mechanism, but an empirical boundary on the transferability of ecological information across scale.

## Decision rule for the Ross result

Do **not** delay the current integrated manuscript simply because the prospective Ross analysis is blocked on API access.

If Ross later produces a strong frozen individual-level result:
- evaluate whether it belongs in a separate behavioural-demography paper;
- only consider integrating it into this manuscript if doing so changes the central inference rather than merely adding mechanistic decoration.

If Ross is null or non-estimable:
- the current v0.4 manuscript remains logically intact because Ross is not part of its evidentiary chain.
