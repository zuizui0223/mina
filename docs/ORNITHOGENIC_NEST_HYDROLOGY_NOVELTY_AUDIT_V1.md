# Ornithogenic snowmelt and nesting feedback: ecological novelty audit v1

**Status:** 2026-10-08; pre-outcome mechanistic hypothesis + public source audit. **Not an empirical result.** Independent of the scientifically frozen Ecology Article PR #189.

## The ecological question

**Can animals change the carrying capacity of an Antarctic breeding island through their own marine-to-land nutrient subsidy, and can this feed back negatively on reproduction through connected snowmelt drainage?**

This is not a test of MacArthur-Wilson area, colony size, unequal recovery, or whether guano fertilizes algae.

The distinctive mechanism is an endogenous breeding-space feedback:

1. Pengu​ins and other marine vertebrates deposit marine-derived phosphorus on coastal snow or in meltwater catchments.
2. Where the nutrient source actually reaches viable snow algae, nutrient-driven chlorophyll/biomass could darken upstream snow relative to comparable clean snow.
3. Snow darkening can increase radiative absorption and potentially accelerate local melt. Weather, water, microbial state, aspect and pre-existing snow thickness are alternative drivers.
4. If additional meltwater arrives at an **occupied and hydrologically connected downstream nest** while eggs or chicks are vulnerable, it may increase inundation, abandonment or offspring mortality. The same melt pulse should *not* create a similar risk at a comparable unconnected nest.
5. Only if these linkages hold does penguin abundance partly modify effective breeding habitat rather than passively respond to it.

**Not established:** the full steps 1–4, a causal mediation, colony-level benefit/cost reversal, and individual nest consequences. Beware that the Guano→algae and snow-algae→albedo portions have been explicitly discussed and partly tested in prior work.

## Prior-art constraints that sharply narrow novelty

- **Ryan and Khan 2026**, DOI 10.1029/2026JG009918: 111 snow samples over 9 West Antarctic Peninsula locations (2024–2025); high phosphate associated with green vs red pigment states. The AGU release (2026-10-07) explicitly discusses guano–green algae–darkening–potential snowmelt. This is **prior art**, not our discovery. Its field survey does not establish nest-level effects or causal mediation to breeding.
- **Gray et al. 2020**, DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-16018-w: 1,679 green snow algal blooms mapped in 2017–2019 Antarctic Peninsula imagery; 60% within 5 km of a penguin colony. Proximity is **not** an effect of penguin-caused fertilization, nor a breeding outcome.
- **Gray et al. 2021**, DOI 10.3389/fpls.2021.671981: green/red snow bloom phenology varies during Jan–Feb, with green layers inside snow and exposure during melt. Such observations do **not** establish that darkening occurred before nest laying.
- **Kim et al. 2026**, DOI 10.1002/jgo2.70001: a 2019 high-wave event eroded accumulated guano substrate at Edmonson Point and coincided with redistribution of nest positions. Penguin-engineered nest substrate and damage are **established prior art**, not new here.
- **2026 freshwater-boundary review**, DOI 10.1016/j.avrs.2026.100386: nesting consequences of precipitation, ponding, runoff, stream change and guano-associated chemistry are established. No demonstration of *living snow algal mediation* of upstream runoff onto a nest is supplied.
- **Broadwell et al. 2026**, DOI 10.1093/ismeco/ycag100: snow/glacier algae exist at Signy Island but their in-situ phenology dataset is Feb–Apr 2024, **after** the 2011–2022 Signy image archive and often late relative to egg/chick vulnerability.
- Penguin-site preference for sloping well-drained nests, snow phenology and island topography is old ecology (e.g., Australian Antarctic Programme guidance; Palmer island phenology literature). Do **not** claim this or a generic guano-habitat feedback as novel.

## Competing biological explanations

**H1 — biotic amplification + hydrological connectivity (candidate).**
An upstream, independently validated green algal darkening event precedes excessive local melt, with higher subsequent flooding/nest failure ONLY in downstream connected sites, conditional on weather and snow depth.

**H0a — weather/terrain only.**
Rain, temperature, insolation, aspect, or snow depth produce the apparent association. Conditioning on those and comparing connected with disconnected nesting sites removes any unique algal effect.

**H0b — direct guano staining / non-algal darkening.**
Pigment/spectral evidence does not support viable green algae; observed darkening and melt result from guano/particles alone.

**H0c — reverse causality / common origin.**
Already-active nests deposit guano where melting is already occurring; bird abundance or shared microtopography causes both blooms and nest outcomes. No algae-before-hazard signature.

**H0d — timing incompatibility.**
Green blooms appear only after chicks have fledged or snow already melted. **Same-season chick/nest feedback is then impossible** at that site-year. Do not substitute next-year abundance as a rescue outcome without a separate prespecified mechanism.

**H0e — upstream/downstream mismatch.**
Algal patches and nesting units share geographical proximity but lie in different meltwater catchments. Proximity within 5 km is not hydrological exposure.

## Prespecified support gates; strict order, no outcome peek

1. **Geotemporal availability:** Confirm exact publication-backed latitude/longitude, named site, year, imagery timestamp for each bloom and nest/camera site. Require a **same-site and same-breeding-season** candidate; broad Peninsula boundaries are not sufficient. On known mismatches: STOP.
2. **Phenological order:** Image-confirm viable algal darkening at day t BEFORE distinct meltwater/runoff onset and BEFORE egg/chick failure observation in a sensitive breeding interval; choose same-season direction in advance. If algae only appear after chicks have fledged: STOP same-season feedback.
3. **Hydrological link:** Confirm an upstream-to-downstream connected snowfield with a DEM and imaging observations; show a comparable unconnected nesting group / catchment with common weather exposure. If 10m satellite pixel cannot isolate necessary habitat, do not claim nest-level causality.
4. **Process separation:** Independent chlorophyll/cell/pigment or validated multispectral classification separates true algae from guano stains; runoff/surface wetness measurable separately from weather and colony counts. Otherwise stop at a descriptive spatial co-occurrence.
5. **Endpoint and effort:** Identifiable fixed nest units with timed, detectability-corrected chick or breeding failure. Weather, snow and observation/camera effort support verified. Predeclared within-site weather and stage controls. No treating nests not visible as dead.
6. **Replication:** At least two genuinely independent site-year exposure events with connected vs disconnected controls before making a general process claim; one event may be a mechanism case study only.
7. **Mechanism test before analysis:** Freeze sampling, hydrological connectivity, nuisance adjustments, null randomization unit and stopping thresholds BEFORE opening chick/failure responses. Do not substitute colony concentration or recovery summaries.

**Phase 1 authorized:** metadata and sample-image framing, site names, coordinates, date ranges, input variable schemas, site counts. **Phase 1 forbidden:** measured algal intensity at target nests, failures, outcome-conditioned site search, significance tests.

## Two independent empirical predictions (if all gates pass)

- **Spatially directional prediction:** Among nest clusters matched on local snowfall, slope, weather, colony and stage, only nest groups DOWNSTREAM OF ALGAE-BEARING SNOWFIELDS show excess failure near measured runoff onset; physically close but disconnected controls do not. A radial-distance-only effect is insufficient.
- **Temporal/negative-control prediction:** The excess should follow greening and runoff, not precede either. Fledging-season events should not predict egg mortality in the same season; models assigning future bloom to earlier nesting outcomes are placebo tests, not mechanism evidence.

Countervailing *early snow clearance could improve nesting* is exploratory ONLY unless greening consistently precedes laying; published Jan–Apr blooming makes early-exposure timing a serious threat.

## Why this might advance island ecology IF successful

A classic island model treats carrying capacity/habitat availability as exogenous. This mechanism would instead give the inhabitants a causal role in creating a *temporally variable hydrological hazard* through imported marine nutrients. The effective breeding habitat would depend not simply on island area, but on animal activity × algal physiology × snowpack stage × drainage-network connection. Negative self-feedback could coexist with density-dependent predator dilution. The novelty is **causally demonstrated animal-mediated habitat feedback across land and marine systems**, not identification of a new plant/alga or a new mathematical concentration index.

## Honest current outcome

NO biological feedback has been demonstrated. NO colony-specific algal/nest outcome pair has yet been verified. It is possible that public sources cannot support this causal test; in that case close this line as **NOT IDENTIFIED** and do not infer a successful mechanism from maps or a model.
