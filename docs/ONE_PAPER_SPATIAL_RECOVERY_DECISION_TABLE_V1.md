# One-paper spatial-recovery hysteresis decision table v1

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** frozen before any SMP abundance magnitude is opened.

## Single paper-level hypothesis

> **Population recovery does not retrace spatial collapse in colonial breeders.**

Discovery evidence comes from Antarctic penguins. The independent confirmatory test is the SMP same-SiteID abandonment–recolonization threshold analysis.

If the frozen state-only scan yields fewer than the prespecified number of spells, SiteIDs, MasterSites or species:

## Stage C confirmatory outcomes

Define:

\[
H=A_{\mathrm{recolonize}}-A_{\mathrm{abandon}}
\]

and species-balanced mean \(T\).

Two inferential checks are mandatory:

1. species-level one-sided sign-flip;
2. structured trajectory-phase circular-shift null.

### Outcome 1 — spatial-recovery hysteresis supported

Required:

\[
T>0,
\]

\[
p_{\mathrm{sign}}\le0.05,
\]

and

\[
p_{\mathrm{phase}}\le0.05.
\]

Paper-level conclusion allowed:

> **Spatial recovery requires a higher surrounding population state than spatial loss at the same breeding sites. Population recovery therefore does not simply retrace spatial collapse.**

One-paper structure:

1. Antarctic penguins: discovery of non-proportional spatial concentration and weak recovery of breeding-space structure;
2. SMP seabirds: prospective same-site confirmation of abandonment–recolonization threshold asymmetry;
3. Discussion: social positive feedback / Allee-like habitat selection as one possible mechanism, not the demonstrated mechanism.

Candidate title:

> **Population recovery does not retrace spatial collapse in colonial seabirds**

Alternative:

> **Abandonment and recolonization occur at different population thresholds in colonial seabirds**

Do not title the paper around Allee effects or social habitat selection.

### Outcome 2 — sign-flip passes, trajectory-phase null fails

Interpretation:

> the raw paired asymmetry is compatible with generic temporal alignment of the observed abundance trajectories.

Action:

- do not claim hysteresis;
- do not add another null;
- current penguin manuscript remains the paper.

### Outcome 3 — positive T but sign-flip fails

Interpretation:

> the direction is not sufficiently replicated across species.

Action:

- no general hysteresis claim;
- species/subgroup rescue analyses prohibited.

### Outcome 4 — T approximately zero / unresolved

Interpretation:

> spatial recovery is broadly compatible with a reversible occupancy response at the measured SiteID scale.

Action:

- the MAPPPD increasing-network result remains a bounded Antarctic observation;
- current penguin manuscript remains standalone.

### Outcome 5 — T < 0

Interpretation:

> recolonization occurs at a lower surrounding population state than abandonment, opposite to the preregistered recovery-barrier prediction.

Action:

- report as falsification of the candidate hysteresis generalization;
- do not reframe it post hoc as a different social mechanism.

## Mechanism boundary even after Outcome 1

A positive result does **not** uniquely identify:

- conspecific attraction;
- an Allee effect;
- public-information use;
- site fidelity;
- social facilitation.

Same-site pairing removes fixed place quality. The trajectory-phase null calibrates generic timing. Time-varying habitat, predators, disturbance or management can still contribute.

The general claim is therefore **history-dependent spatial recovery**, not a unique behavioral mechanism.

## Novelty boundary

Already established in prior literature:

- colonization/extinction and recolonization as distinct processes;
- social information and conspecific density affecting seabird site dynamics;
- dynamic landscapes and hysteresis in ecological theory.

The candidate contribution is narrower:

> **a prospective, multi-species, same-site paired test of whether local abandonment and later recolonization occur at different surrounding population thresholds, linked to an independently discovered Antarctic spatial-recovery anomaly.**
