# Ecosphere manuscript spine v6 — observation-error boundary

## One-sentence contribution

Neighboring Adélie penguin islands retain different demographic states and endpoints under a shared regional decline, but the apparent sub-island temporal-compensation hierarchy is biologically identifiable only if independent colony-count error is bounded below an uncalibrated high-error scenario; effective colony number remains a separate weak but more error-robust conditional state signal.

## Story

1. **Shared direction, different fate.** Five Palmer islands share an exceptionally coherent multi-decadal Adélie decline, while island identity and local endpoints remain distinct.
2. **Observed nested hierarchy.** In the stable-roster COR/HUM/LIT subset, raw Wang–Loreau beta is 1.0737 from colony-code components to islands and 1.0112 from islands to the aggregate; 86.4% of the observed log-beta transition lies at the lower step.
3. **Identification audit changes the interpretation.** Under a fully synchronous latent-subcolony null, independent Poisson and inherited Gamma–Poisson CV10% error cannot reproduce the raw hierarchy, but the uncalibrated CV20% sensitivity can (beta-within p=0.924; log-contrast p=0.448).
4. **Centered signals do not rescue it.** Detrended and annual-growth hierarchy statistics are also compatible with error-only nulls, so they remain sensitivities rather than promoted endpoints.
5. **Protocol context narrows but does not close the gap.** Historical Palmer methods required at least three independent observer counts and targeted ±5% agreement, or ±10% for colonies >1000 pairs, but the public table lacks replicate counts needed to translate those targets into the Gamma–Poisson CV scale.
6. **N_eff is a different signal.** The N_eff coefficient remains +0.1126 after two lagged growth terms and unusual under serial-structure and frozen count-error nulls, while its incremental held-out predictive gain is tiny and unsupported.
7. **External mapping remains triangulation.** Torgersen shows real habitat-structured subcolony attrition, but no public colony-code ↔ GIS polygon crosswalk identifies the exact census metric spatially.

## General ecological point

Hierarchical variability partitioning is an observation-scale statement until lower-level measurement error is bounded. Independent observation error can create apparent component asynchrony that disappears on aggregation. The ecological question is therefore not only *where variability is reduced*, but *whether the reduction is larger than the aggregation of observation noise can generate*.

## What is not claimed

- biological spatial insurance from the raw beta hierarchy;
- measurement-error robustness across the full fixed error family;
- rescue by detrending or annual growth;
- that CV20% estimates Palmer observer error;
- predictive or early-warning value of N_eff;
- causal habitat fragmentation from colony codes.

## Highest-value next evidence

1. recover or estimate replicate-observer count error for the Palmer colony census;
2. crosswalk LTER colony codes to historic/current GIS polygons;
3. replicate the same hierarchy/error audit in another monitored archipelago.

## Terminal rule

Do not add intermediate CV levels, alternate beta definitions, transformed rescue endpoints, new topology indices, or additional same-census climate windows. The next change in biological interpretation must come from external error calibration, spatial crosswalk data, or independent replication.
