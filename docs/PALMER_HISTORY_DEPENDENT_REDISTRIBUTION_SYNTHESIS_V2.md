# History-dependent Adélie colony redistribution — synthesis v2

## What is now established

The Palmer record contains a reproducible demographic reorganization during decline: breeders become concentrated into a smaller effective set of colony-code breeding groups rather than simply thinning proportionally.

A previously frozen colony-wide chick-production state also carries short-lived information about subsequent within-island redistribution. Under the repaired fixed specification, the denominator-separated lag-2 coefficient is positive and robust across the frozen Palmer sensitivities. The effect persists at lag 3 and fades by lags 4–5; the predeclared 4–5-year recruitment-echo contrast is negative.

Measured performance itself has only weak one-year colony-specific memory and no confirmed two- or three-year memory. Past performance nevertheless retains incremental information in the non-causal bridge model after measured next-year performance, prior group size and colony identity.

This supports **history dependence**, but not an identified movement mechanism.

## Independent checks that did not simply confirm the story

### Pre-extinction chick success

Only four durable-extinction events had matched chick data. Their pre-extinction performance deficit was directionally negative but non-confirmatory. Durable disappearance therefore cannot be described as demonstrated reproductive collapse.

### HUMPOP breeder-arrival chronology

The official PAL-LTER archive confirms HUMPOP is genuinely colony-level: raw LOC is archived as Colony and TOTADULTS as Adults. A schema gate therefore passed prospectively.

After the frozen endpoint was constructed, however, only 69 matched colony-seasons survived the required design, below the predeclared minimum of 100. The analysis stopped without estimating an arrival-performance coefficient. The threshold was not lowered.

### Independent nest-level reproductive success

The PAL-LTER REPRO archive provided 2,057 nest records. After a source-semantic repair frozen before model fitting and fail-closed duplicate handling, the primary panel passed its information gate with 63 matched colony-seasons across 15 predictor island-seasons.

The prospectively frozen primary result did **not** validate the earlier signal:

- mean chicks reaching creche per monitored nest -> next-year relative colony growth: beta = 0.0296, p = 0.232;
- lag-2 beta = -0.0164, p = 0.629.

A prespecified binary-any-creche sensitivity was positive (beta = 0.0679, p = 0.0405), but it cannot rescue the failed primary endpoint.

The nest-level state and the earlier colony-wide chick-production state were only weakly correlated (r = 0.132, n = 61). They therefore capture meaningfully different components of reproductive performance.

This is an important narrowing result: **the Palmer signal is not a generic consequence of any reproductive-success metric.**

## External evidence

### Pointe Géologie, Adélie Land

Méheust et al. (2024) analyzed 83 Adélie colonies on three islands from 1998–2021. They removed common temporal variation from colony breeding success and annual growth and reported the following lag profile:

- same-year association: r = -0.345;
- one-year lag: r = +0.375;
- two-year lag: r = -0.073;
- later lags: no significant correlations.

The authors interpreted the positive one-year lag as coherent with prospecting breeders using public information.

For the present program, the useful fact is narrower: **an independent Adélie colony system also contains a positive short-delayed breeding-performance/growth association after common temporal variation is removed.** This provides literature triangulation for a short-lag history pattern, not an independent reanalysis and not proof of public information.

The deposited Figshare raw data are explicitly cited by the paper, but the current runtime cannot access the Figshare article/API/download endpoints. A direct external replication specification has therefore been frozen but remains data-access blocked.

### Pointe Géologie aggregate Dryad control

A separate public Dryad dataset contains 1980–2016 whole-population breeding-pair and chick counts. An aggregate specificity-control contract was frozen before values were opened. The raw file download is currently blocked by the runtime, so no result has been computed.

This test remains useful because a positive denominator-separated lag at whole-population scale would demonstrate that temporal carry-over need not arise from internal colony redistribution.

## What can be said now

The strongest defensible statement is:

> **Adélie colony distributions can retain short-lived demographic information about recent colony performance, but the biological content and carrier of that information remain unresolved.**

At Palmer, the association survives denominator separation, current group size, persistent colony identity and measured short-term performance persistence. It is not reproduced by the primary independent nest-level reproductive endpoint. At Pointe Géologie, a published independent study reports a qualitatively concordant short-delayed colony-level association.

Together these results favor neither a pure static-landscape model nor a simple universal “successful nests attract penguins” rule.

A useful conceptual formulation is:

> **Breeding landscapes have both physical structure and demographic history. Static habitat constrains where colonies can function, while recent colony state can contain transient information about where breeders subsequently accumulate.**

## What is not established

The following remain unresolved:

- whether experienced adults alter colony fidelity according to recent performance;
- whether prebreeders prospect and preferentially settle at successful colonies;
- whether unmeasured environmental states persist longer than the measured performance proxy;
- whether predator activity or snow conditions generate the relevant temporal state;
- whether the Palmer association generalizes quantitatively outside Palmer.

No individual public-information, retention, immigration, prospecting or breeding-dispersal claim is justified from the current aggregate data.

## Frozen stopping rule

Following the failed REPRO primary validation, no further Palmer reproductive endpoint will be searched for confirmation. Egg-lay, hatch, loss and alternative threshold endpoints are closed unless a new hypothesis is justified independently before outcome access.

Future mechanism work is limited to:

1. the already frozen Pointe Géologie Figshare replication when raw data access becomes possible;
2. the already frozen individual band/resight data gate;
3. a prospectively defined independent population panel;
4. new prospective field data.

This prevents the negative independent validation from being converted into a search for a more favorable Palmer endpoint.
