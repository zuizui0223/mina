# Beaufort 1995 north-beach settlement: source origin vs island-capacity change

**2026-10-08 | PR #192 follow-up | previously published historical source material only.**  
**Verdict:** no successful natal-origin-to-north breeder transitions identified in inspected records. The positive observation is occupation of an already ice-free northern nesting beach *during a multi-decade downturn in the larger south-colony counts*. This is a biologically useful opposition to one simplistic growth/saturation-fission narrative, not proof of a different colonization mechanism.

## I. Archival evidence and three distinct spatial objects

**ASPA 105 (2003, 2010 and 2015 plans):**
- The north coast had a ~1 km long, ~50 m wide **ice-free beach** described in the 2003 plan (this is the geomorphic beach, NOT independently mapped usable *nest capacity*).
- January 1995 (= 1994/95 breeding season) at the **west end of the northern ice-free beach**: 2 Adélie pairs, 3 chicks, ~10–15 nonbreeding birds. The 2003 record calls this newly established and *possibly transitory*. This does not certify complete search effort or absence in all earlier years.
- At least three separated layers of **subfossil former penguin-colony deposits** on the north raised-moraine terrace indicate much earlier use of this landscape. They do NOT establish which precise breeding micro-patch was occupied in 1995, nor prove continuous suitability through the preceding decades.
- Official north-beach counts: 525 pairs (2005/06), 677 (2008/09), 989 (2013/14).
- The large **southwestern Cadwalader Beach** main colony held 70,468 pairs in 2013/14. In 1981–2000 its breeding-pair counts were **generally declining**, followed by growth over 2001–2012 (Lyver et al. 2014). The 1995 northern founding is embedded in the *former* long-term phase. This is *not* a controlled test that the main-colony nest density was low in that specific season.
- Field staff banded ~400 near-to-fledging Adélie chicks per field season at **Cadwalader Beach**, the southern main colony, from the mid-1990s; these bands do NOT by default represent a north-beach natal sample.

**LaRue et al. (2013):**
- Main-colony apparent ice-free nesting area estimated from an **occupied guano-stain envelope** minus snow/ice at the **southwestern main colony**; the 543-m retreat is of the ice field north of that main colony. Not an independent time series of **north-beach** nesting options.
- A new/disjunct Adélie subcolony described on the **northeast coast** in 2004 (460 pairs), 957 by 2010; several Beaufort-banded birds observed there. The official management-plan modern north colony is on the **northwest/west end of northern beach**. An exact polygon overlap or site-history crosswalk remains **unverified**. LaRue figures locate/map the southwestern main colony; they do not supply an independently verified 1995 north-beach polygon to match the two narratives.

**Three objects must never be collapsed:**
1. `south_main`: Cadwalader Beach — published main nesting density / ice retreat and routine 400-chick marking.
2. `north_beach`: official northwest beach — 1995 breeding, 2005/06, 2008/09, 2013/14 counts, old ice-free beach, ancient deposits, Ross-banded sightings.
3. `northeast_2004`: LaRue northeast colony — 2004→2010 count, identity with official north beach plausible but not georeferenced.

Official **2015 ASPA Map A** marks the modern northern Adélie colony near 166°52'E on the northwest shore. The eastern cliffs are steep; the separate emperor colony occupies nearby **northeastern fast ice** and should not be confused with terrestrial northern Adélie nest terrain.

The 2021 public USAP-DC metadata advertises penguin banding and resighting 1994–2021, but *no metadata-only review yet verifies a physical Beaufort north/south location key or effort in this system*. ## III. Numerical context: why aggregate source-count regression cannot recover the origins

- Southern Beaufort main colony: 52,335 breeding pairs in 2005 (LaRue 2013); the official north colony: **525** breeding pairs in 2005/06. If the seasonal alignment is accepted, northern pairs equal ~**0.993% of both colony counts combined**. This *numerical share is not a settlement probability*.
- In 2013/14 official main = **70,468** and north = **989**. Northern share is **1.384%** of these two counts.
- The prior founder upper-bound audit gave **285.5** potentially breeding pair-equivalents in 2005/06 under an impossibly favorable perfect-survival age-3 closed model, versus observed **525**. Just **8.554 additional early breeding-pair equivalents** (~18 perfectly paired unseen adult individuals in 1995/96) can close that gap under the same model. Such founders may come from the island's own south colony, from other islands, or from initially unsurveyed cohorts. The mass-balance deficit is not a count of immigrants and cannot establish crossing of any island boundary.
- The small northern count relative to the southwest main colony demonstrates why using the *south main annual breeding total alone* to determine a few founding individuals' origins is hopeless without independent origin/detection evidence; formal precision requires observed sampling error and identifiable cohort process.

## IV. A nontrivial ecological contrast, with strict prior-art limits

**Hypothesis R (recipient opportunity activated):** northern nesting substrate was usable before the settlement event, but occupancy changed because **prospecting/settlement decisions and founder supply** crossed a threshold. Predictions: verified old unoccupied-with-effort years, independent earlier physical suitability, natal-origin and first-breeding evidence; not simply rising region-wide population counts.

**Hypothesis H (new physical habitat opens):** a new low-slope ice-free nesting option appeared shortly before settlement and independently predicted its onset. Predictions: dates of actual north-beach bare ground / snow stability, before/after mapped local slopes and substrate, independent of current guano occupancy.

**Hypothesis F (south colony saturation-fission):** crowding at the source displaced breeders (or prebreeders) to the north. Predictions: south origin tags **breeding** north; local source density or reproductive crowding at founding (not only total colony abundance); an event-specific transition, possibly despite the 1981–2000 declining trend.

**Hypothesis M (multi-colony immigration):** natal-source recruits from one or several Ross colonies founded or augmented northern breeding. Predictions: individually identified **first breeders**, known natal origin and unbiased resighting at north, possibly source diversity; mere sightings of Ross-banded birds fail.

**Crucial distinction:** these alternatives are not guaranteed mutually exclusive. A robust analysis would decompose biological drivers without post hoc classifying 1995 as a clean treatment event.

**Prior art:**
- Munilla et al. 2016, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0147222, explicitly compared mother–satellite, stepping-stone and multi-source island metapopulation founding models for shearwaters. **Discovering multiple founder sources is not an unclaimed seabird theory.**
- Herman & Lynch 2022, DOI 10.1093/ornithapp/duac014, used age-structured ABC to show sustained immigration could explain four new gentoo breeding colonies. **Re-deriving an immigration requirement from counts alone is not new.**
- LaRue et al. 2013 reported Beaufort habitat expansion and Ross visitation decline. **That correlation and direction are prior art.**

The remaining potentially novel island-biogeographic interaction is **whether independent within-island availability of a *specific* nesting option changes *absolute* confirmed breeder settlement across an *island boundary*, after conditioning on changing source age cohorts and physical marine access**, over genuinely independent donor–receiver systems.

## V. The decisive data gate (not met)

Do **not** open prospective individual resight outcomes unless separately authorized within their own locked protocol. Before any new test, demand:

1. `K_north(t)`: mapped, dated bare and nesting-suitable ground on the northern beach, **before** 1995 and after, independent of guano or contemporary occupied nests. Survey effort to verify zeros.
2. `north_beach_id`: georeferenced 1995 northwest / 2004 LaRue northeast / later count polygons; freeze exact site identity or label separate.
3. `birth_year, band_site, natal_site, actual_first_breeding_site, breeding_evidence`: at Beaufort **north and south** and Ross colonies; visits without egg/chick/nest evidence are not treated as first reproduction.
4. effort and detection by site × year; Beaufort visits are infrequent, especially compared with Ross colonies.
5. independently supported age-structured source-cohort denominators and the actual direction-specific absolute settled flux, not visit percentages.
6. independent receiver-site zero→positive events and verified source tag; the already frozen Ross three/six-component panel lacks any such events.

**Current decision:** evidence supports a historical colony-establishment process during a main-colony downward phase on an already ice-free northern shore, together with banded **visitors in both directions**. It does **not** identify whether the founding pairs came from within-island or across-island nor show that new physical habitat caused settlement, changed absolute export, or suppressed neighboring-island rescue. Keep P1–P3 on HOLD, preserve PR #189 and PR #142.
