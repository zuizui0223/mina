# Journal of Biogeography cover-letter draft — integrated manuscript v0.2

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Local demographic reorganization does not
scale into a transferable island-resilience rule in Antarctic penguins,”** for
publication as an Original Article in *Journal of Biogeography*.

The manuscript addresses a simple biogeographical question with two nested
tests: when island populations show strong local demographic organization, does
that island information transfer into a general rule across populations?

We first use a complete 1991–2017 census of five neighbouring Adélie penguin
breeding islands near Palmer Station. Although the island populations share a
highly coherent long-term decline, breeders become progressively concentrated
within islands. In the three islands with stable colony-code rosters, effective
colony number declines by 19–83%, and fixed-composition nulls preserving the
observed island-total trajectories do not reproduce the concentration pattern.

A secondary mechanistic follow-up at Palmer found short-lived
performance-linked redistribution, but a prospectively frozen independent
Signy replication did not support the analogous lag-2 association. We then
test whether static breeding-island architecture explains site-specific
coupling to shared demographic forcing across Adélie, chinstrap and gentoo
penguins over 1980–2025. The prespecified area × habitat-complex interaction is
negative in all three species at the primary 2 km scale, but the preregistered
cross-species permutation test is non-confirmatory
(median gamma_AH = −0.318; 9,999 permutations; one-sided p = 0.0947). A simpler
breeding-space buffering hypothesis is also unsupported. Retrospective
operating-characteristic analysis shows that the design had little sensitivity
to moderate effects near the observed magnitude but would usually detect very
large common interactions (MDE80 ≈ 0.50; MDE90 ≈ 0.54). Apparent cross-radius
sign reversal likewise does not exceed a joint multiradius null (p = 0.0810).

We believe the contribution is therefore not a conventional positive
environment–demography association. It is a boundary on the transferability of
island information. The same system can show strong, repeatable spatial organization within islands
while both a local dynamic correlate and static island descriptors fail to
provide a confirmed, transferable rule at broader spatial scales. For marine-foraging
breeders, this distinction is particularly informative because reproduction is
terrestrially constrained while the dominant trophic resource field lies
outside the island.

The analysis was developed with explicit outcome-blind predictor construction,
pre-outcome estimator recovery gates, frozen observation-error rules and
prespecified permutation inference. Failed candidate estimators and
non-confirmatory results are retained in the reproducibility record rather than
being replaced post hoc. We see this as important to the manuscript's general
message: extracting transferable geographical relationships from complex
natural data requires distinguishing real local structure from information that
actually carries across scales.
