# One-paper hypothesis strategy v2 — spatial recovery hysteresis

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** prospective synthesis; SMP outcomes unopened.

## The single biological hypothesis

> **Population recovery does not retrace spatial collapse in colonial breeders.**

For the same repeatedly monitored breeding SiteID,

\[
H=A_{\mathrm{recolonize}}-A_{\mathrm{abandon}}.
\]

The directional prediction is

\[
H>0.
\]

A confirmatory result additionally requires the observed species-balanced \(H\) to exceed an structured common-phase null.

## Discovery half — Antarctic penguins

The penguin evidence establishes two observations.

### 1. Spatial loss during decline is non-proportional

Across Palmer and Signy, five declining *Pygoscelis* trajectories concentrated breeding effort into fewer effective monitored components beyond fixed-composition thinning and frozen count-error models.

### 2. Numerical increase does not guarantee spatial reconstruction

In the frozen MAPPPD regional extension, all three increasing networks ended with lower effective breeding-site number than their first retained complete season.

This does **not** prove hysteresis because those regional panels do not directly observe abandonment and later recolonization of the same sites under a preregistered threshold test.

It generates the recovery-hysteresis hypothesis.

## Independent confirmatory half — SMP seabirds

The SMP design asks the direct island-ecology question:

> **Does the same breeding site return at the same surrounding population state at which it was lost?**

### Stage A — place identity

Freeze stable species × MasterSite panels and mutually exclusive SiteIDs without count magnitude.

### Stage B — occupancy history

Use only positive / explicit-zero / missing states.

Freeze completed, calendar-consecutive spells:

\[
1\rightarrow0\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow0\rightarrow1.
\]

Require sufficient replication before magnitude opening.

Also freeze the maximal calendar-consecutive complete-year block containing each spell; only blocks of at least 6 years enter Stage C.

### Stage C — threshold asymmetry

For focal SiteID \(j\), use leave-one-SiteID-out parent abundance.

Calculate:

\[
A_e
\]

at abandonment and

\[
A_c
\]

at recolonization, then

\[
H=A_c-A_e.
\]

Aggregate spell → SiteID → MasterSite → species → equal-species mean.

## Two required inferential gates

### Gate 1 — directional asymmetry

Species-level mean \(H\) values must support:

\[
T_{\mathrm{obs}}>0
\]

under the frozen one-sided species sign-flip test.

### Gate 2 — exceed generic temporal drift

The observed \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\) must exceed the 20,000-draw elapsed-time-matched null that preserves:

- SiteID;
- species × MasterSite;
- observed surrounding-abundance trajectory;
- exact abandonment-to-recolonization elapsed time.

Required:

\[
\Delta T
=
T_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathrm{median}(T_{\mathrm{null}})>0
\]

and

\[
p_{\mathrm{drift}}\le0.05.
\]

Only if **both gates** pass is spatial-recovery hysteresis supported.

## Competing interpretation

### Reversible site-quality / basin model

Stable place quality plus current population state determine occupancy.

Prediction:

\[
H\approx0
\]

after same-site pairing and structured temporal-alignment control.

### History-dependent spatial recovery

Current occupancy history changes the effective recovery threshold.

Prediction:

\[
H>0
\]

beyond the frozen structured common-phase null.

Potential mechanisms include social attraction, site fidelity, public information, or other biological memory, but these are not identified by the primary test.

## Literature boundary

The paper must not claim novelty for:

- distinguishing first colonization from recolonization;
- social information in colonial seabirds;
- conspecific attraction;
- Allee effects;
- island-biogeographic hysteresis as a theoretical concept.

Bled et al. (2011) already separated persistence, first colonization and recolonization in Black-legged Kittiwakes.

Burger et al. (2019) already developed a dynamic island-biogeography framework in which colonization and extinction constraints create historical hysteresis.

## Candidate novelty

The candidate contribution is:

> **a prospective multi-species same-site test of whether abandonment and later recolonization occur at systematically different surrounding population thresholds, linked to an independently discovered spatial-recovery anomaly in Antarctic penguins.**

### Both confirmatory gates pass

Then the integrated paper can make the stronger statement:

> **Spatial collapse and spatial recovery occur at different population thresholds in colonial breeders.**

Paper structure:

1. Antarctic penguins discover non-proportional spatial collapse and incomplete reconstruction.
2. SMP independently tests the same-site loss–recovery asymmetry.
3. Discussion places the result in dynamic island biogeography and social habitat-selection theory.

### \(H\le0\) or otherwise unresolved

Do not rescue with:

- alternate vacancy thresholds;
- first-colonization events;
- different abundance transforms;
- species subsets;
- lags;
- traits;
- environmental moderators.

The penguin paper remains the valid bounded result.

## Mechanism boundary

Even a supported result does not prove an Allee mechanism.

Same-site pairing removes fixed place quality.

The structured phase null controls generic temporal alignment with the observed multivariate abundance trajectory.

Neither removes:

- time-varying habitat degradation;
- predators;
- disturbance;
- management;
- unmeasured demographic composition.

Mechanism requires independent information.

## Stop rule

No MAPPPD rescue analysis is opened.
