# Refuge identity is not the transferable rule

**Status:** post-outcome descriptive chronology of the five already-supported concentration trajectories.

The endpoint "fewer effective breeding components" recurs, but the timing and identity of the final dominant component do not.

- **Cormorant:** final dominant begins at rank 4, first becomes dominant only **4 years before the endpoint**, and becomes permanently dominant only **1 year before the endpoint**. Dominant identity switches four times.
- **Humble:** final dominant begins at rank 2 and becomes permanently dominant **20 years before the endpoint**.
- **Litchfield:** final dominant begins at rank 2 and becomes permanently dominant **13 years before the endpoint**.
- **Signy Adélie and chinstrap:** the initial dominant remains dominant throughout the **23-year** retained chronology.

Therefore neither of these is a transferable rule:

> "the initial core is the refuge"

nor

> "a new refuge always emerges late during collapse."

The common process is instead **relative survival under unequal attrition**. Which census component occupies that role can be fixed from the start, sorted early, or turn over late.

This strengthens the shift away from refuge identity as the causal target. The more general state variable is whether a local breeding component maintains enough abundance to stay out of the low-abundance margin.

Combined with the horizon forecast, the next mechanistic question becomes:

> **What physical and demographic conditions allow some penguin breeding patches to maintain a larger local state for years while others are chronically driven toward zero?**

That question can be tested with mapped snow/topography/capacity data without assuming that the same physical type of "refuge" exists in every system.
