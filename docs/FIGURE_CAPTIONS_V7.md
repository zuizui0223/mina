# Figure captions v7 — breeding-component concentration promoted

## Figure 1. Palmer breeding-island network and heterogeneous demographic endpoints

Locations of the six frozen primary island sites (Torgersen, Litchfield, Humble, Christine, Cormorant and Dream) and the non-island Biscoe Point benchmark. Site membership was frozen from APBP/MAPPPD metadata before expanded abundance outcomes were opened [@checastaldo2023]. The map is a local coordinate view rather than a coastline reconstruction. Broader abundance records show heterogeneous endpoints under a shared regional Adélie decline: local extinction at Litchfield, low-abundance persistence on several islands, and strong Adélie-to-gentoo replacement at Biscoe Point.

## Figure 2. Long-term coherence and annual synchrony across five Adélie breeding islands

**Left:** annual breeding-pair abundance on Christine, Cormorant, Humble, Litchfield and Torgersen islands from the complete 1991–2017 synchronized Palmer LTER census. The y-axis is symmetric-log scaled so the observed Litchfield zero state remains visible. Across standardized log-abundance trajectories, PC1 explains **96.4%** of variance. **Right:** Pearson correlations among annual log-growth series for all ten island pairs; median pairwise correlation is **0.373**. This contrast defines the regional long-term background and shorter-term island dynamics; synchrony itself is treated as established scale-dependent context rather than the primary novelty.

## Figure 3. Adélie decline progressively concentrates breeders among colony-code components

**Left:** annual effective colony number, (N_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/\sum_j p_j^2), relative to its first positive-census value on the three islands with unchanged colony-code rosters. Cormorant declined from **3.54 to 2.86** (**−19.1%**), Humble from **4.62 to 2.28** (**−50.6%**), and Litchfield from **5.78 to 1.00** by its final positive census in 2006 (**−82.7%**). **Right:** observed annual (N_{\mathrm{eff}}) slopes (diamonds) compared with the mean and central 95% interval of the most severe frozen fixed-composition count-error sensitivity, Gamma–Poisson CV20%. The null retains each observed island-total trajectory but holds latent colony-code composition constant through time; it therefore represents proportional thinning plus independent count noise rather than biological redistribution. Observed one-sided probabilities under CV20% are **0.0380** for Cormorant and **0.000010** for Humble and Litchfield. Zero of 100,000 simulations produced slopes simultaneously as negative on all three islands (plus-one joint (p=0.000010)). Poisson and CV10% sensitivities also retain all three directional results. The inference is progressive concentration among census breeding components beyond declining total abundance and the frozen error family; colony codes are not assumed to map one-to-one onto physical nesting polygons.

## Figure 4. Prospective mechanism audit

Relative change in held-out prediction error for the finite mechanism family. Positive values indicate lower error after adding the focal predictor; negative values indicate worse transfer. Native scoring metrics are retained in the underlying figure-data table because the frozen analyses use both MSE and RMSE. Annual sea-ice duration provides a small error reduction but has the opposite coefficient direction; the fixed five-year sea-ice test and October snowfall × habitat test worsen transfer. Effective colony number shows a positive raw held-out error reduction, but its gain is **not** supported after synchronized year-block permutation (20,000 permutations; one-sided (p=0.262)). It is therefore plotted as a null-compatible predictive increment rather than a supported forecasting mechanism. The >50-pair group metrics illustrate why predictive improvement alone is insufficient: they improve error but reverse the historical positive-direction hypothesis.

## Figure 5. Colony organization is a conditional demographic state signal and spatially convergent phenomenon

**Left:** partial-regression representation of the effective-colony-number association. Standardized log effective colony number and next-year log population growth are each residualized against island identity, current log abundance and secular year. The fitted slope is **+0.1168**; it is **+0.1185** after one lagged growth term and **+0.1126** after two, retaining **96.4%** of the original coefficient. Under the reused full-series structured null, the two-lag coefficient remains unusual (100,000 independent circular shifts: (p=0.00102); exact covariance-preserving shifts: **5/416**, (p=0.0120)). Its two-lag held-out MSE gain is only **+0.000444**, and the original gain is not unusual under synchronized year-block permutation ((p=0.262)), so the association is not treated as robust out-of-year prediction. Circular-shift Poisson/Gamma–Poisson coupling sensitivities also retain an observed-scale coefficient as rare (maximum (p=0.0078)). **Right:** annual effective colony number for all five islands. The title annotation summarizes independent Torgersen spatial reconstruction: 23 historic active subcolony footprints were reduced to five active footprints by 2022 [@cimino2025]. That mapping, together with subcolony-scale evidence that nesting configuration can affect reproductive performance [@schmidt2021], provides ecological context for progressive concentration but not colony-ID-level validation because public LTER colony codes have not been crosswalked one-to-one to GIS polygons.
