# Integrated manuscript spine v0.1 — process-dependent spatial memory

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Status:** synthesis spine; no new effect search opened.  
**Classification contract:** `contracts/SPATIAL_MEMORY_PROCESS_CLASSIFICATION_V1.md`

## Working title

**Population change can preserve or rewrite the spatial organization of colonial breeders**

Alternative shorter title:

**What population change leaves behind in breeding space**

Running concept: **process-dependent spatial memory**

## One-sentence question

> **What determines whether population change preserves an existing breeding-space template or rewrites it?**

## Claim hierarchy

### Level 1 — directly supported across systems

> **Aggregate abundance change does not uniquely determine spatial reallocation.**

This is supported by the existing Ross, Bird, Emperor, Heard, Palmer, Signy and Beaufort evidence without assigning a common mechanism.

### Level 2 — process-specific empirical signatures

> **Different source-identified processes are associated with different spatial signatures in the available cases.**

- temporary breeding-state/access shock: strong inverse-path recovery;
- persistent attrition: concentration through unequal local loss;
- capacity release: disproportionate growth of newly available/small units and altered export.

### Level 3 — mechanistic hypothesis, not yet a universal law

> **Breeding-site fidelity can carry spatial memory when disturbance suppresses breeding expression without erasing the underlying breeding network; demographic turnover and capacity change can instead alter the template that future breeding expresses.**

Do not promote Level 3 to a universal causal conclusion.

## Why this is stronger than “short-term reversible / long-term irreversible”

Ross rejects a simple duration rule.

- Most of the 2001-2012 redundancy shift occurred in 2001-2002.
- From 2002 to 2012, E3 and E6 increased slightly rather than declining monotonically.
- Other Ross down->up triplets also show strong loss/rebound alignment.

The biological distinction is therefore **what changed**, not simply how long change lasted.

## Evidence architecture

### Case A — Ross Island: acute breeding-state/access perturbation

Role: **primary memory-preserving natural experiment**

Externally documented context:
- B-15A/C-16 altered sea-ice and access conditions;
- published accounts report widespread failed/abandoned breeding;
- aerial census measures breeding pairs / occupied nesting territories;
- independent mark-recapture shows very low movement among colonies for established breeders.

Frozen spatial result:

    1999 N = 207,411
    2001 N = 94,798
    2002 N = 203,996

    loss = 112,613
    rebound = 109,198
    aggregate loss restored = 96.97%

    cosine(loss, rebound) = 0.99695
    inverse-path mismatch = 9.41%

Interpretation:

> A severe temporary breeding disturbance was followed by a rebound that largely re-expressed the previous spatial template.

Boundary:

> The counts do not identify the lost and returning birds individually. Breeding-site fidelity makes this mechanism plausible; it is not direct identity tracking.

### Case B — Palmer + Signy: persistent demographic attrition

Role: **replicated attrition signature**

Palmer frozen result:
- stable-roster island E declines: 19%, 51%, 83%;
- concentration exceeds proportional thinning under the frozen count-error family;
- durable local population/subcolony loss occurs in the ecological background.

Signy frozen result:
- 1998 -> 2009 breeding pairs: 2,688 -> 901;
- E: 3.610 -> 2.539;
- E decline = 29.7%;
- concentration exceeds proportional thinning under the frozen error family;
- independent island-wide monitoring reports long-term Adélie decline and colony disappearance.

Interpretation:

> Persistent population loss was not spatially uniform; unequal attrition changed the relative weight of surviving breeding units.

Boundary:

> The exact local contributions of survival, recruitment, emigration and breeding propensity are not identified for each census component.

### Case C — Beaufort Island: breeding-capacity release

Role: **primary capacity-change contrast**

Externally documented context:
- usable nesting habitat increased 71%;
- island population increased 84%;
- band/resighting evidence indicates Beaufort-to-Ross movement rose to ~3% around 2005 and later declined as local nesting habitat became available.

Corrected spatial result, 2004 -> 2010:
- island total +34.3%;
- new/small unit +108%;
- its share 0.95% -> 1.48%;
- gain = 3.15x proportional expectation;
- effective two-unit number increased slightly.

Interpretation:

> Increasing local capacity allowed more growth to be expressed within the island while inter-island export decreased; capacity changed the spatial level of redistribution.

Boundary:

> The census decomposition does not identify individual movement; the movement result comes from the independent band/resighting study.

## Boundary cases — do not force into mechanism classes

### Bird Island Gentoo

Use only to show:

> positive aggregate change can accompany increased spatial evenness, and annual N/E directions occupy all four quadrants.

Do not call the 1981-2024 interval a capacity or demographic-turnover experiment.

### Heard Island King

Use only as post-result triangulation:

> all observed local units increased, yet dominance reversed and redundancy was non-monotonic.

### Global Emperor

Use only as prospective broad-scale boundary evidence:

> overall decline and concentration coexist with 20 of 50 colonies increasing and all four regional N/E quadrants.

These cases protect the paper from slipping back into a universal “decline concentrates / recovery spreads” rule.

## Proposed Introduction logic

1. Population recovery is usually judged by total abundance.
2. Colonial breeders occupy persistent spatial networks, so abundance and spatial organization are different state variables.
3. Classical island and metapopulation ideas emphasize patch area, connectivity and extinction/colonization, but colonial breeders add strong breeding-site fidelity and seasonal breeding participation.
4. Therefore the same aggregate change can arise from biologically different processes:
   - temporary non-participation;
   - demographic turnover;
   - changing breeding capacity.
5. Prediction: those processes need not leave the same spatial trace.
6. Test with three process-anchored penguin contrasts plus external boundary cases.

## Proposed Results order

### Result 1. Acute disturbance can preserve a recoverable spatial template

Ross loss/rebound path and inverse-path result.

### Result 2. Persistent decline can rewrite relative breeding composition by unequal attrition

Palmer discovery + Signy replication.

### Result 3. Capacity release can redirect redistribution across nested spatial scales

Beaufort within-island proportional residual + independent inter-island movement evidence.

### Result 4. Aggregate direction is not a general proxy for spatial direction

Bird + Emperor primary external tests; Heard as literature triangulation.

## Proposed Discussion logic

### 1. Spatial recovery is not simply abundance recovery

Near-complete numerical rebound can preserve, alter or reverse local composition.

### 2. Breeding-site fidelity is a memory carrier, not always a stabilizer

Fidelity can help re-express a previous spatial template after a temporary participation shock.

But prior theory and Ross work also show that strong site fidelity can slow colonization or trap populations in suboptimal spatial configurations.

Therefore:

> **memory can restore the past, but it can also preserve the wrong past.**

This is more interesting than treating fidelity as uniformly beneficial.

### 3. Islands are both environmental compartments and demographic containers

Same-island colonies can receive shared regional forcing through different access/habitat geometry.

At the same time, changing island capacity can alter whether redistribution occurs:
- within a colony;
- among colonies on the same island;
- between islands.

### 4. Relation to classical island ecology

Do not claim replacement of area/isolation theory.

Instead argue that for mobile colonial breeders:

> island boundaries interact with behavioural fidelity and breeding capacity to determine where demographic change is expressed.

The island is therefore a dynamic demographic container, not just a static point with area and isolation.

### 5. Conservation implication

A recovered headcount does not necessarily mean the same breeding network has been restored.

Conversely, temporary breeding failure can create dramatic census troughs without equivalent loss of the spatial template.

Monitoring should therefore distinguish:
- total breeding abundance;
- breeding-unit occupancy/composition;
- whether lost breeders/sites remain available for re-expression.

## Claims to avoid

Do not write:

- “short-term disturbance is reversible and long-term change is irreversible”;
- “philopatry restores populations”;
- “the same individuals returned to the same colonies”;
- “demographic turnover causes irreversibility”;
- “capacity release always spreads colonies”;
- “the three process classes differ significantly”;
- “all islands behave as demographic containers in the same way.”

## Strongest defensible conclusion

> **Population change has no single spatial inverse. In the available penguin systems, an acute breeding-state disturbance largely re-expressed a prior spatial template, persistent decline concentrated breeders through unequal attrition, and increased breeding capacity redirected growth within an island while reducing export. These contrasts support a process-dependent spatial-memory hypothesis: whether spatial organization is preserved depends on what population change removes or makes newly available.**

## Confirmatory ceiling

This manuscript can be written now as an integrated empirical synthesis.

It cannot honestly claim a prospective test of the Level-3 process-dependent memory law.

A future confirmatory study should freeze process class from natural-history evidence before opening spatial outcomes and use a common pre-disturbance / perturbed / recovery design across multiple systems.
