## Ecological question

The extension asks a narrower and more useful version of "are islands the
population unit?":

> At which nested spatial transition is temporal population variability
> actually reduced: among subcolonies within breeding islands, or among islands
> within the Palmer Archipelago?

This separates two ideas that are easy to conflate.

1. **State hierarchy:** island identity can explain persistent differences in
   abundance and ecological endpoint.
2. **Buffering hierarchy:** temporal compensation can occur among components
   below or above the island level.

The existing five-island analysis already establishes strong island-specific
state differences. The new analysis asks where the compensating temporal
structure resides.

## Why this replaces the fixed-trait CWM extension

A Palmer-Penguins community-weighted morphology series based on fixed
species-level centroids is algebraically driven by species composition. It can
describe replacement, but it does not add a new dynamical level.

The hierarchical variability analysis instead uses the same long-term LTER
population data to connect the ODSP scale problem to the mina ecological
result. It therefore adds a new ecological quantity: the scale at which
temporal covariance reduces aggregate variability.

## Primary method

We use the multiplicative alpha/beta/gamma variability framework of Wang &
Loreau (2014, Ecology Letters 17:891-901, doi:10.1111/ele.12292).

For additive raw breeding-pair abundance:

- alpha variability is the squared abundance-weighted local CV;
- gamma variability is the squared CV of summed abundance;
- beta variability is alpha/gamma and equals the reciprocal of spatial
  synchrony.

Only Cormorant, Humble and Litchfield have an unchanged reported colony-code
roster across all 27 census years. They therefore define the primary nested
sample.

The three-scale partition is

```text
alpha_subcolony
      |
      | beta_within
      v
alpha_island
      |
      | beta_among
      v
gamma_archipelago
```

with the exact identity

```text
beta_total = beta_within * beta_among
```

Christine and Torgersen are excluded from the subcolony-level primary
decomposition because their reported colony-code rosters change through time.
They remain valid for island-level context.

## Primary result

For COR/HUM/LIT, 1991-2017:

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| alpha subcolony CV2 | 0.86121 |
| alpha island CV2 | 0.80209 |
| gamma archipelago CV2 | 0.79319 |
| beta within islands | **1.07370** |
| beta among islands | **1.01123** |
| beta total | **1.08576** |

Thus most of the multiplicative reduction in raw temporal variability occurs
during the **subcolony -> island** transition, not the **island ->
archipelago** transition.

On the additive log-beta scale, the within-island transition contributes
**86.4%** of total log(beta), versus **13.6%** for the among-island transition.
This is a decomposition of the multiplicative beta factor, not an estimate that
86.4% of a causal insurance mechanism has been identified.

All three eligible islands individually have subcolony beta values above the
COR/HUM/LIT among-island beta.

## Fixed robustness checks

### Before Litchfield extinction

Restricting the same three islands to 1991-2006 gives:

- beta within = **1.10300**
- beta among = **1.01784**
- beta total = **1.12268**
- within share of log(beta) = **84.7%**

The hierarchy ordering therefore predates post-extinction zero counts.

### Removing Litchfield entirely

Restricting the full 1991-2017 series to the two persistent islands with stable
colony-code rosters, COR/HUM, gives:

- beta within = **1.08182**
- beta among = **1.00518**
- beta total = **1.08743**
- within share of log(beta) = **93.8%**

The hierarchy ordering therefore is not generated by the extinct island.

### Removing the shared long-term trend

Because log transforms are not additive, these checks are reported as
adjacent-scale covariance synchrony rather than as an exact three-level
alpha/gamma partition.

For linearly detrended log1p abundance:

- among-island beta = **1.16880**
- within-island beta range = **1.768-7.134**
- median within beta = **3.411**
- median within / among ratio = **2.92**
- every eligible island has within beta > among beta

For annual log1p growth:

- among-island beta = **1.50633**
- within-island beta range = **2.679-5.141**
- median within beta = **3.497**
- median within / among ratio = **2.32**
- every eligible island has within beta > among beta

Thus the hierarchy result becomes more, not less, visible after the dominant
shared decline is removed from consideration.

## Relationship to the existing N_eff result

The two results answer different questions.

- **N_eff coefficient:** after abundance, island, year and demographic momentum
  are controlled, more even distribution among current colony codes has a
  small positive association with next-year island growth.
- **Hierarchical beta:** subcolony trajectories contain substantially more
  temporal compensation than island totals do.

Together they justify the bounded statement that internal breeding-patch
organization contains ecological information not present in island abundance
alone.

They do **not** establish that asynchrony causes the N_eff coefficient, that
birds move among subcolonies to create a rescue effect, or that N_eff is an
early-warning indicator.

## Revised conceptual result

The strongest synthesis is now:

> The scale at which Palmer Adélie populations differ is not the same as the
> scale at which their temporal fluctuations compensate. Islands retain
> distinct demographic states and endpoints, but neighboring islands share
> nearly the same long-term direction. Most detectable temporal compensation
> occurs among breeding subcolonies within islands.

A compact version is:

> **islands organize fate; subcolonies organize compensation.**

The compact phrase is useful as an internal spine, but the manuscript should
use the more explicit wording above.

## Manuscript integration

Do not rewrite Ecosphere v0.5 in place yet. If this extension is promoted into
the main paper, create v0.6 and make four bounded changes:

1. **Introduction:** replace the broad "scale problem" paragraph with a direct
   hierarchy question: whether demographic information is concentrated among
   islands or among breeding components within islands.
2. **Methods:** add the roster gate and Wang-Loreau raw-abundance
   decomposition after the colony-organization model.
3. **Results:** add a subsection titled approximately "Temporal compensation
   was concentrated within islands rather than among islands."
4. **Discussion:** reinterpret N_eff as the entry point to a hierarchy result,
   not as a predictor. Keep all causal spatial-insurance and early-warning
   language excluded.

The five-island PC1, annual-growth synchrony and endpoint divergence then become
the upper-level context; N_eff and hierarchical beta provide the lower-level
result.

## Claim boundary

Supported:

- island-to-archipelago raw beta is close to one;
- the exact nested raw decomposition places the larger beta transition within
  islands;
- this ordering survives pre-extinction and Litchfield-removal checks;
- detrended and annual-growth sensitivities show the same qualitative
  hierarchy in every stable-roster island.

Not supported:

- causal spatial insurance;
- dispersal-mediated rescue;
- colony-code/GIS polygon equivalence;
- predictive or early-warning use of beta or N_eff;
- a claim that island identity is ecologically unimportant.
