# Ross Island iceberg shock–rebound audit v1

**Status:** post-result ecological reinterpretation of the frozen Ross V2 interval. This audit does not alter the frozen V2 decision; it changes what the 2001 baseline is allowed to mean biologically.

## Why this audit is necessary

The frozen V2 recovery phase began in 2001 because the published Ross Sea synthesis defined a positive 2001–2012 trend and the periods were fixed before count magnitudes were opened.

After effect opening, the 2001 endpoint proved to be a known system-wide low associated with the B-15A/C-16 mega-iceberg disturbance.

Therefore 2001 cannot be treated as an ordinary equilibrium starting point for recovery.

## What the aerial census measures

The Ross Island aerial census photographs colonies annually from helicopters at 2,000–2,500 ft, using photographs taken as close as possible to 1 December (25 November–8 December).

At that point in the breeding cycle, the photographed colony is represented mainly by one member of each pair occupying/incubating at a nesting territory, with few non-breeders present.

Before 2006 the same Pentax 645 film-camera workflow was used.

Thus the 2001-to-2002 contrast is not plausibly explained by a 2001/2002 camera-system switch.

However, the response variable is best interpreted as **occupied breeding territories / breeding-pair participation**, not total surviving adult population size.

## Independent ecological context

Published Ross Sea work identifies a common low point around 2001 for the southern colonies.

The B-15A and C-16 mega-icebergs altered sea-ice and ocean access. During the initial iceberg breeding season, many adult Adélie penguins failed or abandoned breeding attempts early.

Cape Royds was especially affected by persistent fast ice and recovered slowly; Bird and Crozier were less affected after the initial disturbance and subsequently grew.

This means part of the 2001 trough and 2002 rebound can reflect changes in breeding participation as well as survival, recruitment and movement.

## Stage 1 — shock: 1999 -> 2001

Total occupied breeding territories:

    -54.3%.

All 6/6 frozen components declined.

But spatial redundancy increased:

    E3 +7.4%
    E6 +11.5%.

Three-colony multiplication factors:

- Royds: 0.378x
- Bird: 0.556x
- Crozier: 0.429x

Thus the shock **temporarily equalized** the Ross Island breeding distribution because the proportional reduction differed among colonies and Crozier lost share to Bird.

This is not spatial recovery. It is disturbance-driven compositional equalization.

## Stage 2 — rebound: 2001 -> 2002

Total occupied breeding territories:

    +115.2%.

All 3/3 colonies increased.

All 6/6 frozen components increased.

Yet:

    E3 -12.8%
    E6 -20.6%.

Three-colony multiplication factors:

- Royds: 1.638x
- Bird: 1.546x
- Crozier: 2.400x

At the finer scale, Cape Crozier West increased 2.488x, the largest frozen component multiplier.

Crozier's total share rose:

    70.8% -> 79.0%.

The immediate rebound therefore **reconcentrated** breeding participation after the shock-driven equalization.

## Stage 3 — later trajectory: 2002 -> 2012

From 2002 to 2012:

    N +72.1%
    E3 +2.35%
    E6 +8.31%.

All three colonies increased, but the distribution did not continue to become progressively more concentrated.

Thus the main compositional event is the early shock/rebound transition, not a decade-long monotonic erosion of E.

## Pre-shock versus late state: 1999 -> 2012

Using 1999 as the last complete pre-shock benchmark:

    N +69.3%
    E3 -4.14%
    E6 -4.16%.

Local endpoint changes:

- Royds: -537 pairs (-14.8%)
- Bird: +28,346 (+59.9%)
- Crozier: +115,899 (+74.1%)

Therefore the late state exceeds pre-shock total abundance but does not restore all local nodes equally.

Royds remains below its 1999 occupied-territory count, whereas Bird and Crozier are far above theirs.

## Composition path

Relative to 1999 three-colony shares, total-variation distance is:

- 2001: 0.0493
- 2002: 0.0354
- 2012: 0.0214.

So by 2012 the **shares** are closer to the pre-shock composition than they were during the immediate shock/rebound, despite the large differences in absolute local abundance.

This is why the result should not be described as simple permanent divergence.

## Correct interpretation

The strongest Ross statement is:

> **A shared environmental shock temporarily equalized occupied breeding territories across Ross Island colonies; the immediate rebound was disproportionately expressed at Crozier and rapidly reconcentrated the breeding distribution, after which spatial redundancy fluctuated without a clear monotonic trend.**

A second, longer-term statement is:

> By 2012 total occupied breeding territories exceeded the last complete pre-shock benchmark, but local recovery was uneven: Royds remained below its 1999 level while Bird and Crozier were well above it.

## What the frozen V2 test still means

The V2 prediction was fixed before counts were opened:

    2001-2012 numerical recovery should increase E3.

It failed on the frozen endpoints.

That decision remains unchanged.

But the ecological interpretation is now narrower because the starting year was a disturbance trough.

The frozen result is evidence against **simple spatial deconcentration from the published 2001 recovery breakpoint**, not a general proof that long-term population recovery erodes redundancy.

## Implication for mechanism

The Ross case is now better framed as **shock response and path dependence**.

Potential contributors include:

- colony-specific breeding propensity;
- recruitment and survival;
- movement;
- differential sea-ice access;
- subcolony fragmentation and edge effects;
- nest-site fidelity and hysteresis.

The counts alone cannot separate them.

## Claim boundary

Supported:

- 2001 is a real biological low, not an obvious survey-method discontinuity.
- the response is occupied breeding territories/breeding participation, not total adult abundance.
- shock caused large declines across every frozen component.
- the shock temporarily increased E.
- the immediate rebound increased every component but decreased E sharply.
- 1999-to-2012 recovery was strongly unequal among colonies.

Not supported:

- 2001-to-2002 count growth equals demographic recruitment/population growth one-for-one.
- E declined monotonically throughout 2001-2012.
- Ross alone establishes a general recovery-concentration law.
