# Ross Island V2 result synthesis v2 — disturbance, rebound, and branch dependence

**Status:** post-result biological synthesis. The confirmatory V2 decision is unchanged.

## Confirmatory decision

The pre-effect V2 contract fixed:

- decline: 1985-1999;
- recovery: 2001-2012;
- primary units: Royds, Bird, Crozier;
- prediction for recovery: total breeding abundance increases and E3 increases.

Observed frozen recovery endpoints:

    breeding-territory N: 94,798 -> 351,119
    E3: 1.7286 -> 1.5428
    E6: 2.2919 -> 1.9702.

Therefore the frozen recovery-deconcentration prediction **fails**.

No period or unit is changed to rescue that decision.

## Measurement boundary

The aerial series measures adults occupying nesting territories / breeding-pair abundance around incubation.

It is not a total-adult population census.

Published methods show no obvious observation-method discontinuity across 1999-2002, but published natural history identifies 2001/02 as the initial B-15A / C-16 mega-iceberg disturbance season, when many adults failed or abandoned breeding attempts.

Therefore the 2001 -> 2002 pulse is a **breeding-abundance rebound**, not a one-year doubling of the adult population.

## Process decomposition

### 1999 -> 2001: shock-induced equalization

    N -54.3%
    all six components decline
    E3 +7.4%
    E6 +11.5%
    Crozier share 75.4% -> 70.8%.

The shared shock hit the spatial distribution non-proportionally and temporarily increased evenness.

### 2001 -> 2002: asymmetric breeding rebound

    N +115.2%
    all six components increase
    E3 -12.8%
    E6 -20.6%
    Crozier share 70.8% -> 79.0%.

Three-colony multipliers:

    Royds   1.638x
    Bird    1.546x
    Crozier 2.400x.

Relative to exact proportional rebound from the 2001 composition:

    Royds    -703 pairs
    Bird   -15,954 pairs
    Crozier +16,656 pairs.

The immediate rebound therefore re-concentrated breeding abundance at Crozier.

### 2002 -> 2012: partial spatial re-expansion

    N +72.1%
    all six components increase
    E3 +2.3%
    E6 +8.3%.

Three-colony multipliers:

    Royds   1.377x
    Bird    1.861x
    Crozier 1.691x.

Bird gains share and Crozier loses a small amount.

Thus the system does not continue concentrating after the immediate rebound.

## Pre-shock to long-term endpoint

1999 -> 2012:

    breeding N +69.3%
    E3 -4.1%
    E6 -4.2%.

Colony-level endpoint factors:

    Royds   0.852x
    Bird    1.599x
    Crozier 1.741x.

The total breeding count exceeds the pre-shock total, but Royds remains below its 1999 count and the composition is modestly more concentrated.

## Branch-dependence signature

Similar total breeding abundance occurs in different spatial states:

    1997 decline:
    N = 204,837
    E3 = 1.626

    2002 rebound:
    N = 203,996
    E3 = 1.507.

And:

    1985 decline:
    N = 224,414
    E3 = 1.625

    2004 recovery:
    N = 221,301
    E3 = 1.413.

Thus observed E is not a single-valued function of N.

This is consistent with spatial hysteresis / configuration memory, but is not causal proof because the environmental states differ.

## What the Ross result now means

The strongest process statement is:

> **The iceberg breeding shock and the subsequent breeding rebound followed different spatial paths: the shock temporarily equalized colony shares, whereas the immediate rebound disproportionately restored breeding abundance at Cape Crozier.**

This is stronger than calling 2001-2012 a monotonic concentrating recovery, which the data do not support.

## Mechanism boundary

Local Adélie configuration hysteresis through nest-site fidelity, fragmentation and edge predation is already established in prior work.

PR189 generates the cross-scale hypothesis that such configuration memory can influence **which colony absorbs a shared breeding rebound**.

That hypothesis is frozen separately and remains untested causally.

## External context

Heard Island king penguins provide post-result triangulation that monotonically increasing breeding abundance can accompany non-monotonic E and dominance reversal.

Beaufort provides the opposite local direction: after habitat release, a small/new subcolony gains disproportionate share and E increases slightly.

Together these cases argue against any universal "recovery concentrates" or "recovery spreads" rule.
