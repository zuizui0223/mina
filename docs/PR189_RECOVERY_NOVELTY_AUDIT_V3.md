# PR189 novelty audit v3 — shock-aware positioning

**Status:** post-result literature positioning.

## Not new

Do not claim novelty for:

- Adélie colony-specific vital rates;
- breeding-habitat limitation or dynamic capacity;
- Allee effects in colonial seabirds;
- Adélie "frozen herd" hysteresis;
- aggregate recovery masking local spatial failure;
- reciprocal Simpson / Hill effective-number metrics.

All are established in prior literature.

## What the frozen Ross test actually contributed

The preregistered Ross V2 recovery-deconcentration prediction failed.

But post-result audit shows that 2001 was an externally documented mega-iceberg breeding-disturbance trough.

The process sequence is:

    1999 -> 2001 shock:
    every component down
    E up

    2001 -> 2002 rebound:
    every component up
    E sharply down

    2002 -> 2012:
    every component up
    E modestly up.

Therefore the useful empirical result is not "recovery generally concentrates."

It is:

> **a shared breeding disturbance and its immediate rebound can have opposite spatial allocation patterns.**

The shock temporarily equalized the distribution; the rebound disproportionately restored breeding abundance at the dominant Crozier colony.

## Cross-scale novelty relative to frozen-herd theory

McDowall & Lynch already establish local Adélie spatial hysteresis.

Schmidt et al. already show configuration-linked reproductive-success differences and slow Royds recovery.

PR189 does not rediscover that mechanism.

The generated extension is:

> **can local configuration memory alter the metapopulation-scale allocation of a shared breeding rebound among colonies?**

That cross-scale causal link is not established by PR189; it is the next hypothesis.

## Branch dependence

Ross provides a post-result signature that motivates the hypothesis.

At nearly matched total breeding abundance, pre-/decline and rebound/recovery years occupy different E states.

This demonstrates observational path dependence.

It does not by itself identify the cause.

## Heard contribution

Heard Island king penguins show a different path:

- both monitored colonies increase at every observed interval;
- E is non-monotonic;
- dominant identity reverses.

This independently reinforces the general point that breeding-abundance recovery has a compositional path not determined by total abundance.

It is post-result triangulation, not confirmatory replication.

## Beaufort contribution

Beaufort prevents a universal concentration claim.

After documented habitat release, a small/new subcolony gains disproportionate share and within-island E rises slightly, while inter-island export declines.

This is consistent with dynamic capacity opening a different recovery route.

## Strongest defensible novelty

The strongest current contribution is conceptual-empirical:

> **Spatial collapse and breeding rebound are not necessarily inverse transformations. A shared disturbance can flatten the spatial distribution of breeding abundance, while the immediate rebound restores abundance disproportionately among the same nodes; later recovery can then move redundancy in yet another direction.**

A second, broader contribution from Heard is:

> **breeding recovery can be non-monotonic in spatial redundancy and can replace the dominant breeding node even while all monitored local counts rise.**

## Why this matters

Aggregate breeding abundance, occupancy and spatial composition answer different questions.

A system can recover total breeding abundance without returning to its previous spatial configuration.

That distinction matters for:
- vulnerability to site-specific disturbance;
- conservation dependence on dominant colonies;
- interpretation of "recovery" from colony counts;
- testing hysteresis and spatial memory.

## Claim ceiling

Safe:
- Ross shock and rebound are spatially asymmetric.
- Ross E is not a single-valued function of total breeding abundance in the observed trajectory.
- Heard shows non-monotonic redundancy and dominance reversal during continuous local count increases.
- Beaufort shows a spreading recovery route is possible.

Not yet safe:
- configuration memory caused the Ross colony-level rebound allocation;
- island capacity universally determines recovery path;
- recovering colonial populations generally lose redundancy.
