## Figure 1. Shared regional decline, divergent island endpoints

**A.** Locations of the Palmer breeding-island discovery system. **B.** Annual breeding-pair abundance for the five synchronized Adélie island populations, 1991–2017. Standardized log-abundance trajectories share a dominant long-term component (PC1 = **96.4%**), while local endpoints differ in persistence, severe decline and extinction. This figure establishes the common regional background against which within-island reorganization is evaluated. Site coordinates and trajectories are inherited from the frozen Palmer figure-data package; no new site selection is introduced.

## Figure 2. Decline is accompanied by concentration among within-island breeding components

**A.** Effective colony number, (N_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/\sum_j p_j^2), through time for Cormorant, Humble and Litchfield, the three islands with unchanged colony-code rosters. Effective colony number declined from **3.54 to 2.86** on Cormorant, **4.62 to 2.28** on Humble and **5.78 to 1.00** on Litchfield before local extinction. **B.** Observed annual (N_{\mathrm{eff}}) slopes compared with the mean and central 95% interval of the most severe prespecified fixed-composition Gamma–Poisson CV20% null. Observed one-sided probabilities are **0.0380**, **0.000010** and **0.000010**, respectively; the three-island joint plus-one probability is **0.000010**. The null preserves observed island-total trajectories while holding latent colony composition constant, so rejection indicates redistribution beyond proportional thinning plus independent count error.

## Figure 3. Antarctic-wide island-architecture interaction is directionally concordant but non-confirmatory

**A.** Species-specific area × habitat-heterogeneity loading effects ((\gamma_{AH})) from the frozen 2 km V3 model: Adélie **−0.304**, chinstrap **−1.184** and gentoo **−0.318**. Chinstrap and gentoo satisfy the full point-estimate option–fragmentation crossover geometry; Adélie shows attenuation but not sign reversal. **B.** Frozen 9,999-permutation distribution of the preregistered paper-level statistic, the median (\gamma_{AH}) across the three species. The observed median is **−0.318** and the one-sided permutation probability is **0.0947**; the 5% lower reference quantile is approximately **−0.424**. Species-specific Holm-adjusted tests also do not reject. The figure therefore shows concordant direction without confirmatory cross-species support.

## Figure 4. The non-confirmatory Antarctic result reflects limited detectability and radius instability, not an established scale-dependent rule

**A.** Cross-species median (\gamma_{AH}) at the prespecified 2 km scale and the sensitivity radii: **−0.058** at 1 km, **−0.318** at 2 km and **+0.107** at 5 km. A joint block-preserving multi-radius null shows that the observed 2 km negative → 5 km positive switch plus an observed-size contrast occurs with plus-one probability **0.0810**; the raw sign switch alone occurs with probability **0.2478**. The radius result is therefore interpreted as sensitivity/instability, not established biological scale dependence. **B.** Retrospective detection curve for a common three-species (\gamma_{AH}), evaluated against the realized frozen primary permutation rule on the real observation layout. Detection is **4.7%** for a true effect of −0.30, **38.0%** at −0.40, **81.0%** at −0.50 and **92.7%** at −0.55. Interpolated thresholds are **MDE80 ≈ 0.498** and **MDE90 ≈ 0.539** in absolute (\gamma_{AH}). The observed absolute median (0.318) lies well below those thresholds, so non-rejection does not establish absence of a moderate common interaction, although very large common effects would usually have been detected.
