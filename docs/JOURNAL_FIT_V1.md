# Journal fit v1

## First-shot target: Journal of Animal Ecology

The strongest fit is **Journal of Animal Ecology (JAE), Research Article**.

Why:

- the primary object is animal population ecology rather than a purely spatial biodiversity pattern;
- the paper tests a broad ecological principle: common regional demographic direction can coexist with patch-specific local collapse;
- the 27-year synchronized census and within-island breeding-state analysis fit JAE's population/community/ecosystem animal-ecology remit;
- the negative prospective mechanism tests are useful because they separate pattern from mechanism rather than presenting a post hoc climate correlation;
- the colony-organization result is explicitly predictive and modest, avoiding a causal fragmentation claim.

Current JAE requirements checked on 27 September 2026:

- Research Articles: up to 8,500 words including title page, abstract, references and legends;
- abstract: maximum 350 words, simple factual **numbered statements**;
- keywords: maximum eight, in alphabetical order;
- double-anonymized review with a separate title page;
- main manuscript structure: Abstract, Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, References;
- data availability statement with database/accession numbers or DOIs for public data.

Current scope language emphasizes animal-ecology research that develops, tests and advances broad ecological principles.

## Alternative 1: Oikos

Oikos is plausible if the manuscript is pushed harder toward the conceptual idea of **externally subsidized breeding islands** and scale separation. The editorial bar is riskier because Oikos explicitly prioritizes work that substantially changes understanding of ecological mechanisms, processes or patterns and gives low priority to straightforward extensions of established results. The modest colony-network effect would need to remain secondary to the broader conceptual synthesis.

## Alternative 2: Ecography

Ecography is plausible because the paper analyses ecological variation in space and time. It is less natural than JAE because the primary inference is based on five neighbouring islands in one regional system rather than a broad spatial comparison, and the paper's strongest novelty is demographic scale separation rather than a general spatial model.

## Submission positioning

For JAE, lead with:

1. **animal-demographic scale separation** across synchronized neighbouring populations;
2. prospective falsification of simple environment-to-growth mechanisms;
3. a specific within-island breeding-organization state variable with small held-out predictive value;
4. independent spatial triangulation that keeps causality bounded.

Do not lead with:

- Palmer Penguins morphology;
- Biscoe Point replacement;
- a claim of a new climate driver;
- causal habitat fragmentation;
- the 1.4% colony-network gain as though it were the dominant result.

The central JAE sentence is:

> A common long-term demographic trajectory does not imply interchangeable local population dynamics, and internal breeding-patch organization can carry local vulnerability information that is invisible at the regional scale.
