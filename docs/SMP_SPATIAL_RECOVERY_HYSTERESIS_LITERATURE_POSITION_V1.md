# SMP spatial-recovery hysteresis — literature position v1

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** literature boundary frozen before SMP outcome opening.

## What is already established

### 1. Persistence, first colonization and recolonization are distinct seabird processes

Bled, Royle & Cam (2011, *Ecology*, doi:10.1890/10-0392.1) modeled 20 years of Black-legged Kittiwake nest-site occupancy and explicitly separated persistence, first colonization and recolonization. Their models included local density, site-specific breeding success and neighboring breeding success.

Therefore the present study must not claim novelty for distinguishing recolonization from first colonization.

### 2. Site-dependent regulation / buffer effects are established in colonial seabirds

Kokko, Harris & Wanless (2004, *Journal of Animal Ecology*) showed strong site-dependent breeding-site use in Common Guillemots during population growth.

Bennett et al. (2022, *Journal of Animal Ecology*, doi:10.1111/1365-2656.13674) followed the same population across increase, decline and recovery and tested whether site-quality use changed with population trend. High-quality sites remained disproportionately occupied at lower population size, but new lower-quality sites continued to be established during both decline and recovery.

Therefore the present study must not claim novelty for the buffer effect or for studying site use across growth and decline.

### 3. Allee effects can theoretically delay recolonization of seabird breeding patches

Schippers et al. (2011, *Ecological Modelling*, doi:10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2011.05.022) introduced Allee-type density dependence into a spatial seabird metapopulation model and showed that it can strongly reduce recolonization distance and prolong recolonization of empty breeding patches.

Therefore the present study must not claim novelty for the prediction that coloniality or Allee effects can slow spatial recovery.

### 4. Island-biogeographic hysteresis is already a theoretical concept

Burger et al. (2019, *Frontiers of Biogeography*, doi:10.21425/F5FBG44383) developed a dynamic island-biogeography framework in which colonization and extinction constraints can generate history dependence and hysteresis.

Therefore the present study must not claim invention of island-biogeographic hysteresis.

## What remains open

The Antarctic penguin analyses generated a specific empirical anomaly:

- breeding distributions concentrate beyond proportional thinning during decline;
- regional effective breeding-site number can remain reduced while abundance increases.

Those observations suggest that numerical recovery may fail to reverse spatial loss, but they do not directly test the same breeding place during loss and later recovery.

## Candidate contribution

The candidate new contribution is deliberately narrow:

> **A prospectively frozen, multi-species, same-site empirical test of whether local breeding-site abandonment and later recolonization occur at different surrounding-population states.**

For each physical SiteID:

[
H=A_{recolonize}-A_{abandon}.
]

The test is stronger than a cross-sectional occupancy model because:

1. the same SiteID is paired against itself, controlling fixed place identity;
2. the focal SiteID is removed from the surrounding population abundance;
3. abandonment/recolonization spells are frozen before abundance magnitude is opened;
4. species are the macroecological replication level;
5. a common-phase null preserves each MasterSite block's observed multivariate abundance trajectory while breaking alignment with the frozen events.

## Allowed novelty statement if supported

> Across colonial seabirds, spatial recovery required a higher surrounding population state than spatial loss at the same breeding sites, showing that population recovery did not simply retrace spatial collapse.

## Not allowed even if supported

Do not write:

- "We discovered Allee effects in seabirds."
- "We first showed recolonization differs from colonization."
- "We discovered hysteresis in island biogeography."
- "Conspecific attraction caused the result."
- "Site quality is irrelevant."
- "The same mechanism explains every species."

## Mechanism interpretation

A positive result would be consistent with:

- social attraction / social information;
- site fidelity and established-colony memory;
- low-density positive feedback;
- delayed recruitment;
- persistent biological modification of breeding sites.

But the same pattern could also arise from time-varying habitat degradation, predators, disturbance or management.

The primary paper therefore tests **spatial recovery asymmetry**, not its unique cause.
