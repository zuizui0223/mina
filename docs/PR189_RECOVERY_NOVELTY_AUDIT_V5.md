# PR189 novelty audit v5 — strict literature boundary

**Status:** post-result positioning after comparison with metapopulation recovery and community-recovery literature.

## What is already well established

### Aggregate recovery can differ from local recovery

Metapopulation theory already shows that aggregate abundance can return while local patches remain collapsed, occupancy remains reduced, production remains impaired, or rescue effects govern recovery.

Wilson et al. (2023) explicitly developed this spatial-recovery framework.

Therefore this paper does **not** claim discovery that aggregate population recovery can hide spatial problems.

### Abundance recovery can differ from compositional recovery

Community ecology is even more explicit.

A global meta-analysis of pulse disturbances found that total abundance recovered strongly on average while multivariate community composition showed much weaker recovery.

Long-term restoration studies likewise distinguish recovery of abundance/diversity from recovery of composition.

Therefore this paper does **not** claim the broad principle that "the total can recover without composition."

### Path dependence and hysteresis are established concepts

Do not claim novelty for:
- hysteresis;
- ecological memory;
- Allee effects;
- frozen-herd dynamics;
- dynamic carrying capacity;
- uneven disturbance effects.

## What is narrower and potentially distinctive here

The focal state is **within-species abundance composition among persistent colonial breeding nodes**.

Ross differs from a classic hidden-collapse case:

- all six fixed breeding components remain represented;
- the immediate rebound increases every component relative to the disturbance trough;
- aggregate breeding abundance returns to 98.35% of the pre-shock total;
- yet the vector of local abundances is not restored.

The mismatch is not caused by missing patches.

It is caused by **reallocation among occupied nodes**.

Local fractions of shock loss restored by 2002 range from:

    10.8% to 109.8%.

This is an empirical demonstration of spatial reallocation within a single-species breeding network during a documented natural disturbance/rebound.

## The prospective external contribution

Bird Island Gentoo is not a second preidentified recovery phase.

It is a prospective test of the broader sign-coupling proposition.

A mechanically frozen long-interval comparison yields:

    N +34.2%
    E +15.7%.

The full time series contains all four delta-N / delta-E sign combinations.

Thus the Ross result cannot be generalized into a directional rule such as "positive recovery concentrates."

Instead Bird supports the narrower mathematical/ecological statement:

> aggregate change and relative local change are distinct components of population dynamics.

## Mathematical novelty boundary

The identities:

    d log N/dt = r_bar

and:

    d log p_i/dt = r_i - r_bar

are elementary demographic algebra.

Likewise the Hill-Simpson decomposition is not new mathematics.

Do not sell the paper as a new theorem.

The contribution is using this decomposition to make an overlooked monitoring distinction explicit in a real colonial metapopulation:

    aggregate restoration
    versus
    restoration of the local-abundance vector.

## Relation to Wilson et al. (2023)

Wilson et al. focus recovery metrics on:
- aggregate recovery rate;
- patch occupancy;
- relative production;
- nonrecovery.

Their hidden-collapse regime is especially relevant: aggregate abundance can be near pre-disturbance while many patches remain collapsed.

Ross occupies a different corner of the same conceptual space:

- aggregate abundance nearly restored;
- occupancy of monitored breeding nodes retained;
- every node rebounds from the trough;
- composition nonetheless differs from the pre-disturbance state.

A safe positioning is:

> **an abundance-allocation extension of spatial metapopulation recovery, focusing on relative abundance among occupied nodes rather than patch loss alone.**

Do not say this dimension has never been considered anywhere.

## Relation to community-composition recovery

Community studies measure changes in relative abundance among species.

This paper measures changes in relative abundance among **spatial breeding units of one species**.

The mathematics is analogous.

The biological meaning is different:
- no species turnover is required;
- no patch extinction is required;
- the spatial distribution of breeding effort changes within one population network.

That is the useful niche.

## Strongest novelty statement

> **A documented disturbance can be followed by near-complete recovery of aggregate breeding abundance without recovery of the local-abundance vector, even when every monitored breeding node rebounds; a prospective independent network further shows that positive aggregate change does not have a fixed spatial direction.**

## Why this matters

Management commonly reports colony/species totals.

Those totals can be nearly restored while:
- dependence on particular breeding nodes changes;
- local deficits persist;
- different sites overshoot;
- spatial redundancy changes.

The implication is not that E should replace abundance.

It is that **recovery targets defined only on the aggregate can miss redistribution among persistent breeding sites**.

## Claim ceiling

Safe:
- aggregate and local-allocation recovery are distinct in Ross;
- Ross is not a hidden-collapse-by-occupancy example;
- Bird prospectively shows no fixed sign coupling between aggregate and E;
- disturbance geometry plausibly explains much of Ross's immediate asymmetry.

Not safe:
- first discovery that abundance and composition recover differently;
- first spatial metapopulation recovery framework;
- universal recovery law for penguins;
- causal hysteresis in Ross;
- E as a universally superior conservation metric.
