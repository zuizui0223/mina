# PR189 mechanism closure v1

**Status:** mechanism-search stop record after all prespecified/current clean routes were resolved.

## Why this file exists

The state/allocation paper is empirically supported.

The remaining question was:

> what predicts which breeding nodes receive the contrast-mode rebound?

Several routes were tested under increasingly strict measurement designs.

None supplies a clean, prospectively confirmed general predictor.

Therefore mechanism searching inside the currently opened datasets stops here.

## Route 1 — Bird Island Gentoo operational success index

Pre-effect statistic:

    chicks_t / nests_t
      ->
    next-year breeding-share change.

Frozen result:

    beta = +0.3238
    permutation p = 0.0001.

But post-effect audit found:

- shared current-nest denominator geometry;
- operational chicks/nests values >2 because initial nest counts and later chick counts need not represent a literal fixed cohort;
- a strong denominator-only negative-control signal;
- near-symmetric backward structure.

A post-result ratio-free model remained weakly positive:

    standardized beta ≈ +0.15
    p = 0.0126.

Status:

    **suggestive only; not confirmatory mechanism evidence.**

## Route 2 — Port Lockroy Gentoo ratio-free replication

A denominator-safer late/crèche output test was frozen before outcome opening.

Result:

    **SUPPORT FAIL**

because fixed-subcolony late output was not available at the required spatial resolution.

No earlier chick endpoint was substituted.

## Route 3 — Signy Chinstrap prospective ratio-free replication

Frozen seven-colony roster:

    C15, C18, C46, C47, C79, C80, C81.

Support:

    15 consecutive transitions
    105 colony-season rows.

Predictor:

    late fledgling output residualized against current breeding abundance,
    colony and start-season.

Response:

    next-season breeding share controlling current share.

Frozen result:

    beta_Q = -0.0225
    standardized beta = -0.064
    permutation p = 0.6837.

Six of seven leave-one-colony-out coefficients were negative.

Status:

    **TERMINAL FAIL.**

No alternate lag, earlier chick endpoint, alternate colony subset or ratio is searched.

## Route 4 — Ross subcolony geometry prospective test

This route removed the abundance-denominator issue entirely.

Public Schmidt et al. subcolony tables supplied:

    491 consecutive-transition rows
    114 distinct subcolonies
    Crozier 15 transitions
    Royds 3 transitions.

Frozen focal predictor:

    within-colony z[log perimeter-to-area ratio].

Response:

    log(next active territories / current active territories).

Adjustment:

    current local abundance
    + mapped subcolony area
    + colony × start-season fixed effects.

Frozen result:

    beta_P = -0.0196
    +1 SD multiplier = 0.981
    permutation p = 0.1514.

All 114 leave-one-subcolony-out estimates remained negative, but the geometry-assignment permutation test did not pass.

Colony-specific descriptive signs:

    Crozier beta_P = -0.0288
    Royds   beta_P = +0.0126.

Status:

    **TERMINAL FAIL.**

No alternative terrain/geometry variable is searched in the opened Schmidt tables.

## Program-level mechanism conclusion

The correct conclusion is:

> **The spatial-allocation phenomenon is supported, but no general local predictor of the contrast mode has been prospectively confirmed.**

More specifically:

- reproductive output does not replicate as a prospective predictor across Bird/Signy;
- perimeter-to-area configuration does not pass the frozen prospective growth test;
- published Ross access geometry remains biologically plausible for the focal iceberg episode, but that is external natural-history interpretation rather than a general fitted predictor.

## What remains known mechanistically

For the focal Ross event, independent published work already establishes that the iceberg disturbance changed:

- access to open water;
- sea-ice persistence;
- travel routes;
- breeding dispersal;
- apparent survival

differently among colonies.

Thus:

    shared disturbance × local access geometry

is a plausible case-specific mechanism for unequal rebound.

But PR189 does not identify a transferable node-level rule that predicts contrast-mode allocation across systems.

## Stop rule

For PR189:

- do not search additional lags;
- do not screen Schmidt terrain covariates;
- do not try alternative productivity ratios;
- do not select favorable colony/subcolony subsets;
- do not convert post-result diagnostics into confirmatory tests.

Any new mechanism analysis must begin as a separate project/route with:

1. predictor measured independently of current abundance;
2. outcome and lag frozen before predictor effects are opened;
3. an independent system or genuinely unopened outcome.

## Paper consequence

PR189 is finalized as a **state/allocation paper**, not a mechanism paper.

Main evidence:

1. Ross natural disturbance/rebound;
2. calibrated 9.41% inverse-path mismatch;
3. Bird prospective positive-net-change sign test;
4. Emperor prospective decline sign test.

Mechanism failures belong in a concise Discussion/Supplement section as evidence that the obvious predictors do not yet close the problem.
