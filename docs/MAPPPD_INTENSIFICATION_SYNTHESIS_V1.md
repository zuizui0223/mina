# MAPPPD increasing-network intensification — synthesis v1

**Status:** post-outcome descriptive decomposition. Not part of the frozen Ecology inference.

## Where did growth go?

The three regional networks already known to increase in abundance were decomposed using exactly the frozen site rosters, seasons and observation calibration.

### Adélie — Victoria Land

Total abundance increased **33.1%** (387,917 → 516,140) while effective breeding-site number fell **26.6%**.

- 8/11 retained sites increased and 3/11 decreased.
- Cape Crozier West (CRZW), already the largest site, increased from 144,341 to 250,987 adjusted pairs.
- Its regional share increased **37.2% → 48.6%**.
- CRZW alone contributed **83.2% of net regional growth**.
- The initially largest half of retained sites contributed **95.4% of all gross positive gains**.

This is a strong descriptive intensification pattern: growth occurred across several sites, but most additional breeders accumulated in an already dominant site.

### Gentoo — Central-west Antarctic Peninsula

Total abundance increased **21.8%** while E fell **16.3%**.

- 4/7 sites increased and 3/7 decreased.
- Initially dominant Cuverville Island remained dominant and increased its share **33.9% → 40.0%**.
- It contributed **67.9% of net growth**.
- The initially largest half of sites contributed **95.8% of gross positive gains**.

Again, numerical growth was highly uneven among already occupied sites.

### Gentoo — South Shetland Islands

Total abundance increased **64.1%** and E fell only **1.1%**.

- 4/5 sites increased and 1/5 decreased.
- The initially dominant Ardley Island increased in count but lost share (**37.5% → 33.1%**).
- The initially largest half of sites still contributed **89.4% of gross positive gains**, but growth was much less dominated by the initial largest site.

This panel is closer to diffuse multi-site growth and is an important counterexample to a universal rich-get-richer rule.

## Overlay with the independently frozen 2-km ice-free-area atlas

For Victoria Land Adélie, the dominance of Cape Crozier West is not a trivial ranking by mapped ice-free area.

- CRZW: **614 ha** mapped ice-free area within 2 km; Δ ≈ **+106,646** pairs.
- Cape Crozier East: **771 ha**; Δ ≈ **−6,368**.
- Inexpressible Island: **809 ha**; Δ ≈ **+7,738**.
- Cape Bird South: **928 ha**; Δ ≈ **+1,159**.

Three retained sites have more mapped ice-free area than CRZW, yet none absorbed anything close to its growth. Across the 11 Victoria Land sites the descriptive Spearman correlation between 2-km mapped ice-free area and count change is only about **0.08**.

This atlas metric is a site-centred proxy, not occupied or unoccupied nesting capacity, so it cannot reject habitat limitation. It does show that a simple "more ice-free area → more growth" explanation is insufficient.

## Process implication

The exposed data now support a sharper generated hypothesis:

> **Penguin population growth can first intensify already occupied breeding sites; spatial expansion is a separate transition that may require crowding/resource limitation at established sites to overcome philopatry, founding costs and island separation.**

This matches published East Antarctic evidence that Adélie population growth is constrained when breeding-habitat availability becomes low and that the Windmill Islands recorded their first new breeding-patch colonisation only after roughly half a century of sustained growth.

## Boundary

This result does not show individual dispersal and does not test empty-site colonisation. It only demonstrates that, in two of the three increasing regional networks, the numerical increase was strongly concentrated in a minority of already occupied monitored sites.
