# Torgersen Adélie extinction-order source gate — result v1

The dynamic cascade route **stops at Stage A**.

The public supplementary DOCX was downloaded and audited structurally before opening any dynamic spatial effect:

- **8 tables**, all devoted to snow/weather/model metadata;
- **16 media objects**;
- **0 embedded spreadsheets/GIS objects**;
- Fig. S13 displays total and sub-colony breeding-pair time series graphically;
- no machine-readable table joins historic sub-colony identity, extinction year, area/aspect and polygon/centroid geometry.

The article states that historic 1998/99 perimeters were digitized by the authors from the Patterson et al. map, while 2020/2022 perimeters came from drone/GPS surveys. The cited Duke archive publicly provides raw imagery, orthomosaics, DSMs, flight plans and GCP material, but no exact published historic-subcolony ID ↔ extinction ↔ geometry bridge was located.

Therefore the frozen primary question — whether **loss of nearby occupied Adélie sub-colonies dynamically increases isolation and predicts the next extinction beyond area/aspect** — is not identifiable reproducibly from the located public sources.

No Figure 5/S13 hand digitization, visual polygon matching, distance threshold search, or reopened LTER colony-code crosswalk is allowed.

What remains supported from the publication is the already-known static process:
- 23 historic sub-colonies → 5 active by 2022;
- all 10 south-aspect historic sub-colonies extinct;
- larger historic colonies generally went extinct later (published R=0.75, p=0.0009).

That is useful mechanism context, but not a new dynamic-cascade result.
