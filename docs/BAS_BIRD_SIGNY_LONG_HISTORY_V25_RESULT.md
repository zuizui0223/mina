## Scientific conclusion and scientific guardrails

**The observed negative distant-region growth correlation in V23 is an epoch-specific descriptive outcome, NOT a stable geographic-distance law.** Across 33 source-verified annual windows, far-region Gentoo changes are weakly aligned on average, with positive observed pre-1997 association and weaker negative post-1997 association. A fixed-sign regional synchrony hypothesis does not have support across historical time.

The **1997 cut is observationally meaningful**: Signy population surveys conform to CCAMLR CEMP standards from the 1996/97 season. That regime change is confounded with changing Antarctic marine climate, population trajectories, local breeding/observer methods, changing source coverage and biological density. The pre/post coefficient contrast CANNOT be called an environmental regime shift or an effect of monitoring standardization without independent tests and external data.

This observation moves the ecological question beyond merely whether sites co-fluctuate in 8 selected years: **when and why does spatial synchrony reorganize across regional Antarctic/subantarctic breeding systems?** Potential distinct mechanisms include marine access/prey anomalies, seasonality, density/colony retention, source procedures and demographic lag. None is causally identified by these two original physical island systems, and searching windows or climate lags after inspecting correlations is not a valid rescue.
