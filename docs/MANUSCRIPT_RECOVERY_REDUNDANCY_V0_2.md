# Manuscript spine v0.2 — Recovery is not the spatial reverse of collapse

**Status:** revised after the Ross mega-iceberg shock/rebound audit. Supersedes v0.1 for interpretation.

## Working title

**Breeding recovery is not the spatial reverse of collapse**

Alternatives:

- **A shared disturbance equalizes breeding distribution before an unequal rebound**
- **Opposite local processes produce the same spatial concentration in colonial penguins**
- **Spatial recovery depends on history, not breeding abundance alone**

## Central question

When breeding abundance declines and later rebounds, does spatial organization retrace the path of decline in reverse?

The simple inverse-path expectation is:

    decline:
    local units shrink or disappear
    -> concentration

    recovery:
    those units regrow
    -> deconcentration / restoration.

The evidence instead shows path dependence.

## Measurement boundary

The Ross aerial series measures **occupied nesting territories / breeding-pair abundance near incubation**, not total adult population size.

Therefore throughout the manuscript:

- write "breeding abundance", "breeding-pair counts", or "occupied nesting territories";
- do not equate annual count change with survival/recruitment population growth;
- allow breeding propensity, abandonment, movement, survival and recruitment to contribute to count change.

This is especially important around the 2001 mega-iceberg disturbance, when many adults failed or abandoned breeding.

## Empirical backbone

### 1. Decline — Palmer/Signy

Frozen decline decomposition:

- 0/39 frozen components increased first-to-last;
- joint N-down/E-down transitions contain only ~153 gross gains versus ~7,034 gross losses.

Interpretation:

    concentration by differential attrition.

This is a loss-driven route.

### 2. Ross Island — shared shock, equalization, unequal rebound

The frozen Ross V2 recovery interval remains 2001-2012 and its deconcentration prediction failed.

But 2001 is not an ordinary baseline.

It is a documented common low associated with B-15A/C-16 mega-iceberg conditions and widespread breeding failure/abandonment.

#### Last complete pre-shock benchmark: 1999

    N = 207,411
    E3 = 1.609
    E6 = 2.056.

#### Shock trough: 2001

    N = 94,798
    E3 = 1.729
    E6 = 2.292.

1999 -> 2001:

    N -54.3%
    all 6 frozen components decline
    E3 +7.4%
    E6 +11.5%.

The shock temporarily **equalizes** the breeding distribution.

#### Immediate rebound: 2002

    N = 203,996
    E3 = 1.507
    E6 = 1.819.

2001 -> 2002:

    breeding N +115.2%
    all 3 colonies increase
    all 6 components increase
    E3 -12.8%
    E6 -20.6%
    Crozier share 70.8% -> 79.0%.

The rebound therefore **reconcentrates** breeding participation, with the strongest increase at Cape Crozier West.

#### Later state: 2012

    N = 351,119
    E3 = 1.543
    E6 = 1.970.

Relative to 1999:

    N +69.3%
    E3 -4.1%
    E6 -4.2%
    Royds -14.8%
    Bird +59.9%
    Crozier +74.1%.

Thus aggregate breeding abundance exceeds the pre-shock benchmark, but local restoration is unequal.

### 3. Ross branch-dependence signature

Use only recovery years whose total N lies inside the decline-phase N support.

This mechanically yields two matched-N comparisons:

    1997 decline N=204,837, E3=1.626
    2002 rebound N=203,996, E3=1.507

and:

    1985 decline N=224,414, E3=1.625
    2004 rebound N=221,301, E3=1.413.

Similar current breeding abundance therefore occurs in different spatial states on different historical branches.

This is a **hysteresis-compatible signature**, not proof of endogenous hysteresis.

### 4. Heard Island King penguins — external path triangulation

Spit Bay North and South both increase at every observed interval:

    1963 13 / 5
    1965 36 / 9
    1969 49 / 37
    1980 82 / 400
    1988 215 / 3,100.

E is non-monotonic:

    1.670 -> 1.471 -> 1.962 -> 1.393 -> 1.138.

Dominance reverses:

    North -> South.

Thus even continuous local breeding-count increases can pass through equalization and reconcentration around a new dominant node.

This is post-result external triangulation, not confirmatory replication.

### 5. Beaufort — capacity-release contrast

2004 -> 2010:

- main colony +33.6%;
- new subcolony +108%;
- new subcolony receives ~3.15x its proportional expected gain;
- E rises slightly;
- published inter-island export declines after local nesting habitat expands.

This prevents any universal "recovery concentrates" rule.

## Mathematical bridge

For breeding abundance B_i with shares p_i and E=1/sum(p_i^2):

    d log(E)/dt = 2(r_bar - r_D)

where r_i is the instantaneous rate of change in **measured local breeding abundance**.

Over a finite interval:

    E1/E0 = (G_bar/G_D)^2.

The identity is algebra, not a novel theorem.

Its purpose is to show:

> the sign of total breeding-abundance change does not determine the sign of spatial-redundancy change.

## Conceptual result

Spatial concentration is a state, not a mechanism.

It can arise through:

### Differential attrition

    local losses
    + unequal loss rates
    -> concentration.

### Unequal breeding rebound

    all local breeding counts rise
    + dominant/advantaged nodes rebound faster
    -> reconcentration.

### Dominance reversal

    subordinate node grows fastest
    -> equalization
    -> overshoot
    -> concentration around a new dominant.

### Capacity-opening spread

    low-share/new node receives disproportionate growth
    -> E up.

## Why Ross is now more interesting, not less

The 2001-baseline caveat weakens the simplistic claim:

> recovery erodes redundancy.

But it creates a more biologically specific result:

> **a shared shock and its rebound can move the same metapopulation through different spatial states, and total breeding abundance alone does not specify which state it occupies.**

That is directly relevant to hysteresis and ecological memory.

## Mechanism: what is prior knowledge

Do not claim novelty for:

- colonial Allee effects;
- frozen-herd hysteresis;
- nest-site fidelity;
- edge-predation effects;
- breeding-capacity limitation;
- Ross colony-specific vital rates.

McDowall & Lynch already show hysteretic spatial dynamics from fragmentation, edge predation and nest-site fidelity.

Schmidt et al. show higher edge exposure and lower/more variable reproductive success at Royds and discuss slow post-iceberg recovery consistent with a frozen configuration.

## Generated cross-scale hypothesis

The novel **future** hypothesis is:

> local colonial configuration memory redistributes regional recovery among breeding nodes: nodes pushed into fragmented, low-performance configurations rebuild more slowly than nodes that retain compact/high-performance configuration, so aggregate recovery need not restore the pre-disturbance spatial allocation.

This is not yet confirmed.

## Results order

1. Decline concentration is differential attrition.
2. Ross frozen recovery-deconcentration prediction fails.
3. 2001 is a documented shared breeding-participation shock trough.
4. The shock equalizes composition; the immediate breeding rebound reconcentrates it.
5. Similar total breeding abundance occurs in different spatial states on decline and rebound branches.
6. Heard shows non-monotonic recovery and dominance reversal.
7. Beaufort shows capacity-opening spread.

## Discussion opening

> Recovery is often treated as the reversal of decline, but a population can revisit the same total breeding abundance in a different spatial configuration. On Ross Island, a shared mega-iceberg disturbance reduced breeding counts across every monitored component and temporarily equalized the colony distribution; the immediate rebound increased every component while sharply reconcentrating breeding abundance at Cape Crozier. Similar total breeding abundance before and after the disturbance corresponded to different levels of spatial redundancy.

Then:

> The same concentration metric therefore cannot identify the demographic path that generated it.

## Strongest current claim

> **Breeding recovery is path dependent: total breeding abundance does not uniquely determine spatial organization, and concentration can be generated by unequal losses, unequal rebound, or dominance turnover.**

## Claim boundary

Ross supports:
- shock-driven equalization;
- unequal breeding rebound;
- branch-dependent spatial state;
- incomplete local restoration by 2012.

Ross does not establish:
- total adult population doubling from 2001 to 2002;
- a monotonic recovery-concentration law;
- causal colonial hysteresis.

Heard is:
- independent species/island triangulation;
- post-result selected.

Beaufort is:
- a contrasting capacity-release case;
- not a replication of Ross.

## Next decisive experiment/data analysis

The next major advance requires repeated spatial colony maps, not another count-only time series.

Prospective test:

    at matched current breeding abundance and environment,
    prior fragmentation/configuration predicts subsequent recovery.

Needed variables:

- breeding abundance;
- subcolony geometry/perimeter-area;
- reproductive success;
- usable capacity;
- environmental forcing;
- ideally movement/recruitment.

This would directly test whether known within-colony frozen-herd hysteresis scales up to metapopulation recovery allocation.
