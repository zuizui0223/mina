# Cover letter draft — Ecosphere v2

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Common decline, divergent endpoints:
multiscale demography across Antarctic penguin breeding islands,”** for
publication in *Ecosphere* under the **Animal Ecology** subject track.

The manuscript uses a complete 27-year synchronized census of five neighboring
Adélie penguin breeding islands, broader regional breeding-site records, and
independent spatial reconstruction to ask how a shared regional population
decline is translated into different local ecological outcomes. The system is
unusual in island ecology because penguins obtain their primary food resources
at sea while reproduction remains constrained to discrete terrestrial patches.
This separation makes regional demographic direction and local breeding-patch
fate distinct ecological dimensions.

We do not present the high common long-term component as a new synchrony
phenomenon. Long-term versus annual synchrony is placed in established
timescale-dependent synchrony theory. The biological emphasis is instead on
divergent local endpoints under the same regional decline: persistence at low
abundance, local extinction/vacancy, species replacement at a nearby benchmark
site, and habitat-structured subcolony attrition.

The manuscript also reports prospectively bounded tests that did not support
simple annual or low-frequency sea-ice-duration mechanisms or an October
snowfall-by-habitat interaction. Within islands, effective colony number has a
positive conditional coefficient with next-year growth after abundance, island
and time are controlled. We explicitly do **not** present it as a predictor:
its small held-out improvement is compatible with a synchronized year-block
permutation null (p = 0.262). We then corrected the coefficient null for serial
structure. The coefficient remained extreme under 100,000 island-wise circular
shifts (p < 0.00001), an exact covariance-preserving 416-member shift group
(p = 1/416 = 0.00240), and fixed circular-shift Poisson/Gamma-Poisson
count-error sensitivities (largest coupled-null p = 0.00780). We therefore
retain only a structured-null-robust **conditional association**, not an
early-warning, predictive, or causal claim.

Independent Torgersen mapping provides phenomenon-level evidence that real
breeding footprints have contracted non-randomly across physical habitat, while
the unresolved crosswalk between census colony codes and mapped polygons is
kept as an explicit inferential boundary.

We believe this study fits *Ecosphere* because it combines long-term animal
population ecology, island biogeography, habitat relationships, and
marine–terrestrial coupling in an understudied ecological setting, while
distinguishing robust ecological pattern and association from unsupported
prediction and mechanism.

The source data are publicly archived and cited. Novel analysis code is
publicly accessible for peer review through the project repository and will be
permanently versioned in Dryad or Zenodo if the paper is accepted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
