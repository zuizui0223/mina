# Figure captions — replicated concentration manuscript v0.2

## Figure 1. Declining penguin populations lose effective breeding components

Annual breeding-pair abundance (solid line) and effective breeding-component number, N_eff = 1 / Σ p_j² (dashed line), each indexed to 100 in the first eligible season. The three Palmer panels are Adélie penguin populations with unchanged census-unit rosters; Litchfield stops at its final positive census in 2006. Signy panels use the prospectively frozen 22-season Adélie five-unit panel and chinstrap nine-unit panel. The figure is descriptive; inferential support comes from the fixed-composition count-error nulls summarized in Figure 2.

## Figure 2. Breeding-space contraction recurs across five population units

**Left:** first-to-last change in effective breeding-component number for Cormorant, Humble and Litchfield Adélie penguins near Palmer Station, and prospectively frozen Adélie and chinstrap panels at Signy Island. Effective component number declined by 19–83% in every population. **Right:** strength of evidence against the most severe frozen fixed-composition error model (Gamma–Poisson with 20% multiplicative CV), shown as −log10 of the one-sided Monte Carlo probability. The dashed line marks p = 0.05. Labels give the observed N_eff slope per year. Each null preserves the observed annual breeding-pair total while holding latent relative breeding composition constant, so rejection indicates directional redistribution beyond proportional thinning plus the specified independent count error.

## Figure 3. The same contraction endpoint arose through different internal routes

Post-hoc descriptive decomposition of the initially dominant and ultimately dominant breeding units. Each panel shows the share of breeding pairs carried by the initially dominant unit from the first to final eligible season; where a different unit became dominant, its first- and final-season shares are shown separately. On all three Palmer islands, the initially dominant unit had zero breeding pairs by the final eligible census and dominance shifted to a different unit. At Signy, the initially dominant unit remained dominant and increased its share in both Adélie and chinstrap penguins. These summaries were frozen as descriptive outputs after the primary concentration results were known; they carry no p-values and do not alter the concentration inference.
