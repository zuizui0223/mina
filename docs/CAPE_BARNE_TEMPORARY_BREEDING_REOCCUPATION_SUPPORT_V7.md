# Cape Barne: documented transient reoccupation hidden by the Ross three-colony census grain

**2026-10-08 | published-prior-art site-history audit, PR #192 | NOT new causal discovery**

## Site and observation identity

Cape Barne is a *separate coastal nesting patch* about 3 km south-east of Cape Royds, on the **same physical Ross Island**. It is not a separate island or a band-marked recipient, and not one of the three Royds/Bird/Crozier colony IDs (nor one of the six census subdivisions) in the frozen Ross aerial workbook.

**Primary contemporary account** — Taylor & Wilson, 'Recent increase and southern expansion of Adélie penguin populations in the Ross Sea, Antarctica, related to climatic warming', *New Zealand Journal of Ecology* 14 (1990):
- On **1 December 1988** a small Cape Barne colony had **five occupied pebble nests**, *one with one egg and four with two eggs*, plus five apparently unused nests.
- Nesting birds behaved as experienced breeders; the paper proposed previous recruitment/founding by young birds **a few years earlier**, not necessarily 1988 as the true first year.
- By **22 January 1989**, egg/chick mortality counted to that date was about **23%**, compared by the authors to **~28%** in a recent three-season Cape Royds benchmark (not a matched same-season sample). **Do not infer breeding fledging success, fitness equivalence, or absence of an Allee effect.**
- Source: https://newzealandecology.org/nzje/1883/pdf

**Independent modern field-history synthesis** — Emslie (2026), *Antarctic Science* doi:10.1017/S0954102026100807:
- Cape Barne was occupied much earlier during the Holocene; multiple episodes of small-site occupation and abandonment occurred, including sparse 1960s/1980s/1990s breeding.
- Some pairs nested at Cape Barne and Horseshoe Bay in the late 1990s based on historical observations.
- The author found **no nesting pairs during a visit in January 2001**, and no reported subsequent breeding through the 2024/25 revisits; old intact nest structures were observed in December 2024.
- Authors relate the late-1980s expansion at Cape Barne to Cape Royds' population growth and possibly earlier seasonal fast-ice breakout/open-water access. These hypotheses were **already advanced by prior workers**; concurrent winds, polynya changes and marine prey competition are alternative causal pathways.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0954102026100807

**1990s monitoring coverage caution**: isolated archival records do NOT provide a complete annual detection-corrected 1984–2001 zero/positive panel for Cape Barne. We have a verified 1988 positive and 2001 observed absence, *not* an annual measured 1987 zero→1988 five event. A 2024 survey's lack of breeding also does not automatically prove every intervening season was zero. The exact date of colonization and disappearance is interval-censored.

## What this changes in PR #192

The prior structural result remains true **only for the frozen six aerial census components and three major colonies**: all 26 annual observations from 1985–2012 (78 three-colony unit-years; 156 six-component unit-years) were positive. That is a definition of the monitored **roster**, not evidence Ross Island contained no ephemeral small colonies.

Cape Barne demonstrates that the true breeding landscape can have short-lived reoccupation **without those events appearing as zero→positive in the major-colony count matrix**. Its documented active nests are not just an unverified absence threshold. The correct biological synthesis is:

> The parent Royds colony persisted while a nearby historical nesting patch was briefly active and later abandoned. The fate of nesting *options* at this spatial grain can differ from that of the larger colony without necessarily crossing a physical island boundary.

The 1988 partial chick survival rules out the deterministic claim that small new sites cannot begin reproduction at all. It **does not** identify whether later collapse was due to access, mortality, a delayed recruitment shortage, changes in adult breeding participation, skua predation, site fidelity, or regional marine forcing.

## Competing process hypotheses for future independent test

**Access-switch H_A**: the pebble beach remains physically present but early summer fast-ice breakouts periodically permit or impede safe arrival/ocean access. Needs verified local season-specific fast-ice breakup timing and an independently mapped travel corridor before breeding dates, not just broad sea-ice extent.

**Source-pressure H_N**: colonization at the satellite patch rises with Royds pre-event abundance/crowding, perhaps through prebreeder prospecting; disappearance follows a source cohort decline. Needs local crowding before establishment and evidence that birds breeding there originated at Royds.

**Demographic-trap H_D**: colonization succeeds transiently, but long-term reproductive returns and recruitment fall below replacement as patch stays small / exposed. Needs repeated reproductive output and known-age recruitment at Cape Barne, not a single December/January snapshot.

**Historical occupancy memory H_H**: old nest terrain, repeated earlier breeding and prospecting facilitate reuse beyond purely contemporary area/access. This effect needs observed recruitment/choice conditional on independent site quality, not just the presence of fossil guano/nests.

These are **not assumed mutually exclusive**. A new reoccupation event will be a clean test only if preceded by surveyed absences and accompanied by independently measured environmental exposure and known site identity.

## Prior-art veto and branch boundary

- Do not claim newly discovered intermittent recolonization of Cape Barne. Already explicitly described in Taylor & Wilson 1990; global census Lynch & LaRue 2014; Emslie 2026.
- Do not relabel the single site as an independent island or count it as independent regional metapopulation replication.
- Do not rewrite frozen PR #189's 3/6-node calculation or claim it was erroneous: the **data grain differs**.
- Do not treat archived five nests as evidence of five immigrants from Beaufort or Royds.
- Do not open PR #142 individual resight records by bypassing its official key and outcome-blind gate.

**Decision:** eligible archival **occupied→observed absent** within-island site history (coarse intervals) exists outside the Ross roster; zero→positive timing and its specific cause cannot yet be independently estimated. This weakens any blanket assertion that Ross Island had no site turnover, but does not establish source-capacity-mediated cross-island rescue.
