# Ecosphere cover-letter draft — v0.6

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Common decline, divergent endpoints: hierarchical demography across Antarctic penguin breeding islands,”** for publication as an Article in the Animal Ecology subject track of *Ecosphere*.

The manuscript asks whether the spatial scale that distinguishes demographic states is also the scale at which temporal population fluctuations compensate. Using a complete 1991–2017 census of five neighboring Adélie penguin breeding islands near Palmer Station, we show that the regional decline is strongly coherent while local demographic states and endpoints remain distinct. A nested variability analysis then locates most detectable temporal compensation below the island level: among the three islands with unchanged colony-code rosters, raw beta variability is 1.0737 from breeding components to islands but only 1.0112 from islands to the archipelago. This hierarchy persists before Litchfield extinction, after removing Litchfield, and in detrended and annual-growth sensitivities.

The manuscript also reports a deliberately bounded secondary result. Effective colony number retains a small positive conditional association with next-year growth after abundance, island, time, and the preceding two annual growth intervals are controlled, and the coefficient remains unusual under the previously frozen structured-null family. Its held-out predictive gain is weak and is not presented as prediction, causality, or early warning. Independent spatial work from Torgersen Island is used only as phenomenon-level triangulation because census colony codes are not crosswalked one-to-one to mapped subcolony polygons.

We believe the study fits *Ecosphere* because it uses a long-term ecological data set to separate demographic state, spatial synchrony, and within-patch temporal compensation across nested ecological scales, while explicitly bounding unsupported mechanisms. The data sources are publicly archived, and the novel analysis code and frozen result receipts are accessible for peer review through the project repository. A permanent version-of-record archive will be provided if the manuscript is accepted.
