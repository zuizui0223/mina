# Ecosphere cover-letter draft — v0.8

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Regional decline, local concentration: within-island reorganization of Adélie penguin breeding groups,”** for publication as an Article in the Animal Ecology subject track of *Ecosphere*.

Using a complete 1991–2017 census of five neighbouring Adélie penguin breeding islands near Palmer Station, we ask whether regional population decline simply thins all within-island breeding components proportionally or instead reorganizes the distribution of breeders among them. The five island populations share a strongly coherent long-term decline but retain different demographic endpoints.

Our primary result is a progressive within-island concentration of breeders. In the three islands with unchanged colony-code rosters, effective colony number fell by 19% on Cormorant, 51% on Humble and 83% on Litchfield before local extinction. We tested these trends against a fixed-composition null that retained each observed island-total trajectory and added independent Poisson and Gamma–Poisson count error. Even under an intentionally severe, uncalibrated 20% multiplicative-CV sensitivity, the observed concentration remained unusual on all three islands individually and jointly. The result therefore rejects simple proportional thinning as a sufficient description of decline.

We interpret this as within-island demographic reorganization rather than as identification of a physical habitat mechanism. Public census colony codes are not crosswalked one-to-one to mapped nesting polygons, so we do not claim that snow, topography, predation, movement or habitat quality caused the concentration. Independent Torgersen reconstruction provides phenomenon-level spatial triangulation by documenting contraction from 23 historic active subcolonies to five active footprints and habitat-structured extinction.

A secondary result is that effective colony number remains positively associated with next-year population growth after current abundance, island identity, time and the preceding two growth intervals are controlled. This association survives structured and count-error nulls, but its held-out predictive increment is weak and is not presented as prediction, causality or early warning. An observed nested beta-variability hierarchy is retained only as secondary scale context because a severe uncalibrated count-error scenario can reproduce it.

We believe the study fits *Ecosphere* because it distinguishes two biologically different aspects of population decline: how many breeders are lost and how the surviving breeders are reorganized within island breeding structure. More broadly, it shows that long-term population contraction can have an internal spatial-demographic architecture that is not captured by abundance trajectories alone.

The source data are publicly archived, and the analysis code, frozen contracts and result receipts are accessible for peer review through the project repository. A permanent version-of-record archive will be provided if the manuscript is accepted.
