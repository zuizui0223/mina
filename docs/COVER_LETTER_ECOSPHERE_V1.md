# Cover letter draft — Ecosphere

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“Common decline, divergent endpoints:
multiscale demography across Antarctic penguin breeding islands,”** for
publication in *Ecosphere* under the **Animal Ecology** subject track.

The manuscript uses a complete 27-year synchronized census of five neighboring
Adélie penguin breeding islands, broader regional breeding-site records, and
independent spatial reconstruction to ask how a shared regional population
decline is translated into different local ecological outcomes. The focal
system is unusual in island ecology because penguins obtain their primary food
resources at sea while reproduction remains constrained to discrete terrestrial
patches. This separation lets us distinguish regional demographic direction
from local breeding-patch fate.

We do not present the very high common long-term component as a new synchrony
phenomenon. Instead, we place long-term versus annual synchrony in established
timescale-dependent synchrony theory and focus the biological inference on
divergent local endpoints: persistence at low abundance, local
extinction/vacancy, species replacement at a nearby benchmark site, and
habitat-structured subcolony attrition.

The manuscript also reports several prospectively bounded tests that did not
support simple annual or low-frequency sea-ice-duration mechanisms or an
October snowfall-by-habitat interaction. A census-derived effective-colony
metric remains conditionally associated with next-year growth, but we explicitly
withdraw a predictive interpretation because its small held-out improvement is
compatible with a 20,000-replicate synchronized year-block permutation null
(p = 0.262). Fixed count-error simulations and independent Torgersen mapping
are used only to bound, not inflate, the remaining interpretation.

We believe this study fits *Ecosphere* because it combines long-term animal
population ecology, island biogeography, habitat relationships, and
marine–terrestrial coupling in an understudied ecological setting, while
distinguishing robust ecological pattern from unsupported mechanism.

The underlying source data are publicly archived and cited. Novel analysis code
is publicly accessible for peer review through the project repository and will
be permanently versioned in Dryad or Zenodo if the paper is accepted.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
