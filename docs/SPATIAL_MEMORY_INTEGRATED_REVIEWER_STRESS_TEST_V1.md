# Integrated spatial-memory manuscript — reviewer stress test v1

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Manuscript tested:** `docs/MANUSCRIPT_SPATIAL_MEMORY_INTEGRATED_V0_4.md`  
**Decision:** viable integrated Ecology/JAE-level manuscript framing; not yet a Nature-level causal test.

## Executive assessment

The integration is scientifically better than the standalone Ross reversibility Report because it asks a biological question that survives the failure of a universal decline/recovery direction rule:

> **when does an observed breeding distribution merely disappear from the census, and when is the underlying spatial organization actually rewritten?**

The strongest conceptual move is to separate:

- an **expressed breeding vector** observed in counts;
- a **latent site-affiliated pool** that can persist when adults skip breeding;
- **breeding capacity**, which changes the set of feasible destinations.

The schematic decomposition

[
n_{i,t} \approx S_{i,t} q_{i,t}, qquad n_{i,t} \le K_{i,t}
]

does not claim that (S), (q), or (K) are separately estimated. It clarifies why a 54% breeding-census crash can be compatible with rapid restoration of the previous allocation.

## Major reviewer attacks

### 1. “Ecological memory and spatial recovery are already mature literatures.”

**Severity: high.**

Correct. Ecological memory has long been framed through biological legacies that survive disturbance, and spatial-recovery theory already treats dispersal, local dynamics, network structure, and disturbance geometry as determinants of recovery.

**Required response:** do not claim invention of ecological memory, spatial resilience, or process-dependent recovery.

The narrower contribution is:

> in a mobile colonial population, a biologically relevant spatial legacy can remain latent in surviving individual-to-site affiliations even when those individuals temporarily disappear from the breeding census.

This is an empirical bridge between ecological-memory theory and breeding-site fidelity, not a new general theory of memory.

### 2. “You never observe the latent spatial template directly.”

**Severity: high.**

Correct.

Ross colony counts do not identify individual birds, and the 1999 loss cannot be paired bird-for-bird with the 2002 rebound.

**Defense:** the latent-template interpretation is Level 3 mechanistic synthesis only. Direct results remain:
- 96.97% aggregate loss restoration;
- cosine 0.99695;
- 9.41% allocation mismatch;
- independent breeder movement <0.20% in the later 25-year mark-recapture series;
- common breeder-to-nonbreeder transitions.

Never write “the same birds rebuilt the same distribution.”

### 3. “The Ross reversibility is not exceptional.”

**Severity: high.**

Correct.

Other complete Ross down->up triplets have inverse-path mismatch 9.12% and 10.63%, close to the focal 9.41%.

**Defense:** exceptionality is not the claim. Ross is useful because the cause of the trough is independently documented and aggregate restoration is nearly complete.

The correct claim is:

> a severe externally documented breeding disturbance can leave enough spatial legacy for the next observed rebound to largely re-express the prior allocation.

### 4. “There is only one clean temporary-shock case.”

**Severity: high; main ceiling on impact.**

Correct.

Ross is the only high-confidence A-class natural experiment with the needed three-state design. The 2002-2003-2004 Ross episode is not an independent system.

**Consequence:** the manuscript cannot claim a tested three-class law. It can present a process-anchored synthesis and generate a prospective prediction.

A genuinely high-impact follow-up would need an independent shock system classified before opening the spatial rebound.

### 5. “Palmer/Signy are called demographic turnover without directly estimating turnover.”

**Severity: medium-high.**

Use **persistent demographic attrition** in empirical Results.

The data directly show concentration beyond proportional thinning. Independent literature supports durable colony loss and long-term demographic decline, but the local survival/recruitment/movement decomposition is not identified.

Reserve “turnover” for the hypothesis layer or write “attrition/turnover processes.”

### 6. “Beaufort conflates two datasets and time windows.”

**Severity: medium-high.**

Yes.

The aerial census identifies disproportionate growth of the small/new within-island unit. The band/resighting study independently identifies declining export toward Ross Island as habitat availability increased.

**Defense:** call this a case-level conjunction, not mediation. Do not claim that the 497-pair gain consists of retained emigrants.

### 7. “The three process classes use different estimands.”

**Severity: high if hidden; low if explicit.**

Ross: three-state inverse path.  
Palmer/Signy: persistent concentration against proportional thinning.  
Beaufort: capacity-linked proportional residual plus independent movement evidence.

**Defense:** heterogeneity is explicit and biologically necessary. Do not create an omnibus score.

### 8. “The boundary cases are cherry-picked.”

**Severity: medium.**

Bird and Emperor have prospectively frozen endpoints. Heard is openly post-result triangulation.

Keep those inferential statuses visible in the main table.

The role of these cases is only to falsify sign-locking, not to estimate process-class effects.

### 9. “Why is this island ecology rather than generic population ecology?”

**Severity: medium-high.**

The island contribution must remain concrete:

1. **environmental compartment:** colonies on different parts of the same island receive shared regional forcing through different access/snow/terrain geometry;
2. **demographic container:** changing local breeding capacity can alter within-island redistribution and between-island export simultaneously.

Avoid generic claims that islands are special because they are isolated.

### 10. “Site fidelity is already known to structure animal populations.”

**Severity: high for novelty language.**

Correct. Site fidelity, breeding dispersal, and their response to colony restoration/disturbance are well established in seabirds and other colonial animals.

The novelty is not “fidelity matters.”

The useful new proposition is:

> **observed spatial collapse and loss of latent spatial organization are not the same biological event.**

That distinction becomes testable because breeding-pair censuses measure expression while mark-recapture demonstrates persistent site affiliation among established breeders.

## Prior-art positioning that must enter the manuscript

- Johnstone et al. 2016: ecological memory as information/material legacies surviving disturbance.
- Zelnik et al. 2019: spatial recovery regimes depend on dispersal, local dynamics, and system structure.
- Wilson et al. 2023: spatially structured disturbances and local/regional processes alter metapopulation recovery.
- Site-fidelity literature: fidelity can structure colony use and can either stabilize or constrain redistribution.

The manuscript should say explicitly that its contribution is an **animal demographic instantiation** of these broader ideas.

## What is actually surprising

Not:

> “Penguins returned to familiar colonies.”

More interesting:

> **A breeding distribution can lose more than half of its observed abundance without losing a comparable amount of its recoverable spatial organization.**

That statement separates an observed demographic field from the latent individual-place relationships that can regenerate it.

The contrast with attrition is then:

> **persistent decline can destroy the carriers of that spatial organization rather than merely suppress their expression.**

And Beaufort adds:

> **capacity change can alter the map to which those affiliations and recruits are expressed.**

## Publication ceiling

### Current evidence

Strong enough for:
- Ecology full Article;
- Journal of Animal Ecology if behavioural-demographic framing dominates;
- Ecography if spatial process framing is sharpened;
- potentially Global Ecology and Biogeography only if the island/macroscale component is enlarged without post-hoc case accumulation.

### Not currently enough for

Nature / Nature Ecology & Evolution / Science Advances style claim that a general law of spatial memory has been discovered.

The missing evidence is not another descriptive penguin series. It is an **independent, source-classified temporary disturbance with pre-disturbance, trough, and rebound spatial data**, ideally plus individual fidelity information.

## Recommended manuscript claim

> **Observed breeding structure has both an expressed and a latent component. An acute disturbance can suppress the expressed distribution while leaving enough site affiliation for that distribution to reappear, whereas persistent attrition and changing capacity alter the demographic pool or the breeding landscape itself.**

Use “can” and “in the available cases.” Do not use “determines” in the title or abstract.

## Additional prior-art audit: hidden breeding states

A further literature audit confirms that the individual-level distinction between “alive but not expressed in the breeding census” and “absent/dead” is established prior art.

- Multi-event / robust-design capture–recapture models explicitly estimate breeding propensity with nonbreeders treated as unavailable or unobservable states (Souchay et al. 2014).
- Breeding-site fidelity and temporary emigration can both be high in the same population (Safine et al. 2020).

Therefore the manuscript must **not** claim novelty for latent breeding states, temporary emigration, or the existence of high fidelity despite intermittent breeding.

The narrower synthesis contribution is:

> **individual-level hidden breeding states can scale up to a recoverable population-level spatial allocation, so collapse of the observed breeding vector need not equal destruction of the spatial template that can regenerate it.**

Ross supplies the aggregate spatial-recovery observation; the mark–recapture literature supplies the biologically plausible hidden-state mechanism. These evidence layers remain distinct.


## Recovery-literature stress test

A further prior-art audit lowers another claim from novelty to background.

Community recovery studies show that abundance or function can recover before multivariate composition, and Wilson et al. (2023) explicitly describe metapopulation "hidden collapses" in which aggregate abundance recovers while local patches remain collapsed.

Therefore:

> **aggregate recovery does not imply structural recovery**

is not a novel claim of this manuscript.

The useful converse question is:

> **can severe aggregate breeding-census collapse conceal a spatial allocation that remains recoverable?**

Ross provides a quantified case: -54.3% breeding abundance followed by 96.97% aggregate restoration and near-inverse recovery of the six-node loss vector.

This converse framing should remain the focal novelty. The mismatch magnitude itself is ordinary within Ross and should not be inflated.
