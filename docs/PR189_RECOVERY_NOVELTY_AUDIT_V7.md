# PR189 novelty audit v7 — calibrated final ceiling

**Status:** strict positioning after Ross inverse-path effect sizing, Bird and Emperor prospective results, and literature audit of metapopulation recovery, spatial synchrony and aggregate/compositional variability.

## Broad concepts already established

Do not claim novelty for:

- aggregate versus local recovery mismatch;
- abundance versus composition recovery mismatch;
- spatially heterogeneous disturbance;
- spatial synchrony/asynchrony;
- aggregate versus compositional variability;
- path dependence or hysteresis;
- Allee effects;
- carrying-capacity heterogeneity;
- Hill/Simpson effective numbers.

The current paper is an empirical application/refinement inside those established literatures.

## What Ross contributes

Ross supplies a documented natural shock–rebound experiment in a fixed colonial breeding network.

1999 -> 2001 -> 2002:

- aggregate shock loss = 112,613 occupied breeding territories;
- immediate rebound gain = 109,198;
- 96.97% of aggregate loss regained;
- N2002 = 98.35% of N1999.

Yet local loss-restoration fractions range from 10.8% to 109.8%.

The loss and rebound vectors are strongly aligned:

    cosine = 0.99695.

So avoid language implying wholesale relocation.

The exact inverse-path allocation mismatch is:

    10,270.6 breeding pairs
    = 9.41% of rebound abundance.

The balancing positive excess is concentrated at Cape Crozier West.

This is the appropriate Ross effect size.

## What Bird contributes prospectively

Bird Island Gentoo:

    N +34.2%
    E +15.7%.

The endpoint interval is not a preidentified disturbance-recovery phase.

Its role is prospective sign-decoupling evidence.

Across 42 annual transitions, all four delta-N / delta-E sign combinations occur.

Continuous annual changes are moderately correlated:

    r(delta log N, delta log E) ≈ 0.48.

Therefore say:

- distinct;
- not sign-locked;
- not deterministically linked.

Do not say statistically independent.

## What Emperor contributes prospectively

Global 50-colony posterior-median endpoint:

    N -12.4%
    E -12.8%.

Local point-estimate signs:

    30 down
    20 up.

Eight predeclared regional sensitivities span all four N/E sign combinations.

Across those eight regions:

    r(log N ratio, log E ratio) ≈ 0.004.

This is descriptive and low-n; it is not an independence test.

The emperor result is point-estimate support because joint colony posterior draws are not publicly available for E uncertainty propagation.

## Closest theoretical neighbors

### Spatial recovery theory

Wilson et al. already shows how uneven disturbance, patch occupancy, local nonrecovery, rescue and spatial structure alter aggregate recovery.

Our niche is narrower:

> relative-abundance restoration among retained/observed breeding nodes when the aggregate nearly rebounds.

### Spatial synchrony

Synchrony theory already explains how shared forcing, dispersal and local demography shape correlated local trajectories.

Our niche:

> whether a real rebound reconstructs the pre-disturbance abundance vector.

### Aggregate/compositional variability

Metacommunity theory already separates aggregate and compositional variability.

Our niche:

> within-species spatial breeding nodes in a disturbance/rebound setting.

## Strongest defensible novelty statement

> **We quantify spatial restoration against an exact inverse-rebound reference after a natural disturbance, showing that Ross Island recovered nearly all aggregate breeding abundance while about one tenth of the rebound was spatially reallocated; prospective Gentoo and emperor-penguin tests then show that the sign of spatial change is not fixed by aggregate increase or decline.**

## Why this is not trivial despite the algebra

A skeptical reviewer can say:

> of course a sum does not determine a vector.

The answer must be empirical, not mathematical:

1. the Ross disturbance supplies a real reference vector;
2. aggregate recovery is nearly complete, making the inverse-path test stringent;
3. all monitored nodes remain in the network and rebound from the trough;
4. the mismatch is quantified, not merely asserted;
5. a known ecological mechanism—colony-specific iceberg/access geometry—predicts where mismatch should arise;
6. external prospective tests show the Ross spatial direction is not universal.

## Remaining ceiling

The paper still does **not** explain why particular nodes receive the contrast-mode rebound in a general way.

That requires prospective mechanism prediction from:
- access cost;
- usable capacity;
- local vital rates;
- movement;
- prior configuration.

Without that, **Ecology** is a realistic ceiling; a broader conceptual journal would likely demand prediction of the spatial allocation, not only documentation that it differs.
