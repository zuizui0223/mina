# Integrated v0.4 novelty matrix

This memo separates established results from the claims supported by the integrated manuscript. It is a positioning aid for the cover letter, editor query and reviewer response; it does not create new evidence.

| Question | Established before this study | What this manuscript adds | Claim ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Can colonial seabirds use conspecific reproductive performance when selecting breeding habitat? | Yes. Kittiwake observational work linked previous local reproductive success to breeder redistribution [@danchin1998], and an experiment showed that neighbouring performance can alter attendance, fidelity and recruitment [@boulinier2008]. | Nothing at the mechanism level. Public information is a motivated precedent, not the discovery. | Do not claim first evidence for public-information use. |
| Can Adélie colony breeding performance correlate with subsequent colony growth? | Yes. Méheust et al. reported a strong one-year-lagged breeding-success–growth association and interpreted it as coherent with public information [@meheust2024]. | The Palmer analysis separates the predictor-year adult count from the later demographic transition, controls shared island-transition growth, prior size and persistent colony identity, and estimates the full lag profile. | Lag 1 is not the novelty. |
| Does the Palmer signal persist after the mechanically coupled one-year transition? | Not established by the cited Adélie precedent. | Yes under the fixed-specification repaired analysis: the late-season colony-wide state remains informative at lag 2 and lag 3. | Fixed-specification validation after provenance repair; not fully outcome-blind preregistration. |
| Is the Palmer state a generic measure of reproductive success? | Previously unresolved. | No simple equivalence is supported. Independent monitored-nest mean chicks-to-crèche does not replicate the frozen primary association, and the two standardized states align only weakly in the common-panel diagnostic. | Call it a **late-season colony-wide state**, not generic reproductive success. |
| Is a delayed 4–5-year natal recruitment echo the dominant carrier? | Biologically plausible from delayed maturity but not identified here a priori. | The predeclared lag contrast is strongly opposite to that prediction. | Do not present natal recruitment echo as the main explanation. |
| Does physical breeding habitat matter at Palmer? | Yes. Snow, topography and geomorphology influence subcolony persistence [@cimino2025]. | The local state result adds temporal information beyond persistent colony identity and abundance. | Do not claim state replaces habitat or place is irrelevant. |
| Do Adélie pre-breeders move among Ross Island colonies? | Yes. A 25-year mark–recapture study documents age- and colony-dependent movement and recruitment, with pre-breeders moving more than established breeders [@dugger2026]. | No Ross behavioral result is part of the current manuscript. The published result motivates an individual-level future test. | Do not use prospective Ross analyses as current evidence. |
| Do static island descriptors yield a transferable demographic rule across Antarctic Pygoscelis populations? | Broad island theory motivates area × heterogeneity effects, but this specific Antarctic demographic-transfer hypothesis had not been confirmed. | The frozen 2-km test is directionally concordant across three species but non-confirmatory, and radius sensitivity fails the joint scale-dependence criterion. | Do not rescue with species subsets or raw radius sign reversal. |
| Does dynamic Palmer state “beat” static Antarctic architecture? | Not a coherent prior comparison because the responses, grains and species panels differ. | The manuscript identifies an **evidentiary asymmetry**: local state passes its own frozen information test; static architecture does not pass its own transferability test. | Do not compare effect sizes, variance explained or predictive superiority across Parts I and II. |
| What is the general contribution? | Individual elements—physical habitat, performance-based settlement, and metapopulation movement—are separately established in the literature. | The study separates **local explanatory structure**, **dynamic historical information**, and **transferable macroecological structure**, and shows that information can be valid at one hierarchical level without surviving transfer to another. | Generalize the information-transfer principle, not a specific unproven mechanism. |

## One-sentence novelty statement

> The study does not discover performance-based habitat selection; it shows that, during Adélie decline, a specific late-season colony-wide state carries short-lived demographic history beyond a mechanically coupled one-year association, that this information is not interchangeable with independent nest productivity, and that it does not collapse into a confirmed scale-invariant rule in static Antarctic island descriptors.

## Reviewer-facing distinction

A reviewer citing Danchin/Boulinier or Méheust is **not** identifying a fatal overlap. Those papers set the mechanism precedent and the one-year Adélie precedent. The manuscript should concede both immediately. Its contribution begins only after those precedents: denominator separation, temporal decay, independent metric non-replication, and cross-scale transferability.

## Claims deliberately left unresolved

- whether individual penguins use chick abundance, breeding success or another correlated social cue;
- whether retention, prospecting, immigration or latent environmental persistence carries the Palmer history signal;
- whether the moderate Antarctic-wide area × heterogeneity interaction suggested by the point estimates is real;
- whether the relevant spatial support differs biologically among species.

These unresolved points are part of the paper's inferential boundary, not missing positive results.
