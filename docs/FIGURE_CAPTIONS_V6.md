# Figure captions v6 — count-error boundary added

## Figure 1. Palmer breeding-patch network and heterogeneous assembly endpoints

Locations of the six frozen primary island sites (Torgersen, Litchfield, Humble, Christine, Cormorant and Dream) and the non-island Biscoe Point benchmark. Site membership was frozen from APBP/MAPPPD metadata before expanded abundance outcomes were opened [@checastaldo2023]. The map is a local coordinate view rather than a coastline reconstruction. The ecological design distinguishes true small-island breeding patches from Biscoe Point on Anvers Island. Broader abundance records show heterogeneous endpoints: local Adélie extinction at Litchfield, persistence at low abundance on several islands, and strong Adélie-to-gentoo replacement at Biscoe Point.

## Figure 2. Long-term coherence and annual synchrony across five Adélie breeding islands

**Left:** annual breeding-pair abundance on Christine, Cormorant, Humble, Litchfield and Torgersen islands from the complete 1991–2017 synchronized Palmer LTER census. The y-axis is symmetric-log scaled so the observed Litchfield zero state remains visible. Across standardized log-abundance trajectories, PC1 explains **96.4%** of variance. **Right:** Pearson correlations among annual log-growth series for all ten island pairs; median pairwise correlation is **0.373**. The contrast defines two temporal scales of the Palmer dynamics. Because ecological synchrony is known to depend on timescale, this panel is descriptive context rather than a novelty claim by itself.

## Figure 3. Observed nested variability is sensitive to high uncalibrated count error

**Left:** exact Wang–Loreau raw-abundance beta-variability transitions for the stable-roster colony-code sample [@wang2014]. Bars show excess beta variability above perfect synchrony \(\beta-1\) for aggregation from colony-code components to islands and from islands to the three-island aggregate. The primary 1991–2017 Cormorant/Humble/Litchfield analysis gives \(\beta_{within}=1.0737\), \(\beta_{among}=1.0112\) and total \(\beta=1.0858\); **86.4%** of the observed additive \(\log\beta\) transition occurs at the within-island step. The ordering is retained before Litchfield extinction and after removing Litchfield. **Right:** fully synchronous-subcolony observation-error null for the primary hierarchy contrast \(\log(\beta_{within}/\beta_{among})\). Points show null means and horizontal ticks the one-sided 97.5th percentiles across 100,000 simulations per inherited error model; the dashed line is the observed contrast (**0.05995**). The observed contrast exceeds the Poisson and Gamma–Poisson CV10% nulls (0/100,000 exceedances; plus-one \(p=0.000010\)) but is compatible with the uncalibrated CV20% sensitivity \(p=0.448\). The 20% sensitivity is not an estimate of Palmer observer error. Historical Palmer procedures used repeated independent colony counts with tighter agreement targets [@patterson2001], but replicate-count errors are unavailable in the public analysis table. The figure therefore presents an observed scale pattern with an explicit measurement-error boundary, not proof of biological spatial insurance.

## Figure 4. Prospective mechanism audit

Relative change in held-out prediction error for the finite mechanism family. Positive values indicate lower error after adding the focal predictor; negative values indicate worse transfer. Native scoring metrics are retained in the underlying figure-data table because the frozen analyses use both MSE and RMSE. Text labels report whether the fitted coefficient matches the predeclared direction and whether the complete decision rule was satisfied. Annual sea-ice duration provides a small error reduction but has the opposite coefficient direction; the fixed five-year sea-ice test and October snowfall × habitat test worsen transfer. Effective colony number shows a positive raw held-out error reduction, but its gain is **not** supported after synchronized year-block permutation (20,000 permutations; one-sided \(p=0.262\)). It is therefore plotted as a null-compatible predictive increment rather than a supported mechanism. The >50-pair group metrics illustrate why predictive improvement alone is insufficient: they improve error but reverse the historical positive-direction hypothesis.

## Figure 5. Conditional colony-organization association and external spatial triangulation

**Left:** partial-regression representation of the effective-colony-number association. Standardized log effective colony number and next-year log population growth are each residualized against island identity, current log abundance and secular year. The fitted slope is **+0.1168**; it is **+0.1185** after one lagged growth term and **+0.1126** after two, retaining **96.4%** of the original coefficient. Under the reused full-series structured null, the two-lag coefficient remains unusual (100,000 independent circular shifts: \(p=0.00102\); exact covariance-preserving shifts: **5/416**, \(p=0.0120\)). Its two-lag held-out MSE gain is only **+0.000444**, and the original gain is not unusual under synchronized year-block permutation (one-sided \(p=0.262\)), so the predictor is not treated as a robust out-of-year signal. Circular-shift Poisson/Gamma–Poisson coupling sensitivities also retain an observed-scale coefficient as rare (maximum \(p=0.0078\)). **Right:** annual effective colony number for the five islands. The title annotation summarizes independent Torgersen spatial reconstruction: 23 historic active subcolony footprints were reduced to five active footprints by 2022 [@cimino2025]. External mapping provides phenomenon-level spatial triangulation but not colony-ID-level validation because the public LTER `colony_code` values have not been crosswalked one-to-one to the mapped GIS polygons.
