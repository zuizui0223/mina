# Bird Island gentoo secondary temporal audit v1

**Status:** secondary audit explicitly permitted by the pre-effect contract after the primary endpoint result was recorded.

## Primary result remains unchanged

Frozen 1981 -> 2024 endpoints:

    N: 3,331 -> 4,470 (+34.2%)
    E: 3.569 -> 4.128 (+15.7%).

This is the prospective primary result: long-interval breeding abundance and effective breeding-unit number both increase.

Nothing in the temporal audit replaces that endpoint.

## The path is not monotonic

Across 43 complete six-unit seasons, E varies strongly.

Highest observed E:

    1993: 4.853.

Lowest observed E:

    2021: 3.251.

Thus the positive 1981->2024 endpoint change does not mean spatial redundancy increased progressively over the interval.

A descriptive post-endpoint regression gives:

    log(E) slope = -0.00250/year
    p = 0.0216.

This is not a frozen hypothesis test and is not used to overturn the primary endpoint result. It simply emphasizes that an endpoint comparison and a trajectory answer different questions.

## All four annual sign combinations occur

Across the 42 successive complete-season transitions:

| aggregate breeding abundance | E direction | transitions |
|---|---|---:|
| up | up | 12 |
| up | down | 9 |
| down | up | 8 |
| down | down | 13 |

So within one independently selected, consistently monitored colonial network:

> the sign of annual aggregate breeding-abundance change does not uniquely determine the sign of spatial-redundancy change.

This is a descriptive secondary result, not a separately preregistered frequency test.

## Dominant-unit turnover

The dominant monitored breeding unit changes repeatedly:

- 1981–2000: Johnson
- 2001–2002: Lower Natural Arch
- 2003–2004: Johnson
- 2005–2007: Lower Natural Arch
- 2008–2024: Johnson

The endpoint identity is Johnson -> Johnson, but the path contains temporary dominance turnover.

Therefore endpoint dominant identity also fails to reconstruct the within-interval spatial trajectory.

## Relation to Ross and Heard

### Ross

Ross shows a documented disturbance trough followed by a Crozier-weighted rebound.

Bird Island shows that long-term positive aggregate change can instead end with higher E.

### Heard

Heard shows monotonic aggregate growth with non-monotonic E and permanent North -> South dominance reversal.

Bird Island independently shows non-monotonic E and temporary dominance turnover in Gentoo penguins.

These systems converge on a general descriptive point:

> breeding recovery is a trajectory through composition space, not a scalar change in total abundance.

## Implication for the manuscript

Do not write:

> recovery concentrates.

Do not write:

> recovery spreads.

Write:

> **aggregate breeding-abundance change and spatial allocation are distinct dimensions; either increase or decline in abundance can be accompanied by either concentration or equalization, and dominance can change independently.**

The mechanism that selects among these trajectories remains unresolved.

## Boundary

- The primary Bird Island endpoint is prospective.
- The transition-quadrant counts are secondary descriptive summaries.
- No environmental breakpoint or mechanism is inferred.
- Incubating-nest counts remain breeding abundance, not total adult population size.
