# Ross inverse-path mismatch background-scale audit v1

**Status:** post-result contextual audit. This asks whether the focal 1999–2001–2002 inverse-path mismatch is unusually large relative to other observed Ross compositional reversals.

## Focal effect

For the documented mega-iceberg sequence:

    1999 -> 2001 -> 2002

aggregate shock loss:

    112,613

aggregate rebound:

    109,198

fraction of aggregate loss regained:

    96.97%.

Inverse-path allocation mismatch:

    9.41% of rebound.

Loss/rebound cosine:

    0.99695.

## Background annual compositional scale

Using all fixed six-component complete-year transitions in the Ross frozen count table, excluding the focal 1999->2001 and 2001->2002 transitions:

    n = 23 transitions.

Six-component total-variation distance between successive compositions:

    5th percentile   = 0.0058
    median           = 0.0318
    95th percentile  = 0.0603
    maximum          = 0.0821.

Focal transitions:

    1999 -> 2001 shock TV = 0.0509
    2001 -> 2002 rebound TV = 0.0975.

Thus:
- shock composition change is high but within the ordinary background range;
- rebound composition change is the largest observed successive-transition TV in the frozen series.

The pre-shock to immediate-rebound composition distance:

    TV(1999,2002) = 0.0497

is approximately the 87th percentile of the non-focal successive-transition background.

This is not a formal null because multi-year and one-year comparisons differ and observations are serially dependent.

## Comparable all-down -> all-up sequences

A closer descriptive comparison identifies sequences where:

1. every one of the six components decreases in the first interval;
2. every component increases in the next interval.

Three sequences occur.

### 1989 -> 1990 -> 1991

    loss = 35,834
    rebound = 12,466
    fraction of loss regained = 34.8%
    inverse-path mismatch = 9.12%
    loss/rebound cosine = 0.9941.

### 1999 -> 2001 -> 2002 — focal

    loss = 112,613
    rebound = 109,198
    fraction of loss regained = 97.0%
    inverse-path mismatch = 9.41%
    loss/rebound cosine = 0.9969.

### 2002 -> 2003 -> 2004

    loss = 78,374
    rebound = 95,679
    rebound / prior loss = 122.1%
    inverse-path mismatch = 10.63%
    loss/rebound cosine = 0.9839.

## Key calibration

The focal **9.41% mismatch is not unusually large** among the three available universal-down/universal-up sequences.

Therefore do not claim:

> the Ross iceberg rebound was exceptionally spatially non-reversing.

The focal natural experiment is distinctive for a different reason:

> **the aggregate rebound was nearly complete and externally attributable to a documented disturbance/rebound context, allowing a clean reference-state question.**

The mismatch itself is moderate and consistent with the scale of non-exact reversal seen in other Ross abundance reversals.

## What remains special about 2001->2002

The one-step rebound composition shift:

    TV = 0.0975

is the largest observed successive-transition TV in the frozen six-component record.

But the exact inverse-path mismatch after accounting for which components had been lost is only moderate.

These statements are compatible because the 2001 trough composition itself was unusually equalized.

## Consequence for the paper

Main text should emphasize:

    documented natural disturbance
    + 96.97% aggregate restoration
    + nonzero/moderate spatial mismatch.

Do not emphasize:

    unusually large mismatch.

The calibrated sentence is:

> The rebound restored almost all aggregate breeding abundance and largely retraced the preceding spatial loss, but did not do so exactly; approximately 9% of rebound allocation differed from the exact inverse path, a mismatch similar in magnitude to other complete local down/up reversals in the Ross record.

This substantially lowers any claim that the effect size itself is extraordinary.

## Claim boundary

Supported:

- focal aggregate restoration is near complete;
- exact spatial reversal fails;
- rebound successive-year composition shift is large relative to other adjacent transitions;
- focal inverse-path mismatch is moderate rather than exceptional.

Not supported:

- a unique or unusually strong inverse-path mismatch;
- a new universal magnitude of spatial recovery debt;
- statistical significance of the 9.41% mismatch.
