# Ross Island V2 result: recovery amplified spatial dominance

**Status:** effect opened only after the proportional-baseline correction and V2 contract were committed. The V2 recovery prediction failed.

## Confirmatory decision

Both abundance-direction gates pass.

### Decline, 1985–1999

- log-abundance slope: **-0.01673 per year**
- total N: **224,414 -> 207,411** (-7.6% endpoint)
- three-colony E: **1.6252 -> 1.6095** (-1.0%)
- six-component E: **2.2902 -> 2.0558** (-10.2%)

The frozen P1 direction passes, but the contraction is weak at the three-colony scale and much stronger at the six-component scale.

### Recovery, 2001–2012

- log-abundance slope: **+0.10041 per year**
- total N: **94,798 -> 351,119** (+270.4%; 3.70-fold)
- three-colony E: **1.7286 -> 1.5428** (-10.7%)
- six-component E: **2.2919 -> 1.9702** (-14.0%)

The frozen V2 P2 prediction required recovery deconcentration. It **fails in the opposite direction**.

This failure is not rescued by the earlier V1 story. V1 remains part of the audit trail, but V2 was the active pre-effect prediction when Ross counts were opened.

## The surprising result

Recovery concentration is not caused by colony loss.

From 2001 to 2012 all three biological colonies increased:

| colony | 2001 | 2012 | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cape Royds | 1,367 | 3,083 | +1,716 |
| Cape Bird | 26,317 | 75,696 | +49,379 |
| Cape Crozier | 67,114 | 272,340 | +205,226 |

The same is true at the raw census resolution: **6/6 components increased**.

Yet effective number fell at both scales.

Therefore numerical recovery produced **spatial concentration by differential amplification**: every node grew, but the dominant node grew faster.

## Proportional-growth benchmark

Holding 2001 three-colony composition fixed and scaling only total abundance to the 2012 total gives the expected endpoint counts.

Observed minus proportional expectation:

- Royds: **-1,980 pairs**
- Bird: **-21,779 pairs**
- Crozier: **+23,759 pairs**

Crozier absorbed substantially more growth than expected under proportional recovery.

At the six-component scale most of this over-allocation is concentrated in **Cape Crozier West**.

## Why this matters for the larger story

The program now contains two different routes to the same spatial state.

### Decline

Palmer/Signy showed concentration generated primarily by **differential attrition**:

    some breeding units decline faster
    -> units disappear / lose share
    -> distribution concentrates

### Recovery

Ross Island shows concentration generated by **differential amplification**:

    every breeding unit grows
    -> some units grow much faster
    -> distribution concentrates

So concentration itself does not diagnose population decline, refuge aggregation or site loss.

The same spatial statistic can be produced by opposite demographic arithmetic.

## Beaufort versus Ross

Beaufort remains important precisely because it differs.

At Beaufort, after usable nesting habitat expanded, the new within-island subcolony gained share relative to proportional growth while inter-island export declined.

At Ross Island, regional recovery instead amplified the dominance of Crozier.

The defensible new hypothesis is therefore not "recovery spreads" and not "recovery intensifies."

It is:

> **the spatial form of recovery depends on where residual breeding capacity and demographic advantage are located.**

Capacity release may permit deconcentration, as at Beaufort; heterogeneous growth opportunity may instead amplify already dominant breeding nodes, as on Ross Island.

This contrast is hypothesis-generating, not yet a general causal test.

## Hierarchical result

The scale correction mattered.

During decline, three-colony E changed little (-1.0%) while six-component E fell much more (-10.2%). This suggests much of the decline-side spatial contraction occurred **within the colony census hierarchy**, not simply among Royds, Bird and Crozier.

During recovery, both E3 and E6 fell strongly. Recovery concentration therefore operated at the colony scale as well as the finer component scale.

That phase-by-scale contrast is more informative than a single mixed-resolution E.

## Ecological interpretation

The strongest empirical statement from PR189 is now:

> **population recovery need not be spatial recovery. A metapopulation can more than triple while becoming more concentrated, even when every monitored breeding unit grows.**

For conservation, total abundance and spatial redundancy are therefore different state variables. A numerical rebound can coexist with greater dependence on one dominant breeding node.

For island ecology, the next question is no longer whether islands simply retain or export growth. It is what determines whether new individuals **spread among breeding nodes or amplify existing dominance**, and whether dynamic breeding capacity changes that allocation.

## Audit boundary

- V2 recovery prediction: failed.
- No period or unit was changed after effect opening.
- No colonisation is inferred.
- No individual movement is inferred from Ross aerial counts.
- The Beaufort movement mechanism remains independent external evidence.
- The old V1 pattern is not promoted back to confirmatory status after observing Ross.
