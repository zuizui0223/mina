# Ecological system: externally subsidized Antarctic breeding islands

## Why penguin islands are a special island-ecology system

The Palmer Archipelago penguin system should not be treated as a generic
terrestrial island community with penguins substituted for land birds.

Pygoscelis penguins are **central-place marine foragers** during breeding. The
main food resource is produced and consumed in the surrounding ocean, whereas
the island supplies a discrete breeding surface. This creates an unusual
separation between:

- **trophic environment** — marine prey fields, sea ice, foraging distance and
  spatial niche partitioning;
- **breeding environment** — snow-free ground, slope, elevation, wind exposure,
  drainage and access to landing beaches;
- **biotic interaction arena** — concentrated locally at nesting space and in
  overlapping marine foraging ranges rather than throughout a terrestrial food
  web.

The working phrase for mina is therefore **externally subsidized breeding
islands**.

This is a hypothesis-generating frame, not a claim that biotic interactions are
absent.

## What the literature already tells us

Three points matter for the study design.

### 1. Local breeding habitat can be strongly abiotic

Long-term work near Palmer Station showed that regional sea-ice conditions were
the primary large-scale correlate of Adélie breeding phenology, while
precipitation interacted with island geomorphology to create island-specific
snow accumulation and breeding delays.

Reference:
Cimino MA, Patterson-Fraser DL, Stammerjohn S, Fraser WR. 2019.
*Ecology and Evolution* 9:9334-9349.
doi:10.1002/ece3.5481

Recent landscape work likewise links topography, wind-driven snow distribution
and long-term Adélie colony persistence/extinction.

This makes "island" biologically meaningful as a physical breeding filter even
when neighboring islands share the same regional ocean.

### 2. Competition exists, but it need not be the dominant island-wide process

Classic mixed-rookery studies documented local nest-site competition, including
Adélie nest loss to arriving chinstrap males, and showed species differences in
nest-site use.

References:
Trivelpiece W, Volkman NJ. 1979. *The Auk* 96:675-681.
doi:10.1093/auk/96.4.675

Volkman NJ, Trivelpiece W. 1981. *Wilson Bulletin* 93:243-248.

But food competition is not automatically implied by sympatry. Near Palmer
Station, Adélie and gentoo penguins had substantial dietary overlap but
segregated core foraging areas, and a six-season study did not find evidence
that krill limitation or interspecific prey competition explained their
opposing population trajectories.

Reference:
Pickett EP et al. 2018. *Ecology and Evolution* 8:9764-9778.
doi:10.1002/ece3.4445

The correct mina hypothesis is therefore **biotic interaction is localized and
testable**, not absent.

### 3. Penguins are ecosystem engineers, not passive island occupants

Penguins transport marine-derived nitrogen, phosphorus and organic material to
land through guano, feathers, eggs and carcasses. Penguin-influenced Antarctic
soils can have very high nutrient concentrations and distinct microbial
communities, and marine-derived nitrogen can spread beyond the colony itself.

Representative references:

- Smith JL, Mulder CPH, Ellis JC. 2011. Seabirds as ecosystem engineers:
  nutrient inputs and physical disturbance. In *Seabird Islands: Ecology,
  Invasion, and Restoration*.
- Wang et al. 2020. Penguin-derived nitrogen transfer in Victoria Land.
  *Science of the Total Environment* 709:134496.
- Kim et al. 2018. Direct and indirect effects of penguin feces on Antarctic
  ornithogenic-soil microbiomes. *Frontiers in Microbiology* 9:552.

So the direction of causality can run both ways:

```
island physical filter
        ↓
penguin colonization / abundance
        ↓
marine nutrient import
        ↓
terrestrial soil / microbial / vegetation state
```

That feedback is an island-ecosystem process in its own right.

## mina's ecological model

The main functional-reassembly paper should use the following causal scaffold:

```
REGIONAL MARINE FORCING
sea ice ─ prey field ─ foraging accessibility
                │
                ▼
        species-specific performance
                │
                ▼
ISLAND BREEDING FILTER
snow × geomorphology × ice-free area × landing access
                │
                ▼
     colonization / persistence / abundance
                │
         ┌──────┴──────┐
         ▼             ▼
species turnover   local nest interaction
         │             │
         └──────┬──────┘
                ▼
 community functional phenotype
                │
                ▼
 marine-derived nutrient subsidy to land
```

## Testable hypotheses

### E1 — Abiotic-first breeding filter

If terrestrial trophic interactions are relatively weak at the whole-island
scale, changes in persistence and abundance should align more strongly with
snow/geomorphology and regional sea-ice forcing than with heterospecific
abundance.

This is a comparative hypothesis; it must be tested rather than assumed.

### E2 — Localized competition

Heterospecific abundance should matter most where species actually share
nesting space. A site-level heterospecific-density effect that disappears when
aggregated to whole islands would support localized rather than island-wide
competition.

### E3 — Marine niche partitioning

Coexistence need not imply strong competition for a single island resource.
Where telemetry or diet data are available, spatial/temporal marine niche
partitioning should be treated as an interaction modifier.

### E4 — Biovector feedback

If colony size changes strongly through time, terrestrial nutrient state should
also shift. This can become a later ecosystem-engineering extension using soil,
vegetation, moss or remote-sensing proxies.

### E5 — Externalized-resource island biogeography

For penguins, "island quality" should be modeled as:

```
ice-free breeding habitat
× nesting microclimate
× access to marine foraging habitat
× colony history
```

rather than only classical island area and geographic isolation.

## Consequence for the current long-term analysis

The first MAPPPD analysis remains intentionally simple: estimate species
turnover and the resulting community-weighted morphology.

The next mechanism stage should compare three predictor blocks:

1. **regional marine forcing** — sea-ice timing/duration;
2. **island physical filter** — snow, topography, ice-free nesting area;
3. **biotic crowding/interaction** — heterospecific nest abundance and total
   colony density.

The key result will not be "biotic interactions are unimportant." It will be a
variance/decomposition question:

> At what spatial scale do physical filtering, marine forcing and direct biotic
> interactions each explain penguin island reassembly?

That is the island-ecology contribution of mina.
