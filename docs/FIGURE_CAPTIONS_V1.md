# Figure captions v1

## Figure 1. Palmer breeding-patch network and heterogeneous assembly endpoints

Locations of the six frozen primary island sites (Torgersen, Litchfield, Humble, Christine, Cormorant and Dream) and the non-island Biscoe Point benchmark. Site membership was frozen from APBP/MAPPPD metadata before expanded abundance outcomes were opened [@checastaldo2023]. The map is a local coordinate view rather than a coastline reconstruction. The ecological design distinguishes true small-island breeding patches from Biscoe Point on Anvers Island. Broader abundance records show heterogeneous endpoints: local Adélie extinction at Litchfield, persistence at low abundance on several islands, and strong Adélie-to-gentoo replacement at Biscoe Point.

## Figure 2. A common long-term Adélie decline with only moderate annual synchrony

**Left:** annual breeding-pair abundance on Christine, Cormorant, Humble, Litchfield and Torgersen islands from the complete 1991–2017 synchronized Palmer LTER census. The y-axis is symmetric-log scaled so the observed Litchfield zero state remains visible. Across standardized log-abundance trajectories, PC1 explains **96.4%** of variance. **Right:** Pearson correlations among annual log-growth series for all ten island pairs; median pairwise correlation is **0.373**. The contrast separates a nearly common long-term direction from heterogeneous short-term demographic response.

## Figure 3. Prospective mechanism audit

Relative change in held-out prediction error for the finite mechanism family. Positive values indicate lower error after adding the focal predictor; negative values indicate worse transfer. Native scoring metrics are retained in the underlying figure-data table because the frozen analyses use both MSE and RMSE. Text labels report whether the fitted coefficient matches the predeclared direction and whether the complete decision rule was satisfied. Annual sea-ice duration provides a small error reduction but has the opposite coefficient direction; the fixed five-year sea-ice test and October snowfall × habitat test worsen transfer. Effective colony number is the only primary focal predictor that both improves held-out prediction and matches its directional prediction. The >50-pair group metrics illustrate why predictive improvement alone is insufficient: they improve error but reverse the historical positive-direction hypothesis.

## Figure 4. Within-island colony organization and external spatial convergence

**Left:** partial-regression representation of the frozen effective-colony-number test. Standardized log effective colony number and next-year log population growth are each residualized against the same baseline— island identity, current log abundance and secular year. The fitted slope is **+0.1168**, identical to the frozen multivariable coefficient. **Right:** annual effective colony number for the five islands. The title annotation summarizes independent Torgersen spatial reconstruction: 23 historic active subcolony footprints were reduced to five active footprints by 2022 [@cimino2025]. External mapping provides phenomenon-level spatial convergence but not colony-ID-level validation because the public LTER `colony_code` values have not been crosswalked one-to-one to the mapped GIS polygons.
