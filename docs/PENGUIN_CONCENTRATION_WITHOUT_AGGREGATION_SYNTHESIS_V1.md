# Concentration without aggregation — synthesis v1

**Status:** post-outcome process decomposition of the five already-supported Palmer/Signy concentration trajectories. This is not a new confirmatory result and does not alter Paper 1.

## Main result

The phrase "penguins concentrate into fewer breeding sites" can easily be misread as birds actively moving into refuges.

The component counts show that this is not required.

Across the 39 frozen breeding components in the five Palmer/Signy trajectories:

- **0/39 components increased in absolute count from the first to the last retained positive season**;
- **11/39 nevertheless increased their share of the population**.

Every final dominant component therefore became dominant while **losing**, not gaining, breeders.

Examples:

- Palmer Cormorant final dominant: **79 → 10 pairs**, while share rises **11.6% → 43.5%**.
- Palmer Humble final dominant: **289 → 40**, share **26.6% → 58.8%**.
- Palmer Litchfield final dominant: **109 → 4**, share **21.9% → 100%** before island-level extinction.
- Signy Adélie core A1+A60: **1112 → 845**, share **47.5% → 69.4%**.
- Signy chinstrap C80: **657 → 381**, share **40.0% → 65.6%**.

Thus increasing dominance does **not** imply absolute accumulation of breeders.

## Annual mass-balance decomposition

There are **47** calendar-consecutive transitions in which both total abundance and effective breeding-component number decrease.

Across those transitions:

```
gross losses = 7034 pairs
gross gains  =  153 pairs
gain/loss    = 0.0218
```

Only **14/47 = 29.8%** of joint contraction transitions contain any component with an absolute gain at all.

The weighted gain/loss ratio is low in every population:

- Palmer Cormorant: **4.2%**
- Palmer Humble: **2.2%**
- Palmer Litchfield: **6.4%**
- Signy Adélie: **0.05%**
- Signy chinstrap: **6.9%**

There are occasional years with substantial within-network counter-movement, especially in chinstrap, but these are not the dominant long-term process.

## Ecological interpretation

The replicated decline pattern is better described as:

> **concentration without aggregation.**

Breeding space contracts because some components lose breeders faster than others and reach zero first. The "refuge" is usually a **relative survivor**, not a sink that accumulates birds.

This materially changes the mechanism.

### What the data do not support

They do not require:

```
marginal patch -> mass emigration -> core patch growth
```

and therefore the current aggregate evidence should not be narrated as "everyone moves into the best colony."

### What is sufficient

A much simpler process is:

```
heterogeneous local demographic rates
        ↓
some components shrink faster
        ↓
small components hit zero
        ↓
remaining components gain population share
        ↓
E declines
```

Movement may occur, but is not needed to generate the observed concentration.

## Connection to the next-loss forecast

This matches the independent exploratory forecast result:

- current local size alone places an actual next-year loss at risk rank #1 in **14/15 = 93.3%** of event-years;
- recent local decline and whole-network integrity add little transferable predictive information.

Together, the data suggest a **moving-margin pruning process**:

> patches are progressively pushed into a low-abundance margin by slower upstream environmental/demographic processes; once small, they are the next to disappear.

## Connection to physical Torgersen sub-colonies

Cimino et al. (2025) independently show that physical extinction order is structured by patch characteristics:

- only **5/23** historic sub-colonies remained active in 2022;
- all **10/10 south-facing** historic sub-colonies were extinct;
- larger sub-colonies went extinct later (**R=0.75, p=0.0009**);
- similarly sized south-facing sub-colonies disappeared earlier than north-facing ones.

This suggests that snow/topography and physical capacity can determine **which local population is driven toward the low-abundance margin**, while current local size determines the immediate extinction order.

## The important decline–growth asymmetry

The growing regional MAPPPD networks show the opposite mass-balance process.

During decline:
> **differential attrition** creates concentration.

During growth:
> substantial positive gains occur inside already occupied sites, and in two regional panels most growth is absorbed by a minority of established sites.

Therefore the same spatial endpoint — lower effective breeding-site number — can arise from opposite demographic flows:

```
decline:     unequal losses -> pruning
growth:      unequal gains  -> intensification
```

This is a stronger candidate mechanism for weak spatial recovery than active retreat.

## Generated hypothesis: attrition–intensification asymmetry

> **Penguin breeding networks may dismantle through selective local attrition but rebuild initially through growth of surviving colonies rather than reversal of the extinction sequence.**

This can produce path dependence even without mass movement into refuges during decline.

If philopatry and founding costs make empty sites harder to recolonise than occupied sites are to grow, then:

```
decline -> patch loss -> spare capacity in survivors
recovery -> survivor growth -> delayed recolonisation
```

The decisive future test is therefore not whether breeders move into refuges during decline. It is whether independent recovering populations allocate new breeders preferentially to surviving occupied patches rather than to previously lost but physically suitable patches.
