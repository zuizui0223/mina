# Reviewer audit v0.1 — cross-scale breeding-space concentration

**Status:** pre-submission stress test of the active cross-scale manuscript. No new ecological outcomes are opened here.

## Bottom line

The manuscript is strongest when treated as a **three-step evidence hierarchy**:

1. Palmer discovers within-system concentration.
2. Signy prospectively replicates it across geography and species.
3. MAPPPD tests whether the **direction** persists when components are redefined one spatial level higher.

The third step is a scale-transfer test, not a third independent geographic replication and not a meta-analysis of the same effect statistic.

## Major issue 1 — Are local and regional analyses actually the same test?

**Potential reviewer criticism:** Palmer/Signy use the temporal slope of effective component number, whereas MAPPPD uses the log–log abundance–E elasticity κ. Calling this “replication across scale” could imply a common statistic that does not exist.

**Resolution:** Manuscript language now explicitly defines the shared target as **directional redistribution beyond fixed composition**. Local and regional p-values are not pooled and no common cross-scale κ is estimated.

**Allowed language:** “the same concentration direction persists across spatial levels”, “cross-scale directional recurrence”.

**Do not use:** “the same test replicated at both scales”, “common cross-scale effect size”.

## Major issue 2 — Is MAPPPD an independent geographic replication?

**Potential criticism:** The Central-west Antarctic Peninsula regional network includes Biscoe Point, which is in the broader Palmer-area APBP context.

**Resolution:** It is not described as independent geography. The primary Palmer concentration populations are Cormorant, Humble and Litchfield, whereas Biscoe Point is a separate non-island benchmark in the broader Palmer network. Signy supplies independent geographic replication. MAPPPD supplies the scale-transfer test.

## Major issue 3 — Are the four regional declining panels independent replicates?

**Potential criticism:** Two species are represented within each of the two declining regions, so 4/4 positive effects are not four independent geographic replications.

**Resolution:** The nominal one-sided 4/4 sign probability (0.0625) is explicitly descriptive. The manuscript does not use it as a confirmatory generality test.

## Major issue 4 — Multiple regional p-values

**Potential criticism:** Two of four regional panels have p≈0.015, but no family-wise multiplicity correction is reported.

**Resolution:** The contract froze panel-level support calls, not a family-wide rejection rule. The manuscript now explicitly says no multiplicity-adjusted regional generality test was prespecified. The paper-level inference is based on bounded directional transfer plus the evidence hierarchy, not on counting p<0.05 panels.

**Do not add a post-result multiplicity rule.**

## Major issue 5 — Sparse regional time series

**Potential criticism:** Some eligible regional networks contain only five complete seasons across ten or more calendar years, making κ imprecise.

**Resolution:** This is a genuine limitation. The threshold and deterministic pruning rule were frozen before regional outcomes and cannot be tightened after seeing effects. Report site and season counts for every panel and retain the conservative claim boundary.

**Do not rescue by changing the minimum complete-season threshold.**

## Major issue 6 — Deterministic pruning could select convenient sites

**Potential criticism:** Removing poorly observed sites can alter the network being analyzed.

**Resolution:** Roster selection uses observation coverage only, never count magnitude, κ, concentration or decline direction. The algorithm and tie-breaking rule were frozen before outcomes. Inference is explicitly to the retained repeatedly monitored network, not to complete regional occupancy.

## Major issue 7 — Observation methods differ across MAPPPD

**Potential criticism:** Ground, aerial and image counts are heterogeneous.

**Resolution:** The regional analysis reuses the previously frozen Paper 2 observation cohort and calibration rather than inventing a new method filter. Same-season repeated observations are method-corrected and precision-collapsed; the second regional null adds the frozen observation-error variance.

**Boundary:** this is an observation-error sensitivity, not a full detection model.

## Major issue 8 — Is κ≈0.25 a universal law?

**Potential criticism:** The local five-trajectory common κ≈0.25 may invite quarter-power rhetoric.

**Resolution:** The regional values do not cluster near 0.25. The active manuscript explicitly demotes the exponent and argues that **sign transfers more consistently than magnitude**.

**Do not use:** “quarter-power law”, “universal scaling exponent”.

## Major issue 9 — Does concentration mean the largest colony survives?

**Potential criticism:** Falling E could simply mean retreat into the historically dominant colony.

**Resolution:** The post-hoc route decomposition directly blocks this interpretation. All three Palmer initial dominants disappear; both Signy initial dominants persist and strengthen. The endpoint recurs despite opposite component-level routes.

## Major issue 10 — Does increasing abundance prove hysteresis?

**Potential criticism:** All three increasing MAPPPD networks finish with lower E, which may tempt an irreversible-ratchet claim.

**Resolution:** The manuscript labels these panels descriptive only. The local formal ratchet criterion failed and no regional hysteresis endpoint was frozen.

**Allowed language:** “qualitatively consistent with a slower spatial state”.

**Do not use:** “hysteresis demonstrated”, “irreversible spatial loss”.

## Major issue 11 — Are monitored components interchangeable spatial units?

**Potential criticism:** Colony codes, Signy units and MAPPPD sites differ in physical area and meaning.

**Resolution:** E is defined as effective **monitored component** number. The manuscript never equates E with occupied area or assumes equal-area components. Cross-scale generality is intentionally directional rather than metric.

## Major issue 12 — Mechanism remains unidentified

**Potential criticism:** Concentration may arise from habitat quality, fidelity, recruitment, movement, participation or survival.

**Resolution:** Correct. Aggregate counts cannot identify mechanism. Torgersen mapping and McDowall-style theory provide biological context, not causal attribution.

## What cannot be strengthened with the present data

The following would require genuinely new data and should not be pursued by further same-data analysis:

- seabird-wide or colonial-breeder-wide taxonomic generality;
- a common cross-scale κ;
- independent replication of regional-scale concentration outside the current APBP regions;
- causal mechanism from individual movement or demographic rates;
- formal regional hysteresis;
- a standardized occupied-area interpretation.

## Submission-level claim

> In Antarctic *Pygoscelis*, decline repeatedly redistributes breeding effort toward fewer effective monitored breeding components beyond proportional thinning; the same direction remains visible when the component definition is moved from within breeding systems to regional networks of breeding sites, while effect magnitude and local routes remain contingent.

## Stop rule

The reviewer audit does not authorize new thresholds, geographic clusters, alternate transformations, lags, nonlinear models, trait screens, p-value corrections selected after results, or mechanism searches. Any further increase in generality requires an independent data source.
