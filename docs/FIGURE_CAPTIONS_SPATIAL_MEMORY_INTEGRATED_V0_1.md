# Integrated spatial-memory manuscript — figure captions v0.1

## Figure 1. Expressed breeding abundance and latent spatial structure

Conceptual decomposition of observed breeding abundance among units. For unit (i) at time (t), observed breeding abundance is represented schematically as (n_{i,t}=\min\{K_{i,t},S_{i,t}q_{i,t}\}), where (S) is the latent site-affiliated demographic pool, (q) is breeding expression or participation, and (K) is available breeding capacity. A temporary breeding-state/access shock acts mainly through (q), persistent demographic attrition changes (S), and capacity change alters (K). The decomposition is an interpretation scaffold and is not fitted to the census data.

## Figure 2. Ross Island breeding rebound largely retraced the spatial footprint of loss

Observed 2001→2002 rebound across six Ross Island breeding components compared with the exact inverse-path rebound expected if the observed aggregate gain were allocated in proportion to each component's 1999→2001 loss. The aggregate rebound restored 96.97% of the shock loss; the loss and rebound vectors had cosine similarity 0.99695; 9.41% of rebound abundance would need to be reallocated to produce exact proportional reversal. The balancing positive residual was concentrated at Cape Crozier West. These are descriptive effect sizes, not a formal significance test.

## Figure 3. Persistent Adélie decline was accompanied by concentration among breeding units

Endpoint change in effective breeding-unit number for the three Palmer islands with stable colony-code rosters and for the independent Signy replication. Palmer changes were -19.1% on Cormorant, -50.6% on Humble, and -82.7% on Litchfield before local extinction; Signy declined 29.7% from 1998 to 2009. Bars show descriptive endpoint changes only. Statistical inference is based on the frozen system-specific proportional-thinning and count-error nulls rather than comparison of bar magnitudes across systems.

## Figure 4. Beaufort capacity release coincided with redistribution within the island

From 2004 to 2010, the small/new Beaufort breeding unit increased from 0.95% to 1.48% of island breeding abundance. Given its 2004 share, proportional allocation of island-wide growth predicted a gain of 157.8 pairs; the observed gain was 497 pairs, 3.15 times the proportional expectation. Independent band/resighting evidence from LaRue et al. (2013) showed that movement of Beaufort-born birds toward Ross Island colonies declined after 2005 as local nesting habitat became more available. The two evidence streams are interpreted as a case-level conjunction, not as individual-level mediation.

## Figure 5. Aggregate abundance direction does not determine spatial direction

Change in aggregate breeding abundance versus change in effective breeding-unit number for the prospectively frozen Bird Island Gentoo endpoint, the prospectively frozen global emperor-penguin endpoint, and the eight predeclared emperor ice-region sensitivities. All four directional quadrants occur. The figure is boundary evidence against a universal sign-locking rule; it is not a pooled cross-system effect estimate.
