# PR189 novelty audit v4 — after prospective Bird Island result

**Status:** superseded by `PR189_RECOVERY_NOVELTY_AUDIT_V5.md` after a stricter comparison with metapopulation and community-composition recovery literature.

## What changed

Before the Bird Island test, Ross could still be read as suggesting that numerical recovery tends to reconcentrate breeding abundance.

The Bird Island result prospectively rejects that generalization.

Frozen Bird Island six-unit endpoints:

    1981 -> 2024
    N +34.2%
    E +15.7%.

Therefore the paper's general contribution is not a directional recovery rule.

It is the separation of aggregate and compositional recovery.

## Not new

Do not claim novelty for:

- colony-specific demographic heterogeneity;
- unequal recovery;
- breeding-habitat limitation;
- dynamic carrying capacity;
- Allee effects or frozen-herd hysteresis;
- reciprocal Simpson / Hill effective-number metrics;
- aggregate recovery masking local extinction or reduced occupancy;
- path dependence as a general ecological concept.

## The empirical combination that matters

### Ross — natural experiment

A documented regional disturbance removes 112,613 occupied breeding territories.

The immediate rebound regains 109,198:

    96.97% of aggregate loss.

Yet local restoration fractions span:

    10.8% to 109.8%

across the six fixed components.

The aggregate is nearly restored while the local abundance vector is not.

### Bird Island — prospective independent test

A separately frozen six-unit Gentoo network shows:

    N +34.2%
    E +15.7%.

The largest positive proportional residual occurs in initially small Square Pond.

Thus positive aggregate recovery can move composition in the opposite direction from the Ross rebound.

### Bird temporal audit

Across 42 annual transitions, all four sign combinations of delta N and delta E occur.

This is especially useful because it demonstrates the decoupling **within one system**, without relying only on cross-system comparison.

## Mathematical interpretation

The distinction is exact:

    d log N/dt = r_bar

while:

    d log p_i/dt = r_i - r_bar.

The common mode controls aggregate abundance.

The relative/contrast modes control composition.

A shared forcing term cancels from the compositional equation.

This mathematics is not novel.

The novel use is empirical: Ross and Bird provide complementary, audit-disciplined demonstrations that these modes need not reverse together during breeding recovery.

## Relation to existing metapopulation recovery theory

Existing theory already shows that aggregate recovery can coexist with:

- local nonrecovery;
- lost patch occupancy;
- low production;
- rescue and dispersal effects.

The present work adds a different monitored dimension:

> **how abundance is allocated among still-observed breeding nodes.**

Ross is particularly clean because nearly all of the aggregate shock loss is recovered while most individual nodes remain below their pre-shock abundance and the dominant Crozier component overshoots.

Bird shows that the compositional mode can instead move toward greater evenness during positive aggregate change.

## Strongest defensible novelty

> **Near-complete aggregate breeding recovery does not imply spatial reversal, and even the direction of spatial recovery is not fixed by the direction of aggregate recovery.**

A more mechanistic formulation:

> **the common demographic mode that controls total abundance and the contrast modes that control spatial composition are empirically separable during disturbance and recovery.**

## What Heard and Beaufort now do

They are supporting geometry, not main pillars.

Heard:
- post-result;
- continuous local increases;
- non-monotonic E;
- dominance reversal.

Beaufort:
- corrected proportional baseline;
- small/new subcolony gains share;
- capacity-release/movement natural history.

They illustrate additional paths but are not needed for the core generality claim now that Bird Island supplies prospective external evidence.

## What remains unproven

- which environmental or demographic covariates predict the contrast mode;
- whether access geometry generally controls rebound allocation;
- whether configuration memory explains residual long-term lag;
- whether the result transfers beyond colonial seabirds/penguins;
- whether there is a universal timescale for spatial restoration.

## Claim ceiling

Safe:

> aggregate breeding recovery and spatial recovery are distinct.

Safe:

> Ross nearly restored total breeding abundance without restoring where the lost breeding abundance had been distributed.

Safe:

> Bird Island prospectively shows positive breeding recovery can increase spatial redundancy.

Safe:

> within Bird Island, annual aggregate and redundancy changes occupy all four sign combinations.

Not safe:

- recovery is generally concentrating;
- recovery is generally spreading;
- spatial redundancy is always the conservation-relevant recovery metric;
- configuration hysteresis caused Ross's immediate rebound asymmetry.
