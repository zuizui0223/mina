# SMP trend-symmetry protocol v1

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** fully prospective protocol before receipt of the SMP bulk extract and before any SMP concentration outcome.  
**Branch:** \`research/smp-trend-symmetry-v1\`

## Scientific question

> **Is breeding-space concentration specific to population decline, or can it persist during population increase?**

The hypothesis is generated by Antarctic penguin results but tested independently in the British/Irish Seabird Monitoring Programme.

The Antarctic evidence is not counted as SMP confirmation.

## Why the primary statistic changes

The earlier penguin regional analysis used:

\[
\log E_t=\alpha+\kappa\log N_t.
\]

This is useful within a monotonic decline, but κ changes sign mechanically when abundance trend reverses.

If:

\[
N\downarrow,\quad E\downarrow,
\]

then \(\kappa>0\).

If:

\[
N\uparrow,\quad E\downarrow,
\]

then \(\kappa<0\).

Therefore κ cannot answer the symmetry question.

The SMP primary statistic is instead the abundance-conditioned temporal concentration slope:

\[
\Delta\gamma_i
=
\gamma_i^{obs}
-
\mathrm{median}(\gamma_i^{null}),
\]

where:

\[
\gamma_i=\mathrm{slope}\{\log E_{it}\sim year\}.
\]

The fixed-composition null preserves the exact observed \(N_t\) trajectory.

Thus:

\[
\Delta\gamma_i<0
\]

has the same biological meaning in an increasing or declining population:

> breeding effort becomes concentrated through time more strongly than expected from the abundance trajectory alone under fixed spatial composition.

## Three-stage blind

### Stage A — structural support

Count magnitude is discarded before panel selection.

A candidate panel is species × MasterSite, with mutually exclusive SiteIDs as components.

The composite support rule uses the stricter intersection of the pre-existing SMP support contracts:

- >=3 retained SiteIDs;
- >=10 complete years;
- >=12 calendar-year span;
- stable component identity;
- one count unit;
- compatible methods;
- explicit zero distinguished from missing;
- no overlapping aggregate/child records.

Program gate:

- >=20 eligible panels;
- >=8 species;
- >=15 MasterSites;
- >=3 broad regions;
- >=4 species represented in >=2 eligible panels.

If this fails, stop.

### Stage B — total-trend balance only

Only after the Stage-A roster is frozen may counts be summed internally to annual panel totals.

For each panel:

\[
b_i=\mathrm{slope}\{\log(1+N_{it})\sim year\}.
\]

Only \(b_i\), trend label and support metadata are emitted.

No SiteID-level count magnitude, component share, E, κ, γ or Δγ is exposed.

Symmetry-identifiability gate:

- >=6 increasing panels;
- >=6 declining panels;
- >=3 species in each trend class;
- >=5 MasterSites in each trend class.

If this fails, the increase-versus-decline symmetry hypothesis is not tested.

### Stage C — concentration outcome

Only if A and B pass:

1. calculate \(E_t\);
2. estimate \(\gamma_i^{obs}\);
3. simulate 20,000 fixed-composition trajectories preserving exact \(N_t\);
4. calculate \(\Delta\gamma_i\);
5. fit the frozen macro model.

## Primary macro model

\[
\Delta\gamma_i
=
\alpha+\beta z(b_i)+u_{species}+\epsilon_i.
\]

Interpretation:

### \(\alpha<0\)

An overall tendency toward excess concentration through time.

### \(\beta>0\)

More negative population trends are associated with stronger excess concentration, supporting decline coupling.

### \(\alpha<0\) with weak/absent positive \(\beta\)

Concentration is not tightly determined by current abundance trend.

No equivalence-to-zero claim is made without an independent equivalence margin.

## Frozen secondary symmetry contrast

Estimate:

\[
\mu_- = E[\Delta\gamma\mid b<0]
\]

and

\[
\mu_+ = E[\Delta\gamma\mid b>0].
\]

Prespecified interpretations:

### Reversible tracking

\[
\mu_-<0,\qquad \mu_+>0.
\]

Spatial organization contracts during decline and re-expands during growth.

### Decline-specific contraction

\[
\mu_-<0
\]

with no supported negative concentration among increasing panels.

### Directionally persistent concentration

\[
\mu_-<0,\qquad \mu_+<0.
\]

Both trend classes show excess concentration.

This may be described as **ratchet-compatible** but not as hysteresis.

### Unresolved

Any other pattern.

## Why this is the decisive next test

Paper 1 has an asymmetry of evidence:

- Palmer/Signy provide strong replicated concentration during decline;
- MAPPPD increasing regional networks show that \(E\) can also fall while abundance increases;
- but the MAPPPD increasing panels were not covered by a prospective symmetry test.

SMP closes exactly that gap.

A result with negative \(\Delta\gamma\) in both increasing and declining panels would support a broader ecological idea:

> breeding-space concentration is a directional spatial process partly independent of current population trend.

A reversible result would instead restore the simpler abundance-driven interpretation.

## Claim boundaries

- MasterSites are monitoring networks, not assumed closed populations.
- E is effective monitored-component number, not occupied area.
- No missing SiteID-year is treated as zero.
- No universal exponent is tested.
- No trait analysis is opened in the primary study.
- No trend cutoff is selected after outcomes.
- No hysteresis is claimed from cross-system trend symmetry alone.

## Stop rule

No thresholds, MasterSite definitions, component rosters, trend cutoffs, lags, nonlinear trend classes, species exclusions or alternate concentration metrics are changed after Stage C is opened.
