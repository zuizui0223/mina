# Static place versus demographic state during penguin growth — synthesis v1

**Status:** post-outcome descriptive decomposition of the already-exposed three increasing MAPPPD networks. No new confirmatory claim and no change to Paper 1.

## The simple rich-get-richer hypothesis fails

The prior decomposition showed that most *absolute positive gains* occurred in the initially larger half of monitored sites, especially in Victoria Land Adélie and Central-west Antarctic Peninsula gentoo.

That does **not** translate into a general rule that initially large colonies gain regional share.

Using change in regional share as the response:

| Network | rho(delta share, initial share) | rho(delta share, 2-km ice-free area) | rho(delta share, nearest-site connectivity) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Adélie — Victoria Land | -0.064 | +0.064 | +0.069 |
| Gentoo — CW Antarctic Peninsula | -0.143 | +0.536 | -0.309 |
| Gentoo — South Shetland Islands | +0.100 | +0.800 | +0.205 |

No p-values are attached; n = 11, 7 and 5 sites.

The initial-share correlations are essentially zero in all three networks. Therefore a universal positive-frequency-dependence rule of the form **"the largest colony simply gets proportionally larger"** is not supported by these exposed panels.

## Static habitat amount is also not transferable

The 2-km mapped ice-free-area proxy aligns positively with share gain in the two gentoo networks, but is essentially unrelated in Victoria Land Adélie.

The final dominant monitored site was not the largest mapped-area site:

- Cape Crozier West: initial share rank 1, **ice-free-area rank 4/11**.
- Cuverville Island: initial share rank 1, **area rank 5/7**.
- Ardley Island: initial share rank 1, **area rank 3/5**.

This is consistent with the broader mina result that static island architecture does not yield one transferable demographic rule.

## The sharpest local contrast is Cape Crozier

In Victoria Land:

- Cape Crozier West (CRZW) gained **+0.1142** regional share;
- Cape Crozier East (CRZE) lost **-0.0355** share;
- they are only about **1.51 km** apart in the frozen site coordinates;
- CRZE actually has more mapped ice-free area within 2 km (**771 ha**) than CRZW (**614 ha**).

Thus the largest redistribution in the increasing Adélie network occurs between two immediately adjacent monitored components for which simple geographic isolation and coarse breeding-space amount point in nearly the same direction.

This is a strong descriptive reason not to reduce redistribution to island size or distance alone. Local habitat quality, breeding history, subcolony geometry, access, or density-dependent state must remain candidates.

## What survives mechanistically

The growing networks still show an **intensification phase**: population increase can be absorbed within an existing site network rather than being expressed as broader occupancy.

But the destination of that growth is not described by a universal "largest site wins" rule.

A better process hypothesis is therefore:

> **The expansion–intensification switch is set by site-specific residual capacity and local payoff, not by current colony size alone.**

In the game formulation, (K_i) and (Q_i) cannot be replaced by coarse island area. A site can be large but currently constrained, or geographically adjacent yet have a very different payoff from its neighbour.

## Connection to decline order

Published Torgersen evidence provides the complementary decline-side result: larger subcolonies tend to disappear later, but aspect changes the extinction schedule. South-facing subcolonies disappear earlier than similarly sized north-facing ones.

Together, these results suggest a two-level process:

1. **local environment and capacity determine which breeding components become marginal and when;**
2. **population growth/decline then redistributes breeders through the surviving network, with no universal refuge identity.**

This is a stronger mechanistic target than either a pure area rule or pure conspecific-attraction rule.
