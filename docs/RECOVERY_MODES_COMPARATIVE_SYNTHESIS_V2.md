# Recovery modes comparative synthesis v2

**Status:** updated after the prospective Bird Island Gentoo result.

## Core empirical conclusion

Breeding abundance has at least two separable recovery dimensions:

1. **aggregate mode** — change in total breeding abundance N;
2. **compositional mode** — change in how that abundance is distributed among breeding nodes.

The sign of the first does not determine the sign of the second.

## Evidence hierarchy

### Confirmatory/frozen focal result

**Ross Island Adélie**

The frozen 2001–2012 deconcentration prediction failed.

Post-result natural-history audit then identified the biologically sharper 1999–2001–2002 shock/rebound experiment.

Aggregate shock loss restored by 2002:

    96.97%.

But local loss-restoration fractions:

    10.8% to 109.8%.

Interpretation:

    near-complete aggregate restoration
    without spatial reversal.

### Prospective external result

**Bird Island Gentoo**

Frozen 1981→2024 six-unit endpoint:

    N +34.2%
    E +15.7%.

Interpretation:

    aggregate recovery with spatial spreading/equalization.

This is a prospective counterexample to universal recovery concentration.

### Preallowed secondary temporal evidence

**Bird Island Gentoo annual transitions**

    N up / E up = 12
    N up / E down = 9
    N down / E up = 8
    N down / E down = 13.

All four combinations occur.

Interpretation:

    aggregate and compositional directions vary independently through time.

### Discovery-side decline arithmetic

**Palmer/Signy**

Concentration during decline is generated primarily by unequal losses.

Interpretation:

    differential attrition.

### Post-result triangulation

**Heard Island King**

Monotonic local count increases, non-monotonic E, permanent dominance reversal.

Interpretation:

    compositional path can turn over even under continuous positive local change.

### Mechanistic contrast

**Beaufort Adélie**

Habitat release plus disproportionate growth of a small/new breeding unit, slight E increase and reduced inter-island export.

Interpretation:

    dynamic capacity can be associated with a spreading route.

## What is now ruled out

Not supported:

    decline -> concentration
    recovery -> spreading

as a universal inverse-path law.

Also not supported:

    recovery -> concentration.

The data contain both directions under positive aggregate change.

## Common/contrast decomposition

For local log-change rates r_i:

    aggregate mode = r_bar
    compositional modes = r_i - r_bar.

This is the cleanest unifying language.

Spatial composition is restored only if all local units share the same cumulative multiplier relative to the reference composition.

Therefore:

    restoring N
    does not imply
    restoring p.

## Conservation interpretation

A recovery assessment can give different answers depending on which property is monitored:

- total breeding abundance;
- occupancy;
- spatial allocation/evenness;
- dominant-node identity;
- similarity to a pre-disturbance composition.

None is a universal substitute for the others.

The paper should avoid declaring E itself the one correct recovery metric.

The result is that scalar abundance alone is insufficient to recover the spatial state.

## Strongest synthesis

> **Breeding recovery is multidimensional: the demographic component that restores total abundance and the relative local components that reorganize the breeding network need not reverse together.**

## Role of mechanism

Ross immediate asymmetry:

    disturbance/access geometry + demographic redistribution

is the most parsimonious known mechanism.

Configuration memory:

    secondary residual hypothesis for longer-term lag.

Bird:

    mechanism intentionally not inferred from count-only endpoint.

The next paper/analysis should prospectively predict the compositional mode, not collect more examples with a preferred direction.
