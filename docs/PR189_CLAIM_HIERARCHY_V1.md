# PR189 claim hierarchy v1

**Status:** live audit map after the Ross V2 effect opening, temporal/measurement audits, Heard triangulation, and generated hysteresis hypothesis.

The purpose of this document is to prevent evidence layers from being promoted after the fact.

## Level A — frozen confirmatory result

### A1. Ross V2 recovery-deconcentration prediction failed

Frozen before Ross breeding-pair magnitudes were opened:

- primary units: Cape Royds, Cape Bird, Cape Crozier;
- recovery interval: 2001-2012 with the fixed complete-year roster;
- eligibility: total breeding-pair abundance must increase;
- directional prediction: three-colony E should increase during recovery.

Observed frozen endpoints:

    breeding-pair N: 94,798 -> 351,119
    E3: 1.7286 -> 1.5428
    E6: 2.2919 -> 1.9702.

Therefore:

    numerical breeding recovery: PASS
    predicted spatial deconcentration: FAIL.

No later analysis changes this frozen decision.

### A2. Endpoint breeding-pair counts are higher at every frozen node

From 2001 to 2012:

- Royds +1,716;
- Bird +49,379;
- Crozier +205,226.

All six raw census components also have higher endpoint counts.

Thus lower endpoint E does not require an endpoint decline or disappearance of a monitored breeding node.

This is part of the frozen result because the component mass-balance decomposition was prespecified.

## Level B — prespecified/contract-aligned descriptive interpretation

### B1. Proportional allocation benchmark

Holding 2001 colony shares fixed and scaling only the total to 2012 gives residuals:

- Royds -1,980;
- Bird -21,779;
- Crozier +23,759 pairs.

Therefore the endpoint composition changed disproportionately toward Crozier.

Safe label:

    differential amplification of breeding abundance.

This label describes arithmetic; it does not identify a vital-rate mechanism.

### B2. Scale-specific result

Decline:
- E3 changes only weakly;
- E6 changes much more strongly.

Recovery frozen endpoints:
- both E3 and E6 are lower.

Therefore spatial reorganization depends on census hierarchy and must not be described with one mixed-resolution E.

## Level C — post-result robustness audits

These analyses improve interpretation but are not part of the frozen V2 test.

### C1. Temporal robustness

The 2001-2012 endpoint difference is not a smooth phase-long erosion.

Most redistribution occurs in 2001-2002:

    breeding-pair N +115%
    E3 -12.8%
    E6 -20.6%
    Crozier share 70.8% -> 79.0%.

Across the full frozen recovery years:

    log(E3) ~ year: descriptive p = 0.246
    log(E6) ~ year: descriptive p = 0.358.

A 2002-start sensitivity is broadly flat.

Safe wording:

> an early concentration pulse followed by persistence/fluctuation of a more Crozier-dominated breeding distribution.

Unsafe wording:

> spatial redundancy steadily eroded throughout recovery.

### C2. Measurement interpretation

The Ross series measures occupied nesting territories / breeding pairs near incubation, not total adult population size.

The 2001 low coincides with exceptional B15A/C16 iceberg conditions and documented breeding failure/abandonment.

Therefore:

Safe:
- breeding-pair abundance;
- breeding abundance;
- breeding-distribution recovery.

Unsafe:
- the adult population doubled in one year;
- recruitment alone caused the pulse;
- survival alone caused the pulse.

### C3. Matched-abundance branch comparison

Post-result pairs with nearly equal total breeding abundance show lower E on the recovery branch:

    1997 decline: N=204,837, E3=1.626
    2002 recovery: N=203,996, E3=1.507

    1985 decline: N=224,414, E3=1.625
    2004 recovery: N=221,301, E3=1.413.

This is a branch-dependence / hysteresis signature.

It is not proof of endogenous hysteresis because external environmental states differ.

## Level D — independent but post-result external triangulation

### D1. Heard Island king penguins

Spit Bay North/South:

    1963 13 / 5
    1965 36 / 9
    1969 49 / 37
    1980 82 / 400
    1988 215 / 3,100.

Both breeding counts increase at every observed interval.

Total breeding abundance rises monotonically, but E is non-monotonic and endpoint E is lower:

    1.670 -> 1.471 -> 1.962 -> 1.393 -> 1.138.

Dominance reverses North -> South.

Use:

> external species/island triangulation that numerical breeding recovery can have non-monotonic spatial redundancy and can reconcentrate around a new dominant node.

Do not call this a confirmatory replication because it was identified after the Ross result.

### D2. Beaufort Island contrast

Corrected proportional baseline:

- main colony +33.6%;
- new subcolony +108.0%;
- new unit receives ~3.15x its proportional expected gain;
- within-island E increases slightly.

Independent resighting evidence reports reduced Beaufort-to-Ross export after usable nesting habitat increased.

Use:

> contrasting capacity-release case consistent with spreading rather than concentration.

Do not use it to claim a universal capacity switch.

## Level E — prior literature used for mechanism, not validation

Existing work already establishes:

- colony-specific Ross vital rates;
- higher/stabler reproductive success at Crozier than Royds;
- configuration/edge effects on breeding success;
- Allee effects in colonial seabirds;
- Adélie within-colony frozen-herd hysteresis;
- density-dependent breeding-habitat limitation;
- sustained immigration in new-colony establishment.

These studies make mechanisms plausible.

They do not independently test PR189's frozen endpoint because they overlap systems, periods, or use different response variables.

## Level F — generated mechanism hypothesis

### Configuration-mediated recovery hysteresis

Generated after the Ross result:

> decline can leave breeding nodes in fragmented or socially weak configurations; because configuration changes slowly, later improvement in regional conditions may be expressed disproportionately at nodes retaining favorable configuration or receiving enough immigration/capacity release to escape the low-performance state.

This is a Discussion hypothesis.

It is not a result.

### Prospective predictions

The highest-value prospective tests are:

1. At matched current breeding abundance and external environment, prior configuration predicts future breeding success/recovery.
2. Compactness / low perimeter-to-area / low edge exposure predicts recovery beyond current abundance alone.
3. New capacity produces spreading only when immigration/recruitment is sufficient for establishment.
4. A prospective matched-abundance design shows branch dependence in a system not selected for known hysteresis.

## Level G — explicitly unsupported claims

Do not claim:

- recovery generally concentrates;
- every decline/recovery pair is hysteretic;
- island boundaries universally create demographic thresholds;
- Crozier amplification is explained by one vital rate;
- breeding capacity alone determines recovery allocation;
- E is geographic range or occupancy;
- the algebraic E identities are new mathematics;
- Ross 2001-2002 is a doubling of total adults;
- Heard is a preregistered replication;
- the failed East Antarctica S1 route supports or refutes the hypothesis.

## Manuscript claim ceiling

The strongest claim currently justified is:

> **Recovery of breeding abundance need not retrace spatial collapse. A recovering colonial breeding network can become or remain concentrated even when every monitored breeding count rises, and similar endpoint concentration can arise through unequal losses, amplification of an existing dominant node, or turnover to a new dominant node.**

The causal predictor of which path occurs remains open.
