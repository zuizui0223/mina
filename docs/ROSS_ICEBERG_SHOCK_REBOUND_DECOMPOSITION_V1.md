# Ross Island iceberg shock–rebound decomposition v1

**Status:** post-result decomposition. This does not alter the frozen Ross V2 periods or decisions. It corrects the biological interpretation of the 2001 recovery baseline.

## Why this audit is necessary

The frozen V2 recovery phase begins in 2001 because the phase boundary was set from external literature before Ross Island count magnitudes were opened.

After effect opening, the largest change in both abundance and spatial redundancy was found between 2001 and 2002.

Independent published natural history shows that 2001/02 was not an ordinary low-abundance point. The grounding of mega-icebergs B-15A and C-16 altered sea-ice conditions and access to open water, and many adult Adélie penguins failed or abandoned breeding attempts early in that season.

The aerial series measures adults occupying nesting territories / breeding-pair abundance near incubation. It is not a total-adult population census.

Therefore the 2001 -> 2002 increase cannot be interpreted as one-year adult population growth alone.

## Stable observation protocol

The source study reports that Ross Island colonies were photographed annually:

- from helicopters;
- at approximately 2,000–2,500 ft above ground;
- during the same late-November / early-December breeding window;
- using the same pre-2006 film/manual photographic-count framework in 1999, 2001 and 2002.

Thus there is no obvious observation-method discontinuity exactly at the 2001 -> 2002 transition.

The biological interpretation remains bounded by breeding participation / nesting-territory occupancy.

## Stage 1 — 1999 -> 2001 iceberg shock

Total breeding-territory abundance:

    207,411 -> 94,798
    -54.3%.

All six census components declined.

Yet spatial redundancy **increased**:

    E3: 1.6095 -> 1.7286
    +7.4%

    E6: 2.0558 -> 2.2919
    +11.5%.

Crozier share fell:

    75.4% -> 70.8%.

Bird share rose:

    22.8% -> 27.8%.

Local multiplication factors at the three-colony scale:

    Royds   0.378x
    Bird    0.556x
    Crozier 0.429x.

Thus the shared breeding shock was not proportional. The dominant Crozier colony lost more share than Bird, temporarily making the distribution more even.

This is **shock-induced equalization**.

It is not spatial recovery: total breeding abundance collapsed.

## Stage 2 — 2001 -> 2002 breeding rebound

Total breeding-territory abundance:

    94,798 -> 203,996
    +115.2%.

All three colonies and all six census components increased.

But redundancy sharply decreased:

    E3: 1.7286 -> 1.5074
    -12.8%

    E6: 2.2919 -> 1.8190
    -20.6%.

Three-colony multiplication factors:

    Royds   1.638x
    Bird    1.546x
    Crozier 2.400x.

Crozier share rose:

    70.8% -> 79.0%.

At the six-component scale, Crozier West showed the largest rebound:

    59,170 -> 147,224
    2.488x.

Against exact proportional rebound from the 2001 composition, 2002 residuals at the three-colony scale were:

    Royds    -703 pairs
    Bird   -15,954 pairs
    Crozier +16,656 pairs.

This is **asymmetric breeding rebound / re-concentration**.

Because the aerial endpoint includes breeding participation and territory occupancy, this arithmetic does not identify whether the rebound arose from survival, return to breeding, recruitment, immigration, or their combination.

## Stage 3 — 2002 -> 2012 longer recovery

Total breeding-territory abundance:

    203,996 -> 351,119
    +72.1%.

All six census components again increased.

Spatial redundancy moved in the opposite direction from the one-year rebound:

    E3: 1.5074 -> 1.5428
    +2.3%

    E6: 1.8190 -> 1.9702
    +8.3%.

Three-colony multiplication factors:

    Royds   1.377x
    Bird    1.861x
    Crozier 1.691x.

Bird gained share while Crozier lost a small amount of share.

Thus the post-2002 trajectory is better described as **partial spatial re-expansion / stabilization**, not continued concentration.

## Pre-shock to long-term endpoint — 1999 -> 2012

Total breeding-territory abundance:

    207,411 -> 351,119
    +69.3%.

But the spatial state did not return exactly to 1999:

    E3: 1.6095 -> 1.5428
    -4.1%

    E6: 2.0558 -> 1.9702
    -4.2%.

Colony changes:

    Royds    3,620 -> 3,083   (-14.8%)
    Bird    47,350 -> 75,696  (+59.9%)
    Crozier 156,441 -> 272,340 (+74.1%).

Thus total breeding abundance exceeded the pre-shock state, but the smallest colony had not regained its 1999 breeding count and Crozier held slightly more of the total.

This is a modest long-term **branch difference**, not evidence for a monotonic loss of redundancy.

## The correct Ross narrative

The strongest temporal description is:

    pre-shock composition
        ↓
    iceberg-driven breeding shock
        ↓
    temporary equalization
        ↓
    asymmetric breeding rebound concentrated at Crozier
        ↓
    partial spatial re-expansion / persistent branch difference.

Therefore the frozen V2 recovery-deconcentration prediction still fails over its specified 2001 -> 2012 endpoints, but the biological process is not "recovery steadily concentrated."

It is:

> **a shared disturbance temporarily flattened the breeding distribution, and the rebound restored breeding abundance asymmetrically rather than retracing the shock path in reverse.**

## Why this matters for hysteresis

At similar total breeding abundance, decline/pre-shock and rebound years can occupy different spatial states.

Examples already identified post-result:

- 1997: N = 204,837, E3 = 1.626
- 2002: N = 203,996, E3 = 1.507

and:

- 1985: N = 224,414, E3 = 1.625
- 2004: N = 221,301, E3 = 1.413.

This is a **branch-dependence signature**: E is not a single-valued function of N.

It is consistent with hysteresis, but does not prove endogenous hysteresis because environmental conditions and breeding participation differ among years.

## Claim boundary

Supported:
- 2001 was an externally documented disturbance year with widespread breeding failure/abandonment.
- observation protocol does not show an obvious 2001/2002 method break.
- the shock reduced all six breeding counts while increasing E.
- the 2001/2002 rebound increased all six breeding counts while sharply decreasing E.
- 2002/2012 growth was accompanied by modest E increase.
- 1999/2012 total breeding abundance increased while E remained modestly below its pre-shock value.

Not supported:
- a one-year doubling of total adult population;
- steady concentration throughout 2001–2012;
- proof that endogenous Allee feedback caused the rebound allocation;
- proof that the 1999 and 2012 environments were equivalent.
