# PR189 novelty audit v2 — after Heard Island triangulation

**Status:** post-result literature positioning.

## Prior knowledge that must not be claimed as new

1. **Breeding habitat/capacity can limit Adélie growth.**
   East Antarctic work already links breeding-habitat availability to density-dependent population growth and occupation of poorer terrain.

2. **Ross Island colonies have different vital rates and trajectories.**
   Long-term mark-recapture work already identifies colony-specific survival, recruitment, breeding propensity and movement differences.

3. **Colony geometry can affect reproductive success.**
   Crozier/Royds studies already propose spatial nesting-quality and edge-related feedbacks.

4. **Aggregate metapopulation recovery can mask spatial failure.**
   Existing recovery theory includes hidden collapse, reduced patch occupancy and reduced relative production despite aggregate recovery.

5. **Dynamic island carrying capacity is not a new concept.**
   General Dynamic Model traditions already treat island environmental capacity as time varying.

## What the frozen Ross result adds

From 2001 to 2012:

- total Ross Island abundance increased 3.70-fold;
- all 3/3 biological colonies increased;
- all 6/6 frozen census components increased;
- E3 decreased 10.7%;
- E6 decreased 14.0%.

Thus neither local disappearance nor an endpoint decline in breeding-pair counts is required for lower spatial redundancy during breeding-abundance recovery.

The endpoint pattern is generated by unequal positive change in breeding-pair counts:

    differential amplification.

## What the decline comparison adds

Palmer/Signy concentration is generated largely by unequal local losses:

    differential attrition.

Therefore the same direction of spatial concentration is compatible with opposite signs of local demographic change.

This blocks a common but unsafe interpretation:

    concentration != decline mechanism.

## What Heard adds

Heard Island king penguins provide independent post-result triangulation in another species and island.

At Spit Bay:

    1963 North/South = 13/5
    1965 = 36/9
    1969 = 49/37
    1980 = 82/400
    1988 = 215/3,100.

Both colonies increased at every observed interval.

Total abundance rose monotonically.

E did not:

    1.670 -> 1.471 -> 1.962 -> 1.393 -> 1.138.

The dominant colony changed from North to South.

This shows that recovery concentration can arise not only by amplification of the original dominant node, as at Ross, but by **dominance reversal**.

## Conceptual contribution

The strongest contribution is therefore broader than the Ross effect alone:

> population recovery has a spatial composition trajectory that is not determined by aggregate abundance or occupancy.

At least four quantities can diverge:

1. total abundance;
2. occupancy/persistence;
3. abundance-weighted spatial redundancy;
4. identity/turnover of dominant breeding nodes.

Existing hidden-collapse frameworks focus strongly on occupancy and local failure.

Ross/Heard identify a complementary failure mode:

> every monitored node can grow while redundancy declines, and the identity of the dominant node may either persist or reverse.

## Mathematical contribution boundary

For E = 1/sum(p_i^2),

    d log(E)/dt = 2(r_bar - r_D)

and over finite intervals,

    E1/E0 = (G_bar/G_D)^2.

These identities are algebraic and are not claimed as new mathematics.

Their contribution is interpretive: they identify the demographic allocation quantity needed to distinguish numerical from spatial recovery.

## Strong wording

Safe:

> Strong numerical recovery of breeding-pair counts can coexist with lower abundance-weighted spatial redundancy even when every monitored breeding count increases.

Safe:

> The same concentration state can be generated by differential attrition during decline or differential amplification during recovery.

Safe after Heard:

> Recovery concentration can preserve the identity of the dominant node or emerge after a subordinate node overtakes it, so endpoint abundance and evenness do not reconstruct the recovery path.

Too strong:

- "first demonstration that metapopulations can recover unevenly";
- "first evidence that capacity matters";
- "island boundaries universally regulate recovery";
- "recruitment explains Crozier";
- "all recovering colonial populations concentrate".

## Relation to Wilson et al. recovery regimes

Wilson et al. explicitly model heterogeneity in local productivity, carrying capacity, dispersal and disturbance and quantify aggregate recovery, patch occupancy, relative production and nonrecovery.

PR189 should be positioned as adding an **abundance-distribution axis among occupied patches**, not as replacing that framework.

The empirical question it exposes is:

> after occupancy has recovered or remained complete, how is recovered abundance distributed among the surviving nodes?

Ross and Heard show that this distribution can become more concentrated despite increases in every monitored local breeding count.

## Current novelty level

The result is strongest as a conceptual-empirical extension of spatial recovery ecology:

> **breeding-abundance recovery can raise every monitored local count yet spatially reorganize toward lower effective redundancy; this reorganization can occur through persistence or turnover of dominance.**

The next major advance would be a prospective multi-system test identifying predictors of these alternative recovery paths.
