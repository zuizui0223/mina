# African penguin decline spatial-redundancy test v1

**Status:** pre-effect contract. Frozen before inspecting colony-level occupied-nest magnitudes in the Dryad dataset.

## Independent system

Sherley et al. (2020/2021) African penguin (*Spheniscus demersus*) colony-count dataset.

Dryad DOI:

    10.5061/dryad.vx0k6djp7

Public description:

- occupied-nest counts;
- 18 South African colonies;
- 4 major Namibian colonies;
- 1979–2019;
- colony-level annual observations with explicit NA for years not counted;
- some known true-zero boundary conditions were encoded as 0.1 solely for the source state-space model and must not be treated as observed counts.

The associated publication independently identifies 1989 as the broad population peak and 2019 as the terminal historical low.

Those publication-level facts are known before this contract and are used to freeze the comparison window.

## Biological question

Does a large multi-colony population decline necessarily reduce abundance-weighted spatial redundancy, and if concentration occurs, is it generated by unequal losses among persistent colonies?

This is an independent species and biogeographic system from the Antarctic focal analyses.

## Frozen endpoints

Primary endpoint years:

    1989
    2019.

No alternative year may replace either endpoint based on count direction or E.

## Outcome-blind support audit

Before inspecting occupied-nest magnitudes, inspect only:

- column names;
- colony/site identifiers;
- country/region identifiers if present;
- whether each colony has a recorded value, NA, or source-model anchor value in 1989 and 2019;
- documentation distinguishing true zero, no survey, pre-colonisation and post-extinction;
- count-method metadata.

Do not summarize magnitudes, totals, shares or trends during the support audit.

## Fixed roster rule

A colony enters the **primary paired-count roster** only if:

1. it has an actual occupied-nest count in 1989;
2. it has an actual occupied-nest count in 2019;
3. neither endpoint is the artificial 0.1 model anchor;
4. neither endpoint is structurally missing.

Roster eligibility is based only on endpoint observation support.

No colony may be added/dropped after magnitudes are opened.

## Known-extinction sensitivity

The source documentation states that some NA values represent known extinction rather than unobserved status.

A separate sensitivity may include a zero endpoint **only** where the dataset documentation or associated paper explicitly establishes true absence/extinction at that exact endpoint.

Do not convert generic NA to zero.

This sensitivity is secondary and cannot replace the paired-count primary result.

## Eligibility gate

Let primary-roster total occupied nests be N1989 and N2019.

The decline-allocation test is eligible only if:

    N2019 < N1989.

If this fails on the mechanically fixed paired roster:

- record the decline gate as FAIL;
- do not search for a different year or colony subset.

The publication-level species decline does not override this roster-specific gate.

## Primary spatial endpoint

For t in {1989,2019}:

    N_t = sum_i n_it
    p_it = n_it / N_t
    E_t = 1 / sum_i p_it^2.

Primary effect:

    delta_logE = log(E2019/E1989).

Interpret continuously:

- delta_logE < 0: concentration;
- delta_logE > 0: equalization/spreading;
- no post-hoc meaningful-change threshold.

## Exact local-change decomposition

For each paired colony:

    G_i = n_i,2019 / n_i,1989.

Report:

- absolute change;
- multiplication factor;
- increase/decrease/no-change sign;
- proportional endpoint residual against fixed 1989 composition.

Also compute:

    G_bar = N2019/N1989
    H0 = sum p_i0^2
    w_i0 = p_i0^2/H0
    G_D = sqrt(sum_i w_i0 G_i^2)

and verify:

    E2019/E1989 = (G_bar/G_D)^2.

## Attrition classification

If N declines and E declines, classify the arithmetic:

### Universal attrition

    every paired colony has n2019 < n1989.

### Predominant attrition

    gross losses > gross gains

but at least one paired colony increases.

Report:

    gross_gain = sum max(n_i1-n_i0,0)
    gross_loss = sum max(n_i0-n_i1,0)
    gain_loss_ratio = gross_gain/gross_loss.

Do not infer movement among colonies from these counts.

## Compositional turnover

Report:

    TV = 0.5 * sum_i |p_i1-p_i0|

and dominant colony identity at both endpoints.

This distinguishes retained dominance from dominance turnover.

## Regional sensitivity

If country/region is explicitly encoded, repeat the same fixed-endpoint paired-roster analysis separately for:

- South Africa;
- Namibia.

This is secondary and descriptive.

Do not choose regions based on effect direction.

## Decision logic

### A — decline gate passes, E decreases

External support that decline can concentrate a colonial breeding network.

If losses dominate and few/no colonies increase, this supports the differential-attrition route.

### B — decline gate passes, E increases

External counterexample to any universal decline-concentration rule.

This would show that declining abundance can become more even.

### C — decline gate fails

No decline-allocation conclusion for the paired roster.

## Relation to Palmer/Signy

Palmer/Signy generated the hypothesis that concentration during decline can be produced by unequal local losses rather than active aggregation.

This African-penguin route prospectively tests that **spatial arithmetic** in another species and oceanographic/island context.

It does not test the Ross shock–rebound or colonial-hysteresis mechanism.

## Boundaries

- Response is occupied nest sites / breeding pairs, not total adult population.
- Raw counts vary in survey timing and method as documented by the source.
- Artificial 0.1 state-space anchors are never treated as biological counts.
- NA is never silently converted to zero.
- Colony establishment/extinction requires explicit documentation.
- No causal inference about prey, movement or management is made from E.
