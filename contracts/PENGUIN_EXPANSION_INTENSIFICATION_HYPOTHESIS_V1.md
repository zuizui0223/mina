# Expansion–intensification switching hypothesis v1

**Status:** generated hypothesis frozen before opening the East Antarctic occupancy outcomes.

## Biological question

When Adélie penguin populations grow, why does growth sometimes expand the breeding distribution and sometimes intensify already occupied breeding sites?

The primary subject is the penguin population. Island structure is the environment that changes the payoff of staying, switching, and founding/reoccupying breeding sites.

## Empirical motivation already known before this contract

1. Palmer/Signy declining systems show replicated non-proportional concentration of breeders into fewer effective breeding components.
2. In the frozen MAPPPD regional extension, all three increasing networks end with lower effective breeding-site number; Victoria Land Adélie and Central-west Antarctic Peninsula gentoo combine substantial abundance growth with substantial E decline.
3. Published East Antarctic Adélie work documents genuine breeding-distribution expansion in some regions during long-term population growth.
4. In the Windmill Islands, the first new breeding-patch colonisation in roughly half a century of growth was reported only as density-dependent limitation of breeding habitat/resources began to emerge.

These observations imply that growth can have at least two spatial modes:
- **intensification:** existing occupied colonies absorb growth;
- **expansion:** new or formerly empty breeding patches become occupied.

## Mechanistic game

For an occupied site i:

```
U_stay(i) = Q_i + S(n_i) - C(n_i / K_i) + Phi_i
```

where:
- Q_i = physical breeding-site quality;
- S(n_i) = benefit of conspecific presence/social information;
- C(n_i/K_i) = crowding or resource-limitation cost relative to local capacity;
- Phi_i = philopatry/familiarity advantage.

For an alternative empty or weakly occupied site j:

```
U_move(j) = Q_j + S_neighbor(j) - D_ij - U_j
```

where:
- S_neighbor(j) = information/attraction generated by nearby occupied sites;
- D_ij = effective travel/isolation cost;
- U_j = uncertainty/founding cost at a site lacking an established colony.

A switch or colonisation becomes likely when the best alternative payoff exceeds the occupied-site payoff.

## Core prediction

Population growth should not automatically increase occupancy.

Instead:

> **growth is first absorbed by already occupied breeding sites; spatial expansion occurs only when local crowding/resource limitation becomes strong enough to overcome philopatry, conspecific attraction, and the cost/uncertainty of colonising another site.**

This is the **expansion–intensification switch**.

## Island-ecology predictions to freeze before occupancy outcomes

### P1 — Connectivity
Conditional on survey support, an unoccupied suitable site should be more likely to become occupied when it is better connected to currently occupied breeding sites.

### P2 — Isolation × history
The effect of connectivity should be stronger for colonisation/recolonisation of spatially isolated sites than for persistence of already occupied sites.

### P3 — Saturation/overflow
Where a regional population is growing, colonisation should be associated with evidence that existing breeding habitat is relatively saturated or constrained, rather than occurring in simple proportion to regional abundance.

If the occupancy database lacks abundance/capacity information at the required years, P3 is not estimable and must remain literature-context only.

### P4 — Post-loss recovery
If true extinction→recolonisation events are sufficiently numerous, previously occupied sites should show history dependence: recolonisation probability should not be treated as equivalent to first colonisation of otherwise suitable habitat.

The direction of this history effect must be specified before the occupancy values are opened. No post-outcome direction selection is allowed.

## Distinction from classical expectations

This hypothesis does not claim that classical metapopulation theory predicts immediate colonisation after every increase. It tests a specific extension:

- classic patch-occupancy models emphasize disperser supply and geographic connectivity;
- colonial penguins add strong philopatry, conspecific attraction, local habitat capacity, and founding uncertainty;
- therefore colonisation can be a **thresholded behavioural outcome** rather than a passive proportional overflow process.

## Potential island-environment moderators

The switch threshold should vary with:
- amount of ice-free breeding habitat;
- island/outcrop size;
- fragmentation of usable breeding ground;
- distance to occupied sites;
- coastline/landing accessibility;
- local snow/topographic constraints;
- density of occupied neighbouring sites.

These variables are moderators of penguin redistribution, not the subject of the study.

## Falsifiers

The generated mechanism is weakened if:
1. colonisation occurs commonly at low regional/local density without evidence of existing-site constraint;
2. colonisation probability is unrelated to occupied-neighbour connectivity;
3. geographic isolation and local capacity add no information beyond abundance;
4. growth consistently produces immediate expansion rather than an intensification phase.

## Relation to recovery after decline

The same game generates a prospective recovery hypothesis:

> after a breeding site is lost, recovery may initially increase density in surviving occupied sites rather than immediately rebuild lost occupancy.

The Palmer/Signy exact-zero screen motivates this hypothesis (0 reoccupations within 2, 3, 5, or 10 years among eligible loss spells), but does not establish its cause. The East Antarctic occupancy route is intended to test site-level turnover only if the support audit shows sufficient repeated direct observations.
