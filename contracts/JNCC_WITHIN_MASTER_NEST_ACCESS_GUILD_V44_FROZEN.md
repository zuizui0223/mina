## Precise question (user-selected ONLY comparison 1)
In the **same original Country × Master site monitoring network**, are standardized source breeding-component composition changes stronger in taxa with nests accessible to terrestrial mammal predators than those that predominantly nest on high steep cliff ledges? Thus even a positive association tests a guild-by-place pattern, NOT predator causation. Comparison with Antarctic systems is deferred: no control-group claim.

## Boundaries

Even when species share the same administrative Master name, they can nest in different offshore islets, substrates and terrain, and their migration/prey/competition/census effort differ. A site/Master fixed effect cannot remove species-specific unobserved responses; paired contrast reduces some common geographic context only. Counterfactual: a shared predator may affect predator-accessible species differently, but similar effects could arise without mammals via differing marine prey, occupancy/detection, nesting habitat, life history and disease. Southern Ocean skua predation is a different guild, so Antarctica is not "predator-free" in general. No manuscript submission or closure of V36/V40/V42.
