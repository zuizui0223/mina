# Ross Island Adélie decline–recovery spatial asymmetry v2

**Status:** pre-effect amendment frozen after the Beaufort proportional-baseline audit and before inspection of Ross Island breeding-pair magnitudes. This supersedes V1 for the Ross Island effect test but does not erase V1.

## Why V2 exists

The original generated recovery story used the fraction of absolute population gain occurring in the large established Beaufort colony as evidence for "intensification before expansion." That comparison lacked the correct proportional-growth baseline. Because the new Beaufort subcolony began with only 0.95% of breeders, proportional growth would already place almost all absolute gains in the main colony. Relative to that baseline, the new subcolony grew disproportionately and Beaufort became slightly less concentrated.

This amendment changes the recovery prediction **before Ross Island count magnitudes are opened**.

## Independent system

Public Ross Sea Adélie aerial census, DOI `10.7931/kf06-x745`.

The census rows on Ross Island are hierarchical rather than exchangeable.

### Primary biological units: three colonies

1. Cape Royds
2. Cape Bird = South + Middle + North
3. Cape Crozier = West + East

Primary spatial inference is made across these three colony nodes.

### Secondary census resolution: six components

1. Cape Royds
2. Cape Bird South
3. Cape Bird Middle
4. Cape Bird North
5. Cape Crozier West
6. Cape Crozier East

The six-component analysis is explicitly secondary. It measures redistribution at a finer, mixed colony/subcolony census resolution and may not be interpreted as a three-colony result.

## Phase definition

Phase dates remain unchanged from V1 because they were fixed from external literature and structural support before outcome inspection.

### Decline phase

1985–1999

### Recovery phase

2001–2012, retaining structurally complete years only:

2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012

No year may be added or removed after counts are opened.

## Primary metrics

At the three-colony level, for each retained year:

    N_t = sum_i n_it
    p_it = n_it / N_t
    E3_t = 1 / sum_i p_it^2

At the six-component level, calculate the analogous `E6_t`.

For each phase separately, estimate the temporal directions:

    log(N_t)  = a_N + b_N * year
    log(E3_t) = a_E + b_E * year

The primary spatial endpoint is `b_E` plus the first-to-last change in `E3`.

The abundance–space slope

    log(E_t) = alpha + kappa * log(N_t)

is retained only as a secondary descriptive quantity because both N and E can trend with time. It is not a primary decision criterion.

## Deterministic proportional-growth reference

For each phase, freeze the first retained year's composition as the proportional reference:

    n_i,end(prop) = N_end * p_i,start

Report for each colony:

    proportional_residual_i = n_i,end(observed) - n_i,end(prop)

and report the corresponding change in shares and E.

This is a deterministic biological reference: if every colony changes by the same proportion, shares and E do not change. No Monte Carlo tail probability is attached because no empirical census-error model is available.

## Eligibility gates

A phase is biologically interpretable only if:

- decline phase: `b_N < 0`;
- recovery phase: `b_N > 0`.

If a gate fails, stop the corresponding biological interpretation. Do not change periods.

## Frozen predictions

### P1 — decline is spatial contraction by differential attrition

During decline:

- `b_E < 0`;
- end-of-phase `E3` is below start-of-phase `E3`;
- share changes should be explainable primarily by unequal absolute losses rather than gains into a refuge.

This is the Ross Island test of the Palmer/Signy differential-attrition result.

### P2 — recovery permits spatial deconcentration

The corrected Beaufort case predicts that numerical recovery can spread across breeding units rather than merely enlarge the largest unit.

During recovery:

- `b_E > 0`;
- end-of-phase `E3` is above start-of-phase `E3`;
- at least one initially smaller colony should have a positive proportional residual if deconcentration occurs.

Failure of P2 is a failure of this Ross Island prediction; it must not be rescued by a favorable kappa.

### P3 — decline and recovery use different demographic arithmetic

Report interval and annual gross gains and gross losses.

Expected process contrast:

- decline: spatial change is dominated by unequal losses;
- recovery: spatial change is generated by absolute gains, with any increase in E requiring gains that are disproportionate relative to starting composition.

This tests process asymmetry even if a state variable such as E later returns toward an earlier value.

### P4 — inference is scale-specific

The three-colony result is primary. The six-component result asks whether the same direction appears at finer census resolution.

- concordant E3 and E6 changes: multiscale consistency;
- discordant E3 and E6 changes: scale-dependent redistribution, not a failed dataset.

No six-component statistic may be described as "among-colony" concentration without the three-colony aggregation.

## Decision hierarchy

Strong independent Ross Island support for the revised decline–recovery asymmetry requires:

1. both abundance-direction gates pass;
2. P1: decline `b_E < 0` and E3 decreases;
3. P2: recovery `b_E > 0` and E3 increases;
4. mass balance shows losses dominate the decline transformation while gains generate the recovery transformation.

The six-component analysis and kappa are secondary diagnostics only.

## Relation to the island-boundary mechanism

Ross Island alone cannot test inter-island movement. It tests how abundance is redistributed **within one island** among three established colonies.

The independent Beaufort band/resighting evidence is the only current direct evidence in this program that changing breeding capacity can accompany altered movement across an island boundary.

The combined hypothesis is therefore hierarchical:

    decline -> unequal attrition and contraction
    recovery with available capacity -> within-island deconcentration can occur
    island capacity -> may alter whether redistribution remains within an island or crosses to another island

## Boundaries

- No colonisation claim is made from Ross Island census rows.
- No individual movement is inferred from aerial counts.
- Cape Bird and Cape Crozier subcomponents are nested within colonies; they are not independent colony-level nodes.
- The proportional reference is not a census-error model.
- Beaufort is an external hypothesis-generating/case-mechanism system for this Ross test, not part of the Ross Island primary endpoint.
- Paper 1 is unaffected regardless of outcome.
