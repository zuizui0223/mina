## Title of project / question

Spatial recovery after breeding-site abandonment in colonial seabirds

## Details of research

I am testing whether population recovery retraces spatial collapse in colonial seabirds. The focal prediction is that, for the same repeated breeding SiteID, later recolonization occurs at a higher surrounding population state than the state associated with earlier abandonment. The study was generated by independent Antarctic penguin analyses but SMP will provide the prospective multi-species test. Analysis is preregistered and staged: (1) freeze stable species × MasterSite / SiteID structure without using count magnitudes; (2) use only positive, explicit-zero and missing states to freeze completed occupied→zero→…→zero→occupied spells; (3) only if support gates pass, compare leave-one-SiteID-out surrounding abundance at abandonment and recolonization. The same SiteID is paired against itself, with separate taxonomic/geographic replication and a frozen non-circular temporal null. Outputs will be a peer-reviewed ecological paper and reproducible code.

## Details of data required

Please provide record-level SMP Colony Count / Whole Colony Count data for 1986–2024 for releasable non-sensitive species across Britain and Ireland, preferably CSV or Excel. Needed fields, where available: Species; Country/County; SiteID; Site; MasterSite and identifier; Plot/spatial-level indicator; StartGrid/EndGrid; site category/type/habitat; survey date/year; Method; Unit; Count; Accuracy; Estimate/type; Comments; verification status; and any field identifying nil returns, merged/aggregate records or boundary changes. Please also provide or describe any SiteID/MasterSite history or crosswalk for renames, merges, splits, retirements, replacements or boundary changes. Crucially, please confirm whether a direct Count=0 is a surveyed nil return, whether an absent SiteID × species × year row means not surveyed/missing rather than zero, and how estimated/imputed zeroes can be identified/excluded.
