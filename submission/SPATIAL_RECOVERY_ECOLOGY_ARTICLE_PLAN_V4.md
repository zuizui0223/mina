# Spatial recovery v0.7 — Ecology Article editorial plan v4

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Primary target:** *Ecology* — Article  
**Backup:** *Journal of Animal Ecology* — Research Article

## Title

**Population recovery can retrace local losses without restoring spatial structure**

## Focal result

Ross Island Adélie penguins, 1999→2001→2002:

- aggregate breeding-abundance loss: **54.3%**;
- aggregate loss restored: **96.97%**;
- local restoration range: **10.8–109.8%**;
- median local restoration: **51.8%**;
- Crozier West share of rebound: **80.6%**.

Path reversal was high at both natural spatial grains:

- six components: **90.59%** inverse-path fidelity;
- three colonies: **93.27%**.

State restoration was incomplete and explicitly scale dependent:

- six components: **2.4%** of baseline→trough TV displacement erased;
- three colonies: **28.3%** erased.

Under exact inverse recovery, endpoint composition would have been ~**0.07% TV** from baseline at either grain. Observed endpoint distances were **4.97%** and **3.54%**, respectively.

## One-sentence result

> **Near-complete aggregate recovery was produced by differential local recovery: abundance returned largely where it had been lost, but the previous spatial composition was not completely restored at either natural spatial grain.**

## General ecological problem

Aggregate population recovery is a weighted sum of local recoveries.

When local units differ strongly in size, recovery of a dominant unit can make the regional total look nearly restored while smaller units remain incompletely recovered and spatial dominance changes.

The manuscript therefore addresses a scale-transition problem relevant to:
- disturbance ecology;
- metapopulation recovery;
- spatial resilience;
- conservation monitoring.

## Prior-art boundary

Established:
- trajectory and state are different concepts;
- aggregate recovery can hide local or compositional loss;
- local demographic heterogeneity influences metapopulation recovery;
- spatial metrics depend on grain.

Not claimed as new.

Empirical contribution:
- one documented natural disturbance where aggregate recovery, path reversal, local recovery, and endpoint state can all be quantified;
- direct demonstration that high aggregate and path recovery coexisted with heterogeneous local restoration and incomplete state restoration;
- explicit robustness to biologically natural spatial aggregation.

## Supporting contrasts

### Palmer + Signy

Persistent decline departed from proportional thinning and concentrated breeding structure.

### Beaufort

Growth after nesting-capacity release departed from proportional expansion.

These cases illustrate different ecological sources of unequal local multipliers; they are not replications of Ross.

## Main limitations

- focal path/state analysis is post-result;
- one independent documented shock is focal;
- within-Ross episodes are not independent;
- state-restoration magnitude depends on spatial grain.

These are stated explicitly rather than treated as sensitivity footnotes.

## Desk-reject defense

The paper should not be sold as:
- a new recovery metric;
- a new resilience theory;
- a penguin natural-history anecdote.

It should be sold as:

> **a field natural experiment showing how a nearly recovered population total can be generated by strongly unequal local recovery, so recovery in the same places as loss need not restore the same spatial population.**
