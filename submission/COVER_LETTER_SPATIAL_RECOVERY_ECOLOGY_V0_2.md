# Cover letter draft — Ecology Article v0.2

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Population recovery can retrace local losses without restoring spatial structure,”** for publication as an Article in *Ecology*.

Recovery of a spatially structured population can be evaluated in several ways: by how much total abundance returns, by whether local gains occur where local losses occurred, or by whether the final spatial composition returns to its former state. These quantities are often treated as interchangeable in monitoring, but ecological theory does not require them to agree.

We use a documented mega-iceberg disturbance of Ross Island Adélie penguins to separate these recovery dimensions in a field population. Breeding abundance fell by 54.3% from 1999 to 2001. By the next census, 96.97% of that aggregate loss had returned. Local gains also strongly retraced preceding local losses: inverse-path fidelity was 90.59% and loss/rebound cosine similarity was 0.997.

Yet the spatial state did not return. Only 2.4% of the trough's compositional displacement from the 1999 state was erased, and the 2001→2002 compositional shift was the largest of 25 complete Ross transitions. Given the observed aggregate rebound, exact proportional inverse reversal would have produced a composition only 0.0717% total-variation distance from baseline; observed distance was 4.97%. The difference was generated by a structured residual, dominated by overshoot at Cape Crozier West.

We then place this natural experiment beside two biological contrasts. Persistent Adélie declines at Palmer and Signy departed from proportional thinning and concentrated breeders among fewer effective breeding units. At Beaufort Island, increased nesting capacity was accompanied by disproportionate growth of a small/new breeding unit relative to proportional expansion and by independent evidence of reduced export toward nearby Ross Island colonies.

The manuscript does not claim a new theory of resilience; trajectory-versus-state distinctions are established prior art. Its contribution is an empirical population-level demonstration with a simple ecological message: **a population can recover where it declined without recovering in the same proportions.** This distinction matters for any monitoring program that treats aggregate return or local rebound as evidence that spatial population structure has been restored.

All source datasets are public or publicly archived. The post-result status of the path-versus-state audit and the inferential boundaries of every supporting analysis are stated explicitly. No overlapping Palmer or Ross manuscript is being submitted while this integrated manuscript is active.

Thank you for your consideration.
