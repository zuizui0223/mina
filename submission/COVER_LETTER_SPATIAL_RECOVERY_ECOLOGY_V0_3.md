# Cover letter draft — Ecology Article v0.3

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Population recovery can retrace local losses without restoring spatial structure,”** for publication as an Article in *Ecology*.

Population recovery is commonly evaluated from an aggregate total, but spatially structured populations are sums of local components that need not recover equally. We use a documented natural disturbance to show how strongly those perspectives can diverge.

Giant icebergs were associated with a 54.3% decline in Ross Island Adélie penguin breeding abundance between 1999 and 2001. By the next census, 96.97% of that aggregate loss had returned. Viewed only at the population level, recovery was nearly complete.

Local recovery told a different story. Among six breeding components, only 10.8% to 109.8% of prior local losses were restored; the median component restored 51.8%. Cape Crozier West, the dominant component, supplied 80.6% of all rebound abundance. Consequently, local gains strongly retraced the geography of local losses (90.59% inverse-path fidelity), yet the relative breeding composition did not return. Only 2.4% of the disturbance-state compositional displacement from baseline was erased.

The exact counterfactual makes the discrepancy concrete. Given the observed aggregate rebound, proportional reversal of local losses would have produced an endpoint only 0.0717% total-variation distance from the pre-disturbance composition. The observed endpoint remained 4.97% away because the residual rebound was concentrated toward the dominant Crozier component.

We place this natural experiment beside two independent biological contrasts. Persistent Adélie declines at Palmer and Signy departed from proportional thinning and concentrated breeders among fewer effective units. At Beaufort Island, growth after increased nesting capacity was disproportionately expressed in a small/new breeding unit relative to proportional expansion, while independent band/resighting evidence showed declining export toward nearby Ross Island colonies.

We do not claim a new theory of resilience or a universal recovery law. The general ecological message is empirical and directly relevant to monitoring: **near-complete aggregate recovery can be a weighted outcome of strongly unequal local recovery, and recovery in the same places as loss does not guarantee restoration of spatial structure.**

All source data are public or publicly archived. The focal path-versus-state decomposition is explicitly identified as post-result, and no confirmatory p-value is attached to that reinterpretation. No overlapping Palmer or Ross manuscript is being submitted while this integrated manuscript is active.

Thank you for your consideration.
