## Broad ecological pitch

The manuscript should not be pitched as a penguin natural-history synthesis.

Primary ecological problem:

> **What does an observed population collapse actually remove from a spatially structured population?**

Established recovery literature warns that aggregate recovery can hide structural collapse.

This manuscript adds the converse empirical warning:

> **severe aggregate breeding-census collapse can overstate structural loss when a previous multi-node spatial allocation remains recoverable.**

The comparative cases then identify two contrasting routes by which spatial organization can genuinely change:

- persistent demographic attrition changes relative local contribution;
- changing breeding capacity changes the map of feasible redistribution.

## Why this is broader than the taxon

The logic applies to colonial or site-faithful populations whenever:

- annual census abundance is conditional on participation/detection/state;
- individuals retain associations with persistent spatial units;
- disturbance can alter participation separately from mortality;
- local capacity can change independently of regional forcing.

Potential analogues include:
- colonial seabirds;
- pinniped rookeries;
- bat maternity colonies;
- migratory breeding aggregations;
- other patch-faithful animals with temporary nonbreeding states.

Do not add those taxa as new data to the present manuscript.

## Editorial novelty hierarchy

### Established background

- abundance/function and composition/patch recovery can decouple;
- site fidelity can persist after disturbance;
- nonbreeding / temporary emigration can create unobservable living states;
- spatial recovery depends on dispersal, disturbance geometry and local dynamics.

### New focal observation

Ross:
- breeding census -54.3%;
- next census restored 96.97% of aggregate loss;
- six-node loss/rebound cosine 0.99695;
- inverse-path mismatch 9.41%.

### New integrated interpretation

> **Observed loss of breeding abundance and loss of recoverable spatial organization are different ecological quantities.**

### Comparative support

- Palmer + Signy: persistent decline departed from proportional thinning;
- Beaufort: growth after capacity release departed from proportional expansion;
- boundary cases: aggregate direction does not sign-lock spatial direction.

## Editorial risk

- make post-result classification and heterogeneous estimands explicit.
