# Spatial recovery path-versus-state — Supporting Information v1

## Figure S1. High inverse-path fidelity did not imply state restoration in the complete Ross down→up episodes

The three complete six-component episodes in which every breeding component declined and then increased. (A) Inverse-path fidelity was consistently high (89–91%). (B) The fraction of baseline→trough compositional displacement erased by the rebound was near zero or negative (+2.4%, −29.2%, −53.4%). The episodes are descriptive within-system calibration and are not independent replicates.

## Figure S2. Persistent attrition and capacity release provide contrasting routes to spatial reweighting

(A) Endpoint change in effective breeding-unit number for the three stable-roster Palmer islands and the independent Signy replication. Inference uses the original frozen proportional-thinning/count-error nulls; bars show descriptive endpoint change. (B) Gain of the small/new Beaufort breeding unit from 2004 to 2010 compared with the gain expected if island-wide growth were allocated in proportion to starting composition. Observed gain was 3.15 times the proportional expectation. Independent band/resighting evidence reported reduced Beaufort-to-Ross movement after local nesting habitat became more available.

**Source image:** \`figure4_process_contrasts.png\`

## Inferential boundary

Figure S1 is within-Ross descriptive calibration, not independent replication. Figure S2 contains biological process contrasts, not replications of the Ross disturbance/rebound natural experiment.
