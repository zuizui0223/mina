## Focal question

> **Can a spatially structured population recover where it declined without restoring its previous spatial state?**

## Focal answer

Yes, in the Ross natural experiment:

- amount recovery = 96.97%;
- path reversal = 90.59% inverse-path fidelity;
- state restoration = 2.4% of compositional displacement erased.

## Editorial pitch

The manuscript should be sold as an empirical disturbance-recovery paper, not a new metrics framework.

Core sentence:

> **The population recovered where it had declined, but not in the same proportions.**

The striking counterfactual is that exact inverse allocation at the observed aggregate rebound would have restored the composition almost completely (0.0717% TV from baseline), whereas the observed state remained 4.97% TV from baseline because residual rebound was concentrated at the dominant Crozier component.
