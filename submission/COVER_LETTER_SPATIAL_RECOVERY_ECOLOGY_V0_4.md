# Cover letter draft — Ecology Article v0.4

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Population recovery can retrace local losses without restoring spatial structure,”** for publication as an Article in *Ecology*.

Population recovery is commonly evaluated from an aggregate total, yet spatially structured populations are weighted sums of local components that need not recover equally. We use a documented natural disturbance to show how strongly those perspectives can diverge.

Giant icebergs were associated with a 54.3% decline in Ross Island Adélie penguin breeding abundance between 1999 and 2001. By the next census, 96.97% of that aggregate loss had returned. Viewed only at the population level, recovery was nearly complete.

Local recovery was much less uniform. Across six breeding components, only 10.8% to 109.8% of prior local losses were restored; the median component restored 51.8%. Cape Crozier West, the dominant component, supplied 80.6% of all rebound abundance. Consequently, local gains strongly retraced preceding local losses, yet the relative breeding composition did not fully return.

This conclusion is robust to biologically natural spatial aggregation. Inverse-path fidelity was 90.59% at six-component resolution and 93.27% after aggregation to Royds, Bird, and Crozier colonies. By contrast, only 2.4% of the baseline compositional displacement was erased at six-component resolution and 28.3% at three-colony resolution. Exact proportional reversal at the observed aggregate rebound would have placed composition only ~0.07% total-variation distance from baseline at either grain; observed endpoint distances were 4.97% and 3.54%.

We then place this natural experiment beside two independent biological contrasts. Persistent Adélie declines at Palmer and Signy departed from proportional thinning and concentrated breeders among fewer effective units. At Beaufort Island, growth after increased nesting capacity was disproportionately expressed in a small/new breeding unit relative to proportional expansion, while independent band/resighting evidence showed declining export toward nearby Ross Island colonies.

We do not claim a new theory of resilience or a universal recovery law. Our empirical message is directly relevant to spatial population monitoring: **near-complete aggregate recovery can be a weighted outcome of strongly unequal local recovery, and recovery in the same places as loss does not guarantee restoration of the same spatial state.**

All source data are public or publicly archived. The focal path-versus-state decomposition and spatial-grain audit are explicitly identified as post-result, and no confirmatory p-value is attached to that reinterpretation. No overlapping Palmer or Ross manuscript is being submitted while this integrated manuscript is active.

Thank you for your consideration.
