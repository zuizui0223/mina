# Figure captions — PR189 spatial reversibility Ecology v0.2

## Figure 1. Ross Island breeding abundance and spatial allocation through disturbance and rebound

Aggregate breeding-pair abundance and inverse-Simpson effective breeding-unit number across four anchor states in the fixed six-component Ross Island census: 1999 (last complete pre-shock state), 2001 (documented B-15A/C-16 disturbance trough), 2002 (immediate rebound), and 2012 (later state). The immediate rebound restored 96.97% of aggregate shock loss. Counts represent occupied breeding territories/breeding pairs rather than total adult abundance.

## Figure 2. The Ross rebound largely retraced the spatial pattern of loss

**A.** Observed 2001→2002 rebound for each fixed breeding component versus the exact inverse-path rebound obtained by allocating the observed rebound total in proportion to each component's 1999→2001 shock loss. Log axes retain visibility of the smaller breeding components; the dashed 1:1 line denotes exact proportional spatial reversal. **B.** Observed minus inverse-path rebound for the same six components. Normalized loss and rebound allocations overlapped by 90.6% (cosine = 0.997), 96.97% of aggregate loss was restored, and the half-L1 allocation mismatch was 9.41% of rebound abundance. The balancing positive excess occurred at Cape Crozier West. The mismatch is a descriptive allocation effect size, not an estimate of individual movement or a quantity with a formal sampling-error confidence interval.

## Figure 3. Aggregate breeding change does not fix the direction of spatial reallocation in prospective external tests

Log aggregate-abundance ratio versus log effective breeding-unit-number ratio. Bird Island Gentoo penguins are shown for the prospectively frozen 1981→2024 six-unit endpoint. The global emperor-penguin point and eight source-defined regional sensitivities are shown for the prospectively frozen 2009→2018 posterior-median endpoint. Quadrants indicate the four possible sign combinations of aggregate and spatial change. Bird Island showed positive aggregate change with increased effective unit number; the global emperor network declined and concentrated, while its prespecified regions occupied all four quadrants. These results support the statement that aggregate and spatial directions are not sign-locked; they do not establish statistical independence.
