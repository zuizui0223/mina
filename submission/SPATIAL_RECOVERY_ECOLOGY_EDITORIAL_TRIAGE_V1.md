# Ecology editorial triage — spatial recovery v0.6

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Manuscript:** \`docs/MANUSCRIPT_SPATIAL_RECOVERY_PATH_STATE_V0_6.md\`

## Would the editor understand the paper from title + abstract + Figure 1?

**Yes, after v0.6.**

The three headline numbers are immediately discordant:

- 97.0% aggregate recovery;
- 90.6% path reversal;
- 2.4% state restoration.

Figure 2 then explains the biological source of the discordance:
- local recovery 10.8–109.8%;
- median 51.8%;
- 80.6% of rebound supplied by the dominant component.

## Is the message broader than penguins?

**Yes, if written as a monitoring and spatial-population problem.**

Broad proposition:

> regional recovery is a weighted aggregate of local recoveries and can therefore conceal incomplete or asymmetric local recovery.

More specific contribution:

> high geographic reversal of local losses still need not restore relative spatial state.

Applicable to any persistent population network with unequal local unit sizes.

## Is the paper novelty-dependent on a new metric?

**No.**

- aggregate restoration is standard arithmetic;
- inverse-path mismatch is simple allocation bookkeeping;
- TV and effective-number summaries are standard;
- local restoration ratio is directly interpretable.

The paper should never advertise a framework or metric.

## Does one dominant colony invalidate the result?

**No; dominance is part of the mechanism by which aggregate recovery becomes misleading.**

Crozier West:
- 71.2% of focal loss;
- 80.6% of rebound;
- 109.8% local restoration.

Without Crozier West:
- five-component aggregate restoration = 65.3%.

The paper is therefore explicitly about scale transition from heterogeneous local recovery to an apparently recovered aggregate.

## Does the post-result status kill the paper?

**No, but it lowers the ceiling.**

Requirements:
- state clearly that path/state audit is post-result;
- avoid confirmatory language for focal TV;
- retain the failed original recovery-deconcentration prediction in provenance;
- do not add further favorable metrics.

The ecological observation remains exact and reproducible.

## Is Ecology the right first target?

**Yes.**

Strengths for Ecology:

JAE remains a reasonable backup but would require greater emphasis on demographic mechanisms and local-state transitions.
