# Spatial recovery v0.8 — Ecology Article editorial plan v5

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Primary target:** *Ecology* — Article  
**Backup:** *Journal of Animal Ecology* — Research Article

## Title

**Population recovery can retrace local losses while spatial structure remains altered**

## Editorial question

> **Can a population total recover almost completely because a dominant local component compensates for incomplete recovery elsewhere, even when rebound occurs in the same places as loss?**

## Focal answer

Yes, in the Ross natural experiment.

- aggregate restoration: **96.97%**;
- median local restoration: **51.8%**;
- local restoration range: **10.8–109.8%**;
- dominant Crozier West contribution to rebound: **80.6%**;
- inverse-path fidelity: **90.59%** at six components and **93.27%** at three colonies;
- state restoration: **2.4%** and **28.3%**, respectively.

## General ecological message

Aggregate recovery is a weighted sum of local recoveries.

When local units differ in size and recovery rate, a dominant unit can restore enough abundance to make the regional total appear recovered while smaller units remain incompletely recovered and relative spatial composition changes.

This is relevant to disturbance ecology, metapopulation recovery, spatial resilience, and conservation monitoring.

## Prior-art boundary

Established:
- trajectory and endpoint state are distinct;
- aggregate recovery can hide local or compositional loss;
- local demographic heterogeneity affects metapopulation recovery;
- spatial recovery metrics depend on grain.

Not claimed as new.

The empirical contribution is the natural experiment and the magnitude of the divergence among amount, path, local restoration, and endpoint state.

## Supporting contrasts

- Palmer + Signy: unequal attrition beyond proportional thinning.
- Beaufort: unequal expansion after capacity release.

These illustrate processes that reweight spatial structure; they are not Ross replications.

## Main limitations

- focal path/state analysis is post-result;
- one independent documented shock is focal;
- within-Ross episodes are not independent;
- state-restoration magnitude is grain dependent.

## Desk-reject defense

Do not sell:
- a new index;
- a new resilience theory;
- a penguin-specific anecdote.

Sell:

> **a field natural experiment showing that near-complete aggregate recovery can be generated by strongly unequal local recovery, so geographic reversal of loss does not guarantee complete restoration of spatial population structure.**
