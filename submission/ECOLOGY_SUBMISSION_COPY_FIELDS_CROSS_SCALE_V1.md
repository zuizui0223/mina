## Manuscript title

Breeding-space contraction recurs across spatial scales in Antarctic penguins

## Manuscript type

Report

## Abstract

Population decline can reduce site occupancy mechanically because small local counts increasingly reach zero. We asked whether colonial breeders show additional spatial reorganization after conditioning on that effect. We quantified effective breeding-component number, E = 1/sum(p_j^2), and compared observed change with fixed-composition nulls that preserve the observed abundance trajectory. In three Adélie penguin populations near Palmer Station, E declined by 19–83% beyond frozen count-error expectations. Prospectively frozen Signy Island tests independently supported the same endpoint in Adélie penguins (−37%) and chinstrap penguins (−51%). We then moved one spatial level upward using a frozen MAPPPD archive: among four estimable declining species × regional monitoring networks, all had positive observation-error-calibrated abundance–concentration effects, with individual support in South Shetland Adélie and chinstrap networks. Thus decline did not merely scale down a fixed breeding distribution. Breeding effort repeatedly became concentrated into fewer effective monitored components, and that direction persisted across spatial levels even though effect magnitude and the components that persisted were not universal.
