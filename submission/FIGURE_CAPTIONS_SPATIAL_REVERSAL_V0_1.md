# Figure captions — PR189 spatial-reversal Ecology v0.1

## Figure 1. Ross Island breeding abundance and spatial allocation through disturbance and rebound

Aggregate breeding-pair abundance and inverse-Simpson effective breeding-unit number across four anchor states in the fixed six-component Ross Island census: 1999 (last complete pre-shock state), 2001 (documented B-15A/C-16 disturbance trough), 2002 (immediate rebound), and 2012 (later recovery state). The immediate rebound restored 96.97% of aggregate shock loss, but effective breeding-unit number did not retrace the disturbance monotonically. Counts represent occupied breeding territories/breeding pairs rather than total adult abundance.

## Figure 2. The immediate Ross rebound largely retraced loss but was not an exact spatial inverse

Observed 2001→2002 rebound gains in the six fixed breeding components compared with the exact inverse-path reference obtained by allocating the observed rebound total in proportion to each component's 1999→2001 shock loss. Loss and rebound vectors were nearly collinear (cosine = 0.997), and 96.97% of aggregate loss was restored. Nevertheless, 9.41% of rebound abundance would have to be reallocated among components to reproduce exact proportional reversal; the balancing positive excess occurred at Cape Crozier West. The 9.41% value is a descriptive allocation effect size, not a movement estimate or a quantity with a formal sampling-error confidence interval.

## Figure 3. Aggregate breeding change does not fix the direction of spatial reallocation in prospective external tests

Log aggregate-abundance ratio versus log effective-unit-number ratio. Bird Island Gentoo penguins are shown for the prospectively frozen 1981→2024 six-unit endpoint. The global emperor-penguin point and eight source-defined regional sensitivities are shown for the prospectively frozen 2009→2018 posterior-median endpoint. Quadrants indicate the four possible sign combinations of aggregate and spatial change. Bird Island showed positive aggregate change with increased effective unit number; the global emperor network declined and concentrated, while its prespecified regions occupied all four quadrants. These results support sign decoupling, not statistical independence.
