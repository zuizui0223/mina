# Cover letter — Ecology Report spatial-reversal paper v0.1

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Near-complete breeding rebound is not exact spatial reversal,”** for publication as a **Report** in *Ecology*.

Population recovery is usually summarized by whether abundance returns. Our manuscript asks a narrower spatial question: after a shared disturbance, does breeding abundance return to the same places from which it was lost?

Ross Island provides a documented natural experiment. A mega-iceberg disturbance reduced breeding abundance across six monitored Adélie penguin breeding components from 207,411 pairs in 1999 to 94,798 in 2001. By 2002, 96.97% of the aggregate loss had returned. The rebound largely retraced the preceding loss spatially (cosine = 0.997), but not exactly: 9.41% of rebound abundance would have to be reallocated among breeding components to reproduce an exact inverse path, with the balancing excess concentrated at Cape Crozier West. We calibrate this result conservatively—the mismatch is moderate, not exceptional relative to other Ross down–up episodes, and the historical source does not permit a formal sampling-error interval around the 9.41% effect size.

The broader contribution is tested prospectively rather than inferred from Ross alone. In a separately frozen six-unit Gentoo penguin comparison at Bird Island, aggregate breeding abundance increased 34.2% while effective breeding-unit number increased 15.7%. In a prospectively frozen 50-colony emperor penguin comparison, aggregate abundance declined 12.4% while effective colony number declined 12.8%, but prespecified regions occupied all four combinations of aggregate and spatial directional change. Together these results show that aggregate breeding change and spatial reallocation are not deterministically sign-locked.

We also report clear inferential boundaries. The focal 1999–2002 Ross decomposition was developed after an earlier frozen recovery prediction failed, and the manuscript labels it explicitly as a post-result refinement. We attempted prospectively specified mechanism tests using reproductive output and mapped subcolony configuration; neither supplied a confirmed general predictor. We therefore present the paper as a state/allocation study rather than manufacturing a mechanism claim.

We believe the Report format is appropriate because the manuscript makes one concise ecological statement: **near-complete numerical rebound can leave a moderate, structured failure of exact spatial reversal among persistent breeding nodes.** The result complements established metapopulation-recovery and spatial-synchrony theory by quantifying restoration of the within-species local abundance vector after a real disturbance and by testing the aggregate/spatial relationship prospectively in additional penguin systems.
