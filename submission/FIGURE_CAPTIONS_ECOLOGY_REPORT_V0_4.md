# Figure captions — Ecology Report v0.4

## Figure 1. Declining penguin populations lose effective monitored breeding components

Annual breeding-pair abundance (solid line) and effective breeding-component number, N_eff = 1 / Σ p_j² (dashed line), each indexed to 100 in the first eligible season. Palmer panels show the three Adélie discovery populations with unchanged published colony-code rosters; Litchfield stops at its final positive census in 2006. Signy panels show the prospectively frozen 22-season Adélie five-unit panel and chinstrap nine-unit panel. The figure is descriptive; inferential support comes from the fixed-composition count-error nulls summarized in Figure 2.

## Figure 2. Breeding-space contraction recurs across five population units

**Left:** first-to-last change in effective monitored breeding-component number for Cormorant, Humble and Litchfield Adélie penguins near Palmer Station, and the prospectively frozen Adélie and chinstrap panels at Signy Island. Effective component number declined by 19–83% in every population. **Right:** evidence against the most severe frozen fixed-composition error model (Gamma–Poisson with 20% multiplicative CV), shown as −log10 of the one-sided Monte Carlo probability. The dashed line marks p = 0.05. Labels give the observed N_eff slope per year. Each null preserves the observed annual breeding-pair total while holding latent relative breeding composition constant, so rejection indicates directional redistribution beyond proportional thinning plus the specified independent count error.
