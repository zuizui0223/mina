# Integrated research decision V3 — gross gain and loss (2026-11-25)
## Candidate paper
**Breeding-space reorganization beyond population decline in colonial seabirds: opposing local changes and differential attrition.**
Question: does aggregate growth, stability or decline describe the fate of its local breeding components? Composition can change through both mixed absolute gains and losses, or unidirectional heterogeneous change. The accounting decomposition is exact; the causes are not identified.

## Paper architecture and stop rule
1. Conceptual fixed-roster framework: N, U, D2, G, L and C. Existing mathematical indices and accounting identities are not claimed inventions.
2. Full JNCC all-direction analysis, strict survey-year sensitivity and conditional model simulations. Figure: growth despite local losses and nonzero counterflow.
3. Distinguish mixed versus nonmixed concentration, local data showing different modes; matched-lag accumulation in six known local panels, not representative replication.
4. Dominant-site turnover and Ross recovery mismatch as complementary composition outcomes, not proof of a single mechanism.
5. Conservation implication: aggregate recovery or growth is insufficient to establish recovery of each monitored breeding component. Physical sea ice, salinity, polynya and diet research provides context only until same-grain linkage and adequate independent environmental support. No vegetation, predator or migration mechanism asserted. 
Original source reuse is legitimate comparative research; this is not a meta-analysis of published effect sizes and is not independent of earlier JNCC results. Novelty of the exact accounting framework in colonial seabirds still requires targeted prior-art verification; no claim that compensation, beta diversity, metapopulations, or gain–loss decomposition are new concepts. Manuscript not submitted, frozen main and prior papers remain unchanged. Avoid new endpoints and sources until the scope of the single manuscript and prior-art overlap are settled.
