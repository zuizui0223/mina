# 統合研究の現在地 — 2026-11-25
## New ecological comparison
Question: Is the spatial reorganization of already monitored breeding units uniquely associated with population decline, or does it also occur during stable and increasing population size?
Annual Ross (23) and selected Antarctic MAPPPD (39) exact matched transitions: 62/62 count-corrected squared share changes D2>0, but no actual survey noise calibration, overlapping source, non-independent transitions. Annual median deltaU positive during decline, negative in stability/growth. JNCC independent data system (same JNCC source as prior V30/V46, new endpoint and all-direction comparison, not fresh external replication of the prior JNCC result) original 1321 panels, 604 Country/Master networks, 17 species, common 10 taxa across all direction strata. Fixed groups 912/99/310. Common-species equal mean deltaU +0.08845/+0.06069/+0.08349; decline-growth difference +0.00497 [−0.02992,+0.03752]. Common-species equal mean D2 +0.17405/+0.08883/+0.16029. All exceed the 5–95% model simulation range under both pooled multinomial and one explicitly uncalibrated gamma CV20 fixed-total noise sensitivity. This is conditional on matched original sites and census totals, not corrected census detectability. Network bootstrap valid 1940/1999; 10 species fixed common support. Two-census comparisons do not measure annually synchronized growth rates. Thus **reject the simple claim that concentration is unique to decline** on these observed JNCC panels, do not claim decline causes concentration or growth is equally concentrated by equivalence test.
Important correction to pilot H4: only two source reporting regions provide eligible matched annual panels (not an intrinsic upper bound on finer geographical clusters); the conservative region-level independence gate of eight fails and no environmental response coefficient was fitted. Antarctic and Ross data may overlap. No global transfer claim.
## Prior-art boundary (targeted searches, not systematic review)
Pozzi et al. 2015, PLOS ONE DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0119002: Magellanic penguin colonies vary in growth with size, geography and likely dispersal. Brown et al. 2013, Ecology DOI 10.1890/12-2001.1: colony-size spatial and temporal unpredictability in cliff swallows already considered. Paleczny et al. 2015, PLOS ONE DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0129342: global monitored seabird population trend. JNCC official fourth census https://jncc.defra.gov.uk/our-work/seabirds-count/ tracks size and distribution. Existing abundance-occupancy and density-dependent range models preclude novelty claims for concentration alone. The candidate distinctive contribution is a cross-system all-direction, fixed-roster composition test conditional on the actual abundance change, distinguishing concentration from rearrangement and recovery. Verify novelty of this exact contrast further before submission.
## One paper, no invented chain
Working title: Spatial reorganization of colonial seabirds beyond population change. Core: original paired network database (source identity, count units, temporal windows and duplicate lineage), all-direction composition, dominance turnover, local long-term/fixed roster and recovery. The Ross case illustrates recovery but is not a replicated mechanism. Antarctic marine exposure and diet proxy work remain distinct supporting context unless exact common spatial/temporal grain can identify links. Vegetation/seal causal pathways unresolved; supplementary source audit only. CMIP6 physical risk not biological forecast. No pooling of repeated source cohorts. Distinguish annual and multi-decadal systems; difference not proven timescale effect. Do not claim independent sampling of 604 islands.
