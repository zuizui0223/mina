# Manuscript Results — Land–sea observation support limits for compound Antarctic penguin climate exposures

We reconciled the 39 source Adélie penguin breeding site codes with historical long-term nesting records against static vegetation and lichen detection within 2 km, observed recent 2017–2023 nesting counts and CMIP6 model future changes in air temperature, marine sea ice and model sea-surface salinity. We required exact source site IDs, an independent coordinate match, and the original vegetation–climate location receipts, and checked each model component against the fixed original \`macro_40\` site/variable source. **Thirty-one of the 39 historically studied Adélie sites were supported**, representing 913,743 of the 1,074,279 noncontemporaneous latest recorded census-count weights (**85.06%**). Eight sites lacked the requisite original site/species vegetation rows; no vegetation or population absence was imputed.

The 31-site panel contained **26 positive static mapped vegetation/lichen detections**, while five sites had no mapped polygon but unresolved actual vegetation absence. Twenty-eight sites had at least two observed recent count seasons, and 17 had at least three. A strict co-location *quality filter* requiring all three climate models' native sea-ice and salinity ocean cells within 50 km, plus ERA5 September source grid land fraction ≥0.5, left **five source site codes** (CRZE, CRZW, MAND, PGEO and PHIL). Requiring at least three actual recent census seasons retained only **three codes** (CRZE, CRZW and MAND), of which CRZE and CRZW represent one Cape Crozier breeding district, not two independent geographical experiments. The 0.25° ERA5 land fraction is a coarse land/marine support flag, not on-nest snow or terrestrial habitat availability.

For SSP5-8.5 2081–2100, the full 31 matched-site cohort partitioned into three physically distinct three-model sign-consensus groups: **13 source codes** with both sea-ice decline and modeled marine surface freshening (33.45% of the original 39-site count weight), **14 source codes** with consistent modeled freshening but no all-model agreement on sea-ice decline (51.42%), and **four codes** with common sea-ice retreat but no all-model salinity-decline agreement (0.19%). The corresponding weights conditional on only the 31 land-matched sites were 39.33%, 60.45% and 0.22%. All 13 common retreat/freshening sites had detectable mapped vegetation/lichen, but *this is a static geographic intersection rather than a demonstrated vegetation response to warming*. Under the strict marine plus ERA5 land quality filter, **only the two Cape Crozier source codes** retained joint sea-ice/freshening exposure, representing 242,681 mixed-date recorded census counts (22.59% of the original 39-site weight).

The original 106-location climate atlas defined marine maximum distance using the three **sea-ice** ocean-cell distances rather than both sea-ice and salinity cells. We preserved this original variable and recalculated a stricter all-model, both-variable maximum-distance support. The 50-km classifications did not change for the 31 matched Adélie sites. Across four scenarios, all **124 matched ADPE source rows** reconciled with the original 39-site CMIP6 model-warming, sea-ice and salinity signs without mismatch.

Notably, the 2017–2023 recent census slope was negative at 26 of the 28 sites where at least two recent seasons were observed, while the 1991–2020 original long-term slope was nonnegative at 16 of the sites with a negative recent slope. These slopes were estimated from different durations, survey methods and observation subsets; they cannot establish a recent climate-driven reversal or a future response coefficient.

**Interpretation:** Climate-model physical exposure can be attached to historical Adélie colony sites with static land observations, and warming, ice retreat and surface freshening remain geographically non-equivalent environmental axes. The simultaneous strict land-and-sea source cohort, however, is geographically concentrated and much too small for an independent multi-island climatic effect or climate-to-vegetation-to-nesting transfer. Static mapped polygons represent detectable land context, not temporal greening, colony displacement or future usable nest space. The long-term demonstrated breeding-space concentration and these conditional future physical scenarios remain independent, complementary evidence streams rather than a calibrated demographic forecast.
