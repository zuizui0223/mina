# Results and limitations — baseline compatibility of nearshore CMIP6 and observed penguin colony sea ice

### Model historical baseline biases

The 1995–2014 August–November seasonal SIC at 39 original Adélie penguin breeding site codes was reconstructed from satellite observations and compared with the co-located native historical ocean-grid values of three CMIP6 models. Across the 39 codes, historical absolute SIC errors averaged **11.91 percentage points (pp) for ACCESS-CM2**, **10.58 pp for BCC-CSM2-MR**, and **11.30 pp for CESM2**. The original site-level model and observed SIC had spatial correlations of **0.840, 0.955 and 0.823**, respectively. There were 20, 15 and 17 distinct historical native model sea-ice grid cells for those 39 site codes, indicating pseudoreplication in location-based climate exposure summaries. The historical observed nearshore measure represents a 0–100-km satellite zone, whereas model variables represent native coarse-grid marine cells. Absolute mismatch cannot be interpreted purely as an error in the predicted future change.

For the Cape Crozier original two census codes, historical model SIC exceeded observed means by **8.71–14.37 pp**. The corresponding SSP5-8.5 2081–2100 model-native raw seasonal mean SICs ranged from **80.38 to 86.47%**, but future values after anchoring each model's climate-change anomaly to the observed 1995–2014 site baseline ranged from **67.75 to 74.35%**. The latter are the *source-defined climate scenarios*, not observed future coastal conditions. The raw absolute model and observed baseline are structurally not interchangeable.

### Dependence of site-level climatological novelty on observational anchoring

Under SSP5-8.5 in 2081–2100, the three-model intersection of site codes whose future seasonal SIC fell at least 5 pp below their observed rolling-20-year historical minimum accounted for **22.815%** of the noncontemporaneous recorded-count weight with observationally anchored model changes. Applying the same criterion directly to the *uncorrected model-native future SIC and the observed historical envelope* returned **1.165%**: a diagnostic of incompatible baselines, not a competing valid population scenario or confidence interval. The discrepancy was largely attributable to the inclusion of Cape Crozier after observational anchoring.

The historical data also permitted a same-duration comparison of two original 20-year means, 1991–2010 and 2001–2020, separated by a ten-year shift. The median largest downward 20-year-mean change was **0.307 pp** across 39 sites. Future modeled SIC change exceeded that change by more than 5 pp in all three models at **eight codes with 22.815% of current count weight**, matching the strict subset in the previous ten-year-decade control. However, the historical windows overlap by ten years; the observed 30-year record contains no independent multi-decade 20-year transitions and cannot calibrate temporal return probabilities or penguin population consequences.

### Geographical support sensitivity

Applying an all-model native marine-grid distance requirement ≤50 km and a historical absolute model-observation SIC mismatch ≤15 pp restricted the cohort to **23 of 39 sites** (80.16% of census-weight records). Within that supported cohort, only the two Cape Crozier source codes retained the three-model strict novelty condition, accounting for **22.59 pp of the full 39-site snapshot**. Tightening the historical mismatch to ≤10 pp retained only 15 sites (51.01% of the census weight) and no strict model-common novelty; restricting every model's marine cell distance to ≤25 km left six sites (37.39% of census weight) and also excluded both Crozier codes because CESM2's native cells were >43 km away.

These are **input-support and scale-sensitivity diagnostics**, not evidence that a physically novel colony environment is absent in filtered locations. Site counts, model-cell coverage, recorded-count weights and their denominators must be reported together.

### Scientific interpretation

The physical conclusion should remain conditional: future changes in seasonal sea-ice concentration under three single-member CMIP6 scenarios can be compared with each colony code's own historical multidecadal climatic context, but the resulting census-weighted fraction depends on observational anchoring, source spatial support and concentration of recorded breeding counts. None of the historical model biases, corrected novelty classifications, or incomplete biological temporal tests establish a causal climate response, a demographic hazard boundary or a forecast of future nest losses.
