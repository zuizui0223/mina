# Results draft: multivariate environmental exposure decouples at sampled Adélie penguin breeding sites

We evaluated original CMIP6 August–November mean changes in near-site atmospheric temperature (tas), native ocean-grid sea-ice concentration (siconc), and modeled sea-surface salinity (sos), using 39 original Adélie source-site codes with long-term nesting census histories. Three models (ACCESS-CM2, BCC-CSM2-MR and CESM2), two emission pathways and two 20-year future windows yielded **1,404 original source variable changes**, harmonized to **468 model-site-scenario rows** and **156 site-scenario three-model sign assessments**. The sum of each site's latest original MAPPPD count was 1,074,279, but the observations were noncontemporaneous (2001–2025) and mixed survey targets and methods. We therefore use census counts as *fixed geographic weights*, never as an estimate of future abundance.

All 39 source sites showed warming in every model and future scenario. In late-century SSP5-8.5, all three models agreed in sea-ice decline at 23 sites (44.23% of current sampled census weight), and in model-surface-salinity decline at 32 sites (99.75% of census weight). **Sixteen sites (10 distinct three-model marine-grid fingerprints), representing 43.98% of census weight, had both sea-ice decline and freshening in all three models.** Another **16 source sites, representing 55.77% of census weight**, had consistently decreasing salinity but *not* all-three-model agreement in sea-ice retreat. Thus relatively high and less-changing sea-ice environments, particularly in Victoria Land, do not imply stationary local ocean-salinity conditions under the same modeled warming.

A stronger *physical magnitude* screen required all three models to show at least 5 percentage points sea-ice retreat, at least 0.05 change in original modeled salinity units toward freshening, and at least 2°C near-site atmospheric warming. Under SSP5-8.5 in 2081–2100, this condition was met only at the **two Cape Crozier census codes**, representing **242,681 latest recorded counts (22.59% of all 39-site census weight)**. The two codes are within a single large breeding area and should not be treated as independent ecological replicates. Across models their climate-change ranges were approximately **6.0–12.1 percentage points of reduced SIC**, **0.051–0.363 original units of modeled sea-surface salinity decline**, and **+2.9–6.5°C atmospheric warming**. Tightening the salinity screen to 0.1 removes their three-model agreement, illustrating that these cutoffs are magnitude sensitivities and not ecological thresholds.

The late-century coincident ice-decline/freshening group was reduced from 16 to 12 source codes when requiring all three models' native marine grid cells within 50km of each colony source code (33.56% of the *unchanged original* census-count denominator). Removing both Cape Crozier codes from the physical compound-exposure set and renormalizing the other 37 source codes gave 27.63%. The source sites' physical marine-grid fingerprints and their count weights are separately archived, and each grid fingerprint may represent multiple original census codes.

**Interpretation:** Physical atmospheric warming, spring sea-ice retreat and modeled coastal surface freshening are not interchangeable geographic exposure axes. Existing observational findings of breeding-space contraction and source-site sea-ice covariance remain positive but are not causally transferred into future census counts. Model agreement in climate-change signs does not establish an effect on colony recruitment, survival, nesting success, vegetation or self-sustaining occupancy. The model salinity is not locally measured seawater salinity, and tas is atmospheric temperature, not sea-surface temperature (SST). The climate-source baseline is 1995–2014; model grid resolution and single-member ensemble uncertainty remain central.
