# Results: Future CMIP6 sea-ice covariance does not have a robust common direction across penguin breeding-site model supports

We reconstructed native CMIP6 August–November annual sea-ice concentration (SIC) from nine source-pinned monthly CSV.gz files, representing three original single-member climate models during 1995–2014, 2041–2060 and 2081–2100 under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5. Across 39 sampled Adélie breeding census codes, **11,700 site-model-year SIC values** matched all **585 original site-period seasonal means and annual standard deviations** at floating-point precision. Count weights (1,074,279 records) were derived from each site's latest, noncontemporaneous MAPPPD census, not future population size.

At the same 39 sites in satellite observations during 1995–2014, the annual SD of raw nearshore SIC averaged across sites was **3.252 pp under equal site weighting** and **1.828 pp under fixed count weights**. The corresponding SD of the count-weighted mean of *within-site standardized* SIC anomalies was **0.495 vs 0.699**. This confirms the geographic contrast identified in the original 30-year satellite analysis, while making no inference about population synchrony.

For SSP5-8.5 late century, a site-level annual SIC SD minimum of 0.5pp in each historical/future window retained **33, 22 and 28 codes** within ACCESS-CM2, BCC-CSM2-MR and CESM2 (count-weight coverage 95.3%, 90.1%, 99.0%). The fixed-count-weighted standardized common-anomaly SD changed **0.679→0.644**, **0.769→0.607** and **0.671→0.675**, respectively. The absolute annual SD of raw count-weighted SIC changed **1.893→2.816**, **1.570→1.522** and **2.073→2.232 pp**; neither metric showed a common three-model direction.

When all three models were restricted to exactly the same **16 original census codes** with annual SIC SD ≥0.5pp in both windows (85.38% of count weight, but only 11/8/9 distinct model-native sea-ice cells), standardized common-anomaly SD appeared to decrease in all three models by **−0.0544, −0.1650 and −0.0264**. This negative sign was **not robust to site variance support**: relaxing the SD gate to 0.1pp retained 21 codes (93.19% of count weight) and changed CESM2 to **+0.0149**; restricting to 1.0pp retained 11 codes (39.72%) and changed BCC to **+0.0184**. Additional all-model grid-distance ≤50 km and historical absolute model/satellite SIC mismatch ≤15pp left only 11 codes at the 0.5pp support gate; detrending within each 20-year model period gave a slight **+0.0061** future change in CESM2.

Future model SIC covariance is thus a complementary physical climate scenario with unresolved internal variability and sensitivity to which marine cells can be standardized. It cannot be transferred to a forecast of demographic spatial insurance, nest synchrony, breeding decline, or extinction. The primary biological results remain the empirical breeding-space contraction at Palmer/Signy and separately observed relative breeding composition, neither of which was causally calibrated to this CMIP6 covariance. The model covariance estimates are appropriate as supplementary sensitivity evidence, not the headline ecological effect.
