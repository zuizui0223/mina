# Exploratory empirical result: count concentration depends on spatial and temporal scale within Ross Island

### Data and identifiability

We audited original MAPPPD Antarctic Adélie census source records for six consistently named aerial-count sectors nested within three breeding districts on a single physical island: Cape Bird (BRDM, BRDN, BRDS), Cape Crozier (CRZE, CRZW) and Cape Royds (ROYD). Across 1985–2024, all six sectors had one positive aerial-source census record in each of **31 jointly observed seasons**; missing calendar years were not interpolated. The source count target is not independently verified as interchangeable with nest-pair counts across external surveys. We calculated inverse Simpson effective numbers of **three breeding districts** and of **six census sectors** in each jointly observed season.

The historical overlap with directly observed 1991–2020 August–November OSI SAF nearshore sea-ice concentration (SIC) comprised **23 complete census seasons (1991–2019; 2020 unavailable)** and **19 true one-year census transitions**. This is one physical Ross Island, not three island replicates or six independent climate observations.

### Time-scale and spatial-hierarchy associations

Within the 23 historically overlapping seasons, log total aerial count was negatively associated with the effective number of the three districts (**Pearson r = −0.677**), also for the six-sector effective number (r = −0.677). Across the complete 31-season series from 1985–2024, the three-district r was **−0.662**. However, the direction was not stable over time: the coefficient was **−0.584** among 13 sampled seasons in 1991–2009, **+0.179** in the ten seasons 2010–2019, and **−0.245** in 13 seasons from 2011–2024. The 23-year correlation was attenuated to **r = −0.271** after removing linear calendar-year trends, and the corresponding first-difference correlation for 19 fully consecutive annual intervals was **r = −0.349**. These are descriptive associations, not independent-sample inferential tests.

A particularly clear source-matched case concerns recovery without geographic spreading: total recorded counts across the six Ross sectors increased **from 242,230 in 2023 to 310,423 in 2024 (+28.15%)**, while the effective number of three districts remained approximately unchanged (**1.52836 to 1.52744**). Likewise, 2019–2023 recorded counts declined from 361,456 to 242,230 while effective district number slightly increased (1.503 to 1.528). This demonstrates that census-total responses and spatial allocation across colony districts cannot be conflated. It does not establish individual migration, reproductive success or the mechanism of recovery.

In the 19 directly consecutive historical year intervals, change in the *relative nearshore SIC contrast between Cape Crozier and Cape Bird* was not closely associated with change in Crozier's district share (**Pearson r = −0.056**). These six census sectors are not independent nearshore climate replicates; the result rules out only a demonstrated association in this restricted indicator and support.

### Why the current land–sea panel cannot test temporal snow or vegetation effects

We also verified original 2011–2021 September ERA5 coarse-grid snow-water-equivalent (SWE) for 106 model-linked locations. **83/106** had no extracted interannual variability; among the 39 historically studied Adélie source sites, **36/39** had no SWE variability. Most crucially, **all 31/31** sites that also had static vegetation/lichen maps and recent nesting counts had *identical extracted SWE values across all eleven years*. Some values were consistently positive; this is not observational evidence of zero true snowfall or zero nesting-site snow change.

An independent original-census timing audit yielded **56 same-method, after-November verified, consecutive-year count intervals at 14 land-matched sites** during 2011–2020. Requiring coarse ERA5 grid land fraction at least 0.5 left **18 intervals from only four source site codes**, all with **zero extracted year-to-year SWE change**. Thus no within-site time-varying snow coefficient is estimable from these preexisting data. All six Ross Island census sectors have positive *static* vegetation detections, precluding a vegetation-presence contrast within that island. Static vegetation does not measure future greening or available nest-area change.

### Implications and limits

The previously validated breeding-space contraction at Palmer and Signy operates at within-colony spatial scales. The present Ross Island source census series shows that between-district allocation can exhibit a different, epoch-dependent association with abundance. This is evidence that **the spatial organizational unit is essential to translating colony count change into spatial concentration**, rather than a universal counterlaw to the existing Palmer/Signy result. Historical-to-future physical CMIP6 sea-ice, air-temperature, salinity and static vegetation exposures remain independent observations or scenarios: none supplies a validated future colony-abundance or within-colony breeding-space prediction.
