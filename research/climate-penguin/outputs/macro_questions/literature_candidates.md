# Penguin macroecology: primary-source screen

Research date: 2026-10-29. These are hypotheses, not estimated effects or established novel findings.

## Lead candidate: ice as transport, not just habitat or obstacle

Question: **At comparable sea-ice amount, do direction and seasonal organization of ice movement explain subsequent changes in Adélie breeding populations?** Losing ice may remove a transport system as well as an obstacle. Static distance between colonies does not describe this changing marine environment.

Primary anchor: Jongsomjit et al.2024, Ecology105:e4196, [Going with the floe](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.4196). Two Ross Island colonies and three years of geolocation/ice-motion observations already demonstrate that aligned sea-ice movement supports greater travel distances and affects destinations, while misaligned movement can impede travel. The core analysis includes146 trips for87 known-age birds. This is existing movement ecology, not a new mechanism discovered by mina. The proposed extension is to population-level annual changes over wider places/years. [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70255039).

Opposing explanations:

- Amount-only: sea-ice amount/duration and regional environmental variation adequately explain population responses; direction-aware variables add little predictive information.
- Seasonal transport/exposure: at comparable amount, route-aligned ice motion predicts different subsequent demographic responses. A generic positive effect of faster ice is NOT predicted: departure and return differ, and drift may carry birds away from productive habitat.

Existing mina annual count changes can supply responses. Historical remotely sensed ice-motion fields and independently justified migration regions must be added. Do not infer routes from the count outcomes, use the nearest ice pixel as an individual exposure, or extrapolate two tracked colonies circumpolarly without validation. Distinguish absolute speed, vector direction, sea-ice concentration, and directional support along a defined route. Annual counts cannot distinguish adult survival, breeding propensity, recruitment, and movement. A direction association is not direct proof of energetic benefits.

Implementation gate: audit correlation/coverage of concentration and drift; retain common-support colony-years. Predefine seasonal windows and a small number of biologically grounded lags. Fit count observation error/method effects and colony plus region-year structure, with held-out years/colonies. Compare amount-only against direction-aware predictions. If routes are too uncertain, measure regional drift regimes and describe the result as association with ocean/ice circulation, not individual transport support. Sensitivity to plausible alternative routes is necessary; no route oracle.

Why penguins: these birds cannot use wind-powered aerial migration but can use moving sea ice as support. Their repeated breeding counts provide a measurable terrestrial endpoint of a seasonal marine transport process. This does not require a causal claim about why flightlessness evolved. Terrestrial mammalian-predator absence is background, not an estimable treatment. Vegetation should not be forced into this test.

## Second candidate: demographic neighbours differ from geographic neighbours

Question: Do geographically distant breeding colonies fluctuate together because their birds encounter similar winter environments, while neighbouring colonies with different winter use do not?

Compare geographic-distance and breeding-season environmental similarity against independently measured winter exposure similarity in annual growth covariance. Use common-year annual changes, account for shared trends, method changes, spatial/dyadic dependence, and leave colonies/years out for validation. Colony pairs are not independent replicates. The146 long site-species series and869 annual intervals in the existing audit do not alone establish matched pair support.

This is NOT a new claim that winter matters:

- [Hinke et al.2019 PLOS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0226207) already explicitly proposes that winter mixing creates shared environmental experience aligning regional chinstrap responses. It reports an association between the fraction remaining within500km and population trend. It finds generally low fine-scale overlap despite broad ocean mixing. [S1ZIP](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0226207.s008) contains telemetry and historical nest census. The file is identified from the publisher but was not downloaded in this subtask.
- [Gentoo winter study2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7876601/) documents coastal winter use and potential leapfrog movements. Gentoo is not uniformly sedentary. [Dryad](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.5x69p8d20) metadata verifies manuscript_data.csv2.53MB,71 individuals, colony identifiers, coordinates, life stage, ice and depth. Raw file not downloaded here.
- [Hinke et al.2015](https://doi.org/10.1890/ES14-00287.1) establishes winter spatial/isotopic partitioning between Adélie and chinstrap penguins.
- [Lyver et al.2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0091188) already analyzes Ross Sea Adélie growth synchrony, with mean correlation0.59 among southern colonies. Finding synchrony alone is insufficient novelty.
- [Adélie nonbreeding study2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320725005440) documents tracking62 birds from Terre Adélie over five years and poor protection of wintering habitat by breeding-based protected boundaries. It identifies Movebank4778411162 and [code](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.27947100.v2); access and matching to mina not checked.

Prospective advance: formal prediction of demographic covariance using independent seasonal exposure versus static geography, with uncertainty. Counts do not identify breeding dispersal or rescue effects. Three species do not independently replicate a causal migration or flightlessness effect.

## Alternatives rejected because prior overlap is strong

- [East Antarctic density-dependent nesting limitation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7069296/) already directly tests available breeding habitat and population growth.
- [Beaufort2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3616090/) already links ice-field retreat and increased nesting habitat/population.
- [Windmill Islands2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34109449/) addresses resource limitation and slowing growth after decades of expansion.
- [Comparative genomics](https://doi.org/10.1111/mec.14896) establishes major differences in penguin connectivity. [Gentoo whole-genome study](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/whole-genome-sequencing-reveals-stepping-stone-dispersal-buffered/) already analyzes stepping-stone range expansion; low routine dispersal yet expansion is not an untouched surprise.

Neither candidate can be called unprecedented on this bounded review. Lead candidate is more specifically Antarctic/penguin-relevant; second has stronger direct prior linkage between winter mixing and trends. Both need more than the current counts to identify the proposed mechanism, but neither needs to shrink the research to four terrestrial patches.
