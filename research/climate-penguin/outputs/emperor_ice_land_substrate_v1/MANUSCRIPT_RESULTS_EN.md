# Results draft: Land versus mobile sea-ice breeding substrate does not simply determine colony-loss concentration

We examined a proposed macroecological contrast: Adélie and other Pygoscelis penguins breed on fixed terrestrial sites, whereas emperor penguins breed primarily on seasonal fast ice. We posited that the latter's spatial concentration during decline might be driven more by whole-colony disappearance than by relative allocation among colonies. The contrast was tested descriptively with the original **50 emperor colonies×10 years (2009–2018)** from LaRue et al. (2024), using the authors' publicly released Bayesian model posterior marginal colony estimates, and original 2009/2018 terrestrial MAPPPD site×species×method records. All input CSV Git blobs were verified byte-identical to the original public sources.

### Same distributional index, different posterior estimands

For a fixed roster of colonies or census source sectors, we calculated the inverse Simpson effective number, \`N_eff=(sum N_i)^2/sum(N_i^2)\`. An arithmetic fixed-composition null scales all first-year weights by the observed second-year total and therefore predicts unchanged \`N_eff\`; it **does not** reproduce the observation-error-aware Palmer/Signy null.

The 50 source emperor posterior *marginal mean* adult indices summed to **253,323 (2009)** versus **230,445 (2018)** (**−9.03%**). Effective colony number from marginal means declined **25.537→22.883 (−10.39%)**. Summing each individual colony's marginal median, rather than computing the posterior median of their joint sum, yielded **239,178→209,572 (−12.38%)** and a median-derived \`N_eff\` of **25.312→22.079 (−12.77%)**. The latter reproduces previously quoted percentages but is not the original paper's inferred global change, which had a joint posterior median decline of approximately **−9.5%** and posterior decline probability **0.814**. Neither a posterior mean nor credible interval for \`N_eff\` can be recovered from individual colony marginal summary tables without joint posterior samples.

### Modelled-zero turnover does not account for most emperor concentration

Among 50 emperor colony IDs, **47** had positive modelled adult indices in both endpoint years, **two** became modelled zero (BBAY, UMBE) and **one** changed from modelled zero to positive (LEDD). The two eventually zero-valued colonies contributed only **0.313%** of initial modelled abundance. Setting just those two colony values to zero in 2009, without changing remaining relative shares, yielded an N_eff loss of **0.61%**; the actual full-panel marginal-mean N_eff change was **−10.39%**. The 47 persistent positive colonies alone exhibited **−10.51%** N_eff change. Therefore, most of the documented colony-level spatial concentration reflects altered *relative size among still-modelled-positive colonies*, not arithmetic removal of the two zero-modelled endpoints. Modelled zeros cannot be interpreted as irreversible local extinctions or annual migration.

Among the eight published emperor fast-ice regions, five exhibited declining modelled abundance. Four of these also lost effective colony number, but the **Weddell Sea** displayed a **−20.36%** abundance index change accompanied by a **+22.98%** effective-colony increase. This is a concrete regional counterexample to a universal colony-loss/concentration response under changing ice breeding conditions.

### A terrestrial Pygoscelis comparator demonstrates the spatial-unit limitation

Fixed source-site, same species/region/method terrestrial 2009/2018 MAPPPD counts produced only **five** panels with two or more observed sites. The largest Adélie panel comprised **six aerial census sectors from three breeding districts on one Ross Island**. Its total recorded counts increased **352,246→380,867 (+8.13%)** while inverse Simpson N_eff for six sectors fell **1.875→1.750 (−6.67%)**. At the three-district level, N_eff fell **1.471→1.423 (−3.30%)**. Hence terrestrial colonies can show increased total counts with more concentrated relative sector allocation. Other terrestrial panels had only two to four sites and retain only source sites with positive counts at both endpoints, so they are not valid samples for estimating complete colony disappearance.

Comparing 50 modelled Emperor *colony* indices distributed around Antarctica to six Adélie *census sectors* within a single island is explicitly **not** a matched-scale substrate-effect test. The previously positive Palmer and Signy results pertain to within-colony discrete breeding-space contraction, not between-colony movement or turnover. The data currently allow the simple mechanism that mobile breeding substrate makes whole-colony loss the principal source of concentration to be rejected **at the level of this arithmetic model-output decomposition**. Establishing a substrate-driven biological mechanism instead requires comparable year-specific coordinates, occupancy corrected for non-detection, coherent colony-level abundances and multi-scale spatial units for both substrate classes.
