# Independent 10-year Antarctic seasonal SIC history as a climate-novelty benchmark

**No demographic forecast / no biological hazard cutoff.** The prior 20-year rolling historical
SIC ranges had 11 strongly overlapping windows over just 30 calendar years, which make a
small departure from those ranges easy to obtain. This audit retains all **39 original Adélie
census-site codes**, reconstructs 30 original annual Aug–Nov nearshore satellite SIC means,
and groups them into three **non-overlapping** ten-year periods: 1991–2000,
2001–2010, and 2011–2020.

For each original site, the historical comparison is the **largest observed SIC decrease
between consecutive non-overlapping 10-year means**, floored at zero. The CMIP6 model
20-year future SIC scenario is evaluated by its *drop from* the observed reference
1995–2014 twenty-year average. Its projected drop is compared with that site-specific
historical decrease, using a 0, 2 or 5 pp extra margin. An independent secondary check
asks whether the projected 20-year mean is lower than even the minimum of the three
historical disjoint 10-year means.

This comparison is not a matched-window statistical test: historical independent windows
are each 10 years and the scenario input is a 20-year mean; there are only two independent
observed decade-to-decade transitions per site, not sufficient to estimate a probability
distribution. The source-specific climate model cells and historically mixed/cross-year
count weights are retained. The three model conditions are not independent probability draws.
Large colonies at Cape Crozier are a spatial leverage effect, not necessarily more
sensitive biologically.

**Goal:** establish whether physical novelty robustly exceeds a more conservative
historical decade-scale change benchmark. Keep the existing source-checked
20-year envelope, 60–80% hypothetical cutoff envelope, and all positive penguin
breeding-space observations unchanged. Does not change frozen Paper1/Paper2 or main.
