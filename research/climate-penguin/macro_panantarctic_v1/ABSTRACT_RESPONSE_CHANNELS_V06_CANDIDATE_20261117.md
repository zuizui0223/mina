# Candidate v0.6 abstract — climate-linked responses and the spatial grain of penguin breeding

**2026-11-17 | mina PR #214 | candidate hypothesis-led abstract, NOT a replacement of source-pinned v0.5 and NOT a journal submission.**

## Proposed title

**Climate-linked trophic variation and spatially structured breeding change in Antarctic penguins**

## Abstract (candidate, evidence-bounded)

Antarctic penguins may respond to marine environmental change through resource use, population size, and redistribution among breeding colonies. These responses need not follow the same environmental gradient, yet are often conflated when evaluating climate vulnerability. We combined source-audited nest counts for three *Pygoscelis* species (2,456 original records across 595 catalogued breeding sites), 1991–2020 satellite sea ice, coastal ocean reanalysis at 588 sites, and published individual-level diet archives to contrast environmental and biological changes across observational scales. The marine environment varied along a temperature–sea-ice axis (60.4% of principal-component variance) and a separate, largely salinity-associated axis (33.4%). In repeatedly counted Adélie colony rosters, nest-count-weighted latitude shifted 88–107 km south between the 1990s and 2010s, whereas corresponding nest-weighted nearshore sea-ice concentration differed by only 0.02–0.63 percentage points. Gentoo-to-Adélie nest ratios increased at all six strictly source-matched colonies in two regions, while their observed two-species nesting subtotal increased at five of six, distinguishing relative composition change from absolute loss. In harmonized one-year-ahead tests using only preceding-year ice, previous nest-share distributions predicted Palmer's five-island allocation better than regional ice alone (17 tests; total-variation error 0.055 versus 0.159). However, two complete-census colony networks in Victoria Land and the South Shetlands did not reproduce this strong ranking: simple time trends performed best, and site-level ice contrasts were small. At Signy Island, gentoo fish use covaried with preceding sea ice over nine breeding seasons; a four-season sequential retrospective holdout reduced local gentoo fish-share error by 46% relative to a historical-mean baseline, but the same relationship transferred poorly to Palmer. Under SSP5-8.5 at century end, three-model mean sea-ice loss across 271 counted sites was −28.63 percentage points with equal site weights but −13.28 when weighted by observed nest medians; apparent species differences depended strongly on common geographic support. Together, these observations indicate that local resource use, relative species composition, and colony nesting allocation carry information at different spatial and temporal scales. They do not identify a universal climate-driven demographic response, individual breeding dispersal, or a forecast of future colony persistence.
