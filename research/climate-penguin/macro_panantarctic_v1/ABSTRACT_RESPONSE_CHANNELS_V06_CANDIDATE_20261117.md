# Candidate v0.6 abstract — climate-linked responses and the spatial grain of penguin breeding

**2026-11-17 | mina PR #214 | candidate hypothesis-led abstract, NOT a replacement of source-pinned v0.5 and NOT a journal submission.**

## Proposed title

**Climate-linked trophic variation and spatially structured breeding change in Antarctic penguins**

## Abstract (candidate, evidence-bounded)

Antarctic penguins may respond to marine environmental change through resource use, population size, and redistribution among breeding colonies. These responses need not follow the same environmental gradient, yet are often conflated when evaluating climate vulnerability. We combined source-audited nest counts for three *Pygoscelis* species (2,456 original records across 595 catalogued breeding sites), 1991–2020 satellite sea ice, coastal ocean reanalysis at 588 sites, and published individual-level diet archives to contrast environmental and biological changes across observational scales. The marine environment varied along a temperature–sea-ice axis (60.4% of principal-component variance) and a separate, largely salinity-associated axis (33.4%). A distinct exploratory source-matched analysis (860 Adélie colony-seasons, 88 sites, 13 regions) found that a satellite-reconstructed δ¹⁵N isotope proxy was positively associated with salinity after sea ice, seawater temperature, acquisition day, site, and region-by-season controls (β = +0.834‰ per salinity unit; partial residual R² = 2.88%). Geographic uncertainty was substantial (13-region cluster interval −0.016 to +1.684), and a following-season negative control also associated with the isotope proxy, precluding inference about prey switching or a directional salinity effect. When confined to the four source regions where two-species colony co-censuses occurred, the original isotope–salinity coefficient attenuated to +0.080‰ per salinity unit (227 isotope site-seasons; cell-cluster interval −0.343 to +0.503), and none of the 860 isotope records overlapped an actual two-species co-census at the same colony and season. Consequently, a common climate-to-diet-to-colony-composition response chain is not established. In repeatedly counted Adélie colony rosters, nest-count-weighted latitude shifted 88–107 km south between the 1990s and 2010s, whereas corresponding nest-weighted nearshore sea-ice concentration differed by only 0.02–0.63 percentage points. Gentoo-to-Adélie nest ratios increased at all six strictly source-matched colonies in two regions, while their observed two-species nesting subtotal increased at five of six, distinguishing relative composition change from absolute loss. In harmonized one-year-ahead tests using only preceding-year ice, previous nest-share distributions predicted Palmer's five-island allocation better than regional ice alone (17 tests; total-variation error 0.055 versus 0.159). However, two complete-census colony networks in Victoria Land and the South Shetlands did not reproduce this strong ranking: simple time trends performed best, and site-level ice contrasts were small. At Signy Island, gentoo fish use covaried with preceding sea ice over nine breeding seasons; a four-season sequential retrospective holdout reduced local gentoo fish-share error by 46% relative to a historical-mean baseline, but the same relationship transferred poorly to Palmer. Under SSP5-8.5 at century end, three-model mean sea-ice loss across 271 counted sites was −28.63 percentage points with equal site weights but −13.28 when weighted by observed nest medians; apparent species differences depended strongly on common geographic support. In those same counted sites, the three-model mean also indicated coastal surface salinity freshening (nest-weighted Δsos = −0.257 in the model's native 0.001 units). However, all three models agreed on salinity decrease at 179 of 271 sites, and on simultaneous sea-ice and salinity decreases at only 152 sites (59.1% of recorded nest-median weight), illustrating geographically heterogeneous joint physical exposure rather than a future biological response. Across the same original 595 sites, the spatial association between projected sea-ice and salinity changes differed markedly among the three models (late-century SSP5-8.5 correlations +0.743, −0.296 and −0.333; within-region +0.709, −0.332 and −0.079), showing that future marine axes cannot be inferred from sea-ice loss alone. Together, these observations indicate that local resource use, relative species composition, and colony nesting allocation carry information at different spatial and temporal scales. They do not identify a universal climate-driven demographic response, individual breeding dispersal, or a forecast of future colony persistence.

## 2026-11-18 source-verified spatial coupling of future sea-ice and freshening (three models)

The three original CMIP6 source models do **not** agree that projected ice retreat and low-salinity changes covary at the same individual known colony locations. At the frozen 595 original source sites for SSP5-8.5/2081–2100, model-specific spatial r(ΔSIC, ΔSOS): **ACCESS-CM2 +0.743, BCC-CSM2-MR −0.296, CESM2 −0.333**. Removing mean source-reporting-region physical differences retains model-specific correlations **+0.709, −0.332, −0.079**, respectively. Reducing climate pseudo-replication by using one `sos` native model ocean cell×original region (source 103/87/98 distinct model cells, 105/94/102 cell×region items) yields within-region **+0.404, −0.178, −0.350**. The within-region sign remains unchanged in all 18 original one-region-omission checks for each of the three models. Across SSP2-4.5/SSP5-8.5 and both future periods, ACCESS-CM2's coupling changes sign over time, whereas the other two models stay negative; this is *model structural uncertainty*, not an experimentally confirmed ice–salinity causal path.

A separate historical same-month 1995–2014 original-source comparison on 588 actual near-colony marine sites (378 distinct GLORYS wet cells) found **within-region site r for model SOS vs GLORYS salinity only +0.204,+0.208,+0.248**, versus **model SIC vs OSI SAF source sea ice +0.676,+0.367,+0.610** (same ACCESS/BCC/CESM2 order). Different source physical grid resolutions, GLORYS reanalysis rather than direct field measurements, and historical model internal variability prohibit labeling these correlations as future trend skill. Nevertheless, they reinforce that **source-region coastal salinity patterns are less faithfully resolved than ice within this particular three-model audit**. The positive 860 historical optical δ¹⁵N–salinity association remains, but direct biological response projection is not defensible.
