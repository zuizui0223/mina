# Candidate v0.6 abstract — climate-linked responses and the spatial grain of penguin breeding

**2026-11-17 | mina PR #214 | candidate hypothesis-led abstract, NOT a replacement of source-pinned v0.5 and NOT a journal submission.**

## Proposed title

**Climate-linked trophic variation and spatially structured breeding change in Antarctic penguins**

## Abstract (candidate, evidence-bounded)

Antarctic penguins may respond to marine environmental change through resource use, population size, and redistribution among breeding colonies. These responses need not follow the same environmental gradient, yet are often conflated when evaluating climate vulnerability. We combined source-audited nest counts for three *Pygoscelis* species (2,456 original records across 595 catalogued breeding sites), 1991–2020 satellite sea ice, coastal ocean reanalysis at 588 sites, and published individual-level diet archives to contrast environmental and biological changes across observational scales. The marine environment varied along a temperature–sea-ice axis (60.4% of principal-component variance) and a separate, largely salinity-associated axis (33.4%). A distinct exploratory source-matched analysis (860 Adélie colony-seasons, 88 sites, 13 regions) found that a satellite-reconstructed δ¹⁵N isotope proxy was positively associated with salinity after sea ice, seawater temperature, acquisition day, site, and region-by-season controls (β = +0.834‰ per salinity unit; partial residual R² = 2.88%). Geographic uncertainty was substantial (13-region cluster interval −0.016 to +1.684), and a following-season negative control also associated with the isotope proxy, precluding inference about prey switching or a directional salinity effect. When confined to the four source regions where two-species colony co-censuses occurred, the original isotope–salinity coefficient attenuated to +0.080‰ per salinity unit (227 isotope site-seasons; cell-cluster interval −0.343 to +0.503), and none of the 860 isotope records overlapped an actual two-species co-census at the same colony and season. Consequently, a common climate-to-diet-to-colony-composition response chain is not established. In repeatedly counted Adélie colony rosters, nest-count-weighted latitude shifted 88–107 km south between the 1990s and 2010s, whereas corresponding nest-weighted nearshore sea-ice concentration differed by only 0.02–0.63 percentage points. Gentoo-to-Adélie nest ratios increased at all six strictly source-matched colonies in two regions, while their observed two-species nesting subtotal increased at five of six, distinguishing relative composition change from absolute loss. In harmonized one-year-ahead tests using only preceding-year ice, previous nest-share distributions predicted Palmer's five-island allocation better than regional ice alone (17 tests; total-variation error 0.055 versus 0.159). However, two complete-census colony networks in Victoria Land and the South Shetlands did not reproduce this strong ranking: simple time trends performed best, and site-level ice contrasts were small. At Signy Island, gentoo fish use covaried with preceding sea ice over nine breeding seasons; a four-season sequential retrospective holdout reduced local gentoo fish-share error by 46% relative to a historical-mean baseline, but the same relationship transferred poorly to Palmer. Under SSP5-8.5 at century end, three-model mean sea-ice loss across 271 counted sites was −28.63 percentage points with equal site weights but −13.28 when weighted by observed nest medians; apparent species differences depended strongly on common geographic support. Together, these observations indicate that local resource use, relative species composition, and colony nesting allocation carry information at different spatial and temporal scales. They do not identify a universal climate-driven demographic response, individual breeding dispersal, or a forecast of future colony persistence.

## Dated coastal geography lineage gate — do not mistake nominal years for physical change

The Biscoe near-site 1989 and 1998 maps have distinct record IDs but their mapped line geometries are *not independent*: 91.87% of 1989 mapped ice shoreline length coincides exactly with 1998 lines, and all 1998 rock shoreline length has identical 1989 mapped counterparts within 2km. ## 2026-11-17 correction: positive salinity result belongs in the evidence ledger

**Do not treat failure to identify the causal mediator as failure of the observed salinity association.** Two positive, source-audited endpoints are available:

1. **Physical dimension:** full 588-site original GLORYS/OSI SAF 1993–2020 panel: PC1 temperature/ice 60.365% and chiefly salinity PC2 33.377%. PCA orthogonality is not causal independence.
2. **Biological isotope proxy:** in an independent original-source linkage from published Youngflesh 2026 optical spectra, 860 site×seasons (88 sites, 82 wet cells, 13 regions) yield conditional salinity β=+0.8341458422‰/salinity unit, +2.88018% residual partial R² after site FE, region×season FE, original November–February sea ice, seawater temperature and observation day. The 82-wet-cell cluster 95% interval is [+0.389,+1.280]. The **13-source-region conservative interval includes zero** [−0.016,+1.684]; therefore this is *exploratory positive association*, not a uniform Antarctic salinity effect. The 1,000 within-region wet-cell salinity-trajectory swaps have a partial R² median 0.116% against true 2.880%, but are not an exchangeable causal randomization test.

**Proposed manuscript placement:** keep the dual marine-axis result in the main physical Results. The δ¹⁵N×salinity positive association should appear as an explicitly scoped **additional exploratory biological Results paragraph / potential candidate panel** in the hypothesis-led working version, rather than silently disappearing into a negative source-gate appendix. No retrospective p-value promotion, no impact on frozen main v0.5 or its four figures without an explicit later editorial decision.
