# Distinct thermal–ice and salinity dimensions of Antarctic coastal climate do not translate uniformly into penguin breeding assemblages

**Working manuscript v0.3, 11 November 2026 — source-verified exploratory Antarctic macroecology — NOT SUBMITTED.**
**One-paper continuation of v0.2**, preserved at MANUSCRIPT_MACRO_V0_2.md. Separate from frozen penguin Paper 1/2, warming v0.6, and the exploratory bright/dark PR #215. Primary source-provenance result: [full 18-region original source analysis](RESULT_ORIGINAL_588_18REGION_MARINE_AXES_JA_20261111.md) and [dual-axis species response diagnostics](DUAL_MARINE_AXIS_PENGUIN_RESPONSE_RESULT_JA_20261111.md).

## Abstract

Environmental change around Antarctica may simultaneously reshape sea-ice conditions, hydrography, terrestrial habitats, and the breeding assemblages of ice-associated and ice-tolerant penguins. Yet a common spatial environmental gradient need not correspond to one temporal climate trajectory or a transferable biological response. We reconstructed method-consistent original observed nest counts for Adélie, chinstrap, and gentoo penguins at **595 known Antarctic breeding colonies across 18 reporting regions** (2,456 original site–species–season counts), and linked those known colonies to original OSI SAF satellite sea-ice concentration (1991–2020; 71,400 original site–months). At **588 colonies**, the nearest source-verified GLORYS12V1 wet marine grid fell within 25 km, permitting a balanced **28-year, 1993–2020 joint marine SIC–salinity–temperature analysis (16,464 colony–years)**. Standardized marine PCA separated a thermal–ice axis (**PC1 60.4%, SIC loading −0.707, water temperature +0.706**) from a nearly orthogonal surface-salinity dimension (**PC2 33.4%, salinity loading +0.998**). This contrasted with the earlier six-region subset, in which salinity appeared to contribute strongly to PC1. Regions accounted for **70.7% of pooled thermal–ice PC1 variance**, but the temporal trajectories were not uniformly monotonic: **13 of 18 regions** had higher fixed-axis mean scores in 2001–2010 than in both 1993–2000 and 2011–2020. Using only original records of **both species at the same colony in the same nominal season**, we identified **351** species-pair colony–seasons; neither physical PC axis showed a robust, geographically transferable relationship with within-colony nesting ratios across the three species pairs. A weak salinity-oriented signal in gentoo relative to chinstrap nests persisted after site and calendar-year adjustment, yet its sign reversed when the central-west Antarctic Peninsula was excluded; site+year residual explanatory power was **<1%**. Contemporaneous mapped terrestrial vegetation/lichen/algal cover likewise remained a **static geographical context**, not an observed greening time series. Antarctic marine climates therefore have distinct physical dimensions and regionally variable histories that cannot be reduced to a single empirically established ‘subantarcticization’ index or extrapolated directly to continental penguin community change.

**Keywords:** Antarctic coastal ecology; Pygoscelis; sea ice; surface salinity; potential seawater temperature; marine environmental dimensionality; macroecology; spatial versus temporal covariance; breeding-colony monitoring; survey effort.

## Introduction

A common conceptual account of warming Antarctica links decreasing sea ice, freshwater influx, expansion of ice-free terrestrial habitats, and shifts from ice-associated to subantarctic-associated organisms. Although these processes can coincide, they operate through different physical mechanisms and are observed at different spatial and temporal grains. Sea-ice freezing can reject brine into seawater, melting can dilute the surface ocean, and ocean transport and vertical mixing may alter either tendency. A regional association between low sea ice and reduced salinity consequently does not by itself demonstrate that local sea ice and salinity will change together over decades. Nor does a spatially warmer marine environment imply that a penguin colony is experiencing a directly measurable climate-driven change in its breeding population.

This distinction is fundamental for colonial seabirds. Penguin nest counts are available only for particular surveyed breeding sites and years, with uneven coverage across Antarctica. The absence of a colony–species record does not establish ecological absence, and nest-count ratios from the same nominal breeding season do not necessarily have exactly matched survey dates, observer effort or source citations. Closely spaced colonies also often share the same satellite pixels or ocean reanalysis cells, so even large numbers of records may represent only a few effectively independent spatial environments. We therefore need to establish which patterns are physically source-supported and which biological observations can be compared at the same observational unit before interpreting a broad warming-to-biota narrative.

Here we examine two connected but distinct questions. **First**, do sea-ice concentration, surface salinity, and potential seawater temperature form one Antarctic-wide spatial and temporal marine axis, or do they occupy multiple environmental dimensions with heterogeneous regional histories? **Second**, where the three pygoscelid penguins were genuinely counted together at one colony in one breeding season, do changes in their relative observed nests correspond to either of the physically identified marine dimensions after controlling for colony and shared survey-year structure? As an intentionally separate terrestrial subpanel, we ask whether source-mapped positive terrestrial polygon area follows the same geographic marine gradient, without treating that static map as annual greening. The work is **exploratory, source-verified macroecology**, not a search for post-hoc significant lag lengths or a climate-driven demographic forecast.

## Materials and Methods

### Original Antarctic colony and survey cohorts

Original Antarctic Penguin Biogeography/MAPPPD-derived site–species–season observations were reconstructed using source-pinned GIS and count files, with nonnegative true observed nest counts and the most frequently used known original census method retained for each species at each original colony site. This retained **2,456 observed counts for three species across 595 unique colony-site identifiers in 18 original reporting regions**; source years span 1892–2025. We do not treat unrecorded species, missing years, or nonselected methods as biological absences or zero nest counts. To describe longer-term species-specific trends we also retain the separately source-verified **100 site–species series at 77 sites** with ≥5 actually observed census years over ≥10 years during 1991–2020. This biological cohort is not identical to the environmental atlas of 595 localities.

Geographic dependence was audited using the original 250-km polar projected source grid and original source-region labels. Neither blocks nor region labels are biological islands or independent randomized climate treatments. Actual repeated observations at a single colony and multiple colonies sharing marine cells were not counted as separate ecological experiments in interpretation.

### Sea ice, reanalysis hydrography, and exact temporal matching

Original EUMETSAT OSI SAF OSI-450-a1 v3.1 Southern EASE2 monthly 25-km gridded satellite sea-ice concentration was obtained for all 595 breeding-locality coordinates for August–November each year over **1991–2020**, using the fixed original September 1993 valid marine pixel mask and a 100-km nearshore set of native ocean grid cells. The full month table comprises **71,400 colony–months**; publisher NetCDF source SHA-256s, geolocation, marine status flags, and monthly site means were independently verified.

Original Copernicus Marine GLORYS12V1 202311 reanalysis supplied sea-surface practical salinity (source variable *so*) and sea-surface potential seawater temperature (*thetao*). A source fixed September 2011 wet-ocean mask was mapped to all 595 original breeding sites using WGS84 geodesic nearest-cell distances, with a **maximum eligible cell-to-colony distance of 25 km**. This yielded **588 source sites in all 18 reporting regions** (378 distinct wet reanalysis cells); seven sites were excluded without imputation. The original 40-site grid map and 34 source-eligible cell identities were reproduced, followed by independent numerical reconstruction of all 1,360 2011–2020 original salinity and temperature site–months at those 34 sites to machine precision. Reanalysis source data for the 588 sites are complete for August–November 1993–2020: **65,856 authentic colony–months**, giving **16,464 authentic colony–years** once four complete original SIC, salinity and thetao months are aggregated. This primary physical panel contains the same 588 localities in all 28 years; it contains no fabricated salinity before 1993 or interpolated missing penguin counts. GLORYS is a data-assimilating model reanalysis rather than independently observed in situ salinity, and these coastal point exposures are not measured penguin foraging ranges.

### Marine environmental axes and region–time separation

We standardized source SIC, GLORYS surface salinity and GLORYS water temperature across the fully observed **588 site × 28-year** matrix and fit PCA on the three-dimensional source observations. PC1 was oriented so that its seawater-temperature loading is positive, and PC2 so that its salinity loading is positive; these sign conventions do not assert physical causal directions. We reported all three explained fractions, loadings and pairwise original-variable correlations. We decomposed PC1 score variance between and within original reporting regions. To assess geographic dependence, PCA was repeated with **one source region–year per row**, and with **one original wet marine grid cell–year per row**; repeated site–years from the same marine cell were not treated as independent in that sensitivity. A distinct temporal PCA removed each source site's 28-year mean of each physical variable before standardizing and refitting PCA. Thus the spatial PC1 cannot be interpreted as a common temporal warming axis unless temporal structure is also supported.

For each source reporting region we retained the fixed full-PCA PC1 loading and calculated its means in **1993–2000 (eight years), 2001–2010 (ten years), and 2011–2020 (ten years)** and its 1993–2020 28-year OLS slope. The unequal length of the early era was explicitly retained. Deleting one calendar year at a time provided a descriptive sensitivity of each regional trend; we did not choose different calendar cutoffs according to the response. Reports include colony counts, unique source wet cells and geographic blocks for every region. These trend summaries are descriptions of reanalysis climate at known breeding localities, not proof of anthropogenic climate attribution.

### Original simultaneous species-pair nest counts

For the three fixed pairs (Adélie–gentoo, chinstrap–gentoo, and Adélie–chinstrap), a biological response was used only when **both species actually had an original observed nest count at exactly the same source colony and breeding season within 1993–2020**. Original method agreement was recorded explicitly, while original zero nests remained zero and unrecorded species were never treated as zero. Relative nesting composition is **log(1 + observed nests of the second species) − log(1 + observed nests of the first species)**. This is a measured nest-count ratio, not verified species occurrence, an abundance share of all unsurveyed species, or a demographic fitness parameter.

The same-season observations comprised **98, 165, and 88 source records respectively** (351 total), with 97/98, 165/165, and 88/88 showing the same original census method for both species. Descriptive pooled and within-site Pearson correlations were calculated for the two physical PCA scores. For a simultaneous two-axis site-level association, source sites with ≥2 genuine dual-count years were within-site centered, and each colony given equal total residual weight in a two-PC OLS of the observed log nesting ratio. Each physical PC score was divided by its standard deviation in the **entire source 16,464-site-year physical panel** before biological comparison. The fixed physical axes were never refit to maximize correlation with penguin counts.

**Post-result safeguards:** Because even same-site observations may track a common year-wide signal, we additionally projected both physical PC scores and the nest ratio onto genuine original colony and calendar-year fixed-effect indicators using equal-site-weighted Frisch–Waugh–Lovell residualization. We reported two-PC coefficients and algebraic residual degrees of freedom and examined the sign after leaving out one original reporting region or polar 250-km block at a time. We also repeated the analysis after separately excluding source recorded zero-nest observations; this is a source sensitivity, not an ecological pseudoabsence model. These analyses were motivated by exploratory patterns, **not independently preregistered**, and their within-sample residual *R*² values cannot be compared to an out-of-sample prediction. To separately describe genuine long-term compositional trajectories, site-level slopes used ≥3 real dual-count seasons spanning ≥10 years, with biological and climate slopes fit over the **same actual counted years** rather than different climate-decade windows.

### Static terrestrial mapping

We retained the original 2017–2023 mapped terrestrial 2-km composite as a **single static spatial habitat observation per source site**. Mapped positive union area covers vegetation, lichens and algal terrestrial features, not just flowering-plant greening; a mapping non-detection is not verified plant absence. The complete physical cohort overlapped **167 positive mapped colony sites across eight regions**. We compared original static positive area ranks with **each site's 1993–2020 average** marine PC1 score, then used original region-demeaned log-positive-area comparisons. No annual terrestrial time series, ice-free ground trend, or vegetation change rate was fabricated.

## Results

### A large, independently reproduced 18-region marine atlas

The verified satellite and hydrographic sources yielded a spatially and temporally balanced **588 × 28 environmental matrix across all 18 original Antarctic reporting regions**, encompassing **378 distinct native ocean reanalysis cells**. The prior 34-site/2011–2020 original physical PCA was exactly reproduced (PC1 70.943%, original physical loadings matched within 10⁻⁸), demonstrating that new findings do not arise from changing the source variable definitions or prior data files. The 595-site geographic inventory and true nest observation universe were not modified.

### The apparent one-axis physical regime splits into thermal–ice and salinity dimensions at continental scale

On all 16,464 genuine source colony–years, the standardized marine climate PC1 explained **60.365%** of physical variance and contrasted low SIC (**−0.7073**) with higher seawater temperature (**+0.7058**), with negligible salinity contribution (**−0.0383**). PC2 explained **33.377%** and was almost entirely surface salinity (loading **+0.9977**); PC3 accounted for 6.257%. The source SIC–temperature correlation was **−0.8098**, whereas SIC–salinity was **+0.0539** and temperature–salinity **+0.0100**. In comparison, the original limited 34-colony/6-region panel showed salinity loading **−0.489** on PC1; that local covariation was therefore not a continent-wide single-index rule.

The physical separation persisted under source geography controls. When one original region–year was given equal weight, PC1 explained **65.14%**, and the PC1 loading direction remained aligned with the full result (cosine 0.981); when each unique original ocean reanalysis cell–year was given one vote, PC1 explained **60.47%**, with salinity loading still near zero (−0.0545). Original source reporting-region means accounted for **70.7% of full PC1 variance**. After instead removing each original site's 28-year means, the **within-site temporal** PCA PC1 explained 55.25%, and salinity's loading on temporal PC1 was +0.336. The spatial and temporal covariance structures are therefore related but not identical; the latter is variation over years, not automatically a monotonic climate trend.

### Regional environmental histories are not monotonic or geographically interchangeable

At the fixed original full-source PC1, **13/18 reporting regions** had higher average scores in the **2001–2010** decade than in both 1993–2000 and 2011–2020. This descriptive mid-period peak also appeared in **10/13 regions** with at least three originally mapped colonies, two 250-km blocks and two unique wet marine cells. For example, central-west Antarctic Peninsula region scores averaged **+0.365, +0.888, +0.445** in the three periods; South Shetlands **+1.181, +1.586, +1.093**; and Victoria Land **−1.645, −1.623, −1.642**. These values are aligned to the same full-period source PCA, not refit by era.

The 1993–2020 linear PC1 trend had a positive direction in 11 reporting regions and a negative direction in seven; restricting to regions meeting the above multi-site/multi-block source support yielded **eight positive and five negative**. Reanalysis coverage and geographical structure limit the independence of these regional signs. They do not establish a uniform monotonic warming exposure, a cooling of Antarctica in general, or a biological advance/retreat.

### Penguin breeding assemblages do not show one transferable response to either physical axis

For the original method-consistent co-count sample, the pairwise within-site Pearson associations with the thermal–ice PC1 were only **−0.008** for gentoo/Adélie, **+0.061** for gentoo/chinstrap and **−0.042** for chinstrap/Adélie; the corresponding salinity-PC2 associations were **−0.033, −0.102, and +0.008**. Although the biological dataset comprised 351 real dual-count records, the informative repeated monitoring supported only **11, 20, and nine source colonies** for these three pairs, clustered in **three, five, and three polar blocks**, respectively.

In a descriptive equal-colony-weighted within-site model with **both physical PCs simultaneously**, salinity-PC2 coefficients per global one-standard-deviation score were **+0.068, −0.187, and +0.055**, and the two physical covariates explained only **0.54%, 2.09%, and 0.65%** of within-site original nesting-ratio variance. No universally strong two-axis species-response structure appeared. The gentoo/chinstrap association was the only weak source-specific candidate with a relatively stable negative PC2 direction in the same-source sensitivity excluding original recorded zero nests (PC2 −0.137).

When the source included calendar-year fixed effects as well as colony effects, the gentoo/chinstrap salinity-PC2 coefficient remained negative (**−0.176**), and it remained negative after excluding actual zero nest counts (**−0.083**). Yet the additional physical PC axes explained only **0.48% of the already site-and-year-residualized nesting-ratio variance**. Deleting one original 250-km block at a time kept the salt-PC2 sign negative (−0.254 to −0.007); **deleting an original reporting region reversed it**, with the slope ranging from −0.183 to +0.072. Specifically, excluding the **central-west Antarctic Peninsula** changed the coefficient from −0.176 to **+0.072**; just two source regions remained informative after that deletion. The other species-pair coefficients changed sign under year/zero or geographical sensitivity. Thus even this weak salt-associated signal cannot be declared a continent-wide biological mechanism.

An independent descriptive time-series unit—colonies with both species actually counted in ≥3 seasons spanning ≥10 years—was available at only **seven sites for gentoo/Adélie, 15 for gentoo/chinstrap, and six for chinstrap/Adélie**, each pair confined to **two source regions**. The correlation of source site-level gentoo/chinstrap nesting-ratio slopes with same-census-year salt-PC2 slopes was **−0.196** (region-demeaned −0.158), insufficient to identify a generalized response kernel. Neither source family establishes species immigration, competitive displacement, or time-delayed climate debt.

### Terrestrial positive mapped area is not an annual greening trajectory

The **167 site / eight region** positive-mapped terrestrial union-area subpanel had a descriptive across-site Spearman association of **+0.237** with full-period marine PC1 site means, but the within-region log-area Pearson relationship was **−0.095**. The mapped land observations comprise a 2017–2023 composite of multiple terrestrial biological classes and cannot be interpreted as contemporaneous annually changing vegetation. A prior 34-site positive vegetation-only subset had a larger association, but its mapping class, climate axis and site frame differ; that contrast cannot establish a shared coast-wide greening process.

### Secondary source patterns retained from the original macro paper

The separate original **100 long colony–species series** showed region-demeaned SIC-trend versus log nest-trend correlations of **+0.666 Adélie, −0.513 chinstrap, +0.017 gentoo**, but spatial block-bootstrap intervals for all three crossed zero. The old **173-site positive vegetation mapped cohort** yielded pooled SIC–land-area correlation **−0.384**, versus **+0.097 after reporting-region demeaning**. These are distinct source denominators and observation windows, and they reinforce scale-dependent interpretation rather than adding independent p-values supporting one biological mechanism.

Finally, the separate original observed **fixed same-colony 1990s–2010s nest-weight panel** showed an Adélie source-weighted latitude shift **+88.41 km among 26 strictly revisited site IDs**, but this reflects the relative reallocation of nest weights among *existing surveyed colonies*, not measured individual penguin movement, new colonization or tracking of an ecological sea-ice threshold. Direct original 60%-SIC marine contours showed regionally opposite movements. These supplementary physical and biological geographical descriptors are not a climate-velocity ratio or a measured lag.

## Discussion

### The physical mechanism is not a single ‘subantarcticization’ clock

A strong sea-ice/temperature covariance is real in these source fields, but the originally proposed single warming–freshening–greening–gentoo transition axis does not remain one-dimensional when sampled across all 18 Antarctic reporting regions. Sea-surface salinity provides a second spatial environmental degree of freedom. The sign of its relationship to ice and water temperature varies with the observation universe and whether spatial site means or time anomalies are analysed. That outcome is physically plausible because brine rejection, glacial freshwater, ocean advection and mixing do not imply one globally shared salinity direction. The GLORYS source also assimilates oceanographic information; statistical orthogonality in the source PCA is **not** proof that the mechanisms are causally independent.

### Regional source regimes and actual temporal paths must be distinguished

The 18-region source atlas provides a major advance over interpreting the Antarctic Peninsula as if it represented the full continent. Yet even the original reporting regions vary in their number of colony points and independent ocean cells, with some regions including one or a few source localities. The mid-period PC1 peaks and differing 28-year signed trends show why a latitudinal space-for-time substitution can be misleading. A fixed geographic gradient describes *where* colonies are exposed to warmer, less icy ocean conditions. It does not independently tell us how rapidly, how monotonically, or even in which direction an individual region experiences a given reanalysis-based physical coordinate.

### Penguin numbers can differ without identifying a salinity niche transition

The restricted gentoo/chinstrap PC2 observation is worth retaining as a targeted hypothesis, precisely because we know its modest size and geographic fragility. A consistent sign under single 250-km block deletion, year controls and recorded-zero sensitivity does not counteract the fact that deleting the central-west Antarctic Peninsula reverses the sign of the relationship. Likewise, source sites and year records share climate cells and monitoring histories; strong model residual degrees of freedom under an ordinary linear algebra formula are not independent spatiotemporal experiments. Direct salinity effects on prey or on penguin energetic performance, seabird foraging access, competition, or species dispersal have **not** been measured.

The scientifically relevant departure from the original one-axis hypothesis is consequently not that warming has no effect on penguins. It is that *physically coherent environmental gradients may have geographically nontransferable biological associations*, and that population-count responses cannot be read mechanically from the position of a region on a climate PCA. Candidate mechanisms include phenology, seasonal foraging conditions, prey changes, icebergs and colony access, survey effects and historical demography, but these are external hypotheses rather than outcomes established by the present data.

### The sea–land connection is a separate temporal identification problem

The terrestrial data contain positive detections of vegetation, lichens and algae in one multi-year composite. They can be used to describe where mapped land features co-occur with marine conditions, but not whether marine retreat leads annual greening or whether penguin occupation precedes new vegetation. Published vegetation-expansion estimates for the Antarctic Peninsula have recently been debated in the scientific literature. An independent, source-consistent long-term remote-sensing vegetation series and actual comparably surveyed penguin breeding habitat occupancy would be necessary to test a coastwide sea–land community response sequence.

### Limits, novelty, and inference boundary

The strength of this work is the **original-source alignment and cross-scale analysis** of marine physical observations with genuine colony nests: all 18 Antarctic regions, 28 years, separate salt and thermal-ice coordinates, actual same-year two-species count pairs, and explicit regional, spatial-cell, survey-year and source-zero guards. It is not the first description of Adélie decline, gentoo expansion or a Ross Island colony contrast, and the study cannot isolate greenhouse attribution or the causes of regional salinity changes. The ecological novelty should be framed as the **scale and transfer boundary** between physically constrained dimensions and repeated observed colony assemblage changes.

The study could be improved decisively through actual within-region longitudinally comparable censuses in additional independent Antarctic source regions, biological foraging/marine access measurements, and a temporally explicit land cover archive. Until then, the results justify **no universal species-specific response coefficient, no measured climate-tracking lag, and no 2050/2100 penguin breeding forecast**. Descriptive source calculations and complete model/weight sensitivity are supplied as reproducible analysis records rather than converted into unwarranted inferential certainty.
