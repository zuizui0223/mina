# Spatial reallocation of Antarctic penguin nesting differs from changes in coastal sea ice

**Working manuscript v0.5 (reviewer-checked biology-first revision), 16 November 2026.** Draft PR #214. Original source-backed retrospective observations; **not submitted**. This draft replaces v0.4 as the *reading version*, while v0.4 and all sensitivity reports remain unchanged as source-audit provenance. The paper makes neither future population forecasts nor causal climate-attribution claims.

## Abstract

Changes in Antarctic penguin nest counts are often interpreted alongside sea-ice trends, yet environmental and biological observations may occupy different spatial and temporal domains. We reconstructed **2,456 method-consistent observed nest counts** of Adélie, chinstrap and gentoo penguins at **595 previously catalogued colonies** in 18 reporting regions, retaining missing survey years as missing. At **588 colonies**, 1993–2020 sea-ice, temperature and salinity records yielded a thermal–ice principal component explaining **60.37%** of variance and a separate salinity component explaining **33.38%**, rather than one uniform marine environmental dimension.

Across genuinely revisited Adélie colony networks, nest-count-weighted latitude became **107.31 km (19-site roster)** or **88.41 km (26-site roster)** more southerly between the 1990s and 2010s. This direction survived survey-year and geographic-support sensitivity, but the change in nesting concentration reversed sign between the rosters. Nest-weighted nearshore sea-ice exposure changed by only **+0.022 and +0.625 percentage points**, respectively. The apparent site-equal latitude–nest-change correlation dropped from **+0.754 to +0.108** after original-region centering in the 26-site roster. In two regions, strictly source-matched gentoo/Adélie nest ratios increased at **six of six** colonies, although combined observed two-species nests increased at **five of six**.

Environmental coverage was not biological coverage: six Adélie sites failing the marine wet-grid match represented **68% of source nesting weight** in the three-decade-complete panel, and only **65 colonies in six regions** supported long-term three-variable climate–nest analyses. The apparent strongly synchronized salinity anomalies of 2019–2020 also lacked comparable continent-wide in-situ verification. We identify a robust **reallocation of recorded nesting among fixed known colonies** and local changes in relative species composition, but not a transferable climate-response coefficient, individual migration, or a climate-driven future nesting forecast.

**Keywords:** *Pygoscelis adeliae*; coastal hydrography; colony census; hierarchical spatial variation; observer coverage; climate-niche inference.

## 1. Introduction

Marine change can alter food access, the costs of commuting to breeding sites and the relative fortunes of co-occurring seabirds. Yet a change in nest counts at fixed colonies is not synonymous with a change in the breeding range, relocation of identifiable birds or movement of a climatic niche. Moreover, broad latitudinal gradients may reflect large differences between ocean sectors rather than a consistent response within each region. Antarctic penguins offer a direct test of these observational distinctions because the landscape of documented colonies is spatially extensive but their repeated, comparable nest counts are geographically uneven.

Regional Adélie (*Pygoscelis adeliae*) population differences are already established: the Antarctic Peninsula has declined while parts of Victoria Land and East Antarctica have increased (Lynch & LaRue 2014). More recent work has documented actual gentoo colonization of newly exposed terrain (Cimino et al. 2026) and geographically uneven reproductive responses of Adélie and gentoo penguins on Ardley Island (Machado-Gaye et al. 2025). Broader distribution models project suitable marine habitat for multiple penguin species (Ramírez et al. 2026), but occurrence-derived projections are not counts of breeding nests. Therefore, the ecological task is not to claim newly discovered regional trends or actual continent-wide migration. We distinguish (i) changes in nesting weights among fixed known colonies, (ii) changes in local relative species composition, and (iii) changes in original nearshore marine measurements, using their true observational supports. These quantities need not move in concert.

We used three **operational observational contrasts**, not three mutually exclusive climate-causal hypotheses. First, we compared the statistical dimensions of near-colony sea ice, temperature and salinity, while separating spatial gradients from changes through time. Second, within genuinely revisited colony rosters, we independently calculated nest-count-weighted latitude, nest-count-weighted *measured nearshore sea-ice exposure* and the relationship between source-site latitude and count change within versus across reporting regions. Actual climate-niche tracking could preserve an existing exposure rather than increase it: neither a small exposure change nor a divergent latitude trend constitutes a decisive test of tracking. Third, for strictly co-recorded species pairs we distinguished changes in their log nest ratios from changes in the observed sum of both species' nests. These are arithmetic and geographic contrasts with distinct denominators, **not** confirmation that environmental forcing caused, failed to cause or was compensated by changes in breeding.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Original biological census records and observation domains

We used source-audited Mapping Application for Penguin Populations and Projected Dynamics (MAPPPD)-derived original colony identifiers, their geographic coordinates and count-source citations. For each colony and species we retained the most frequently used known census method, obtaining **2,456 genuine site–species–season observed nest counts** from **595 known colony identifiers in 18 original reporting regions** (original observation years vary widely from 1892 to 2025). We never recoded a missing species, an unsurveyed colony or an unobserved year as zero nests. An actually recorded zero was retained.

This is a frozen **three-species, method-selected analytical subset**, not the entirety of the living MAPPPD collection, whose October 2026 v5.0 interface lists 729 count-bearing sites and 5,487 counts across six species. Different derived panels have different eligibility criteria. A repeated census-method label does **not** guarantee matched survey date, complete colony footprint, observer effort or phenological stage. A ground–drone comparison at 12 Antarctic Peninsula colonies found material differences among some contemporaneous nest counts (Hilton et al. 2026). Thus even within-site same-method trends remain vulnerable to changing coverage and breeding phenology; reselecting observed years does not remove these biases.

### 2.2 Marine physical observations, reanalysis and static terrestrial cover

We used original EUMETSAT OSI SAF OSI-450-a1 v3.1 native sea-ice scenes for **August–November 1991–2020** and each known colony's fixed original 100-km nearshore set of eligible source pixels. The complete 595-site seasonal source panel contains **71,400 site–month** records. For surface potential seawater temperature and practical salinity we used Copernicus Marine GLORYS12V1 (original 202311 source) for **August–November 1993–2020**. A fixed, source-validated maximum **25-km geodesic distance to the nearest wet marine grid cell** retained 588 colony sites, across all 18 reporting regions, but only **378 distinct wet reanalysis cells**. The common balanced three-variable physical panel contains 588 × 28 = **16,464 observed-source site–years**.

We standardized the three full-panel seasonal source variables and performed a single fixed PCA across all 16,464 site–years. PC1 was signed as warmer/lower ice, PC2 as higher surface salinity. We checked original geographical region weighting and unique wet-grid-cell weighting separately from the site-weighted PCA. We also compared 1993–2000, 2001–2010 and 2011–2020 without refitting the axis at each era. The original Copernicus quality document records the atmospheric forcing change from ERA-Interim to ERA5 at the end of 2018 / beginning of 2019; to avoid interpreting a potentially source-affected salinity common-time mode as an independent Antarctic physical discovery, we repeated the fixed-source temporal diagnostics without 2019–2020. Separate UK Met Office EN4.2.2 original raw 2017–2020 profile records provided a real-data support audit, not a substitute for genuine matched 18-region field salinity.

Available Plantarctica terrestrial vegetation/lichen/algal mapping was used **only as a static 2017–2023 composite**. It does not provide a repeated-measure estimate of greening and is not used in a dynamic mediation or causal marine-to-land model.

### 2.3 Geographical nesting redistribution

For each species we assembled the **same observed colony IDs** in the first (1991–2000) and final (2011–2020) decade, with each site's decade-median *actually observed* nest count as its weight. We set north–south distance to 111.195 km per degree latitude and defined the nest-count-weighted change as the difference in the fixed-site weighted mean latitude, with a positive value denoting more southerly reweighting. Thus, the location of each original colony remains *fixed*. As source-support sensitivity, we compared (i) 19 Adélie sites with at least two genuine census years in all three decades, (ii) 26 with at least two years in both endpoint decades, and (iii) 35 with at least one in both endpoints. The rosters are *nested*, not independent continental replicates.

We decomposed the changing fixed-site weighted latitude into between-reporting-region changes in observed nest shares and within-region reweighting. A separate descriptive EPSG:3031 block identity allocated within-region change to between 250-km blocks versus within blocks; this attribution varies with the grid width and origin and is not an intrinsic ecological interaction scale. The original site–year counts were reselected within source colony and decade, and single observed years, sites, 250-km blocks and regions were omitted as pre-specified source-support diagnostics. Conditional simulation quantiles do **not** estimate Antarctic population confidence intervals.

We also computed the site-equal Pearson correlation between south latitude and log[1 + last decade-median nests] minus log[1 + first decade-median nests], first pooled and then centered within each original source region. This differs intentionally from a nest-count-weighted centroid. Original annual observed nearshore sea-ice concentrations were aggregated into the same original source decade windows and decomposed via the exact symmetric two-factor identity into physical SIC changes and changing source nest weights. The source 60%-SIC offshore isopleth was described by fixed 10° longitude sectors, *not* treated as a biological habitat threshold or individual route.

### 2.4 Species-pair composition and three-species nesting subtotals

To avoid false absences or mixing separate reporters, a strict species-pair comparison required counts for both species in the **same site, season, original source citation and method**, with at least five genuinely observed common years over a ≥10-year span in 1993–2020. The resulting 18 site–pair trajectories occurred at 12 known colonies in two original reporting regions (183 observed pair–site–years, with species-pair overlap). We fitted each site's observed log(1 + second-species nests) minus log(1 + first-species nests) slope per decade, and checked first/last actual-year exclusions. We separately calculated both constituent species' trends and the observed *sum* of their nests; the latter is not fixed by a species ratio. At three colonies with complete genuine same-year observations of all three species, 34 site–years allowed an exact three-species subtotal and relative gentoo share. Pair comparisons sharing the same colony and census year are not independent replicates.

### 2.5 Limits on inferring marine drivers from nesting changes

Finally, we considered 85 separately reconstructed long-term site–species trends at 65 original colonies in six regions with genuinely matching GLORYS/OSI SAF years and sufficient census support. Thermal–ice PC1 and salinity PC2 entered the same original-source-region-adjusted nest-trend regression, accompanied by post-result geographic leave-region and 250-km block-resampling sensitivity. This is descriptive; six different coefficients are not independent biological experiments. We verified that six repeatedly counted Adélie colonies on the Ross Sea/Victoria Land side were rejected by the 25-km GLORYS marine wet-cell gate and quantified the *nest-count-weight*, not merely the number of sites, lost by that choice. Environmental decades beginning in 1993 and nesting-count decades beginning in 1991 are explicitly different temporal denominators.

No climate-only future nest forecast, new species absence, habitat suitability shift, delayed causal response, or statistical significance result is claimed from these comparisons.

### 2.6 Source-pinned future marine conditions and historical nesting-weight exposure

To quantify how the geographic distribution of genuinely observed nests affects exposure to a changing marine climate *without predicting future nests*, we expanded a previously validated original CMIP6 extractor from 106 study coordinates to the same **595 catalogued colony coordinates**. The original **27 model Zarr stores**, three models (ACCESS-CM2, BCC-CSM2-MR, CESM2; one source-fixed member each), three original physical variables and each model's historical wet-cell selection were fixed without changing the extraction algorithm. We retained August–November and the **1995–2014 model-historical baseline**, contrasted with SSP2-4.5/SSP5-8.5 during 2041–2060 and 2081–2100. The 595-site output contained 21,420 genuine future model/variable/site records and 7,140 site–scenario/variable three-model summaries; all complete-year checks passed. Model surface-air temperature (tas) is **not** the observed marine potential seawater temperature (thetao) used in the physical PCA, and was not substituted into that PCA.

For biological weighting, we selected all real 2011–2020 count years within the frozen MAPPPD source and calculated a true observed nest-count median **separately for each site×species**, then summed these three-species medians at each source site. This provides a fixed 2011–2020 observational nesting-weight proxy, **not** an actual synchronous community nest census nor a future number of nests. Missing species or years were not set to zero. We compared each climate scenario's three-model mean change with (i) equal site weight across all 595 known sites, (ii) equal site weight across 271 genuinely counted sites, (iii) observed nest-median weight across those same 271 sites, and (iv) nest-median weight among 158 counted sites whose original ocean grid centers for SIC and salinity were ≤50 km from each colony in all three models. The spatial-support check refers to grid precision, not bird foraging range. We also reported species-specific weighting, reporting-region deletion, and three-model ranges. All are **physical scenario exposures**, never fitted biological warming-response coefficients or protection-area fractions.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Two physical marine dimensions and heterogeneous temporal histories

The standardized full-panel marine PCA had **PC1 = 60.365%**, with SIC loading −0.707 and surface temperature +0.706, and **PC2 = 33.377%**, with salinity loading +0.998. Actual 16,464 site–year source correlations were SIC–temperature **r = −0.810**, SIC–salinity **r = +0.054**, and temperature–salinity **r = +0.010**. The spatial environmental gradient therefore did not reduce to simultaneous monotone sea-ice loss, warming and freshening. The PCA split survived withholding the final two years (**1993–2018: PC1 60.15%, PC2 33.49%**).

With fixed full-period axis scores, **13 of 18** reporting regions showed a higher mean thermal–ice PC1 during 2001–2010 than in both earlier and later periods (10/13 under a geographical replication support gate). Full-period linear PC1 slopes were positive in 11 regions and negative in seven; these are not 18 independent climate-forcing experiments. A pronounced original GLORYS salinity PC2 region-common annual fraction of **52.14%** during 1993–2020 fell to **29.29%** if 2019–2020 were withheld. The original 2019/2020 salinity drops coincided with the recorded change of reanalysis atmospheric forcing; a completed original 2017–2020 EN4 profile-source check had **2,414 eligible upper-10-m near-colony profiles**, but **zero of 18 regions** with adequate four-year, matched-month/multiple-platform sampling. Thus a genuinely continental physical freshening event is **not confirmed**. We retain salinity as a **source-based spatial dimension**, not as an independently validated universal temporal trend or identified breeding driver.

### 3.2 Southerly Adélie nest reweighting without confirmed sea-ice tracking

From the 1990s to 2010s, nest-count-weighted mean latitude became more southerly by **+107.31 km at 19 Adélie colonies**, **+88.41 km at 26** and **+99.60 km at 35**. In the strict 26-colony set, deleting one full source reporting region retained **+65.61 to +102.81 km**; deleting a 250-km block retained **+38.89 to +125.83 km** (ranges are deletion diagnostics, not CIs). Across **20,000 within-colony observed-census-year re-selections**, the original 19-, 26- and 35-colony estimate remained positive in every simulation. The same directional result did not hold for the geographically restricted gentoo 12-site panel.

The geography of reweighting was not a consistent within-region south-latitude growth slope. Across the 26 sites, the pooled equal-site south-latitude versus log nest-change correlation was **r = +0.754**, but the source-region-demeaned correlation was only **+0.108** (19- and 35-site versions: +0.788 vs −0.053, and +0.716 vs +0.070). The strong pooled covariance was therefore a regional-composition result, not independent evidence that each degree of latitude had a transferable growth effect. Within the strict 26-site set, 19 Victoria Land colonies accounted for most surveyed source nesting weight and increased their summed decade-median counts from **599,007 to 977,995**; each of the six central-west Peninsula and South Shetland source sites declined. These are observed-site subtotals, *not* a complete continental population trend.

Across the strict 26 sites, the centroid shift partitioned as **+32.84 km between reporting regions** and **+55.57 km within regions**; most of the latter occurred among source 250-km polar blocks. The decomposition in the 19-site cohort differed (+65.48/+41.83 km), and the interpretation of a block-total mathematical bound ceased to hold for all shifted origins at grid widths ≥400 km. The geographic *sign* is better supported than any intrinsic 250-km biological scale.

Importantly, the same original fixed biological rosters' nest-weighted nearshore SIC exposure changed by only **+0.022 percentage points** (19 sites) and **+0.625** (26), even though their weighted geography shifted south. For the fixed Victoria Land subset alone, source nests reweighted 48–60 km southerly while corresponding source nearshore SIC **decreased by 0.53–0.72 percentage points**, contrary to a universal expectation that geographic southerly change is a direct increase in observed nearshore ice exposure. A fixed three-decade Adélie 19-site panel also showed +99.55 km southerly reweighting in the first decade transition and +7.76 km in the next, whereas original marine 60%-ice isopleth comparisons in the five associated longitude sectors were +7.10 km and +31.81 km, respectively. These non-equivalent metrics have different phases; no individual bird movement, biological climatic debt or climate tracking rate can be inferred.

### 3.3 Local breeding-assemblage composition changes without a fixed nesting subtotal

In the strict same-source/same-method long-term panel, the gentoo/Adélie nest ratio increased at **6/6 monitored colonies**, with a median log-ratio slope of **+0.882 per decade**; Adélie nesting slopes were negative and gentoo slopes positive at all six. Yet **five of six** observed two-species nesting subtotals increased between the first and last genuinely paired census. The six source colonies had **four decreasing and two increasing** corresponding nearshore SIC trends, showing that monotonically decreasing SIC at every colony was not a necessary observed condition for this particular relative species-ratio shift. Gentoo/chinstrap ratios increased in 7/9 and chinstrap/Adélie ratios in 3/3 sites, but those other comparisons share sites and have narrow geographic support.

In the more restrictive three-colony and **34 genuine three-species site–year** panel, gentoo nesting share rose at Ardley, Lion's Rump and PCHA, but observed three-species nesting subtotals changed by **+37.67%, −25.25%, and +27.72%**, respectively. Relative dominance and local subtotal abundance thus measure different aspects of recorded reassembly. No inference is made that gentoo penguins directly displaced Adélie birds from the same physical nest locations, or that all three-species counts cover unsurveyed birds.

### 3.4 Physical coverage does not imply biological climate-response identifiability

Among the 588 climate-eligible original site locations, the long-term three-variable breeding-change test was limited to **85 site–species series, 65 true colonies and six reporting regions**. All six source-region-adjusted two-PC nest-trend regression coefficients had 250-km block-resampling intervals spanning zero. A nominal single-axis Adélie salinity-PC2 correlation changed sign when thermal–ice PC1 entered the same model. Using full 28-year marine slopes rather than actual census-year marine slopes altered the same original biological contrasts. No geographically transferable, time-aligned climate coefficient has therefore been established.

Source matching itself materially changed biological inference support. In the original 19-site three-decade-complete Adélie colony roster, six sites failing the GLORYS ≤25-km marine wet-cell gate accounted for **68.02% of the 2010s observed nesting weight**; the same six accounted for **33.98%** in the 26-site endpoint cohort. Restricting to GLORYS-eligible sites changed the apparent southerly nest reweighting from **+107.31 to +69.09 km** (19-site cohort) and **+88.41 to +38.89 km** (26-site cohort). Within this restricted marine-source subpanel, the fixed physical thermal–ice score and the source-weight redistribution terms had opposing signs in a symmetric accounting decomposition, but region deletion could reverse estimated component directions. These selected records do not establish an adaptive preference for colder, higher-ice sites.

### 3.5 Source-nest-weighted climate exposure contrasts differ from site-equal model means

The complete fixed-source CMIP6 projection now provided the original three-model August–November physical changes at **all 595 known colony coordinates** (18 reporting regions). Genuine source counts in 2011–2020 were available at **271 distinct sites** (331 site–species median nest records; 16 regions). Their non-synchronous three-species source median sum was 2,848,329.5 nest units for weighting only. Of these sites, **158** satisfied the all-three-model marine-cell ≤50-km source proximity condition and represented **80.21% of the counted median-nest weight**; this high weight fraction does not imply full biological population coverage.

Under **SSP5-8.5 in 2081–2100**, model-mean SIC change was **−21.44 percentage points** across all 595 equal-weight source sites and **−28.63 pp** across the 271 genuinely counted equal-weight sites, but only **−13.28 pp** when those exact 271 sites were weighted by the observed source nest medians. Restricting to the 158 spatially closer marine-source sites yielded **−19.80 pp equal-site** versus **−11.77 pp nest-weighted**. The same 271 recorded nest weights yielded a +3.74°C model-mean **surface-air** temperature change (+4.19°C equal-site); they do not indicate reduced physiological or demographic warming sensitivity. For all four SSP/period combinations, source-nest weighting lowered the magnitude of model-mean SIC loss relative to equal weight over the same 271 sites. In the end-century SSP5-8.5 scenario, the source-nest-weighted SIC changes differed by counted species (Adélie **−11.26 pp**, chinstrap **−17.53 pp**, gentoo **−34.84 pp**); these are consequences of species-specific surveyed geography, not fitted trait responses.

The observed nesting weights were highly geographically concentrated: Victoria Land contributed **49.46%** of the 271-site source median-nest sum but had only **−2.09 pp** nest-weighted model SIC change. Excluding that original reporting region altered the 271-site weighted estimate to **−24.23 pp**. Original three-model estimates on the 271 sites under SSP5-8.5/end-century ranged from **−9.89 to −19.76 pp** when weighted; this is model disagreement, not an ecological confidence interval. The physical results cannot verify a transferable climate–growth dose-response coefficient from a different proposed 265-series census cohort. See the [complete source-pinned exposure audit](climate_risk_frozen_20261116/CMIP6_595_COUNT_WEIGHTED_PHYSICAL_EXPOSURE_RESULT_JA_20261116.md).

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 What the observations establish

The best-supported biological finding is a *conditional reorganization of nesting abundance among already known and revisited colonies*. The southerly sign of Adélie nest-weighted reallocation persists across nested original census rosters, source-year re-selection and selected geographic deletions. However, it arises through changes in the shares of existing colony networks, rather than measured changes in colony coordinates, individual dispersal or a complete biological range. This distinction matters especially for colony-breeding birds: abundance reallocation changes where most recorded nests occur even if no new physical site has appeared, while its relation to local population growth, nest-patch occupancy or colonization remains unidentified.

The second biological finding is that local species-composition changes need not sum to a fixed number of nesting pairs. Source-matched gentoo gains relative to Adélie persisted across six monitored colonies, including locations without declining local SIC, and the local two-species nest subtotal often rose. This does not disprove climatic influences; it argues against replacing geographically explicit environmental and demographic processes with an assumed one-dimensional ice-driven species substitution.

### 4.2 Why spatial physical gradients cannot be directly transferred into temporal population change

The original environmental census is far broader than the matched repeated biological census, and high mapped *site* coverage is not high *nest-count weight* coverage. The loss of six major Ross Sea source colonies from GLORYS spatial matching illustrates why apparent marine-climate explanatory variables can be poorly aligned with the biology they ostensibly explain. Sea-ice measurements near a colony are not realized individual foraging routes. Within-region and between-region temporal changes are also not exchangeable: the strong full-cohort latitude–growth correlation diminished almost entirely after source-region centering.

The fully source-pinned 595-colony CMIP6 extension makes an additional conservation-relevant distinction measurable: in the actually counted 271 sites, the site-equal model-mean future sea-ice reduction is much larger than the reduction averaged under fixed observed nest-count weights. This geographic contrast persists within the all-three-model ≤50-km marine-grid cohort, but nearly half the observed median nest weights occur in one reporting region, Victoria Land. Thus **nest concentration can coincide with reduced sea-ice-loss exposure in this model panel without establishing demographic buffering, biological refugia or protected foraging areas**. Air warming remains substantial and model spread is considerable. This result concerns the current spatial composition of recorded nests held fixed under future model climate; the cross-sectional marine gradient cannot be multiplied by a region-confounded nesting-growth coefficient to generate future survival or abundance.

A predominantly salinity environmental axis can be a stable geometric feature of a marine reanalysis without establishing a real continent-wide synchronized freshening episode. The GLORYS 2019–2020 forcing-era sensitivity and failure of comparable EN4 in-situ coverage rule out using this temporal mode as a well-confirmed salinity driver. Conversely, the absence of a transferable climate coefficient in our restricted nesting panels is a problem of sampling support and causal identification, not evidence that marine climate has no effects on penguins.

### 4.3 Macroecological novelty, prior knowledge and bounded generality

Regional differences in Adélie population trajectories are **not new** (Lynch & LaRue 2014), and colony abundance can respond to local processes beyond ice: Cape Royds dynamics have been interpreted in relation to icebergs, open-water access and ocean circulation (Ainley et al. 2024), while landscape-driven snow and wind influence subcolony persistence near Palmer Station (Cimino et al. 2025). The new contribution is the narrower integration of original site identities: source-fixed count-weighted geography, a distinct accounting of nearshore sea-ice exposure, pooled versus within-region latitude associations, and exact source-matched paired nest counts separating species composition from nest subtotals. These observational decompositions do not independently test the prior ecological mechanisms. The physical two-axis analysis provides a broad, source-defined environmental frame; our biological analyses show **where the available data do not identify a transferable climate-response coefficient**, without implying that climate-niche tracking is biologically absent.

This is not a global estimate of habitat loss or island metapopulation dynamics. A monitored colony is not the same unit as a within-island nesting patch. The previously documented contraction of breeding components at Palmer and Signy can motivate theory about nesting aggregation but is **not pooled quantitatively** with a continent-wide effective number of colony sites. In the present records the latter's direction reverses with the inclusion of a small number of very large sampled colonies; no common abundance-decline-to-concentration law is established across spatial grains.

### 4.4 Next necessary evidence

To distinguish climatic forcing from demographic inertia, prey limitation, breeding-space changes, colony social processes and uneven censusing requires genuinely comparable census programs across independently sampled Antarctic regions, matched to the relevant foraging season and marine exposure. To test regional salinity change requires independent upper-ocean profile time series with comparable season, depth, location and instrument coverage, not interpolation of unsampled cells. To test greening as a dynamic terrestrial mechanism requires repeated land observations rather than a single composite map. These are future external evidence requirements, not parameters that can be recovered by repeatedly fitting this same frozen observational panel.

## 5. Conclusions

At recorded Antarctic penguin breeding colonies, **marine climate has distinguishable physical dimensions; Adélie nesting weights have become more southerly in a restricted, consistently observed colony network; and locally increasing gentoo relative nesting does not imply a uniform change in all monitored nests**. These statements concern different physical and biological observational domains. Neither a continent-wide southward climate-niche tracking rate nor a warming-driven demographic forecast follows from their combination. The central macroecological lesson is that geographical nesting reallocation, relative species composition and realized local marine conditions must be resolved at compatible spatial and temporal scales before interpreting their apparent correspondence as a climatic response.
