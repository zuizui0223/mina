## Abstract

A spatial marine gradient does not automatically describe the temporal change of a breeding colony. We assembled **2,456 method-consistent original observations of nests** of Adélie, chinstrap and gentoo penguins at **595 Antarctic breeding colonies in 18 reporting regions**, and linked their known sites to 1991–2020 OSI SAF sea ice. Of these, 588 colonies had coastal GLORYS12V1 salinity and temperature reanalysis support, providing **16,464 source colony–years (1993–2020)**. A fixed standardized marine PCA resolved a thermal–ice dimension (**PC1 60.37%, SIC −0.707 and seawater temperature +0.706**) and a separate surface-salinity dimension (**PC2 33.38%, salinity +0.998**). This separation persisted when 2019–2020 observations were excluded (PC1 **60.15%**, PC2 **33.49%**).

The previously striking 18-region salinity PC2 common-calendar-year fraction (**52.14%**; thermal–ice PC1 **19.71%**) declined to **29.29%** (PC1 **20.69%**) after omitting 2019–2020. Original GLORYS salinity was below the pre-2019 mean in all 18 source reporting regions in both years, and in 368/378 and 363/378 distinct ocean cells, respectively. The source publisher documents a coincident change from ERA-Interim to ERA5 forcing in January 2019, but its relationship to the derived salinity pattern is not established. A completed 2017–2020 original EN4 raw-observation audit obtained 2,414 qualifying upper-10-m, ≤100-km profiles, but none of the 18 regions had adequate matched month/instrument coverage in all four years; strong temporal salinity synchrony is therefore held as an **unverified, source-dependent observation**, unlike the stable descriptive spatial PCA.

Across fixed genuinely observed Adélie colony rosters in the 1990s and 2010s, original nest-count-weighted mean latitude shifted **88–107 km south** in the strict 19- and 26-site panels despite observation-cohort-dependent, even opposite changes in nesting concentration. Nest-weighted nearshore SIC changed only **+0.022 and +0.625 percentage points**, respectively, so relative southern reweighting is not demonstrated sea-ice tracking. Biological associations had narrower support: **85** matched colony–species long-term series at **65 colonies in six regions**, with spatial-block intervals overlapping zero for all region-adjusted two-PC nest-trend coefficients, and **18** strictly shared-source colony–species-pair series from only **12 colonies in two regions**. Gentoo-to-Adélie observed nest ratios rose at six of six strictly paired colonies, whereas two-species nest subtotals rose at five of six. Among 34 genuine common three-species colony–years from three colonies, gentoo nesting share rose at all three, but three-species nesting subtotals rose at two and fell at one. Thus relative breeding-assemblage reweighting and total observed nests are separable, without demonstrating an Antarctic-wide climatic response or a causal species displacement. Separately, the Adélie effective number of monitored colony sites fell **29.98%** in a 19-site three-decade cohort even as its sitewise median nest-count sum rose **78.48%**, but the concentration direction reversed to **+8.15%** in a 26-site endpoint-only cohort containing seven extra genuinely observed sites. Census-roster selection and large-colony leverage therefore preclude a universal Antarctic concentration claim. Contemporary mapped vegetation, lichens and algae are a **static** terrestrial comparison, not a temporal greening record. These source-backed results expose an important mismatch between broad physical climate coverage and the geographically restricted ecological records required to test its temporal biological transfer.

**Keywords:** Antarctic marine macroecology; Pygoscelis; breeding assemblage; marine climate dimensionality; sea-ice concentration; salinity; source-year sensitivity; ecological observation support; regional transfer.

## Introduction

A common conceptual account of warming Antarctica links decreasing sea ice, freshwater influx, expansion of ice-free terrestrial habitats, and shifts from ice-associated to subantarctic-associated organisms. Although these processes can coincide, they operate through different physical mechanisms and are observed at different spatial and temporal grains. Sea-ice freezing can reject brine into seawater, melting can dilute the surface ocean, and ocean transport and vertical mixing may alter either tendency. A regional association between low sea ice and reduced salinity consequently does not by itself demonstrate that local sea ice and salinity will change together over decades. Nor does a spatially warmer marine environment imply that a penguin colony is experiencing a directly measurable climate-driven change in its breeding population.

This distinction is fundamental for colonial seabirds. Penguin nest counts are available only for particular surveyed breeding sites and years, with uneven coverage across Antarctica. The absence of a colony–species record does not establish ecological absence, and nest-count ratios from the same nominal breeding season do not necessarily have exactly matched survey dates, observer effort or source citations. Closely spaced colonies also often share the same satellite pixels or ocean reanalysis cells, so even large numbers of records may represent only a few effectively independent spatial environments. We therefore need to establish which patterns are physically source-supported and which biological observations can be compared at the same observational unit before interpreting a broad warming-to-biota narrative.

Here we examine two connected but distinct questions. **First**, do sea-ice concentration, surface salinity, and potential seawater temperature form one Antarctic-wide spatial and temporal marine axis, or do they occupy multiple environmental dimensions with heterogeneous regional histories? **Second**, where the three pygoscelid penguins were genuinely counted together at one colony in one breeding season, do changes in their relative observed nests correspond to either of the physically identified marine dimensions after controlling for colony and shared survey-year structure? As an intentionally separate terrestrial subpanel, we ask whether source-mapped positive terrestrial polygon area follows the same geographic marine gradient, without treating that static map as annual greening. The work is **exploratory, source-verified macroecology**, not a search for post-hoc significant lag lengths or a climate-driven demographic forecast.

## Materials and Methods

### Original Antarctic colony and survey cohorts

Original Antarctic Penguin Biogeography/MAPPPD-derived site–species–season observations were reconstructed using source-pinned GIS and count files, with nonnegative true observed nest counts and the most frequently used known original census method retained for each species at each original colony site. This retained **2,456 observed counts for three species across 595 unique colony-site identifiers in 18 original reporting regions**; source years span 1892–2025. We do not treat unrecorded species, missing years, or nonselected methods as biological absences or zero nest counts. To describe longer-term species-specific trends we also retain the separately source-verified **100 site–species series at 77 sites** with ≥5 actually observed census years over ≥10 years during 1991–2020. This biological cohort is not identical to the environmental atlas of 595 localities.

Geographic dependence was audited using the original 250-km polar projected source grid and original source-region labels. Neither blocks nor region labels are biological islands or independent randomized climate treatments. Actual repeated observations at a single colony and multiple colonies sharing marine cells were not counted as separate ecological experiments in interpretation.

### Sea ice, reanalysis hydrography, and exact temporal matching

Original EUMETSAT OSI SAF OSI-450-a1 v3.1 Southern EASE2 monthly 25-km gridded satellite sea-ice concentration was obtained for all 595 breeding-locality coordinates for August–November each year over **1991–2020**, using the fixed original September 1993 valid marine pixel mask and a 100-km nearshore set of native ocean grid cells. The full month table comprises **71,400 colony–months**; publisher NetCDF source SHA-256s, geolocation, marine status flags, and monthly site means were independently verified.

Original Copernicus Marine GLORYS12V1 202311 reanalysis supplied sea-surface practical salinity (source variable *so*) and sea-surface potential seawater temperature (*thetao*). A source fixed September 2011 wet-ocean mask was mapped to all 595 original breeding sites using WGS84 geodesic nearest-cell distances, with a **maximum eligible cell-to-colony distance of 25 km**. This yielded **588 source sites in all 18 reporting regions** (378 distinct wet reanalysis cells); seven sites were excluded without imputation. The original 40-site grid map and 34 source-eligible cell identities were reproduced, followed by independent numerical reconstruction of all 1,360 2011–2020 original salinity and temperature site–months at those 34 sites to machine precision. Reanalysis source data for the 588 sites are complete for August–November 1993–2020: **65,856 authentic colony–months**, giving **16,464 authentic colony–years** once four complete original SIC, salinity and thetao months are aggregated. This primary physical panel contains the same 588 localities in all 28 years; it contains no fabricated salinity before 1993 or interpolated missing penguin counts. GLORYS is a data-assimilating model reanalysis rather than independently observed in situ salinity, and these coastal point exposures are not measured penguin foraging ranges.

### Marine environmental axes and region–time separation

We standardized source SIC, GLORYS surface salinity and GLORYS water temperature across the fully observed **588 site × 28-year** matrix and fit PCA on the three-dimensional source observations. PC1 was oriented so that its seawater-temperature loading is positive, and PC2 so that its salinity loading is positive; these sign conventions do not assert physical causal directions. We reported all three explained fractions, loadings and pairwise original-variable correlations. We decomposed PC1 score variance between and within original reporting regions. To assess geographic dependence, PCA was repeated with **one source region–year per row**, and with **one original wet marine grid cell–year per row**; repeated site–years from the same marine cell were not treated as independent in that sensitivity. A distinct temporal PCA removed each source site's 28-year mean of each physical variable before standardizing and refitting PCA. Thus the spatial PC1 cannot be interpreted as a common temporal warming axis unless temporal structure is also supported.

For each source reporting region we retained the fixed full-PCA PC1 loading and calculated its means in **1993–2000 (eight years), 2001–2010 (ten years), and 2011–2020 (ten years)** and its 1993–2020 28-year OLS slope. The unequal length of the early era was explicitly retained. Deleting one calendar year at a time provided a descriptive sensitivity of each regional trend; we did not choose different calendar cutoffs according to the response. Reports include colony counts, unique source wet cells and geographic blocks for every region. These trend summaries are descriptions of reanalysis climate at known breeding localities, not proof of anthropogenic climate attribution.

### Original marine regime synchrony and source-grid sampling

We independently reconstructed all fixed physical PC1 site–year scores (maximum source numerical difference 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁵) and salinity-positive PC2 scores from the fully aligned, source-verified 588-colony × 28-year environmental matrix. Within each of 18 original reporting regions, annual PC1 and PC2 were aggregated in two ways: **one vote per original observed colony** and **one vote per unique GLORYS wet ocean cell**, first averaging all original colony scores assigned to that cell. We verified all 504 original reporting-region–year physical PC1 means and source SIC, salinity and seawater-temperature means against the prior archived source to numerical precision. Thirteen regions had at least three actual source colony sites, two original 250-km blocks and two distinct original wet ocean cells, forming an a priori geographical support sensitivity based only on source availability.

For each axis and region subset, we centered the **28 actual annual regional scores** on their own region-specific 1993–2020 mean and standardized by their 28-year temporal standard deviation. The fraction of the regional standardized anomaly sum of squares represented by the equal-direction cross-region **common calendar-year mean** summarized synchronous same-sign fluctuations. We separately computed the first temporal empirical orthogonal function (EOF1) variance fraction from the full 28-year × region anomaly matrix, reporting positive and negative EOF1 regional loadings to prevent an anti-synchronous mode from being misread as universal concordance. The median off-diagonal between-region annual correlation was used as another descriptive comparison. **These temporal-coherence fractions are not the marine three-variable PCA explained-variance fractions** and must not be numerically conflated.

A reproducible **1,000-shift source phase-reference** (fixed seed 20261112) circularly rotated each region's observed 28-year series independently, preserving the within-region values and cyclic series structure but destroying the original alignment of years across regions. We compared observed common-year and EOF fractions with those reference distributions as a **diagnostic**, not a formal causal or spatially independent p-value. After seeing the initial result, we added a clearly labeled post-result sensitivity subtracting each region's 28-year linear OLS trend before restandardizing and recomputing the same metrics. This distinguishes common long-term co-trending from non-monotonic short-term synchronization. Such detrending can also discard genuine low-frequency climate variation; it is not uniquely a causal climate residual.

### Post-result 2019–2020 physical source-year diagnostic

We repeated the fixed 18-region and source-supported 13-region annual common-year calculation after omitting the last **two consecutive years, 2019–2020**, from the original GLORYS physical source. Each region's annual PC1/PC2 scores were recentered and standardized **within the retained window** (1993–2018; separately 2011–2018); therefore the window contrasts are descriptive and have different time supports. Both site-equal and unique-ocean-cell-equal within-region aggregation were retained. A separate implementation verified the common-year fraction using the algebraic identity with average pairwise region correlation, to tolerance <1e−12.

For each original 378 wet GLORYS ocean cells, we measured the actual 2019/2020 August–November salinity departure from that cell's 1993–2018 source mean, then computed the same audit for all 18 source region means. We also refit the original three-variable climate PCA on 588 geographically unchanged colony sites across 1993–2018. This is a **post-result source QA**, not a new independent observation or a climate-driven penguin response test. The [GLORYS12V1 2023 quality document](https://documentation.marine.copernicus.eu/QUID/CMEMS-GLO-QUID-001-030.pdf), Sections II.1 and V, records a change in atmospheric forcing from ERA-Interim through 2018 to ERA5 beginning January 2019. The forcing change coincides with a source-pattern shift but is **not proven to cause it**. Detailed numerical source provenance is retained in [the 2019–2020 diagnostic](GLORYS_2019_2020_SOURCE_GATE_JA_20261115.md).

### External raw in-situ salinity coverage gate

To evaluate whether the physically strong reanalysis-salinity temporal coherence could be externally verified from genuine observed ocean measurements, we performed a separate original-profile **coverage and quality-control diagnostic**, not a retrospective hypothesis test. We accessed the original publisher UK Met Office Hadley Centre EN4.2.2 **raw monthly quality-controlled temperature/salinity profile NetCDF records**, not the objectively analyzed 1° EN4 gridded salinity, for August–November **2015**. The official 727-MB annual ZIP was accessed using verified HTTP 206 byte ranges to retrieve only the four monthly original files; the publisher ZIP per-member CRC32s and reconstructed monthly NetCDF SHA256 hashes were archived. The whole annual ZIP SHA256 was **not** computed. The raw profile data were not redistributed under their noncommercial licence.

Each valid profile was assigned once to its nearest geographically eligible original colony, using ellipsoidal WGS84 distance rather than counting it repeatedly at every colony. To qualify as an observed upper-surface salinity datum, latitude/longitude position QC, salinity profile QC, and salinity level QC had to equal the original accepted code **1**, with finite observed salinity in [28,38] at an actual corrected measurement depth within **0–10 m**. Source observations up to 20 m or 250 km from the original colony were recorded as separate **coverage sensitivities** and were not treated as the same physical exposure as the 100-km OSI SAF sea-ice average or GLORYS uppermost reanalysis cell. Instrument family and original platform IDs were aggregated to avoid interpreting repeated measurements from one instrument as independent population/environment units. These data provide a 2015 source **observation-density gate only**; one year cannot estimate a 28-year regional synchronization correlation. EN4 uses observational sources potentially also assimilated into GLORYS, so an external observational processing product is not necessarily independent in its original instruments.

### Fixed EN4 2017–2020 raw-profile and actual-instrument comparison gate

We retrieved the actual official UK Met Office EN4.2.2 g10 original raw quality-controlled profile NetCDF members for **August–November in 2017, 2018, 2019, and 2020** (16 source months) using publisher HTTP206 exact byte-range ZIP reads with original-member CRC32 and per-month uncompressed SHA256 verification. We reused the fixed original 588 eligible colony coordinates, original 18 regional labels, WGS84 nearest-colony assignment and original profile location, salinity-profile and salinity-level QC=1. The strictly comparable observation was the shallowest actually measured level at 0–10 m and original-colony distance at most 100 km; missing observations were not interpolated as zero or replaced with gridded EN4 objective analysis. Salinities outside 28–38 were not used. Individual raw values, platform IDs and precise source profile positions were not redistributed.

The source comparison was fixed **before reading these 2017–2020 outcomes**: each region×calendar month×year required at least three distinctly identified instrument platforms. A given month was eligible only when all four original years met this criterion, and a regional 2017–2018 versus 2019–2020 platform-equal mean contrast required at least two eligible common months. A relaxed 250-km / 20-m depth calculation was kept as a separately labeled source-support sensitivity, not an equivalent primary environmental exposure. EN4 profile inputs could have been assimilated into GLORYS, so even a well-covered EN4 comparison would not be a fully independent ocean experiment. The observed in-situ source gate, its original SHA/CRC receipts and the success or failure of actual-region support were preserved as a genuine [GitHub Actions run #43681294518](https://github.com/zuizui0223/mina/actions/runs/43681294518), artifact **14149243392**.

### Geographical and climate-support selection in biological cohorts

From the unchanged 595 known MAPPPD colony sites, 588 geographically supported coastal climate sites span 18 reporting regions and 43 original 250-km polar grid blocks. The 85 temporally matched long-term site–species breeding-count series cover only 65 distinct colonies in six regions and 11 blocks. The 18 strict shared-citation species-pair series cover only **12 distinct sites in two regions and four blocks**. To assess observational support independently of a measured biological response, we computed the 1993–2020 mean fixed physical PC1 for each of the 588 climate sites and defined four equally populated climatological quartiles (147 source sites each). The 65 long-term-count sites were distributed 15/15/18/17 across these quartiles; the 12 strict paired-count sites were distributed **0/3/5/4**, with **no colony in the coldest/highest-ice quartile**. This is source sampling availability, not a biological preference for or absence from cold water. See the [source support gate](CLIMATE_VS_BREEDING_SUPPORT_SCOPE_JA_20261115.md).

### Original simultaneous species-pair nest counts

For the three fixed pairs (Adélie–gentoo, chinstrap–gentoo, and Adélie–chinstrap), a biological response was used only when **both species actually had an original observed nest count at exactly the same source colony and breeding season within 1993–2020**. Original method agreement was recorded explicitly, while original zero nests remained zero and unrecorded species were never treated as zero. Relative nesting composition is **log(1 + observed nests of the second species) − log(1 + observed nests of the first species)**. This is a measured nest-count ratio, not verified species occurrence, an abundance share of all unsurveyed species, or a demographic fitness parameter.

The same-season observations comprised **98, 165, and 88 source records respectively** (351 total), with 97/98, 165/165, and 88/88 showing the same original census method for both species. Descriptive pooled and within-site Pearson correlations were calculated for the two physical PCA scores. For a simultaneous two-axis site-level association, source sites with ≥2 genuine dual-count years were within-site centered, and each colony given equal total residual weight in a two-PC OLS of the observed log nesting ratio. Each physical PC score was divided by its standard deviation in the **entire source 16,464-site-year physical panel** before biological comparison. The fixed physical axes were never refit to maximize correlation with penguin counts.

**Post-result safeguards:** Because even same-site observations may track a common year-wide signal, we additionally projected both physical PC scores and the nest ratio onto genuine original colony and calendar-year fixed-effect indicators using equal-site-weighted Frisch–Waugh–Lovell residualization. We reported two-PC coefficients and algebraic residual degrees of freedom and examined the sign after leaving out one original reporting region or polar 250-km block at a time. We also repeated the analysis after separately excluding source recorded zero-nest observations; this is a source sensitivity, not an ecological pseudoabsence model. These analyses were motivated by exploratory patterns, **not independently preregistered**, and their within-sample residual *R*² values cannot be compared to an out-of-sample prediction. To separately describe genuine long-term compositional trajectories, site-level slopes used ≥3 real dual-count seasons spanning ≥10 years, with biological and climate slopes fit over the **same actual counted years** rather than different climate-decade windows.

### Strict shared-citation matched species-pair trajectories

As a targeted original-source sensitivity to the single-species trend associations, we reconstructed the previously publisher-audited 277 genuine same-colony/same-breeding-year two-species records with matched original source citations and counting method. Matching the original OSI SAF/GLORYS physical panel retained 273 pair-site-years within 1993–2020. We fixed the support gate at at least five observed paired census years across at least ten calendar years per colony and species pair, yielding 18 long-term species-pair trajectories from 12 colony sites, two reporting regions, four 250-km blocks and ten original GLORYS marine cells (183 observed pair-site-years). All 183 eligible records were from the original ground counting method. This support restriction uses no nest-count direction. The three prespecified contrasts were gentoo/Adélie, gentoo/chinstrap and chinstrap/Adélie, all computed as the paired same-year log1p nest-count difference; unmeasured species/year combinations were never coded as zero. For each contrast we fitted a per-site OLS slope per decade and reported Theil–Sen, first-to-last, first-year-excluded and last-year-excluded sensitivity. This is a post-result reanalysis, not independent replication or independent PR registration. A common year and citation do not prove identical census day, spatial footprint or effort.

### Observed-colony allocation concentration across independently fixed census rosters

As a separate descriptive biological accounting analysis, we measured whether the original survey-observed nest-count allocation among known colony identifiers became more or less concentrated between 1991–2000 and 2011–2020. The existing verified original colony source yields a fixed *complete three-decade* cohort of **43 colony–species records** with at least two real source census years in each of 1991–2000, 2001–2010, and 2011–2020 (**19 Adélie, 12 chinstrap, 12 gentoo**). A more inclusive but internally still fixed *paired-end-decade* cohort requires at least two genuinely counted years in each of the first and last decades and yields **50 records (26/12/12)**. All 43 source colony–species rows in the complete three-decade cohort match the end-decade cohort exactly for both endpoint nest medians, climate support, colony and method-consistent source ID. This explicitly tests the influence of requiring intermediate-decade source coverage, rather than inventing changes in unobserved colonies.

For each species, roster and decade, we used the site-specific median of actual observed nest counts, divided by the sum of such medians across the *same source colony roster*, to derive relative weights `p_i`. The concentration proxy was the **Hill q=2 effective number of monitored colony sites `1/Σp_i²`**. As a distinct weight-sensitivity control we also calculated Hill q=1 (`exp[-Σp_i log p_i]`), the share of the largest site, and leave-one-source-site, 250-km polar block and reporting-region-out diagnostics. Sums of site-specific medians were **not simultaneously observed total population estimates**. Effective *observed colony site counts* cannot be treated as island breeding-patch area, actual species range, true population effective size or an independent ecological experimental replication. The complete [source selection and arithmetic contract](allocation_20261115/REPORT_JA.md) documents the post-result scope.

### Static terrestrial mapping

We retained the original 2017–2023 mapped terrestrial 2-km composite as a **single static spatial habitat observation per source site**. Mapped positive union area covers vegetation, lichens and algal terrestrial features, not just flowering-plant greening; a mapping non-detection is not verified plant absence. The complete physical cohort overlapped **167 positive mapped colony sites across eight regions**. We compared original static positive area ranks with **each site's 1993–2020 average** marine PC1 score, then used original region-demeaned log-positive-area comparisons. No annual terrestrial time series, ice-free ground trend, or vegetation change rate was fabricated.

## Results

### A large, independently reproduced 18-region marine atlas

The verified satellite and hydrographic sources yielded a spatially and temporally balanced **588 × 28 environmental matrix across all 18 original Antarctic reporting regions**, encompassing **378 distinct native ocean reanalysis cells**. The prior 34-site/2011–2020 original physical PCA was exactly reproduced (PC1 70.943%, original physical loadings matched within 10⁻⁸), demonstrating that new findings do not arise from changing the source variable definitions or prior data files. The 595-site geographic inventory and true nest observation universe were not modified.

### The apparent one-axis physical regime splits into thermal–ice and salinity dimensions at continental scale

On all 16,464 genuine source colony–years, the standardized marine climate PC1 explained **60.365%** of physical variance and contrasted low SIC (**−0.7073**) with higher seawater temperature (**+0.7058**), with negligible salinity contribution (**−0.0383**). PC2 explained **33.377%** and was almost entirely surface salinity (loading **+0.9977**); PC3 accounted for 6.257%. The source SIC–temperature correlation was **−0.8098**, whereas SIC–salinity was **+0.0539** and temperature–salinity **+0.0100**. In comparison, the original limited 34-colony/6-region panel showed salinity loading **−0.489** on PC1; that local covariation was therefore not a continent-wide single-index rule.

The physical separation persisted under source geography controls. When one original region–year was given equal weight, PC1 explained **65.14%**, and the PC1 loading direction remained aligned with the full result (cosine 0.981); when each unique original ocean reanalysis cell–year was given one vote, PC1 explained **60.47%**, with salinity loading still near zero (−0.0545). Original source reporting-region means accounted for **70.7% of full PC1 variance**. After instead removing each original site's 28-year means, the **within-site temporal** PCA PC1 explained 55.25%, and salinity's loading on temporal PC1 was +0.336. The spatial and temporal covariance structures are therefore related but not identical; the latter is variation over years, not automatically a monotonic climate trend.

### Regional environmental histories are not monotonic or geographically interchangeable

At the fixed original full-source PC1, **13/18 reporting regions** had higher average scores in the **2001–2010** decade than in both 1993–2000 and 2011–2020. This descriptive mid-period peak also appeared in **10/13 regions** with at least three originally mapped colonies, two 250-km blocks and two unique wet marine cells. For example, central-west Antarctic Peninsula region scores averaged **+0.365, +0.888, +0.445** in the three periods; South Shetlands **+1.181, +1.586, +1.093**; and Victoria Land **−1.645, −1.623, −1.642**. These values are aligned to the same full-period source PCA, not refit by era.

The 1993–2020 linear PC1 trend had a positive direction in 11 reporting regions and a negative direction in seven; restricting to regions meeting the above multi-site/multi-block source support yielded **eight positive and five negative**. Reanalysis coverage and geographical structure limit the independence of these regional signs. They do not establish a uniform monotonic warming exposure, a cooling of Antarctica in general, or a biological advance/retreat.

### Source-year concentration qualifies the originally strong regional salinity synchrony

Using all original 18 reporting regions and 1993–2020 observations, the full-period annual region-common fractions were **19.71% (thermal–ice PC1)** and **52.14% (salinity PC2)**. The source-supported 13-region comparison gave **17.80% and 58.74%**, confirming the originally archived numerical result. The earlier unique-wet-cell weighting, one-region omission, circular phase-reference and 28-year regional linear-detrending diagnostics likewise reproduce the same full-source quantities; these diagnostics are **not independent biological experiments or attribution tests**.

The post-result diagnostic of the two consecutive source endpoint years substantially changed the interpretation. Excluding **2019 and 2020** yielded regional temporal common-year variance fractions of **20.69% PC1 and 29.29% PC2** (1993–2018, 18 regions), and **18.77% versus 32.65%** for the 13 better-supported regions. Over 2011–2020 the original fractions were **22.12%/71.28%** (PC1/PC2), but over **2011–2018** they were **23.74%/35.21%**. The salinity temporal contrast thus persists descriptively at reduced magnitude, but the full 28-year value is **highly sensitive** to including 2019–2020.

The original 378 distinct wet GLORYS source cells were treated once each. In **2019**, 368/378 had lower August–November salinity than the same cell's 1993–2018 average; in **2020**, 363/378 were lower (median cell departure **−0.718 practical-salinity units**). All **18/18** original source reporting-region mean salinities were below their preceding average in both 2019 and 2020. The GLORYS12V1 publisher documents a switch in atmospheric forcing from ERA-Interim to ERA5 beginning January 2019, making a source-process discontinuity a material competing explanation. The present data **cannot** determine whether this feature represents genuine oceanographic change, a model forcing/assimilation response, or a combination. The 2015 EN4 in-situ coverage pilot is not independent verification for these two years.

In a separate explicitly **post-result within-source negative-control check**, the same 378 wet-grid source cells were evaluated for all three original physical variables using each cell's 1993–2018 mean and standard deviation. Salinity was more than two baseline SD below its own mean at **239/378 cells in 2019 and 310/378 in 2020**; the corresponding below−2SD counts were **7 and 11 for GLORYS seawater temperature**, and **0 and 8 for the site-aggregated OSI SAF nearshore SIC**. Thus the unusually widespread low-salinity mode was not accompanied by a comparably uniform cooling or nearshore sea-ice reduction in the **same original source-cell cohort**. The 378 cells are spatially dependent, and the coastal SIC site buffer is not identical to a GLORYS wet-grid pixel. These source-conditional negative-control comparisons do not independently establish the salinity event's physical reality or identify any data-assimilation mechanism. [Full source diagnostics and frozen reproducible script](GLORYS_2019_2020_CROSSVARIABLE_DIAGNOSTIC_JA_20261115.md).

An additional **source-only first-difference check** grouped the 588 sites into their 378 original wet cells and compared every consecutive August–November mean transition from 1994 to 2020 (27 annual transitions). The median cell-level salinity change from **2018 to 2019 was −0.42344**, the largest negative median annual step in the source period; **346/378 cells declined**, and **188/378 cells** had a change more than two cell-specific 1994–2018 annual-difference SD below the reference. In 2019–2020 the median step was **−0.20505**, with 298/378 cells declining and 54/378 below the same −2SD cutoff. A historically negative regional sign is not unique to 2019: **2010 also had 18/18 regions with negative within-region cell-median steps**, but its spatial median was only −0.20486 and 28/378 cells crossed −2SD. The new inference is therefore **the relative magnitude of the 2019 step**, not the discovery of the first all-region negative year. These spatially dependent grid cells are not independent tests, and this remains an original-GLORYS diagnostic rather than independent field evidence. [All 27 source annual steps and independent arithmetic QA](GLORYS_2019_YEAR_ON_YEAR_STEP_GATE_JA_20261115.md).

By contrast, refitting the original three-variable marine PCA on the same 588 source colony sites for **1993–2018** retained the spatial dimensionality: standardized PC1 **60.15%** and PC2 **33.49%**, compared with full-source **60.37%/33.38%**; salinity-positive PC2 loading remained **+0.9968**. The environmental atlas has **588 original colonies, 18 regions and 43 polar 250-km grid blocks**. ### Original raw hydrographic observations remain too sparse to validate the 18-region salinity coherence

The genuine EN4.2.2 **2015 August–November source profiles** totalled **170,401 global records**. Of these, **657 source profile locations** were within 250 km of one of the 588 matched original Antarctic breeding localities before quality and depth filtering. At the primary **0–10 m measured depth and ≤100 km colony separation** gate, only **385 profiles in five of 18 original reporting regions** passed source position, salinity-profile, and salinity-level QC. Of those, **371** were assigned to the **South Shetland Islands** and the remaining 14 were spread across just four other regions; the 13 remaining original source regions had no qualifying records under this 2015 source filter. By month, the primary sample fell from **250 profiles in August** to **114 September, 18 October, and three November**. A single original region had three or more distinct qualifying platforms, and a single region had qualifying source records in all four months.

These are **source availability comparisons, not alternative samples to optimize ecological significance**. As a consequence, the strong **GLORYS** regionally coherent salinity-year signal cannot be labeled independently **in-situ verified or refuted** on the available 2015 observation-support pilot. The sparse, instrument-concentrated observational coverage and potential overlap of EN4 inputs with observations assimilated into GLORYS are material limits on attributing the temporal salinity mode to an independently observed physical mechanism.

### Penguin breeding assemblages do not show one transferable response to either physical axis

For the original method-consistent co-count sample, the pairwise within-site Pearson associations with the thermal–ice PC1 were only **−0.008** for gentoo/Adélie, **+0.061** for gentoo/chinstrap and **−0.042** for chinstrap/Adélie; the corresponding salinity-PC2 associations were **−0.033, −0.102, and +0.008**. Although the biological dataset comprised 351 real dual-count records, the informative repeated monitoring supported only **11, 20, and nine source colonies** for these three pairs, clustered in **three, five, and three polar blocks**, respectively.

In a descriptive equal-colony-weighted within-site model with **both physical PCs simultaneously**, salinity-PC2 coefficients per global one-standard-deviation score were **+0.068, −0.187, and +0.055**, and the two physical covariates explained only **0.54%, 2.09%, and 0.65%** of within-site original nesting-ratio variance. No universally strong two-axis species-response structure appeared. The gentoo/chinstrap association was the only weak source-specific candidate with a relatively stable negative PC2 direction in the same-source sensitivity excluding original recorded zero nests (PC2 −0.137).

When the source included calendar-year fixed effects as well as colony effects, the gentoo/chinstrap salinity-PC2 coefficient remained negative (**−0.176**), and it remained negative after excluding actual zero nest counts (**−0.083**). Yet the additional physical PC axes explained only **0.48% of the already site-and-year-residualized nesting-ratio variance**. Deleting one original 250-km block at a time kept the salt-PC2 sign negative (−0.254 to −0.007); **deleting an original reporting region reversed it**, with the slope ranging from −0.183 to +0.072. Specifically, excluding the **central-west Antarctic Peninsula** changed the coefficient from −0.176 to **+0.072**; just two source regions remained informative after that deletion. The other species-pair coefficients changed sign under year/zero or geographical sensitivity. Thus even this weak salt-associated signal cannot be declared a continent-wide biological mechanism.

An independent descriptive time-series unit—colonies with both species actually counted in ≥3 seasons spanning ≥10 years—was available at only **seven sites for gentoo/Adélie, 15 for gentoo/chinstrap, and six for chinstrap/Adélie**, each pair confined to **two source regions**. The correlation of source site-level gentoo/chinstrap nesting-ratio slopes with same-census-year salt-PC2 slopes was **−0.196** (region-demeaned −0.158), insufficient to identify a generalized response kernel. Neither source family establishes species immigration, competitive displacement, or time-delayed climate debt.

### Species-ratio changes are not interchangeable with changes in observed nesting totals

To distinguish the numerator and denominator of a relative species shift, we separately reconstructed each species' observed `log1p(nests)` trend from the *identical* source- and method-matched colony-years. At all **six** gentoo/Adélie colonies (66 original paired site-years), Adélie nest-count slopes were negative and gentoo slopes positive, including when the first or last census year was omitted for each species. Nevertheless, **five of six** two-species observed nest subtotals rose from the first to the final paired survey; **five** had positive log-subtotal slopes over all observed years, of which **four** retained positive slopes under both single-endpoint deletion checks. Among the nine gentoo/chinstrap colonies, **eight of nine** two-species subtotals rose, even though the gentoo/chinstrap log ratio increased at only seven. In contrast, the three Adélie/chinstrap paired subtotals all declined, but the contrasts have different geographical coverage and are not comparative independent replicates.

In the more stringent **34 same-site years with original observations of all three species** (21 Ardley, 7 Lion's Rump, 6 PCHA), the Adélie-plus-chinstrap nest subtotal declined at all three sites while gentoo counts rose at all three. **Three-species observed nest totals nonetheless increased at Ardley (+37.67%, 4,927→6,783) and PCHA (+27.72%, 1,230→1,571), but decreased at Lion's Rump (−25.25%, 9,771→7,304)**. Corresponding gentoo observed-nest shares rose from 74.65%→94.77%, 97.56%→99.05%, and 27.04%→51.88%, respectively. These are site-specific first-versus-last *observed* census contrasts with unequal year spans, not census-adjusted estimates of entire community abundance. They show that a similar relative gentoo shift can accompany either an increase or decrease in the observed three-species subtotal. This is arithmetic differentiation of observed counts, not proof of colonization, displacement, carrying capacity change, or climatic mediation. ### Terrestrial positive mapped area is not an annual greening trajectory

The **167 site / eight region** positive-mapped terrestrial union-area subpanel had a descriptive across-site Spearman association of **+0.237** with full-period marine PC1 site means, but the within-region log-area Pearson relationship was **−0.095**. ## Discussion

### The physical mechanism is not a single ‘subantarcticization’ clock

A strong sea-ice/temperature covariance is real in these source fields, but the originally proposed single warming–freshening–greening–gentoo transition axis does not remain one-dimensional when sampled across all 18 Antarctic reporting regions. Sea-surface salinity provides a second spatial environmental degree of freedom. The sign of its relationship to ice and water temperature varies with the observation universe and whether spatial site means or time anomalies are analysed. That outcome is physically plausible because brine rejection, glacial freshwater, ocean advection and mixing do not imply one globally shared salinity direction. The GLORYS source also assimilates oceanographic information; statistical orthogonality in the source PCA is **not** proof that the mechanisms are causally independent.

### Stable spatial climate dimensionality does not validate a synchronous salinity process

Three-variable coastal marine reanalysis values exhibit a stable descriptive split between ice–thermal PC1 and the predominantly salinity PC2, including when the final two source years are excluded. This distinction rules out treating the recorded Antarctic coastal physical settings as one universally varying `subantarcticization` axis. However, its interpretation should not be promoted to an independently observed time-coherent biological forcing: the previously impressive 52.14% PC2 common-year fraction falls to 29.29% on the same source regions when the 2019–2020 source years are omitted.

The official GLORYS12V1 switch from ERA-Interim to ERA5 forcing in January 2019 is contemporaneous with the widespread source-value decline. This does **not** establish that the forcing switch caused the salinity pattern, nor disprove a real physical event. It establishes that independent, time-aligned upper-ocean salinity observations or a controlled model comparison are needed before claiming pan-Antarctic salinity synchrony or using the apparent event as an explanatory input for penguin nesting changes. The previously audited 2015 EN4 source profiles do not validate the 2019–2020 salinity pattern.

### Limits of confirmation from raw ocean observations

A targeted 2015 EN4 raw-profile audit placed a tangible bound on possible independent validation of the 18-region GLORYS result. The original source QC, depth and geography yielded only five of 18 regions and 12 identified platforms near the monitored colonies, dominated by a large repeated sample in the South Shetlands and by the early August–September months. A subsequent **completed 2017–2020 raw-observation extension** secured all 16 official Aug–Nov source months but found **no Antarctic region** with at least three distinct platforms in each of four years for two matched months at ≤100 km and 0–10 m. Observations shifted among regions by year (2018 South Shetlands versus 2019/2020 Victoria Land). Only a relaxed 250-km, 20-m Victoria Land October–November comparison passed, with a small positive four-year contrast (+0.08914); its different geographic and temporal support is not evidence against the pan-Antarctic GLORYS source pattern. This is an empirical **observability limitation**, not evidence that the salinity synchrony is physically false. It does mean that model-data-assimilative reanalysis synchrony cannot currently be presented as broadly reproduced by independent in-situ seasonal observations. A full raw historical profile analysis would have to account for sampling density, changing instruments and the emergence of Argo floats, and regional absence of observations must remain missing. Observationally weighted, 1° gridded EN4 analysis could provide a weaker **analysis-product sensitivity**, but ocean cells without genuine profiles would remain inferred, not independent measurements.

### Regional source regimes and actual temporal paths must be distinguished

The 18-region source atlas provides a major advance over interpreting the Antarctic Peninsula as if it represented the full continent. Yet even the original reporting regions vary in their number of colony points and independent ocean cells, with some regions including one or a few source localities. The mid-period PC1 peaks and differing 28-year signed trends show why a latitudinal space-for-time substitution can be misleading. A fixed geographic gradient describes *where* colonies are exposed to warmer, less icy ocean conditions. It does not independently tell us how rapidly, how monotonically, or even in which direction an individual region experiences a given reanalysis-based physical coordinate.

### Penguin numbers can differ without identifying a salinity niche transition

The restricted gentoo/chinstrap PC2 observation is worth retaining as a targeted hypothesis, precisely because we know its modest size and geographic fragility. A consistent sign under single 250-km block deletion, year controls and recorded-zero sensitivity does not counteract the fact that deleting the central-west Antarctic Peninsula reverses the sign of the relationship. Likewise, source sites and year records share climate cells and monitoring histories; strong model residual degrees of freedom under an ordinary linear algebra formula are not independent spatiotemporal experiments. Direct salinity effects on prey or on penguin energetic performance, seabird foraging access, competition, or species dispersal have **not** been measured.

The scientifically relevant departure from the original one-axis hypothesis is consequently not that warming has no effect on penguins. It is that *physically coherent environmental gradients may have geographically nontransferable biological associations*, and that population-count responses cannot be read mechanically from the position of a region on a climate PCA. Candidate mechanisms include phenology, seasonal foraging conditions, prey changes, icebergs and colony access, survey effects and historical demography, but these are external hypotheses rather than outcomes established by the present data.

### The sea–land connection is a separate temporal identification problem

The terrestrial data contain positive detections of vegetation, lichens and algae in one multi-year composite. They can be used to describe where mapped land features co-occur with marine conditions, but not whether marine retreat leads annual greening or whether penguin occupation precedes new vegetation. Published vegetation-expansion estimates for the Antarctic Peninsula have recently been debated in the scientific literature. An independent, source-consistent long-term remote-sensing vegetation series and actual comparably surveyed penguin breeding habitat occupancy would be necessary to test a coastwide sea–land community response sequence.

### Relative species replacement is not identical to monotonic local sea-ice exposure

Direct source-matched same-colony comparisons corroborate long-term *relative* gentoo gains against Adélie within a restricted two-region sample even where the observed local sea-ice linear trend increases. However, this mismatch is not evidence that marine forcing does not matter. Census timing, delayed demographic effects, breeding habitat, diet, recruitment and correlated broader climate regimes remain unresolved. Species-ratio trends do not establish displacement of the same nest patches, colonization, or direct competition. This small paired biological subpanel strengthens the reason to resist transferring continental physical-axis coefficients to future nest counts, but cannot turn the two-region result into an Antarctic-wide law. In particular, the nearshore 100-km SIC and GLORYS 25-km wet-cell proxies are not individual foraging pathways or local nest microhabitat.

### Relative replacement is not a zero-sum abundance measurement

Same-colony compositional turnover and its sign cannot be treated as a fixed-sum exchange of nests. In the source-matched gentoo/Adélie subpanel, gentoo nests increased while Adélie nests decreased across all six colonies, but their observed summed nest counts rose at five. The full three-species subset is geographically smaller and more heterogeneous: two monitored colony totals rose and one fell, despite gentoo nest share rising in each. Thus *relative reassembly*, *absolute component trajectories*, and *local breeding subtotal* are three different ecological descriptions. This does not infer direct replacement of physical nest patches, competition, demographic compensation, or a warming-driven future colony count. The exact observational cohort remains limited to two reporting regions and highly overlapping 250-km blocks.

### Census timing constrains biological transfer between marine dimensions

The longer-term colony analysis adds a biological sampling limitation to the independently observed geographical climate scales: environmental gradients can be reconstructed for 588 colonies over 28 years, but the three-variable matched biological test has only 85 site–species trajectories, drawn from six reporting regions with sparse independent within-region spatial replication. Source-season matching is necessary, but regional method differences, uneven census timing, spatially shared marine cells and nonstationary environmental trajectories make any pooled apparent response uncertain. A statistically distinct salinity-related physical axis must not be turned into a salinity-caused penguin niche shift without an independently supported biological effect. We therefore interpret the 85-series result as a **time-support and geographic-transfer diagnostic**, not an additional post-hoc positive biological endpoint.

### Geographic reallocation, nesting concentration and realized ice exposure are different outcomes

For the **same two end decades**, the Adélie colony-network count-weighted latitude changes southerly in every observation-roster and one-geographical-unit-deletion comparison, whereas Hill q=2 concentration changes sign with the inclusion of just seven additional colonies. This is a substantive limitation on equating poleward geographic reweighting with the local within-colony nesting-component contraction previously seen at Palmer/Signy: site codes and nesting patches are not interchangeable spatial units. Nor does a southward count-weighted latitude imply meaningful recovery of the original 100-km nearshore sea-ice buffer, whose count-weighted change was only +0.022 pp in the 19-site source cohort. The region-partitioned latitude shift is an accounting change in the distribution of monitored nests among **unchanged fixed geographical source points**, with no confirmed colony colonization, individual migration, improved foraging suitability, or pan-Antarctic shift in observed species range. These are **noncausal descriptive bounds**, not a fitted lag or an estimate of climate-tracking success.

### Why colony-level concentration does not reproduce patch-level contraction automatically

These fixed observed-colony analyses provide a direct boundary on transferring previously reported Palmer/Signy *within-colony nesting component* contraction into a universal Antarctic macro-colony claim. Nesting patch counts and colony site codes have different observation grains and denominator rules. The 19-site Adélie panel offers an empirical case where observational site-median totals increase while the distribution across monitored colony sites becomes more concentrated; however, the 26-site endpoint panel and one-site omission reverse the q=2 direction. Missing the 2000s count requirement can exclude an exceptionally large actually observed colony, which is a selection property of the observation roster, not an observed extinction. Thus no common demographic-collapse → spatial-concentration mechanism follows across scales. The single-paper ecological inference remains geographical reweighting of monitored nests and restricted same-site species composition, with original sea-ice and hydrographic exposure contextualized but not causally linked to concentration.

### Total counted nests, spatial allocation and species composition are different ecological states

The cross-scale biological synthesis should not reduce all penguin breeding change to one demographic response variable. Three observed states are distinct: **(i)** totals of genuinely measured colony-specific nest-count medians within a fixed temporal and geographic source roster, **(ii)** their normalized distribution among sampled colony codes (Hill effective colony count and geographic reweighting), and **(iii)** the relative nesting contribution of species actually counted at the same colony and year. All three depend on explicit sampling support. The 19-site Adélie cohort demonstrates increased count medians alongside increased spatial dominance, while the subset-to-superset comparison with 26 endpoint-observed sites reverses the latter result. Within strict mixed-species observations, gentoo/Adélie relative nesting increases at six sites while their observed two-species subtotals increase at five; and with all three species genuinely observed, the gentoo nesting share rises at all three sites even though total observed nests fall at one. These are measurable forms of **breeding-network reassembly**, not a zero-sum or monotonically shrinking space mechanism.

By comparison, Antarctic marine ice–thermal PC1 and salinity PC2 represent **available source physical variation**, not automatic predictors of any of the three breeding states. The GLORYS salinity abrupt 2019 source signal is observationally unconfirmed at the relevant regional/monthly support; the static vegetation map cannot establish temporal land habitat mediation. The proposal that climate drives a common population response should therefore be restricted to a future independent same-effort, same-year, multi-region analysis, not elevated from the present assemblage source descriptions.

### Limits, novelty, and inference boundary

The strength of this work is the **original-source alignment and cross-scale analysis** of marine physical observations with genuine colony nests: all 18 Antarctic regions, 28 years, separate salt and thermal-ice coordinates, actual same-year two-species count pairs, and explicit regional, spatial-cell, survey-year and source-zero guards. It is not the first description of Adélie decline, gentoo expansion or a Ross Island colony contrast, and the study cannot isolate greenhouse attribution or the causes of regional salinity changes. The ecological novelty should be framed as the **scale and transfer boundary** between physically constrained dimensions and repeated observed colony assemblage changes.

The study could be improved decisively through actual within-region longitudinally comparable censuses in additional independent Antarctic source regions, biological foraging/marine access measurements, and a temporally explicit land cover archive. Until then, the results justify **no universal species-specific response coefficient, no measured climate-tracking lag, and no 2050/2100 penguin breeding forecast**. Descriptive source calculations and complete model/weight sensitivity are supplied as reproducible analysis records rather than converted into unwarranted inferential certainty.
