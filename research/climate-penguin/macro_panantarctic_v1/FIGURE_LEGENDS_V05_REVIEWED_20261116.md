# Main figure legends — original-data biology-first Antarctic macro manuscript v0.5

**Source status:** figure captions reviewed 16 November 2026; all panels constructed from original source-audited count/sea-ice/GLORYS records already cited in the [manuscript](MANUSCRIPT_MACRO_V0_5_COMPACT_BIOLOGY_FIRST_WORKING.md) and [figure data contract](FIGURE_CONTRACT_V05_BIOLOGY_FIRST_20261115.md). Figures describe observational quantities rather than modelled species absences, bird movement or validated climate niches.

**Figure 1. Geographical extent and narrowing biological support for near-colony Antarctic marine environmental comparisons.** Points show the polar-stereographic positions of 595 catalogued source colony identifiers. Coloured circles denote the 65 unique colony sites with genuine long-term counts aligned to all three original marine variables, and triangles denote the 12 unique colony sites supporting at least one strict same-citation, same-method species-pair trajectory (18 overlapping pair-site trajectories). Crosses denote seven colony locations without a GLORYS wet ocean cell within the fixed 25-km distance gate. Concentric reference lines are latitude parallels, *not* modelled coastlines. Source sampling coverage is not contemporary penguin species-range coverage or presence–absence data. In the fixed three-decade Adélie panel, six of the source-unmatched colonies represented 68.02% of the 2010s observed nesting median sum, demonstrating that the fraction of marine-matched **sites** is not the fraction of marine-matched **nesting weight**.

**Figure 2. Statistical separation of ice–temperature and salinity variation in original Antarctic coastal source records.** Signed loadings on the first two components of a single standardized principal-component analysis fitted to sea-ice concentration (OSI SAF; fixed original nearshore 100-km support), potential seawater temperature and practical salinity (GLORYS12V1) over 1993–2020. The dataset contains 588 coastal-colony locations observed in 28 years (16,464 source site–years) with 378 distinct original GLORYS wet ocean cells. The first component explains 60.37% of standardized variation (sea ice −0.707, temperature +0.706); the second explains 33.38% (salinity +0.998). Principal components are **orthogonal by mathematical construction**; they do not establish two causally independent climate mechanisms or biological preferences. Removing the source years 2019–2020 preserved their broad loadings and fractions; the late salinity temporal synchrony remains source dependent and independently unconfirmed.

**Figure 3. Southerly change in observed Adélie nesting weights with little net increase in nearshore sea-ice exposure.** Source nest-count-weighted latitude changes from the 1991–2000 to the 2011–2020 decade within three *nested*, fixed-roster observation sets: 19 colony sites counted at least twice in all three decades; 26 counted at least twice in both endpoint decades; and 35 counted at least once in both endpoints. Total southerly changes were 107.31, 88.41 and 99.60 km, respectively, represented by a mathematical symmetric decomposition into between-original-region nest-share changes, within-region between-250-km-block changes, and within-block site reweighting. Values for original nearshore nesting-weighted sea ice (mean August–November) were +0.022, +0.625 and +0.908 percentage points. The 19/26/35 sets overlap; these are not three independent samples, and the decomposition depends on source boundaries and 250-km grid size. No individual penguin or colony coordinate was observed moving.

**Figure 4. Gentoo-to-Adélie relative nest-count changes are compatible with different local two-species subtotals.** Six strict source-matched colony records (only two original reporting regions), each with both species counted at the same colony in at least five common genuine years spanning ≥10 years. Horizontal axis: per-decade slope of log(1 + observed gentoo nests) minus log(1 + observed Adélie nests) at each site. Vertical axis: percentage change in the observed *sum* of the two species' nest counts between the first and last common census seasons. Point symbols distinguish four colonies with decreasing and two with increasing nearshore August–November sea-ice trend slopes on original matching census years. All six ratios increased; five site subtotals increased and one declined. Original citation and method match do not guarantee identical within-season survey date, complete footprint or effort. Paired nests are not an estimate of the entire Antarctic population or direct competitive displacement.

**Caveat common to all four figures:** The analysis cohorts overlap, source reporting regions are geographically uneven, and the original 588-site marine score and six-site biological contrasts have different observational denominators. Figure labels and source tables cannot establish a causal warming effect, climate-niche tracking, full species-range changes or future nest-number predictions.
