## Derived contrast

Reported mean ground-cover categories (percentage points), impacted vs protected:
- bryophytes 1.7 vs 73.1
- lichens 11.5 vs 21.1
- Deschampsia antarctica 1.0 vs 3.6
- Prasiola crispa 45.1 vs 0.4

Sum of categories 59.3 vs 98.2; sum excluding Prasiola 14.2 vs 97.8. The latter difference is −83.6 percentage points, while Prasiola differs by +44.7 points. Thus the algal-cover contrast offsets 44.7/83.6 = 53.47% of the summed non-algal contrast, giving net difference −38.9 points. This is algebraic cross-sectional contrast, not a time-resolved replacement process. Prasiola is a green alga, not a vascular plant. It accounts for 76.05% of the summed cover categories in impacted plots versus 0.41% in controls. Category sums are not assumed to be an independently measured area union.

Table III substrate-specific lichen-cover fractions: rock 41.4/77.2 = 0.5363 (−46.37% relative), soil/moss 0.3/7.8 = 0.03846 (−96.15%). Relative cover contrasts are from the same field study, not independent effect-size samples.

Tabulated richness 40 vs 47, exclusive 25 vs 32, hence implied shared 15, union 72. The text also states 74 infrageneric taxa in the whole survey, so do not assert a reconciled taxon roster. Using only the internally consistent Table III group richness/exclusive counts, Sørensen dissimilarity = 57/87 = 0.65517; Simpson turnover component = 25/40 = 0.625; nestedness residual = 0.03017; turnover/dissimilarity = 95.39%. This is a descriptive partition of the tabulated groups, not a validated species-by-site reanalysis. Authors already report substantial compositional and growth-form differences; do not claim new discovery of disturbance-driven lichen turnover.

## Interpretation

Independent existing field evidence shows that a single cover metric can conflate very different ecological components. A green alga associated with nutrient enrichment may partly mask the contrast in bryophytes, lichens and vascular grass. Palmer seal detections and mapped vegetation cover have a positive local association (separate 2020 seal survey and 2017–2023 vegetation product); this does not identify facilitation or contradict Signy protected/impacted macroplot results. Palmer mapped classes cannot be assumed to resolve Prasiola, and the designs and sites are not comparable causal contrasts. An actual cross-region test requires taxon- or functional-group-resolved mapped vegetation, calibrated independent seal-use exposure and consistent observation support. No pooled effect or new claim of negative causal seal pressure.

Decision: retain the supported local decline of fur seal counts across all six Signy zones. Park the 2009–2018 linkage pending original spatial vegetation data and audited pre-endpoint census support; do not switch time windows or use 2024 counts. The published-aggregate arithmetic is mechanistic context, not a new original empirical result. No request sent.
