# Movement-identification and manuscript decision (2026-11-25 JST)

**Decision: do not frame Adélie/chinstrap as immobile and gentoo as mobile.** The direct matched-count test (SYMPATRIC_ADPE_GEPE_RESULT_20261125.md) is a no-go for a stable numerical-compensation advantage and has insufficient df for the full same-site climate contrast. The six strict same-census sites in PR214 retain opposite Adélie/gentoo count trends; fixed-known-site nest-weighted southward displacement is an observed change in weights, not a measure of the migration of birds. Neither establishes zero breeding dispersal, local deaths, natal recruitment, new-colony formation, or competitive replacement. The asserted 4/3/100% broad compensation figures and Bird Island p=.0001 are not independently validated by this matched test; do not substitute them for it. Do not treat multiple sites or years from the same sources as independent evidence of a single causal pathway.

## Independent published evidence reviewed

- Dugger et al. 2010, PNAS, DOI via https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20566874/ : multistate mark–recapture in the southwestern Ross Sea, 1996–2007; breeding adult movements usually below 1%, but movement Royds to Bird exceeded 3% in iceberg-affected years. This contradicts a categorical inability to change breeding colony, and demonstrates context dependence.
- Dugger et al. 2026, Front Ecol Evol, DOI 10.3389/fevo.2026.1868960 (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2026.1868960/full): 25-year mark–recapture at three Ross Island colonies, 1996–2020. Highest estimated intercolony movements in pre-breeders vary 0–12%, established breeders <0.20%; observation effort differs and authors caution about movements outside study network. Related programme to prior recovered raw data, not independent validation of its counts. These are local, stage-specific estimates, not all Antarctica and not the same transition as successful natal recruitment.
- Clucas et al. 2018, Molecular Ecology DOI 10.1111/mec.14896, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30308702/ ; open primary data DOI 10.5061/dryad.bs30388, https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.5061/dryad.bs30388 . Comparative genomewide analysis reports relatively low differentiation in Adélie and chinstrap over extensive geographic distances versus strong differentiation among gentoo populations. Genetic connectivity over generations is not a contemporary individual breeding-dispersal probability; population history and incomplete lineage sorting may contribute. The finding argues against a simple species-wide mobility ordering. No VCF downloaded or fit: repeating published genetic comparison would not address the specific annual dispersal question.
- Levy et al. 2016, Ecology and Evolution DOI 10.1002/ece3.1929; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26933489/ : inferred occasional long-range dispersal and range expansion despite high gentoo natal philopatry. Genomic inference, not annual transitions.
- Korczak-Abshire et al. 2021, Biology Letters DOI indexed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497589/ ; data DOI 10.5061/dryad.5x69p8d20: winter telemetry from five Peninsula breeding colonies, 71 individual files in repository. This measures movement/exploration outside breeding, not confirmed breeding-site changes. No raw file retrieved this turn. Do not use winter track extent as adult breeding dispersal or infer source of colonists.
- Herman & Lynch 2022, Ornithology, DOI 10.1093/ornithology/duac014, https://academic.oup.com/condor/article/124/3/duac014/6568418 : age-structured abundance model with published vital rates inferred sustained immigration at four new gentoo colonies. Counts are available in supplement and penguinmap.com; may overlap MAPPPD, not a new independent set. This is already prior art for distinguishing local recruitment from immigration by demographic modelling; inference is conditional on vital rates and model assumptions. Do not claim first quantitative demonstration of gentoo immigration.
- Marine Ornithology 2026 DOI 10.5038/2074-1235.54.2.1707, https://marineornithology.org/article?rn=1707 : Point 8 Anvers colonization case study, first observation 2019 winter, first nesting 2020, successful chick rearing 2023. This provides temporal evidence for establishment following habitat exposure, not direct tracking of source individuals. Indexed abstract inspected; landing-page open returned cache miss; appendix/raw records not retrieved. Not a matched three-species dispersal study.

## One defensible main question and next identification gate

Distinguish realized breeding-distribution change (fixed sites changing their count weights, observed vacancy/establishment only with verified prior surveys) from movement of individuals. Possible question: *Do co-occurring penguin species differ in the demographic processes underlying changing breeding distributions?* This is a hypothesis, not supported mechanism at present. Age and stage of movement, not an intrinsic binary mobility ranking, is the relevant biological distinction. The same colony coordinates control macro geography, not realized marine use, recruitment or survey process.

Existing direct results: same-site opposite long-term count trends at six sites; observed distributional reweighting in fixed rosters. Existing falsifications: unstable matched numerical compensation, insufficient marine model support, unstable PGEO spatial bridge, no universal positive polynya–productivity response. Keep the isotope and productivity results in their own evidence scopes; do not assemble a causal chain out of them, multiply coefficients or claim that positive local productivity necessarily implies increasing later population size. Do not use this as evidence of a cross-species causal contrast or start fitting more count-only models to rescue it.

For a genuinely decisive follow-up, obtain matched multicolony encounter histories for both taxa in one region, with confirmed nest attendance, natal versus established-breeder status, survey effort and detection. Compare state-specific transition rates over the same seasons and colony network, separating apparent survival, nonbreeding, recruitment and intercolony moves. Alternative demographic feasibility analysis needs externally calibrated species- and life-stage-specific survival/recruitment bounds, synchronous observed counts and verified new colony absence/presence, and would address conditional net immigration rather than movement probability. A same-region contemporary raw paired encounter history was not located by this targeted search. Existing published studies and recovered audit are sufficient to close the categorical mobility claim; no data contact, no new raw movement fit, no further same-data threshold search, no main merge or manuscript alteration.
