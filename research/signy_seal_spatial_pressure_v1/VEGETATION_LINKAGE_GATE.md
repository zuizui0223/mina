# Signy plant–seal spatial linkage: source-only gate, 2026-11-24

Independent extension of PR219 (not a reopening of PR218 or an alteration of the completed six-zone seal result). Cannone et al. 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2022.01.074, explicitly hypothesized that warming and release from fur-seal disturbance accelerate native vascular plant expansion. Do not claim novelty for that mechanism. Testability of spatially differentiated exposure is the question. Plant surveys 1960, 2009, Jan–Mar 2018; paper states georeferenced 20–25 m sites, population-size classes and field disturbance. Public data DOI explicitly given as 10.6084/m9.figshare.18282695. The paper is not proof that the deposited table contains coordinates or longitudinal site IDs.

Sources: Figshare article API https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/18282695 (identity by DOI, publication metadata; record file size and MD5/SHA256), DataCite https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.6084/m9.figshare.18282695, original seal map Dunn et al. 2025 Fig. 2 (10.1111/gcb.70290, NERC OA PDF), BAS land-cover 2020 original geodata DOI 10.5285/af80c82c-b6a0-4978-92e0-efe063c390af (rock, moraine, ice, lakes; not plant cover), BAS reference map https://data.bas.ac.uk/maps/1011 (UTM 23S). Web PDF screenshot failed; do not claim digitized zone geometry. The published six zones describe coastal seal census extent, not necessarily complete inland polygons. Do not fill inland space using nearest coastal zone or assign sites by latitude alone.

Audit only: inventory all publisher files, preserve checksums and field names; record coordinate and plot-ID field candidates, year-label coverage and count of coordinate-complete rows without opening plant abundance outcomes; inspect GIS layer CRS/schema if present. Stop if identity or integrity cannot be verified. Retain source receipts; do not republish original vegetation data. Map georeferencing requires original figure, at least six distributed reliable landmarks with independent check points, recorded transform residuals, six explicitly identified zone footprints and uncertainty buffers; no invented boundaries. If only coastal strips are shown, restrict to securely mapped strips or establish actual exposure catchments independently. If geographic support fails, seek author GIS coordinates/zone boundaries, no ecological fit.

Temporal gate: plant change in 2009–2018 cannot be explained by the already executed 2016–2024 vs 2010–2014 contrast; this includes post-response seal counts. Candidate exposure, to freeze only after support is confirmed, is 2004–2008 versus 2013–2017 February counts (equal years, at least four valid per fixed zone/period) or a pre-2018 interval exposure specified before opening joint outcomes. Never use 2018+ counts as causes of 2018 plants; 2018 Jan–Mar plant survey and February census can overlap. 1960 precedes seal census; no imputed pre-1974 local counts. Keep these two plant species separate, distinguish occupied-site change from genuine colonization requiring survey footprint/absence evidence, no repeated plants-as-independent zone replicates. At most six independent spatial exposure units on one island, so a zone-level descriptive contrast at best. No regression with many covariates or nominal plant-point sample size; no proof of causal trampling, nutrients, warming or interaction. Different surveyed footprints, ice-free opportunity, elevation and spatial dependence must be addressed before any comparison.

If data only have aggregate summaries or no coordinates, close the linkage gate and request the original GIS (1960/2009/2018 survey footprint, positions, size classes, disturbance codes, CRS) and official seal zone boundary vectors. Existing seal six-zone findings remain intact.
