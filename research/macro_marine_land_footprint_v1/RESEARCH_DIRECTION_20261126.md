# Research direction — Antarctic macroecology, 2026-11-26
User requested return to macroecology after local mechanism explorations V17–21. Main objective: quantify reorganization of the marine–terrestrial link at continental and regional spatial grains. Do not proliferate local mechanistic reanalyses or treat separate modules as a demonstrated causal chain.
1. Current geographic coupling: full known breeding atlas and positive observed modern subsets against the entire independent static Plantarctica map. Unique mapped area inside geographic buffers, class-specific results, no overlapping-area double counting. V22 fixed contract and executable workflow.
2. Temporal reorganization: identical observed-site/species cohorts in historical windows; distinguish count total from geographic allocation via proportional change reference. Evaluate marine (sea ice/salinity/polynya) context and terrestrial receptor geography on compatible supports only. Existing CMIP6 physical exposures are climate scenarios, not demographic projections. Expand temporal support with original MAPPPD v5 records, after source overlap/method checks; 595 historical fixed atlas and v5 729 locations must not be called complete synchronous counts.

Historical extension only if adequate independent georeferenced breeding evidence: USAP 600028 radiocarbon workbook, Emslie 2001 Antarctic Peninsula 21 abandoned colonies on 12 islands, Emslie & Woehler 2005 Windmill 17 abandoned colony sites (13 dated), Wood 2025 current and abandoned locality metadata. DOI 10.15784/600028; DOI 10.1017/S0954102001000414; Wood 10.1038/s41467-025-56925-4. These are source candidates, not a combined validated inventory. Ancient remains date and last occupation are different; control present breeding proximity, elevation, wetness, ice-free land and survey history. Keep actual repeated plant measures separate from static map. Do not open a historical local follow-up without macro support.
Prior-art boundary: Santora et al. 2020 GEB 10.1111/geb.13144 already relates penguin clustering to marine features; Bokhorst et al. 2019 10.1016/j.cub.2019.04.038 already shows N footprint and size; Otero et al. 2018 10.1038/s41467-017-02446-8 global seabird nutrient budgets; Koerich et al. 2025/2026 10.1002/ecog.08166 continental moss SDMs with seabird distance and wetness; Walshaw et al. 2024 10.1038/s41561-024-01492-4 vegetation atlas. Static distance or nutrient budget alone is not novelty. Target is observed reallocation of geographic source proximity relative to the same receptor landscape under environmental change; whether this yields substantive biological novelty remains to be assessed, no novelty guarantee.
V17–21 and earlier local results retained as mechanisms/context; not primary new paper. Existing original polynya, breeding-space and Antarctic v0.6 drafts/main unchanged. No manuscript merge/submission.
